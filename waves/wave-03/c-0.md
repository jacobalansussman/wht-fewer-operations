**Verdict in four lines.**
1. The circuit is right. I replayed it with my own code at every size g-1 claims and one size further, in both counts. Nothing failed.
2. Count (O): OpenAI's circuit model charges exactly what g-1 charges, and 4608 is the right comparison at 512 points.
3. As far as I can find it is new as a number: the best published count of this kind (Alman and Rao, 2023) spends 23 operations where g-1 spends 20. The method is theirs, not new.
4. It is a constant in front of n log n. It does not change the order, and the family it belongs to provably cannot.

Words. N = 2^k points. H_N is the Walsh-Hadamard matrix (entries 1 and -1). A *gate* is one step of a straight-line program.
Count (G): a gate is any a*u + b*v, so scalings are free. Count (O): OpenAI's circuit model, one charge per addition,
subtraction or multiplication by a constant. The *textbook* is the usual butterfly program, N log2 N gates in either count.

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

- (G) H_8 x in 19 gates, exact over the integers, each output with factor 1. By tensor product, 54 gates at 16 points and 304 at 64.
- (O) H_8 x / 2 in 20 operations (19 additions or subtractions, one halving). Three layers give H_512 x / 8 in 3840; 512 final
  multiplications by 8 give H_512 x itself in 4352, against the textbook's 4608.
- Inside the model "one merge tree plus a flip walk", 19 is the exact minimum at 8 points.
- Attached by k-cross: the same wiring computes the cube of any 2 by 2 kernel, up to a scaling of each output.

## 3. The count-(O) line

**Does OpenAI's model charge what g-1 charges? Yes** [read: `R/OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/Core.lean`].
- A gate is `add i j`, `sub i j` or `scale c i`, with c any complex constant. The size is the number of gates.
- Outputs name wires, so permutations and fan-out are free. A zero wire is free.
- The circuit must compute the matrix itself, not a multiple. So the final multiplications are owed, and g-1 pays them.
- My programs are explicit lists of these three gates, evaluated with that file's semantics.

Two things to keep in view.
- That file states OpenAI's theorem for the complex Fourier matrix. Applying its gate set to H_N is our transfer, a fair one.
- The published theorems live in the RAM model [read: `RAM.lean`, `Goal.lean`]. There one work unit pays for a bounded number
  of primitive steps and the bounds are O() and o() statements. A constant such as 5/6 is invisible in that model.
  The halving is available there: scalars can be added and inverted, so 1/2 costs a bounded number of steps [read].

**Is 4608 the right comparison? Yes at 512 points, with one addition.**
- The best published explicit count I can find is Alman and Rao's: (23/24) N log2 N + N - 1 when 3 divides log2 N [read, through
  a summarising reader, not the PDF by eye]. At 512 points that is 4927, worse than the textbook. It does not win until 2^24 points, and there by one operation [derived from their formula].
- So at 512 the textbook's 4608 is the number to beat, and g-1 beats it by 256.
- The asymptotic comparison is their constant 23/24. g-1's is 5/6.
- For the order, no constant times N log N is the comparison at all. OpenAI's theorem and the published 8.7625e-4 saving are already below every such constant for large n.

## 4. Is it known?

**The method is known. The number, as far as I can find, is not.**

Josh Alman and Kevin Rao, "Faster Walsh-Hadamard and Discrete Fourier Transforms From Matrix Non-Rigidity", STOC 2023,
arXiv:2211.06459 [read]:
- Their idea: write a small H as a low-rank matrix plus a sparse one, halve once per brick, push all doublings to the end.
- 4 points: 7 additions and 1 halving. This is tonight's "4-point brick" (7 in count (G)). Total 7/8 N log N additions.
- 8 points: 22 additions and 1 halving, so 23. Total (23/24) N log N + O(N). They call it the first gain on the textbook.
- Same accounting as g-1: a halving per brick, about N multiplications by a power of two at the end.

Side by side, per 8 points:

| | additions | halvings | operations | leading constant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| textbook | 24 | 0 | 24 | 1 |
| Alman and Rao | 22 | 1 | 23 | 23/24 = 0.958 |
| g-1 | 19 | 1 | 20 | 5/6 = 0.833 |

Additions alone: Alman and Rao's best is 7/8 = 0.875; g-1's is 19/24 = 0.792.

What g-1's circuit is in their language [ran, `rank1.out`]: with one row negated, H_8 is "every row equal to one sign vector"
plus a matrix with 24 non-zero entries, all 2 or -2. That is low rank plus sparse with rank ONE, where they used rank two.
The 24 corrections cost only 12 gates because they are nested and four of them are pieces the merge tree already holds.

How hard I looked: four web searches (one extended) and the citing-paper list at Semantic Scholar, which showed only 7 entries and
is surely incomplete. Nothing with a constant below 23/24, and no 19 or 20 for H_8. A 2024 seminar abstract by Alman mentions
"very recent progress" with co-authors without detail; I could not tell what it covers. So: **new as far as I can find, not established.**

**What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": not linear.**
- A fixed brick tensored up gives (brick cost / (k 2^k)) N log N. The order changes only if that ratio tends to zero as the
  brick grows, which is the original question again.
- Every wire of this circuit is a vector of 0, 1, -1. Between such vectors a gate has |a| + |b| at most 3 [derived, checked by
  cases]. Morgenstern's determinant argument (1973) then forces at least N log2 N / (2 log2 3) = 0.315 N log2 N gates.
  g-1 says this itself (its wall A) and it is right. The constant for sign-vector circuits lies between 0.315 and 0.792.

## 5. Does it scale?

**Growth law observed: a fixed constant times N log2 N.**

| points | (G) gates | ratio to textbook | (O) operations | ratio to textbook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 19 | 0.792 | 28 for H_8 itself | 1.167 |
| 64 | 304 | 0.792 | 384 | 1.000 |
| 512 | 3648 | 0.792 | 4352 | 0.944 |
| 4096 | (formula) 38912 | 0.792 | 45056 | 0.917 |

The (O) ratio falls toward 5/6 and stops there.

I pushed the model once further than g-1 [ran, `walk2.out`]. g-1's exact minimum covers merge trees made of cosets of subgroups
(blocks of addresses closed under one shift). I took every balanced merge tree: any pairing of the 8 addresses and any grouping
of the pairs into two fours, 315 trees in 4 symmetry classes. Exact minimum over all centres:

| class of trees | how many | best count |
|---|---|---|
| cosets of subgroups (g-1's) | 21 | **19** |
| second class | 42 | 21 |
| third class | 84 | 22 |
| fourth class | 168 | 23 |

So 19 stands, and only the subgroup trees reach it. Not covered by either of us: lopsided merge trees, two trees, 16 points exactly.

## 6. In the user's terms

- Circuit size, count (O), for n = 2^k with m = floor(k/3) at least 1: **n k - n m / 2 + n**. When 3 divides k: (5/6) n log2 n + n.
  It beats the textbook for every k from 9 on.
- Circuit size, count (G), n a power of 8: (19/24) n log2 n.
- Saving exponent: **zero**. Nothing toward 1e-1, nothing toward linear size.
- Worth one line in a notebook: if the literature check holds, this improves the published constant for the transform from
  23/24 to 5/6. That is below the standing bar and I did not pursue it.

## 7. The attached class claim (k-cross)

**Holds, with one exception** [ran, `klass.out`].
- I kept only the wiring and solved every coefficient myself, kernel by kernel, as an exact linear system.
- 300 random kernels with four non-zero rational entries (297 distinct cross ratios ad/(bc)): the circuit exists and replays for all 300.
- Kernels with a zero entry, such as subset sums [[1,1],[1,0]]: my recipe divides by zero. There the textbook needs only 12 gates, so 19 is no use.

What it means. In count (G) a kernel matters only through its cross ratio, so this is a one-parameter identity. The 19 gates
use nothing special about the transform. That agrees with the known theory, where non-rigidity is a property of Kronecker powers
of any small matrix (Alman, STOC 2021 [from memory, not re-read tonight]). It also says the circuit is blind to what makes a kernel
cheap: at the subset-sum end of the family the true cost drops to half and the identity does not see it.

## 8. Corrections and limits

- g-1 section 8 says it did not search the literature. It should cite Alman and Rao: the 4-point brick and the halving trick are theirs.
- "The first gain in OpenAI's count tonight" is true of tonight. In print the first such gain is theirs (23 against 24).
- "Exact minimum" means inside the model. I widened the model once; lopsided trees and all circuits outside it are open. The known
  lower bound at 8 points is 15, so 16, 17 and 18 are undecided.
- I did not check g-1's statement that nothing beats 8, 24, 64 for H itself at 4, 8, 16 points in count (O).
- I did not check its ledger tables, the hub theorem, or wall B.
- Alman and Rao's formula for sizes where 3 does not divide log2 N is taken from a summary; the comparison at 1024 points in `big.out` rests on it.
