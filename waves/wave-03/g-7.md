# g-7-thirdturn: the transform is "multiply the label by a cube root of one"; every such renaming ties, at 4 and at 16 points

2026-10-10, 60-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing reviewed, nothing in Lean.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `g-7-thirdturn/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, [read] = from a report,
[guess] = marked guess. Counts: (G) = a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings and relabellings free; (O) = OpenAI's count.
Everything below is count (G) unless a line says (O). No claim below the best known count is made anywhere in this report.

Words. S = the transform in its symmetric form, kernel (-1)^(p1.y2 + p2.y1), the address cut in two halves [d-4].
T = the free sign change (-1)^Q0, Q0(x) = p1.p2. R = T S / sqrt(N). A *form* is a linear combination of the inputs.
A *seed* is a form f; its *cycle* is f, fR, fR^2. B = the N x N matrix whose rows are the cycles of (N-1)/3 seeds and one
form fixed by R. A *half transform* = the transform applied to the first half of the address only (sqrt(N) separate copies
of the sqrt(N)-point transform). A *twist* = the transform preceded by one of the N free sign changes
T_a = (-1)^(Q0 + w(a,.)), w the pairing in the kernel. The *centraliser* = the matrices that commute with the relabelling;
multiplying B by one of them gives another valid B. *Pair sharing* = my yardstick for 16 points, defined in section 4.

## 0. In eight lines

- The seed is true as stated, at 4, 16 and 64 points [ran]: R^3 = I, trace 1, R is orthogonal, fixed space of dimension
  (N+2)/3, R is the tensor power of the 4-point R, and T(N) <= cost(B) + cost(B^-1) for every B of the brief's kind.
- **What R is, exactly** [ran at 4, 16, 64]: after a half transform, R is a pure relabelling with no signs at all,
  (a,b) -> (b, a+b). Read the pair (a,b) as one label in (the field with four elements)^(k/2), k = log2 N: R is **multiplication of the
  label by a cube root of one**; T is the field's conjugation and S is the two together. The (N-1)/3 three-cycles are the points of the projective
  space over that field: 1, 5, 21. And a coordinate seed runs through the three classical kinds of form:
  coordinate -> row of the transform -> sign vector of a quadratic form (an eigenvector of S) -> coordinate.
- **A classical object turned up** [ran at 16 points]: the bases of this kind that every twist of the transform keeps are
  27 in number, each "meets" 10 others and is "skew" to 16, and the transform keeps 15 of them and exchanges two sixes:
  the configuration of the 27 lines on a cubic surface with a double six (section 2b). It names the symmetry group of the idea; it lowers no count.
- **A second one** [ran at 4, 16, 64]: the fixed parts of the coordinates are the largest sets of equiangular lines:
  3 in the plane, 10 in dimension 5, 28 in dimension 7, 36 in dimension 15. g-0's mirror holds the 16 of dimension 6.
- **4 points, exact:** the least cost(B) + cost(B^-1) over all seeds is **8**, not 7 [derived, checked on 5,418 bases].
  The idea in its pure form is a program that keeps only N values at a time and reads backwards the way it reads forwards;
  the 7-gate brick keeps a fifth value.
- **16 points:** in about 112,000 bases tried, with seeds from the 4,320 "stabilizer" vectors and then from the whole freedom
  the brief names, the least cost(B) under pair sharing is **32, and the least cost(B) + cost(B^-1) is 64**, the textbook, to
  the last gate; thousands of different bases tie, none goes below [ran]. With the 7-gate brick allowed the half-transform
  basis gives 28 + 28 = **56, the best known count**. No basis found does better; my yardstick cannot see bricks in mixed
  bases (section 8).
- The critic's question, answered by the count: **cost(B) hides exactly half a transform and cost(B^-1) the other half**
  (32 and 32 of 64 at 16 points; 4 and 4 of 8 at 4 points). Section 6 says why, in three lines.
- State: the idea is alive as a description and dead as a shortcut in every form I could run. It turned into one clean
  number to aim at (section 7): a discount on "many copies at once" of 6.7 per cent at every squaring would be a saving of 1e-1.

## 1. The seed, checked [ran, `basics.py`, `basics.out`, exact fractions]

| N | R^3 = I | trace | R R^T = I | fixed space | R = tensor power of the 4-point R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | yes | 1 | yes | 2 | - |
| 16 | yes | 1 | yes | 6 | yes |
| 64 | yes | 1 | yes | 22 | yes |

[derived] R has minimal polynomial x^3 - 1, so over the rationals its normal form is: the number 1, (N+2)/3 times, and the
2 x 2 block of x^2 + x + 1, (N-1)/3 times. A relabelling with (N-1)/3 three-cycles and one fixed point has the same normal
form, so R = B^-1 P B over the rationals; the rows of B are f, fR, fR^2 for each seed and one fixed form. The curator's
derivation is right. S = sqrt(N) T B^-1 P B, T and P free, hence T(N) <= cost(B) + cost(B^-1).
At 4 points the seed x0 gives x0, x0+x1+x2+x3, x0+x1+x2-x3, as the brief says [ran]; the fixed forms are spanned by x0+x1
and x0+x2 [ran].

## 2. What the renaming is (three readings; the second one is the find)

**2a. Coordinate, row of the transform, quadratic form.** [ran at 4, 16, 64] For every address x: e_x R = +-(row x of S)/sqrt(N),
and e_x R^2 = +-(-1)^(Q0 + w(x,.))/sqrt(N), a sign vector of a quadratic form, which is an eigenvector of S; one more step
returns to e_x. So R turns the standard basis into the basis of transform rows into d-4's eigenbasis and back. At 4 points
the cycle of x3 is (x3, a transform row, the helper x0-x1-x2-x3 of the 7-gate brick) [derived]: **the brick's helper is the
third member of a coordinate's cycle.**

**2b. Multiplication by a cube root of one.** [ran at 4, 16, 64] Take the half-transform basis: forms
v(a,b) = sum over p1 of (-1)^(a.p1) x(p1, b). Then v(a,b) R = v(b, a+b), with sign +1 for every label, at all three sizes.
Write the label as z = a + b*w in the field with four elements (w^2 = w + 1), one field element per pair of address bits.
Then (a,b) -> (b, a+b) is z -> w*z, coordinate by coordinate. So:

> after a half transform, R is "multiply every label by w"; T is "(a,b) -> (a+b, b)", which is the field's conjugation
> z -> z^2; S is "(a,b) -> (b,a)", which is z -> w * conj(z). The six maps z -> w^i z and z -> w^i conj(z) are the whole
> group generated by S and T. (The rule for R is [ran]; the rules for S and T separately are [derived] from it, signs not run.)

[ran, `stats16.out`] At 16 points all sixteen sign changes T_a = (-1)^(Q0 + w(a,.)) times S are relabellings in this ONE
basis, each with one fixed label and five three-cycles ([derived] z -> w*z + c, a different shift c for each coset of the plane).
[derived] The same holds for the half transform along any of the planes on which w vanishes (15 at 16 points, 135 at 64).
Each is a B of the brief's kind, each has sqrt(N) non-zeros in every row, each costs a half transform. This is wall W1 seen
from inside.

**The 27 lines.** [ran, `lines27.py`, `lines27.out`] At 16 points the pool holds exactly **27** bases (each: one vector
and its images under address shifts and characters) that R maps to themselves, and then so does every twist T_a S:
- 15 are the half transforms (4 non-zeros per row); 12 have all rows flat (16 non-zeros);
- S keeps each of the 15 and swaps the 12 in six pairs; T does the same;
- call two bases "meeting" when every row of one spreads over 4 rows of the other, "skew" when over all 16 (no other
  value occurs): **every basis meets exactly 10 others and is skew to 16**;
- [ran, `lines27b.out`] a half-transform basis meets 6 half-transform bases and 4 flat ones; a flat basis meets 5 flat ones
  and 5 half-transform ones; the 12 flat bases fall into two sixes, no two of the same six meet, each meets five of the
  other six, and **S swaps each flat basis with the one basis of the other six that it does not meet.**
This is, number for number, the configuration of the 27 lines on a cubic surface with one of its "double sixes" marked:
27 lines, each meeting 10; two sixes a1..a6, b1..b6 with a_i meeting b_j exactly when i differs from j; 15 further lines.
The transform is the symmetry that exchanges a_i with b_i and keeps the 15. [derived] The reason: the address shifts and characters form a 4-dimensional space over the
field with four elements on which R is the scalar w; a basis kept by R is a plane of that space on which a Hermitian form
(linear in one argument, conjugate-linear in the other) vanishes, and that form's surface over the field with four elements is the Fermat cubic surface, with (2+1)(2^3+1) = 27
lines. Next sizes by the same count: 3 at 4 points [ran: three planes], 891 at 64 points [derived, not run].
The symmetry group of the idea at 16 points is therefore the group of the 27 lines (the unitary group in 4 dimensions over
the field with four elements; its simple part has order 25,920 [recalled]) [derived]; d-4 met E8 at 256 points through the eigenvectors of S,
this is E6 at 16 points through the cube root. I did not use this group in a search.
[derived] The bases in which S and T are relabellings separately are only the 15: they must be kept by the conjugation as
well. So "share one B between all the T's" allows 27 bases at 16 points, 12 of them dearer than a half transform, none cheaper.

**The fixed space carries the largest sets of equiangular lines.** [ran, `equiang.py`, `equiang.out`, exact, N = 4, 16, 64]
The fixed part of a coordinate x (its projection onto the fixed space U of R) depends on the class of x: the classes
Q0 = 0 and Q0 = 1 are perpendicular to each other, and inside a class any two fixed parts make the same angle:

| N | class Q0 = 0: how many, in what dimension, cosine | class Q0 = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 3 in dimension 2, cosine 1/2 | 1, of length zero (this is the brick, section 3) |
| 16 | 10 in dimension 5, cosine 1/3 | 6 in dimension 1 (all parallel) |
| 64 | 36 in dimension 15, cosine 1/5 | 28 in dimension 7, cosine 1/3 |

3 in the plane, 10 in dimension 5, 28 in dimension 7 and 36 in dimension 15 are the largest possible numbers of
equiangular lines in those dimensions [recalled]. g-0 found the 16 lines of dimension 6 in the mirror at 16 points; the
third-turn's fixed space holds the others. [derived] By the rule g-0 used (turn the circuit around: the cost of a set of
forms is governed by how many of these points lie on lines through two others), "no three on a line" is why reading the
fixed space is dear, here as there. At 16 points the 15 forms x_a +- x_b on the six addresses with Q0 = 1 lie wholly in
the turned part, and their cycles have 2, 8 and 8 non-zeros and add up to zero [ran, `turned16.out`]: the 16-point
cousins of the brick's identity. They cost more than they save in every arrangement I tried by hand (about 47 against 32).

**2c. What is left of the brief's freedom.** B is free up to the centraliser of the relabelling (about N^2/3 parameters), but
only if one asks for R alone, not for the other fifteen twists. That freedom is what sections 3 and 4 search.

## 3. First experiment: 4 points, exact

**The least cost(B) + cost(B^-1) is 8.** [derived]
- If no row of B is a coordinate, B has four different non-coordinate rows, so cost(B) >= 4; a row of B^-1 is a coordinate
  only if a row of B is, so cost(B^-1) >= 4.
- If a row of B is a coordinate x_i, it is a seed (no fixed form is a coordinate), and the other two rows of its cycle have
  four non-zeros each: two different such forms need 4 gates. For B^-1 with the seed x0 I checked by hand, for every fixed
  form, that its three non-coordinate rows cannot all be single gates. [ran, `four_coord.out`] For the seeds x0, x1, x2 and
  every fixed form a(x0+x1) + b(x0+x2) with |a|, |b| up to 12 (549 bases): neither B nor B^-1 can be made without a helper,
  so 4 + 4 again. x3 is not a seed at all: the three members of its cycle add up to zero.
- That last fact is the brick [derived]: x3 - (its transform row)/2 + (the helper x0-x1-x2-x3)/2 = 0 is the identity
  "row 3 of S = 2 x3 + helper". The 7-gate saving lives exactly where the idea's basis degenerates, and it happens only at
  4 points (the squared length of the fixed part of a coordinate is (1 + 2 T_x / sqrt(N))/3, zero only for N = 4, T_x = -1).
- 8 is reached twice over: seed x0+x3 gives B = (x0+x3, x1+x2, x0-x3, x1-x2), two butterflies in, two out: the textbook.
  Seed x0 with fixed form x0+x1 gives the chain B = (x0, x0+x1, x0+x1+x2+x3, x0+x1+x2-x3), 4 gates, inverse 4 gates.
[ran, `basics.out`] 5,418 invertible B (seeds with entries -2..2, ten fixed forms): none has a lower bound under 8; the test
"all rows are single gates" is exact.

So against the exact minimum 7 in count (G) the pure idea loses by one, and it equals count (O)'s exact 8.
What the failure is made of: B^-1 (relabel) B, with B computed in place, is a program that never holds more than N values.
[ran, `inplace4.py`, `inplace4_p3.out`, `inplace4_p5.out`, `inplace4_p7.out`] Least number of gates for the 4-point
transform when no extra value may be kept (every gate overwrites one of its two operands), exhaustive meet-in-the-middle:
modulo 5 more than 7 (385,089 states forward, 432,864 backward); modulo 7 more than 7 (2,076,310 forward, 4,964,382
backward); modulo 3 a 7-gate program exists, which I did not lift (modulo 3 the numbers 2 and -1 are the same, so it is
probably an accident of that field). "None modulo 5 and 7" is not "none". [guess] Over the rationals the answer is 8 and
the brick's saving is bought with the fifth value.

## 4. 16 points: the count

**Yardstick (pair sharing).** A new node u + c*v is made whenever the same pair with the same ratio occurs in two or more
targets; then each target is finished alone. It is an upper bound in count (G); it has no helper wires, so it gives 8 for
the 4-point transform, 32 for the half transform at 16 points and 64 for the 16-point transform [ran]. Read every number
below as "textbook-style sharing".

**The pool.** [ran, `search16.py`] The orbit of a coordinate under butterflies, the sign changes (-1)^(x_i x_j) and (-1)^(x_i),
and address additions: 4,320 vectors. R permutes them: 27 fixed (15 with four non-zeros, 12 with sixteen), 1,431 three-cycles.
Non-zeros of the three members of a cycle:

| (1,16,16) | (2,8,8) | (4,4,4) | (4,4,16) | (4,16,16) | (8,8,8) | (16,16,16) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 240 | 75 | 320 | 240 | 480 | 60 |

The 75 cycles of type (4,4,4) are exactly the half-transform vectors of the 15 planes [ran]. A row with four non-zeros and an
odd number of minus signs always brings a row with sixteen non-zeros into its cycle [derived from the table and one line].

- Bases that mix three, four or five different planes reach 32 as well, never 31 [ran, `stats16.out`]: d-4's "hybrid bases
  tie" holds for the non-perpendicular ones too.
- The chain basis is a different animal and it ties too: its rows are sums over sub-cubes (x0; x0+x1; x0+x1+x2+x3;
  x0+...+x7; the sum of everything; ...) with 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 non-zeros (121 in all), and its inverse has 64 non-zeros,
  as few as the half transform. It is the subset-sum factorisation H = Z D Z^T of i-cross seen as a renaming in threes [derived from the printed rows].
- With the brick allowed: the half-transform basis is four copies of the 4-point transform, 4 x 7 = 28, and its inverse 28:
  **56**, the best known count. Thirdturn with the plain basis IS the 56-gate circuit.

They are **not one object**, but they meet at the same total: thirdturn 28 + 28 = 56, the mirror at least 16 + 10 + 30 = 56

## 6. Why it ties: three statements

1. [derived] **Transposition.** Turn the circuit for B around: it computes u -> (transform of F^T u), where F holds the seeds,
   i.e. the transform of every vector in a fixed subspace of dimension (N-1)/3, given by its coordinates. So cost(B) is at
   least the cost of the transform on a third of the space (minus N). B does hide a transform, restricted.
2. [derived, sketch] **Even rows.** If all rows have four non-zeros on a plane with an even number of minus signs, a program
   without cancellation needs 16 pair nodes (rows that are sums of two pair nodes have rank at most the number of pair
   nodes) plus 16 finishing gates: 32. (A row made as a three-term node plus one input uses a node that serves no other
   even row; argued, not enumerated.)
   Going below needs rows with an odd sign pattern or rows that are not pool vectors; the first kind drags a 16-term row
   into its cycle, the second kind is what the "whole freedom" searches tried.
3. [derived] **Uncertainty in threes.** non-zeros(f) * non-zeros(fR) >= N for each consecutive pair of a cycle, so a cycle has
   at least 3 sqrt(N) non-zeros and B at least about N sqrt(N). That alone does not forbid linear size (prefix sums are
   dense and cheap); what the searches say is that the cheap dense families (chains of sub-cube sums) tie as well.

## 7. "Yes, and": four changes of the idea

1. **Any seeds, not pool seeds** (the whole centraliser). Ran, section 4: ties.
2. **One B for several T's.** Section 2b: it only narrows the choice, to 27 bases at 16 points, the 15 half transforms and
   12 dearer ones [ran for the pool; derived beyond it].
3. **No extra wire.** T(N) <= 2 * (least number of in-place gates that turn R into a relabelling by conjugation). A clean search
   problem (a walk in the conjugacy class of a relabelling). At 4 points its answer is 4 + 4, and the in-place minimum for the
   transform itself is more than 7 modulo 5 and modulo 7. Reading: everything that has ever beaten the textbook here keeps an extra value.
   The published program's "scratch copies" lead is the same statement at large size [guess].
4. **Turns of other orders.** [derived, not run] For any free twist G = M1 S M2 (M1, M2 relabellings with scalings) and any
   cheap normal form K of G (a relabelling when G has finite order; a companion matrix, at most N - 1 gates, always),
   T(N) <= cost(B) + cost(B^-1) + cost(K) whenever B G = K B. The half-turn (G = S, rows f, fS) is the mirror's cousin, the
   third-turn is this brief. A sign change by a general quadratic form gives turns of higher order. They cannot have
   sparser cycles: non-zeros(f G^i) * non-zeros(f G^(i+1)) >= N around the cycle gives, by the inequality between the
   arithmetic and the geometric mean, at least sqrt(N) non-zeros per row on average for a turn of ANY order, and the
   half transform has exactly that. So order 3 loses nothing against other orders, and gains nothing either.

**The number to aim at.** With the half-transform basis the idea is T(N) <= 2 * (sqrt(N) copies of the sqrt(N)-point
transform at once). If many copies at once came at a discount d against separate copies at every squaring of N, then
T(N)/N would grow like (log N)^(1 + log2 d): d = 7/8 at ONE level gives 56 and nothing more (the factor is kept, not
compounded); d = 0.933 at EVERY level would be a saving of 1e-1. [derived] This is j-manyatonce's question with a number on it.

## 8. Criticism, last

- Nothing here lowers a count. 8 at 4 points is above the known 7; 64 and 56 at 16 points are the textbook and the known best.
- "32 is the least" at 16 points is annealing on a yardstick, not a proof: about 112,000 bases out of a space of 10^15 pool
  choices and a continuum beyond. The yardstick has no helper wires, so a mixed basis that used helpers better than four
  bricks (under 28) would be invisible to it. I give one reason it is unlikely (a helper lives on one plane; a plane with
  fewer than four rows gains nothing from it) and no run.
- The 4-point proof is complete for seeds without a coordinate row and for the seed x0; for x1 and x2 it rests on a run
  over fixed forms of height up to 12. The in-place statements are modulo 3, 5 and 7 only (section 3).
- "Largest possible" for the equiangular sets is recalled from the literature, not checked tonight; the sets themselves
  and their angles are exact runs.
- The "27 lines" are identified by their meeting pattern (27; 10 and 16; 6 + 4 and 5 + 5; two sixes exchanged by S; all
  ran) and by a hand argument for the reason, not by building the cubic surface. The 27 bases are counted inside the pool of real stabilizer vectors only.
- The field-with-four-elements reading is the classical Weil representation of SL2 over two elements; it is a way to say
  the textbook, not a new algorithm. What may be worth keeping is the dictionary (label = field element, transform =
  conjugation, twist = cube root) and the fact that the brick's helper is a member of a coordinate's cycle.
- 64 points: two families evaluated and two five-minute walks (4,841 bases in all), which is very little at that size.
