Words. N = 2^k points. H is the matrix of signs (-1)^(number of common 1-bits of row and column); its rows are the outputs.
Count **(G)**: a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free. Count **(O)**: OpenAI's count, every addition, subtraction and
multiplication by a constant costs one. The *textbook* is N log2 N in both counts (for H x itself).
A *piece* is one output restricted to a block of inputs and zero elsewhere. A *hub* is a wire that is one output on the
lower half of a block and a different output on the upper half.

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

- (G1) For every k >= 2 there is a program for the N-point transform with exactly (3/4) N k + N/4 gates, every gate
  a*u + b*v with a, b in {+-1, +-2}, and every output exactly its row of H with factor 1. Sizes 4, 8, 16, 32, 64: 7, 20, 52, 128, 304.
- (G2) It rests on one lemma and one sign rule, and was run by its authors for k = 2..6 only.
- (O1) The same wiring, with wires held at chosen powers of two, gives H x / 4 at 16 points in 60 operations of count (O)
  (52 additions or subtractions and 8 halvings), "against 64".
- (O2) For N = 4^t: H x / sqrt(N) in (7/8) N k + N/4, and H x itself in (7/8) N k + (5/4) N.
- Not claimed by e2-0: any change of order. Its own first line says "still N log N".

## 3. The induction beyond k = 6: it is a proof  [derived, then confirmed by section 2]

Take a block of size m with quarters LL, LR, RL, RR and a quarter-size sign pattern y (index g < m/4). Suppose we hold
A = (y, y, 0, 0) and B = (0, 0, y, -y). For a sign e asked of this block, put tau = +1 if g < m/8, else -1, and c = -e*tau.
- hub M = A + c*B = (y, y, c*y, -c*y): one gate.
- the piece (y, y, e*y, e*y) equals M + 2e*W, where W is y on RL if tau = +1 and y on RR if tau = -1: one gate.
- the piece (y, -y, e*y, -e*y) equals -tau*M + 2*tau*W', where W' is y on LL if tau = +1 and y on LR if tau = -1: one gate.
Check: (y, y, e*y, e*y) - M = (0, 0, (e-c)*y, (e+c)*y), and c = -e*tau kills exactly one of the two entries. The same for the second.
The only question an induction could fail on is whether W and W' are held. They are: the left half was asked for its "+"
pieces and the right half for its "-" pieces, so inside each half the quarter pattern g sits on the lower quarter when
g < m/8 and on the upper quarter otherwise, and that is exactly where tau sends W and W'. This is the same sentence at every
size, so it holds for every k. At the top the hub is A + B and yields four outputs (two as above, two more by
"other = -first + 2A" and "other = first -+ 2B"). Count: D(2) = 1, D(m) = 2 D(m/2) + 3m/4, T(N) = 2 D(N/2) + 5N/4, hence
T(N) = (3/4) N log2 N + N/4. I found no gap. (G2) stands, and "derived for every k" in e2-0 is justified.

What is wrong with "60 against 64":
1. **Different functions.** 60 is the price of H x / 4; 64 is the textbook price of H x. For H x itself the family costs
   76 at 16 points, which is above 64. e2-0 says this in its table and its "not checked" line, but its headline does not.
2. **The fair rival for H x / 4 is also 64, so the 60 is real, for that function.** The 4-point brick (7 additions and one
   halving for H x / 2) is Alman and Rao's Algorithm 9; twice it gives H x / 4 at 16 points in 64. So "60 against 64" is
   true once both sides compute H x / 4. Within the night it is already beaten: h-3 reports 56 for H x / 2 at 16 points [read, its first lines, not replayed by me].
3. **OpenAI's statement wants H x itself and cannot see a constant.** [read, `R/OAI/Computability/FourierTransform/RAM.lean`
   lines 150 to 190] `add`, `sub` and `scale` are each billed one, as count (O) says; but the machine also bills every
   composition, loop step and array read (lines 229 to 245), and the theorem is an O-statement. h-3 read the goal file and
   found the same [read h-3 section 1; I did not open `Goal.lean`]. A factor 7/8 or 3/4 in front of N log N is invisible there.
4. **Can the N final multiplications be avoided on this wiring?** [derived, for scales that depend on the level only] A gate
   "piece = +-hub +- 2*witness" is free of multiplications only if the witness (two levels down) is held at twice the scale
   of the piece. Inputs and outputs of H x both have scale 1, so along k levels a mismatch of k/2 doublings must be paid
   somewhere; paying it at the top costs N/2 (witnesses) + N/2 (half pieces) + N/4 (hubs) = 5N/4, the same as the authors'
   N/4 + N. I see no cheaper place. [ran, `scales.py`, `scales_16_rule.out`] A search over one free power of two per wire at 16 points, started from the authors' rule (40 annealing runs),
   found nothing below 60 for a common factor and nothing below 76 for H x itself. Started from random scales the same search
   only reached 101 and 107 (printed in my session, not saved to a file), so it is a weak, local tool: it says the rule is a local optimum, no more.

So the nearest true statements: "H x / 4 at 16 points in 60 operations (O)" and "H x itself in (7/8) N k + (5/4) N for
k even: 76 at 16 points, equal to the textbook at 1,024 points, first below it at 4,096 points".

## 5. Is it known?  [read tonight: arXiv:2211.06459v2 through a fetched text, three web searches; a short search]

- **The 4-point member is known.** Josh Alman and Kevin Rao, "Faster Walsh-Hadamard and Discrete Fourier Transforms From
  Matrix Non-Rigidity", arXiv:2211.06459 (STOC 2023). Section 6.1, Algorithm 9: H_4 as (rank one) + 2*(sparse), 7 additions or
  subtractions and one halving; Theorem 6.1: (7/8) N log N additions, (1/8) N log N halvings, N - 1 multiplications by a power
  of two. They credit the 4-point decomposition to Alman, arXiv:2102.11992 (2021). Their main Theorem 1.1 uses an 8-point
  decomposition (23 operations in place of 24): (23/24) N log N + (13/12) N operations, of which (22/24) N log N additions.
  The fetched text also has them saying they could not design an improved algorithm from a decomposition of H_16 [read in a
  machine summary of the page; not checked against the PDF by eye].
- **The family beyond 4 points I did not find anywhere.** In Alman and Rao's own accounting (halvings and power-of-two
  multiplications counted) the family reads, for N = 4^t: (3/4) N log N additions + (1/8) N log N halvings + N multiplications.
  That is fewer additions than their 7/8 and fewer operations in total than their 23/24: e2-0's section 6 survives my
  check. At 16 points: 76 against 78.7 from their formula, both above the textbook's 64.
- **Not read, so "new" is not established:** Alman and Li, arXiv:2509.14489 (September 2025); Alman, Guan and Padaki,
  arXiv:2211.05217 (they count wires of circuits with gates of any number of inputs: a different count; I did not convert it
  to gates); Sergeev's notes of 2022 on the same wire count; anything from 2026. OpenAI's own result is below every constant
  for large N, so in that paper's terms a constant is not news.
- **What the known theory predicts for "linear or not".** Morgenstern's determinant bound (1973): a gate a*u + b*v can multiply
  the largest determinant available by at most |a| + |b|. Here |a| + |b| <= 3 and det H = N^(N/2), so any program with these
  coefficients has at least (N log2 N) / (2 log2 3) = 0.3155 N log2 N gates [derived from the theorem as e2-0 states it;
  I re-did the arithmetic only]. The family is at 0.75. **No program with coefficients 1 and 2 is linear, whatever its wiring.**

## 6. Does it scale, and what does it give in the user's terms

- Growth law observed, k = 2..13, exact at every size: gates = (3/4) N log2 N + N/4; the saving below the textbook is exactly
  (N/4)(k - 1). The ratio to the textbook falls 0.875, 0.833, 0.8125, ..., 0.769 and tends to 3/4. It does not keep falling.
- In the user's terms: **a circuit of size (3/4) n log2 n + n/4 in count (G); (7/8) n log2 n + (5/4) n in count (O) for H x
  itself (n a power of 4). Saving exponent: none.** A fixed factor in front of n log n is a saving of 0 in the exponent of
  the log; it is not a step toward 1e-1 and not toward linear size.
- Why it cannot be pushed: the mechanism saves one gate per hub and there are N/4 hubs per level, at every level, forever.
  To get a falling constant the number of pieces held per level would have to fall with the level; e2-0's section 5 argues
  this is impossible in its class ("wall lemma"). I did not check that lemma (time); the Morgenstern line above already
  closes the door for these coefficients without it.
- As a small unit for the published engine: not assessed here. h-3 argues it would change the cost of a move by a constant only.
