# g-0-mirror: the cheapest basis of the mirror at 16 points is 24 gates, exactly; the whole route then ties 56, by proof

2026-10-10, 60-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing reviewed, nothing in Lean.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `g-0-mirror/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, [read] = from a report,
[recalled] = from memory, not checked tonight, [guess] = marked guess.
Gate count: everything below is count (G) (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free) unless a line says (O).

## 0. In six lines

- The first experiment has an exact answer and it did not need the big search. **At 16 points the least number of gates for six
  independent forms of the mirror is 24** (4 gates per form). Upper bound: a 24-gate circuit, replayed in exact fractions, every
  output checked to be an eigenform, rank 6 [ran]. Lower bound 24: a short hand argument resting on three facts that I checked by
  machine [derived + ran]. The brief's two ends were 20 and 34: the truth is 24, and the freedom of choosing the basis is real.
- Why it was easy: turning the circuit around (the transposition rule) makes the problem small. **cost(C) = 2(N - r) + x**, where
  x is the least number of helper points one must add to N fixed points of the r-dimensional mirror so that everything can be
  reached from r of them by drawing lines through two points. At 16 points: 20 + x, and x = 4.
- The N fixed points are a classical object: **the 16 equiangular lines in 6 dimensions** (the sign vectors of length 6 with an even
  number of minus signs). The four helpers are vectors with two non-zeros (the roots of the D6 lattice); each sits on 4 lines
  through pairs of the 16.
- The whole transform by this route costs 16 + 10 + G(B) + G(B') + (a 6 x 6 middle), and **G(B) + G(B') + middle >= 30 for every
  choice** [derived]. So the route gives at least 56 at 16 points: the best known count, again to the last gate. The curator's
  rough "20 + 30 + 16" was too pessimistic (the true floor of the route is 56, not 66) but the conclusion stands: no record.
- What makes 16 cheap disappears at 64: there the shortest linear relation among the fixed points has 8 terms, not 4, so no
  helper lies on two lines through pairs of them [derived]. I did not get to run 64 points. The growth law is open.
- State: alive at small size, with a better question attached (section 6).

## 1. The object, in the form that made it small

S is the 16-point transform with kernel (-1)^(p1.y2 + p2.y1) (address cut in two halves) [read: d-4]. Its eigenvalue -4 has a
6-dimensional eigenspace, "the mirror". C0 = the six sign vectors (-1)^Q of the elliptic quadratic forms, as rows (6 x 16).
[ran, `model.py`] C0 S = -4 C0; C0 C0^T = 16 I; C0^T C0 / 16 = (I - S/4)/2, the projector onto the mirror.

**Turn the circuit around.** [recalled: the transposition rule for linear circuits; derived for this case] A circuit for a
6 x 16 matrix C with g gates, read backwards, is a circuit for C-transpose with g - 16 + 6 gates. C-transpose has 6 inputs and
16 outputs, and its outputs are the 16 columns of C. Any basis of the mirror is C = A C0 with A invertible, so its columns are
the 16 columns of C0 seen in other coordinates. Hence:

> cost(C) = 10 + G, where G is the least number of gates that reach the 16 columns of C0 (16 fixed points of a 6-dimensional
> space, up to scale), starting from ANY six points of that space, each gate producing a point on the line through two points
> already present.

All 16 points must appear and only 6 are free, so G = 10 + x with x = the number of points used that are not among the 16
("helpers", whether they are start points or gates). **cost(C) = 20 + x.** The brief's lower bound 20 is x = 0. The same holds
at every size: cost(C) = 2(N - r) + x(N) = N + sqrt(N) + x(N). **A linear-size basis of the mirror is exactly x(N) = O(N).**

**The 16 points.** [ran] The columns of C0 are the 16 vectors with entries 1 and -1 of length 6 with an even number of minus
signs, up to an overall sign. Square length 6, inner products 2 or -2: 16 lines at one common angle (cosine 1/3), the largest
such set in 6 dimensions [recalled]. In general the Gram matrix is (I - S/sqrt(N))/2, so the mirror at 64 points is 64 lines in
28 dimensions at cosine 1/7, at 256 points 256 lines in 120 dimensions at cosine 1/15 [derived]. (d-4 saw the 120 at 256 points
as the roots of E8 up to sign; at 16 points the companion lattice is D6.)

Facts about the 16 points [ran, `model.py`]:
- F1. No three are on a line (all 560 triples have rank 3).
- F2. Exactly 60 sets of four lie in a plane (these are the 60 eigenforms of the other eigenspace with four non-zeros).
- F3. Each of the 120 lines through two of the 16 points ("secants") contains exactly one vector with two non-zeros (a "root":
  entries like e_i + e_j or e_i - e_j). There are 30 roots; each lies on exactly 4 secants; the 4 secants use 8 different points.
  Two secants without a common point meet in 180 cases (60 x 3) and 30 roots x 6 pairs = 180, so **they meet only at roots**:
  every other point lies on at most one secant [derived from the two counts].
- F4. 80 lines hold three roots (e.g. e1 - e2, e2 - e3, e1 - e3). A line through two roots never holds one of the 16 points (a
  combination of two roots has at most 4 non-zeros, the points have 6) [derived].

## 2. The exact minimum: x = 4, cost(C) = 24

**Upper bound** [ran, `search.c`, `search_x4.out`, `replay16.py`, `replay16.out`]. Helpers restricted to the 30 roots, every set
of 4 roots, every choice of 6 start points among the 16 + 4: 4,680 of the 27,405 sets of four roots work. One of them, replayed:
start points t0 t1 t2 t4 t5 t8 (six of the 16 points); helpers e5 - e6, e5 + e6, e4 - e5, e3 - e4; 14 gates of the form
"root = half the difference of two points" (4 gates) and "new point = old point plus or minus twice a root" (10 gates).
Turned around it is a forward circuit with **24 gates** on the 16 inputs; its six outputs were checked one by one:
f S = -4 f, and rank 6. For this set of four roots 15,139 different start sets work (with 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 of the 16 points
among them: 64, 960, 4,247, 6,608, 3,260) and every one gives 24 forward gates [ran]. The six forms of the printed circuit have
11, 6, 8, 6, 6, 8 non-zeros, entries 0, 1, -1 and one 2: not characters, not perpendicular to each other.
In count (O) [derived, not replayed in that form]: scale each root by 2 and every gate becomes a plain addition or subtraction,
so the same basis costs 24 additions and no scalar multiplication.

**Lower bound** [derived; uses F1, F3, F4]. A gate needs a line holding at least 3 points of W = (16 points) + (helpers); a line
with m points of W can supply at most m - 2 gates; 10 + x gates are needed. With x = 3 helpers:
- lines with one helper are secants through it: at most 4 if it is a root, at most 1 otherwise (F3);
- lines with two helpers: none holds a point if both are roots (F4); otherwise at most 1 gate each beyond what was counted.
- Three roots: 12, plus 1 if the three roots are on a line; 13 are needed, so they must be on a line; but then the three roots'
  secants miss 4 of the 16 points entirely, those 4 must be start points, and the remaining 2 start points cannot produce the
  rest. Two roots and one other: at most 11. One root: at most 9. No root: at most 6.
So x >= 4 for helpers that are ANY rational points, not only roots. Machine cross-check for roots only: x = 3 fails for all
4,060 sets [ran, `search_x3.out`]. x <= 2 fails by the same count (at most 8 + 2 lines against 12).
What the lower bound rests on that I did not re-derive: the transposition rule (standard) and F3's step from two counts.

**Reading against the brief.** c(16) = cost(C)/r = 24/6 = **4.0**. The character basis is 34 (5.7 per form) [read], the
no-helper floor is 20 (3.3 per form). So the answer at the first size where it could fail is: the freedom is real and worth
10 gates of 34, and it stops 4 above the floor for a reason one can name (no three of the 16 points are on a line).

## 3. The whole transform by this route: at least 56, by a count

Let B = the six start points used for C, B' = the six start points used for putting the mirror back (E). Then [derived]
S x = 4 (x - 2 E M C x) with M the 6 x 6 table of inner products between B' and B, and

> T(16) <= 16 + (10 + G(B)) + cost(M) + G(B').

(When B' is the dual basis of B the middle M is the identity and costs nothing. The brief's "cost(E)" is G(dual of B).)
With t(B) = how many of the 16 points are in B: G(B) = 10 + x >= 10 + max(4, 6 - t(B)).
Two of the 16 points are never perpendicular (inner product 2 or -2). So:
- if B holds two or more of the 16 points, every one of the 16 points in B' gives a row of M with two non-zeros, i.e. a gate;
  and if B' is the dual basis of B it holds none of the 16 points, so G(B') >= 16;
- running through the cases (t, t') = (2+, 2+), (2+, 1), (2+, 0), (1, 1), (1, 0), (0, 0) gives
  **G(B) + G(B') + cost(M) >= 14+14+2, 14+15+1, 14+16, 15+15, 15+16, 16+16: never below 30.**

So this route costs at least 16 + 10 + 30 = **56** at 16 points, the best known count (eight 7-gate bricks). I did not find a
pair (B, B') that reaches 56: in a random sample of 120 of the 15,139 start sets the dual basis never reached all 16 points
without further helpers [ran]. The bound is for the three-box shape "C, then M, then E, then add x"; a circuit that shares wires
across the boxes is not covered.
This is one more exact tie, and this time the reason is visible: **being cheap to read (many of the 16 points among the start
points) and being cheap to write back (the dual basis) exclude each other, because the 16 points are never perpendicular.**
The 10 gates won on one side are lost on the other, gate for gate.

## 4. What changes at 64 points (not run)

[derived] Every eigenform has at least sqrt(N) non-zeros [read: a-2], and the linear relations among the N points of the mirror
are exactly the forms of the other eigenspace. So the shortest relation has sqrt(N) terms: 4 at 16 points (F2), **8 at 64**,
16 at 256. At 64 points: no three of the 64 points on a line, no four in a plane, any seven independent, and no point other
than the 64 lies on two secants. The 16-point trick (a helper on 4 secants, 3 new points for 4 gates) has no direct copy.
Its natural copy is a helper that is a sum of 4 points in several ways (half of an 8-term relation); a new point then costs
about 3 gates instead of 1 unless half-sums are shared [guess].
Bounds I can state: x(64) >= 0 gives cost(C) >= 72 (2.57 per form). A recursion through 4 x 16 points,
cost_minus(4N) <= 6N + cost_plus(N) + 3 cost_minus(N), with 24 for the mirror at 16 and at most 36 for the other space, gives
cost(C) <= 204 (7.3 per form) [derived, not replayed]; it uses no freedom at the 64-point level and is certainly not the minimum.
So c(N) is 3 at 4 points, **4.0 at 16 (exact)**, between 2.57 and 7.3 at 64. That is not a growth law yet.
I did not use steiner.py: the reduced problem is the better search space (28 start points, 64 fixed points, count helpers).

## 5. Regularities to keep

1. cost of a mirror basis = 2(N - r) + (helpers). The floor 2(N - r) = N + sqrt(N) is linear and has slope 2 per form.
2. The fixed points are equiangular lines; at 16 points they are the half-spin vectors of D6 and the useful helpers are its roots.
3. At 16 points every one of 15,139 start sets for one helper set gives the same 24. The minimum is hugely degenerate.
4. Start sets may contain roots (64 of them have only 2 of the 16 points and all 4 roots).
5. The tie at 56 comes from "never perpendicular". For a different target matrix whose points CAN be perpendicular the same
   count would go below; which flat matrices have that? (The 4-point transform does, trivially: r = 1.)

## 6. Mutations (an hour each)

1. **x(64) in the reduced model.** 64 sign vectors of length 28, 28 free start points; helpers from the pool "sums of 2, 3, 4
   of the points with the most representations"; count helpers. A pool search like `search.c` with rank tests modulo a prime
   (then exact replay) fits the Linux machine. The number wanted is x(64)/36: near 0.4 as at 16 points, or growing.
2. **The other eigenspace at 16 points** (10 forms; 16 lines in 10 dimensions at cosine 1/5; relations = the 6-dimensional
   mirror). Same program, start sets of 10. Not run tonight.
3. **Break the three boxes.** The bound of section 3 dies if C and E share wires. The forward 24-gate circuit and a 14-gate
   write-back have helper wires in common (the same four roots): search for one circuit that uses them once.
4. **A different splitting.** S = 4(2 P_plus - I) through the 10-dimensional space, or the order-3 form of brief `thirdturn`:
   run the same "never perpendicular" count there; if the two-sided cost is again 30, that is a conservation law worth proving
   in general: (helpers of B) + (helpers of dual B) >= r.
5. **The count as a lower-bound tool.** "Lines with m points supply m - 2 gates" plus "shortest relation has sqrt(N) terms"
   might give x(N) >= c N log N or might give nothing; try it at 64 by hand before any search.

## 7. Criticism, last

- The 24 is a result about a basis of a subspace, not about the transform. For the transform this route cannot beat 56 at
  16 points in its three-box shape, and I have no circuit at 56 by it.
- The lower bound 24 is a hand argument with machine-checked ingredients; it was not confirmed by an exhaustive search over a
  finite field, and nobody has reviewed it. The x = 3 machine check covers roots as helpers only.
- "Linear size is exactly x(N) = O(N)" restates the brief; it is a cleaner question, not progress on the order.
- 64 and 256 points were not run. The trend the brief asked for (c at 16, 64, 256) has one exact point.
- One run timed out (a slow dual-basis loop); the reported sample of 120 is what finished.
- The equiangular-lines and D6 identifications are classical [recalled]; I did not read the web tonight.
