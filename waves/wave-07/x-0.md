**No circuit below a known count and no growth law below N log N. Nothing here changes the order or any constant.**
What there is: the answer to the brief's question (yes, a gate can do both jobs; no, it cannot matter for the order, and why),
one exhaustive fact at 4 points, a restricted exhaustive search and a regularity at 8 points that I cannot explain, and a
sharper statement of what a linear circuit must contain.

Words. N = 2^k points. *Wire*: a value the program holds, a vector over the N inputs, always scaled to whole numbers without a
common factor (scaling is free). *Form*: a wire with entries 0, +1, -1. *Height* M of a wire: its largest entry in absolute
value; a wire *holds d digits* when M < 2^d. *Dirty wire*: a wire that is not a form (height 2 or more); "form + 2 x form on a
disjoint set" is exactly "height 2". *Gate*: w = (a u + b v)/g, a and b whole numbers without common factor, g the common factor
of a u + b v. *Budget* of the gate: log2((|a| + |b|)/g). *Connected*: two inputs are connected when some held wire is non-zero
on both, or when a chain of such wires links them; the inputs fall into *groups*. *Merge*: a gate that connects two groups
that were not connected before. *Join* (the brief's word): a gate whose two parents live on disjoint sets; every merge is a join, a join inside one connected group is not a merge.
*Volume of a gate* (new here, it makes "carries volume" a number): let Delta be the largest N x N determinant that can be cut
out of all the wires held so far; the gate's volume is log2 of the factor by which Delta grows when the gate's wire is added.
It depends only on the order of the wires, not on which parents one names. The volumes of a circuit for the transform add up
to at least (N/2) log2 N, and a gate's volume is at most its budget (this is Morgenstern's argument). *Floor*:
f(N) = N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N (7, 19, 47 at 4, 8, 16 points).

## 2. Three lemmas [derived tonight, unreviewed]

**Lemma 1 (merges).** Group the inputs by "connected". At the start there are N groups, at the end one, a gate lowers the
number by at most one: exactly N - 1 gates are merges. Let w = a u + b v be a merge, u in group A and v in group B. Then g = 1
(u and v are primitive on disjoint sets). Before the gate every wire lives inside one group, so a non-zero N x N determinant
takes exactly |A| wires from A and |B| from B and Delta is a product over the groups. After the gate, a determinant that uses w
uses |A| - 1 wires of A and |B| of B, or |A| and |B| - 1; in the first case it is a times a determinant that was already
there, in the second b times. So Delta grows by at most max(|a|, |b|), and |a|, |b| are at most the height of w.
*A merge between forms that gives a form brings no volume. A merge brings at most d units when its wire holds d digits. All
merges of a circuit together bring at most (N - 1) d.*
[ran, `lemma_check.out`] On all 6,357 gate lines printed tonight (2,343 of them merges): no volume above the budget, no
merge above log2 of its larger coefficient; all 1,901 merges with coefficients 1 bring exactly 0; merges with a coefficient
2, 3, 4, 5 bring up to 1, 1.585, 2, 2.322: the bound of the lemma is attained.
This is the precise form of s-cross's "a join carries no volume is a property of the one-flag class": it is a property of
every circuit whose merges produce forms, on any sets whatever.

**Lemma 2 (digits).** Let u, v, w be primitive, not parallel, entries at most M, and g w = a u + b v. Take two coordinates
i, j with D = u_i v_j - u_j v_i not zero. Then a/g = (w_i v_j - w_j v_i)/D and b/g = (u_i w_j - u_j w_i)/D, each at most 2 M^2 in
absolute value. So the budget is at most log2(4 M^2). If w is a form the numerators are at most 2M and the budget is at most
log2(4M). [ran, `budget.py`, exhaustive in dimension 2, and in dimension 3 for M = 1, 2 with the same answer]:

| height M | largest (|a|+|b|)/g, any gate | units | w a form | merge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 1.585 | 3 | 2 |
| 2 | 8 | 3.000 | 5 | 3 |
| 3 | 21 | 4.392 | 8 | 5 |
| 4 | 43 | 5.426 | 12 | 7 |
| 5 | 73 | 6.190 | 16 | 9 |
| 7 | 157 | 7.295 | 24 | 13 |

(From M = 3 on: 4 M^2 - 6 M + 3 with u = (M-2, 1-M), v = (M-1, -M), w = (M, M-1); forms 4M - 4; merges 2M - 1 as a budget,
though by lemma 1 a merge's volume is at most log2 M.) The bound 2d + 2 is sharp for one gate taken alone.

**Lemma 3 (floor for bounded height).** A circuit for the transform whose wires all have height at most M >= 2 has at least
N - 1 + ((N/2) log2 N - (N - 1) log2 M) / log2(4 M^2) gates; for forms, N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N / log2 3 (15 at 8 points, 36 at
16; m-cross quotes an open range 15 to 19 at 8 points, I did not check that its 15 is this one). Proof: lemmas 1 and 2 and
the total (N/2) log2 N.
Consequences. (a) For a fixed pool the count is at least (N log2 N)/(4 log2 M + 4) in the long run: the order stands.
(b) C N gates need log2 M >= (log2 N)/(4C) - 1 roughly: entries like N^(1/(4C)). s-cross wrote "coefficients that grow like a
power of N OR wires that hold several binary digits"; by lemma 2 these are one condition, a coefficient cannot be large between
wires that are short. (c) Gates whose three wires have height at most a fixed bound bring O(1) units each, so in a linear circuit
they bring O(N) in all; if the tallest wire has height N^c, at least about N/(4c) gates must involve a wire taller than that
fixed bound. Tall wires are not a garnish: they are a constant fraction of a linear circuit.
(d) The published program has fewer than N log N gates in the long run, so its primitive wires have unbounded height; u-5
measured order 1 inside one invocation, so the height comes from the nesting, as s-cross fact D says for coefficients.

## 4. What the failure is made of, and two ways round that I tried

The failure: at the floor every gate that is not a merge brings exactly one unit, and a gate that brings two is always
accompanied by one that brings none.
*Yes, and (1): let the merges carry.* That is the specimen above. It works as a mechanism and lemma 1 says what it is worth:
there are N - 1 merges in any circuit, so the most it can ever save is N - 1 gates, and with wires of d digits (N - 1) d units.
After the last merge the same wire "half + 2 x input" is an ordinary coefficient-2 gate. **The number of two-job gates grows
like N, never like N log N.**
*Yes, and (2): let one gate bring several units.* Lemma 2: at most 2d + 2 with d digits, and the table shows single gates do
reach it. What I looked for is a circuit in which such a gate is not paid back. At 4 points there is none (exhaustive, entries
up to 3). The reason at 4 points is not volume at all: 3 merges and 4 outputs are 7 gates already when the first full wire is
not an output, and g-1's proof excludes the other case. Volume starts to bind only where (N/2) log2 N exceeds N, from 8 points
on, and there the walks found the tall gates only one gate above the floor.
*What I did not get round:* a wire of height M in a circuit for the transform has to be brought back to height 1 at the
outputs, and in every specimen the gate that does this either is the tall gate itself (then its partner had to be made, one
gate) or brings no volume. I have no proof that this is forced. It cannot be forced for all N: see section 5.

## 5. The better question

**G(N, M): the least number of gates for the N-point transform when every wire has height at most M.**
- [derived] G(N, M) >= N - 1 + ((N/2) log2 N - (N - 1) log2 M)/log2(4 M^2). Upper bound: nothing better than the forms record.
- [ran] G(4, 1) = G(4, 2) = G(4, 3) = 7, the same 228 circuits. [read] G(4, M) = 7 for every M (g-1).
- [ran] G(8, 2) <= 19; no 18 in the walks, and none at all in the restricted class of section 3.
- [derived] "The minimum circuits are clean" cannot be true for all N. If it were, lemma 3 with M = 1 would give
  N - 1 + 0.315 N log2 N for every circuit, and the published program has fewer gates than that in the long run (only for N
  beyond anything one can write down, because its exponent is 1 - 8.76e-4). So there is a first size at which a dirty wire
  belongs to a minimum circuit. The published construction puts it absurdly far out; the true answer may be 8, 16 or 64.
  **Finding that first dirty wire, at any size, is the smallest instance of "a gate that brings more than one unit and is not
  paid back".** It is a finite search question, and it is the one I would hand on.
- What a linear circuit must look like, from lemma 3 (b), (c): a constant fraction of its gates work on wires whose entries are
  powers of N, and each output is one gate from two such wires with budget up to log2(4M). The only wires of that height the
  night has are the D-weighted subset sums of H = Z D Z^T (height up to N) and whatever the nesting of the published unit makes.
  No pool of bounded height contains a linear circuit, so no search of the night could have found one.

## 6. Criticism, last

- Nothing here lowers a count. The three lemmas are a refinement of Morgenstern's argument that s-cross already used; lemma 3
  is close to the classical "bounded coefficients give N log N" with "bounded wires" in place of "bounded coefficients". I have
  not checked the literature for it tonight. They are an hour old and unreviewed.
- Lemma 1 needs "a merge" in my sense (two groups not connected before). The brief's "join" is wider; for joins inside a group
  I have only the remark that their budget is 1.
- The volume of a single gate is defined through the largest determinant over all held wires. It depends on the order in
  which wires are listed, and the total can exceed (N/2) log2 N (4.58 at 4 points). Statements like "this gate brings 2 units"
  are statements about one order. The gate counts do not depend on it.
- Only the 4-point statements are exhaustive without restriction, and only for entries up to 3; the 8-point exhaustive
  search fixes six wires of one known circuit. The 8-point walks are local, start from one
  circuit, cap the coefficients tried at 4 in the two widest pools (a restriction, lemma 2 allows up to 2 M^2), and my walker
  without kicks did not rediscover the 19 from the staircase 20 (with kicks it did, at once). "No 18 found" is weak. "Every 19 sampled is clean" is
  213 samples, not all distinct, around one starting circuit.
- The 16-point run is 164 circuits around the 52 and says nothing.
- "Three forms" as a pool (entries up to 7) ran only at 4 points (walk) and for 24 moves at 8 points.
- Section 5's "there is a first dirty wire" uses the published theorem and lemma 3; if lemma 3 is wrong the statement falls.
- The Linux machine was too loaded to help; its outputs are kept but unused.
