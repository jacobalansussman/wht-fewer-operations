## 0. Verdict in ten lines

1. **The growth law holds and reproduces the published figures** [ran]. It is the repository's own rate lemma read in the
   limit, and as mathematics it is the standard root of a divide-and-conquer recurrence. Nothing new, nothing wrong.
2. **The closed form** saving = L / (ln k + L), L = ln(1/(1 - rho)), is correct [derived, one line; one instance ran].
3. **Ports are exactly the subspaces that meet their perpendicular only in 0.** x-6's programs do define a port the way
   w-cross read it, for the transform. I prove it in three lines for every h and r, and ran it over every complementary
   pair at h = 4, 5, 6 including dimension 3 (x-6 stopped at h = 5 and dimension 2).
4. **"kappa at most 2 at r = h/2" holds**, and more strongly than x-6 said: for every scatter matrix, symmetric or not,
   and every sub-family of ports [derived; graph ran at h = 4 and 6].
5. **"r <= h/3 for symmetric scatters" is overstated.** The proof uses M M = kappa M, not symmetry. A symmetric positive
   scatter need not satisfy it. For r between h/3 and h/2 nothing excludes kappa above the threshold: at h = 7, r = 3 my
   best bound is kappa <= 3.44 against a threshold of 2.92. So **0.098 is not a cap of the mechanism; it is the cap of
   one sub-class.** The on-paper figure at h = 7, r = 3 is 0.126 (three merged blocks) or 0.101 (four blocks).
6. **No what-if block list has a legal network behind it.** x-6 says so itself. The 0.147, 0.098 and 0.078 are correct
   arithmetic on lists that nobody can build, each with helpers and scratch copies costing nothing.
7. One wording point on the published figure: the unit's root is 8.762482e-4 and the Lean theorem states 8.762479e-4.
   "8.7625e-4" is that figure rounded up; a saving of exactly 8.7625e-4 is proved false for this block list.
8. **Known theory.** "Which targets know which sources, and how few totals serve them all" is the index coding problem;
   the least number of totals is the minimum rank of the knows graph. That theory gives kappa <= 2 at r = h/2 at once and
   says the open range is a minimum-rank question on a triangle-free graph.
9. **In the user's terms: nothing.** No circuit, no new exponent. The mechanism cannot give linear size (at least three
   blocks per array; linear needs fewer blocks than arrays). Whether it can pass 1e-1 on paper is open in the range
   h/3 < r < h/2, and stays on paper until someone builds a three-stage word and a fat-port scatter.
10. Smallest failing case of the overstated step: h = 5, r = 2 for the statement as worded; nearest true statement in 3.

## 1. The claim, stated precisely

Words. *Unit*: the published network; W arrays, each to be transformed along m directions. *Block*: a run of moves on one
array with no addition in between; its *rank* is the number of moves. *Port* of dimension r: an r-dimensional set of
address words that a source skips inside one run of the helper circuit. *t knows s*: the unwanted share of source s in
target t can be removed by additions alone. *Scatter matrix* M: M[t][s] = how much of source s target t holds after
reading the totals; M[t][t] = 1 and M[t][s] = 0 unless t knows s. *kappa* = (number of pairs) / rank(M).

x-6 claims:
- (a) The engine proves O(n (log n)^z) for every z with  sum over the unit's blocks of (rank/m)^z < W. Its root gives
  8.7625e-4 and 7.4745e-4 for the two certificates and 1.2815e-3 for the README's what-if.
- (b) k equal blocks per array and a skipped share rho give saving ln(1/(1-rho)) / (ln k + ln(1/(1-rho))).
- (c) A port of dimension r is a subspace P with a complement L such that every block of the matrix on (a coset of L) x
  (a coset of P) has rank at most 1. For the transform these are the P meeting P-perp only in 0, with L = P-perp.
- (d) At r = h/2, kappa <= 2, below the threshold 5h/(4r) = 2.5, so the mechanism dies there.
- (e) Scatters "of the published kind (symmetric, M M = kappa M)" need r <= h/3.
- (f) Ceilings with shears, helpers and copies free: 0.147 (r = h/2, three merged blocks), 0.098 (r = h/3, merged),
  0.078 (r = h/3, the generator's four blocks).

**(c) Ports** [ran, `ports6.py`, every complementary pair (P, L) tested by x-6's rank test, written afresh]:

| address bits h | dimension 1 | dimension 2 | dimension 3 | generic kernel and subset sums |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8 of 15 | 20 of 35 | | 4 and 6 (coordinate subspaces only) |
| 5 | 16 of 31 | 80 of 155 | | 5 and 10 |

In every row the ports of the transform are exactly the subspaces meeting their perpendicular only in 0, always with
L = P-perp. 714,240 pairs tested at h = 6, r = 3.
[derived] Why, for every h and r: on rows a0 + l and columns x0 + p the entry is a sign times (-1)^(l.p). That block has
rank 1 only if l.p is a function of l plus a function of p; l.p is zero when either is zero, so l.p = 0 throughout. Then
L lies in P-perp, equal dimensions give L = P-perp, and "complement" says P and P-perp meet only in 0. So w-cross's
reading is a theorem, not a coincidence of counts.
Growth [ran, pattern is mine]: dimension 2 gives 4, 20, 80, 336 at h = 3 to 6, about 4^h / 12; about half of all
subspaces at every dimension tried.
One gap between x-6's ports and the engine's [read: `R/tools/ccheck.py`, rule L1]: the engine moves only along words of
odd weight and stores bases whose words are odd and pairwise evenly overlapping. 4 of the 20 and 20 of the 80 ports of
dimension 2 contain no odd word [read: x-6's `ports2.out`]. The engine could skip such a port; it could not pay it back
later with its own moves. The fat-port what-ifs assume it can.

**(d) kappa at r = h/2** [ran: the knows graph at h = 4, r = 2 and h = 6, r = 3 is a perfect matching, 10 and 320
separate pairs] [derived]: P_s inside P_t-perp with equal dimensions forces P_s = P_t-perp, so each port knows exactly
one other. M is then made of 2 x 2 blocks with 1 on the diagonal, each of rank at least 1. So rank(M) >= v/2 for every
M and every sub-family. Claim (d) holds, without x-6's restriction "groups of equal size".

**(e) r <= h/3**. The algebra is right [derived, re-done]: M M = kappa M gives N N = (kappa - 1) + (kappa - 2) N for
N = M - 1, so unless kappa = 2 every non-zero entry of N sits in a triangle of the knows graph, and three mutually
orthogonal ports of this kind need 3r <= h. [ran] The knows graph has no triangle at (h, r) = (5, 2) and (7, 3), and
1,120 at (6, 2).
What is wrong is the label. Symmetry is never used; M M = kappa M is. A symmetric positive M of rank d is the table of
inner products of v unit vectors in d dimensions, and M M = kappa M says in addition that those vectors are spread
evenly. The E8 scatter has that property; nothing forces it.

## 3. The failing step, its smallest case, and the nearest true statement

General bound [derived; known, section 4]: a set of ports no two of which know each other gives an identity block inside
M, so rank(M) is at least the size of the largest such set, for any M.

| h, r | ports | each knows | triangles | largest such set found | kappa at most | threshold 5h/4r, 3h/2r |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4, 2 | 20 | 1 | 0 | 10 | 2.000 | 2.500, 3.000 |
| 5, 2 | 80 | 4 | 0 | 34 | 2.353 | 3.125, 3.750 |
| 6, 2 | 336 | 20 | 1,120 | 63 | 5.333 | 3.750, 4.500 |
| 6, 3 | 640 | 1 | 0 | 320 | 2.000 | 2.500, 3.000 |
| 7, 3 | 5,376 | 0 or 8 | 0 | 1,565 | 3.435 | 2.917, 3.500 |

[ran, `ports6.py`. The sets were found by a greedy search, so the kappa column is a valid upper bound for the family
of ALL ports of that size and may be lowered by a better search. It is not a bound for sub-families.]

- **Smallest case where (e) as worded is not proved: h = 5, r = 2.** No triangles, so no scatter with M M = kappa M has
  kappa above 2. For all 80 ports together any M has kappa <= 2.36, below the threshold: dead. For a sub-family I have
  only kappa <= 4 (a connected graph in which each vertex has 4 neighbours can be coloured with 4 colours [memory:
  Brooks]), which is above 3.125. Not closed.
- **h = 7, r = 3 is open outright**: kappa <= 3.44 for the whole family against the five-stage threshold 2.92.
- **h = 6, r = 2 is the live case of x-6's mutation "fat-port-rank"**: triangles exist, bound 5.33, thresholds 3.75
  and 4.5. Splitting the ports into orthogonal triples would give kappa = 3, not enough.
- **Nearest true statement.** Scatters with M M = kappa M and kappa above 2 exist only for r <= h/3. For every scatter,
  kappa <= 2 at r = h/2. For h/3 < r < h/2 the knows graph has no triangle and kappa is at most (ports) / (minimum rank
  of that graph), which nobody has computed.
- **Effect on the ceilings.** 0.147 stays out of reach (r = h/2 is dead). Between r = h/3 and h/2 the on-paper
  ceiling is not 0.098: at h = 7, r = 3 the block lists give 0.1257 (blocks 4, 7, 7 of 21) and 0.1011 (three blocks of
  4 and one of 6) [floats]. Both are above 1e-1 and neither is excluded by anything in x-6 or here. Both need a
  three-stage word for fat ports, a scatter with kappa above 3.5, and helpers and copies that cost nothing. The
  published units lose a factor 4 to copies and another 4 to helpers [read: x-6 section 6; first factor re-ran: 4.00].

## 4. Is it known

- The growth law is the engine's own whole-block lemma [read: `R/tools/gen/foldlib.py`: (t-1) M(z) + W c < t W, and
  "W <= M(z) fails at every fill"]. As mathematics it is the characteristic root of a recurrence
  c(m) = (1/W) x sum of c(rank): Akra and Bazzi, 1998 [memory].
- Ports: non-degenerate subspaces for the dot product over two elements; classical geometry, no single source needed.
- kappa and the knows graph: **index coding with side information** (Birk and Kol 1998; Bar-Yossef, Birk, Jayram and
  Kol, FOCS 2006 and IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 2011) [memory]. One sender, v receivers, each wants one
  message and already knows some others; the least number of linear broadcasts is the minimum rank of the
  side-information graph (Haemers 1979). "Totals" are the broadcasts, "knows" is the side information. Known bounds:
  minimum rank >= largest set with no two knowing each other; for symmetric positive M, Lovasz's theta number (1979).
  The repository already has a network-coding note [read: `R/notes/network-coding/` exists; not opened tonight].
- Line systems at 60 and 90 degrees are root systems: Cameron, Goethals, Seidel and Shult, 1976 [memory].
- **What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": not linear.** It bounds how many shears one total can serve.
  It says nothing that would reduce the number of blocks per array, and the law needs fewer blocks than arrays for z = 0.
  For triangle-free graphs the minimum rank can be far below half the vertices in general (Codenotti, Pudlak and Resta,
  2000) [memory], so the literature does not close h/3 < r < h/2 either; it only names the question.

## 5. Does it scale

- Ports: confirmed one size further (h = 6, all three dimensions). Count grows like a constant times 2^(r(h-r)).
- The saving of the published design does not grow with h: 8.76e-4 at h = 9, 7.47e-4 at h = 22 [ran].
- The ceilings do not depend on h at fixed rho = r/h; they are functions of rho and the number of blocks only.
- x-6's "one block per array gives n log log n" is a what-if that the floor note argues cannot occur in the engine: its
  inequality (sum over blocks of 1 - 2^(-rank) >= W (1 - 2^(-m))) fails for one block of rank below m per array, and
  holds for two or more [read: `wht38/floor/NOTE.md`; the check of the what-if lists against it is mine, derived].
  Two blocks per array with 7.4 % skipped (saving 1e-1) is not excluded by that floor. No network is known.

## 6. In the user's terms

- Circuit size for n points: none.
- Saving exponent: none beyond the published 8.762479e-4. The largest figures here are arithmetic on block lists.
- Linear size: out for this mechanism. Three blocks per array at least, and z = 0 needs fewer blocks than arrays.
- A saving of 1e-1: not shown possible, and not shown impossible. x-6's "caps at 0.098" should read "0.098 for scatters
  whose vectors are evenly spread; open up to r < h/2". The first sizes to decide are (h, r) = (6, 2) and (7, 3).
