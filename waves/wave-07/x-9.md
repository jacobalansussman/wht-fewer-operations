**No circuit below a known count and no growth law below N log N. The idea as stated does not change the order, and I can say
why in three short lemmas that hold for every coefficient.**

Words. N = 2^k *points*; H the transform (entries +1, -1); its rows are the *outputs*. A *node* is a block of 2^j consecutive
inputs; its two halves are L and R. A *product wire* on a node is (1, s_1) x ... x (1, s_j): on each address bit b the second
half is s_b times the first; s_b is the *slope* on that bit. Slope 0 means "only the first half", slope infinity "only the
second half". A row of H is the product wire with every slope +1 or -1. A *piece* is a row cut down to a node. The *lower
tuple* v of a product wire on a node of level j is its slopes on the lowest j - 2 bits; a *cell* is (node, v). Inside a cell
every wire is 4 numbers, one per quarter (L1, L2, R1, R2), times the same vector v on each quarter; I write the 4 numbers as
a 2 x 2 matrix [[L1, L2], [R1, R2]]. *Hub*: M_t = (1, 1, t, -t), that is (v,+1 on L) + t (v,-1 on R). *Witness* of an output
O through a wire M: the other operand of the gate, O - a M. *Sign vector*: entries 0, +1, -1 up to one common factor.
*Magnitude class* of a wire: a set of points on which its entries are equal up to sign.

## 1. The picture  [derived; checked by `facts.py`, items 1 to 4, exact fractions]

In a cell a product wire is a 2 x 2 matrix of rank 1, (1, y)^T (1, x): x the slope on the lower bit, y on the upper bit. The
products are the points of a surface (determinant = 0) carrying two families of lines: x fixed (the textbook step: a piece on
L joined to the same piece on R) and y fixed. A hub is a matrix of rank 2, a point off the surface.
- A gate "hub + witness = product" is a line through the hub meeting the surface twice. So the hub pairs the products two by
  two: output = mirror image of the witness, W -> W - (B(W, M)/det M) M with B the polar form of the determinant.
- This mirror exchanges the two families of lines. For the hub (1, p, t, t q), that is (slope p on L) + t (slope q on R):
  a witness of slope s on R gives the product (x, y) = (p, t (q - s)/(p - s)); a witness of slope s on L gives
  (q, t (s - q)/(s - p)). [ran, 200 random rational cases.] So a hub makes outputs whose lower slope is p or q, nothing else.
- For the transform p = 1, q = -1: an output of upper slope r needs the witness of slope **s = (r + t)/(r - t)**. For r = 1
  this is the curator's s = (1+t)/(1-t); for r = -1 it is 1/s. The witness is a single quarter (s = 0 or infinity) exactly when
  t = r or t = -r. With t = +1 or -1 the hub IS the 2 x 2 transform written as four numbers, (1, 1, 1, -1), and the four
  witnesses are the four quarters. [ran]
- The orbit of four: t -> (1+t)/(1-t) has order 4 and gives t, s, -1/t, -1/s. **What the fourth weight is** [derived]: -1/t
  is the same hub read from the other half, (v,-1 on R) - t (v,+1 on L) up to a factor, and -1/s is its witness slope. So the
  orbit is {hub, witness, mirror hub, mirror witness}: nothing is left over for a new use. Together with x -> -x and x -> 1/x
  the map generates a group of 8 acting on slopes; its short orbits over the Gaussian rationals are {0, infinity, 1, -1}
  (the family) and {i, -i}.
- t = i: s = i, and the six slopes 0, infinity, 1, -1, i, -i are closed under every hub with t in {1, -1, i, -i}. But "a hub
  that is its own witness" is not what happens: the witness is the PRODUCT (v, i) on a half, the child's hub is the
  rank-2 wire (1, 1, i, -i); they are different wires. [ran: t = i costs 28, 76, 192, 464, as any other value.]

## 3. Three lemmas that do not care about coefficients  [derived; small exhaustive checks]

**Lemma A (agreement).** Let O be a row of H, M any wire, w = O - a M with a not 0. Off the support of w, a M = O, so M
takes only the two values +1/a and -1/a there. *A wire can be one gate from an output, with a witness missing a set S, only
if it is a sign vector on S.* Outputs reached with different |a| use disjoint magnitude classes. True over the rationals,
the Gaussian rationals and modulo any odd prime.
**Lemma S (support).** For any wire M and two different rows O1, O2, the supports of the witnesses O1 - a1 M and O2 - a2 M
cover together at least N/2 points. Proof: off both supports O1/a1 = M = O2/a2, so O1 and O2 are proportional there, and two
different rows are proportional on at most N/2 points. [ran, `facts.py` item 5: all wires modulo 5 and 7 at 4 points, modulo
3 and 5 at 8 points (390,624 wires, 175 million cases): the least union is exactly N/2.]
**Lemma P (cap).** Let M have a magnitude class of lambda N points, and let n rows be one gate from M through that class
with witnesses of at most phi N points, where 2 - 2 phi - lambda > 0 (for a sign vector: phi < 1/2). "Through that class"
means that the witness vanishes on part of it (by lemma A it vanishes inside one class only). On the class M is mu times
a sign vector sigma; a row reached through it
agrees with +-sigma on at least (1 - phi) N points of the class and disagrees on the rest of the class, so its inner product
with sigma (put 0 outside the class) is at least (2 - 2 phi - lambda) N in size. The rows of H are orthogonal of squared length
N, so the squares of these inner products sum to at most N x lambda N. Hence **n <= lambda / (2 - 2 phi - lambda)^2**. This grows
with lambda: the best wire is a pure sign vector, n <= 1/(1 - 2 phi)^2. Read the other way: **a wire one gate from n outputs
has witnesses of at least (N/2)(1 - 1/sqrt(n)) points**, whatever its coefficients.
[ran, `cap.py`, `cap2.py`, all wires modulo 5 at 8 points; modulo 5 the magnitudes are 1 and 2 only:]

**What they say about this brief.** The lemmas apply inside a cell (treat each quarter as a point). M_t with t not +1, -1
has two magnitude classes of half the cell each (for t = i: two classes "up to sign", the same thing). With quarter
witnesses (phi = 1/4, lambda = 1/2) the cap is 1/2: no output at all. With half witnesses (phi = 1/2) it is 2 per class: the
four outputs of section 1, each with a witness twice as large as in the family. A growing coefficient does not leave the
0, +1, -1 world for a better one; it leaves the only world in which a hub has small witnesses.
**The curator's "keep asking".** Can one helper serve three levels in place of two? Not as a rule [derived, lemma S]: of
the witnesses of one hub at most one is smaller than a quarter of the hub's cube, so all the others sit at most two levels
below it; an eighth piece can be the witness of at most ONE output per hub. This holds for hubs of any number of pieces and
any coefficients. The 19 uses exactly this exception: g8 and g9 each reach one row with a single input, three levels down.

## 4. Going around, four times

1. *The witness is nobody's output: make it one.* [ran] Every node exports both the pieces of top slope +1 or -1 (hub parts)
   and the pieces of top slope s (witnesses). With t = i the scheme closes on the six slopes of section 1, and the witnesses
   of the witnesses are plain quarters. It is consistent and exact, and it is the family plus N/2 wires per level: 5/4.
2. *Let the hub's parts be weighted too, so that the weighted pieces are used twice.* [derived] The hub makes outputs whose
   slope on the bit below it is p or q, the top slopes of its two parts (section 1). In a one-chain program no other gate of
   the three shapes changes that slope afterwards. So p, q = +1, -1 at every level: each node must export, for every lower
   tuple, a piece of top slope +1 or -1. That is N/2 forced wires per level, and they are exactly the family's.
3. *Many pieces, weights t, t^2, t^3, ... (a hub of hubs: one gate per node from the two children's hubs).* [derived, lemma A]
   An output agrees with such a wire on one magnitude class only, that is on one piece; the witness covers all the others.
   Geometric weights make the witness as large as it can be. Equal magnitudes are forced, and then lemma P caps the count.
4. *The Fourier transform, where a root of unity is natural* (e2-0 mutation 6). [derived; ran at 4 points only,
   `fourier4.py`] On the chain of
   residue classes a cell holds four characters with slopes (p0, rho), (p0, -rho), (-p0, i rho), (-p0, -i rho), rho^2 = p0.
   The hub (1, p0, t, -t p0) gives the first two with quarter witnesses when t = rho, and the last two when t = i rho; with
   t = rho the last two need witnesses of slope -i p0 and i p0, weighted pieces that are not characters. So the staircase
   needs two hubs per cell there, 4 gates per cell, the textbook rate. The Walsh-Hadamard saving is the accident that both
   pairs of outputs want the same t; a free t cannot serve t and i t at once. At 4 points [ran]: with t = 1 rows 0 and 2
   have single inputs as witnesses and rows 1 and 3 need x0 - i x2 and x0 + i x2; with t = i it is the other way round.
   [recalled] Those twisted pairs are what the split-radix Fourier algorithm forms; [derived] in count (G) split-radix
   obeys T(N) = T(N/2) + 2 T(N/4) + 3N/2, which is N log2 N again, so it is no door either.

## 5. The floor of the class  [derived; unreviewed; three gate shapes only]

Class: wires are (i) product wires with any slopes on the nodes of one chain of halvings and (ii) hubs (one product on L) +
t (one product on R) with the same lower tuple, any t. Gates: (a) a textbook step along the top bit (two of: a product on
the node, the same lower tuple on L, on R); (b) the hub from its two parts; (c) product = hub + witness, the witness inside
one half.
Claim: at least (3/4) N log2 N + N/4 gates. Sketch. Each wire is one gate, so count wires. Top node: N outputs. By section 1
an output of lower slope p needs p on both halves (a) or a hub with p among its two part slopes (c); so for every lower
tuple v each half holds at least one wire with that tuple and top slope +1 or -1, and by the same argument one level down,
and so on: at least 2 wires of the children per cell, at every level. If a cell has 2 or 3 child wires it needs a hub; with
4 it needs none. So (child wires + hubs) per cell is at least 3, and there are N/4 cells per level: 3N/4 per level for
levels 2 to k, plus the N outputs: N + (k - 1) 3N/4. e2-0's family attains it: 7, 20, 52, 128, 304. t does not appear.
Not covered (these are the hatches of this floor): a step along a lower bit (two products on the same node that differ in
one lower slope); hub + hub; a witness covering the whole node; hubs whose two parts have different lower tuples; anything
off the chain. e2-0's wall lemma covered the same three shapes with coefficients +1, -1; what is added is "for every t".
[read] x-2 found tonight, by annealing at 8 points with wires of entries up to 2 allowed, that no 19-gate circuit used one.
Lemma P is a reason: a last gate with a small partner wants a sign vector.

## 6. Growth law, the wall, and the question it turns into

**Growth law** [ran to 64 points, derived for all k]: gates = (3/4) N log2 N + N/4 + (N/2) x (levels off the short orbit).
Rate 3/4 for t = +1, -1; 5/4 for t bounded and different, for t = k, t = 2^k, t = N. The hoped law "rate like 1/log t" does
not exist in this family, and by section 5 not in its class.
**The wall, named: one magnitude per agreement.** A gate whose partner is small forces the other operand to be a sign
vector where the partner is absent (lemma A). In whole numbers without common factor [derived]: if M equals +-mu off the
partner's set T, the gate is O = (+-M + b w)/mu. The hub's coefficient is 1; everything large is b, and b w is exactly
"M minus the output" on T. So a large coefficient in such a gate only removes what was put into the hub on T. The
smallest picture [derived]: on two points, hub M = (1, t), partner w = (0, 1); the outputs are (1, 1) = M + (1 - t) w and
(1, -1) = M - (1 + t) w, and their determinant in terms of (M, w) is -(1 + t) - (1 - t) = -2 for every t. In the family
[ran] the largest coefficient grows with t (17 at 16 points for t_j = 2^j, 65537 at 32 points for t_j = 2^(2^(j-1))) and
the outputs are the same rows. The budget of s-cross's ledger is spent and no volume comes of it.
The volume count says C N gates need coefficients like N^(1/(2C)) that DO bring volume. [guess, not proved] Gates of the
kind "hub plus small witness", which is the kind every saving of the night is made of, cannot be those gates; in a gate
that earns volume both operands have several magnitudes on most of the points and cancel there.
**The better question** [guess]. The one mechanism known that turns large coefficients into +1, -1 by cancellation is the
identity (1 - 2)^n = (-1)^n inside H = Z D Z^T, where D holds (-2)^|S| and each output is a sum of 2^|S| terms. A hub with
coefficient t is its one-step version (the Cayley map (1+t)/(1-t)), and it cancels only at t = +1, -1. Is there an identity
of intermediate depth: a wire with entries of d magnitudes, d growing slowly, that is one gate from many outputs with a
partner of the same kind? Lemma A does not forbid it when the partner covers everything; lemma P does not apply to it.
A concrete form: pairs of wires (u, v) for which the N points (u_x, v_x) of the plane lie on two parallel lines, for many
outputs at once and with many different points on each line.

## 8. Limits and criticism, last

- Nothing is in Lean or reviewed. Every circuit count in section 2 is an exact replay of an explicit program; the counts are
  those of ONE construction rule per schedule (hub first, then textbook steps), not minima over all programs with those hubs.
- "No coincidence for any t" is an argument (section 2) plus a scan of 22 and 9 values per level; the scan alone proves nothing.
- Lemmas A, S, P are three-line arguments and I believe them; the exhaustive checks are modulo small primes at 4 and 8
  points, where modulo 5 only two magnitudes exist. Lemma P is tested, not proved tight, beyond the cases in the table.
- The floor of section 5 is a hand sketch for three gate shapes. It is the least secure statement here; its four uncovered
  gate kinds are listed and I did not run a search that includes them.
- Section 4, item 4 (Fourier) is by hand; only its 4-point case was run.
- The "better question" is a guess about where to look, not a result. This report closes one door (a growing coefficient on
  a two-piece hub) with a reason; it opens none.
