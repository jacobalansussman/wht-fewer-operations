# z-0: the height lemmas (x-0 lemmas 1 to 3, x-2 section 4.2, x-8's bridge), criticised

11 October 2026, 04:51 to about 05:20 EDT, 30-minute box. Skeptic z-0, one agent. Private; nothing in Lean, nothing published.
Marks: [ran] = my own program tonight, exact whole-number arithmetic unless said (files in `z-0/`); [derived] = by hand tonight,
checked by nobody else; [read] = from tonight's reports or from the web tonight; [recalled] = from memory, not re-read; [guess].
Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free. Count (O), OpenAI's, is larger, so every lower bound
here holds for it too.

**Verdict: the lemmas hold, for circuits whose wires are whole-number vectors, and they are Morgenstern's 1973 theorem with one
small bridge added. They do not say "linear or not". No circuit, no saving exponent.** Three things said about them tonight are
too strong, and one hypothesis is missing from the statement (section 4).

Words. N = 2^k points; H = the transform, entries +1, -1. *Wire*: a value the program holds, read as its vector of
coefficients on the N inputs and scaled to whole numbers with no common factor (*primitive*). *Height* M of a wire: its
largest entry in size. *Form*: a wire of height 1. *Gate*: g*w = a*u + b*v with u, v, w primitive, a and b whole numbers
without common factor, g the common factor of a*u + b*v. *Budget* of the gate: log2((|a|+|b|)/g). *Delta*: the largest
N x N determinant that can be cut out of the wires held so far (1 at the start, at least N^(N/2) at the end). *Volume* of a
gate: log2 of the factor by which Delta grows when its wire is added. *Group*: inputs linked by wires that are non-zero on
both; *merge*: a gate that links two groups.

## 1. The claims, stated exactly

- **L1 (x-0 lemma 1).** Every circuit that ends connected has exactly N - 1 merges. A merge w = a*u + b*v multiplies Delta by
  at most max(|a|, |b|), and max(|a|, |b|) is at most the height of w.
- **L2 (x-0 lemma 2; x-2 section 4.2).** If u, v, w are primitive whole-number vectors of height at most M, not parallel, and
  g*w = a*u + b*v, then (|a|+|b|)/g <= 4 M^2. Exact largest values 3, 8, 21, 43, 73, 157 for M = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7.
- **L3 (x-0 lemma 3).** A circuit for H whose wires all have height at most M (M >= 2) has at least
  N - 1 + ((N/2) log2 N - (N - 1) log2 M) / log2(4 M^2) gates; for M = 1, N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N / log2 3.
  x-2's form, (N/2) log2 N / (2 + 2 log2 M), is the same bound without the merge term.
- **Bridge (x-8).** For a whole-number matrix with orthogonal primitive rows, the entropy (minus the sum of p log2 p with
  p = entry squared over squared row length) is at most 2 log2 |det|, with equality exactly when every entry is 0, +1 or -1;
  an in-place move row_i <- (a row_i + b row_j)/g multiplies the determinant by a/g.
- **Consequence drawn.** C*N gates need a constant fraction of the gates on wires of height about N^(1/(4C)).

[derived] More coordinates can never raise these numbers, for every M: if some pair of coordinates has
u_i v_j - u_j v_i = +-1, the three vectors cut down to that pair are primitive and it is a 2-coordinate case; if no pair has,
the value is at most 4 M^2 / 2 = 2 M^2, which is at most 4 M^2 - 6 M + 3 once M >= 3 (and 8 = 8 at M = 2, 2 < 3 at M = 1).
x-0 ran 3 coordinates only for M = 1, 2 and x-2 wrote "more coordinates can only remove cases" (not true as written: a cut
to two coordinates need not be primitive); this closes that gap.

  with volume above max(0, budget), no merge above max(|a|, |b|), no budget above 4 M^2.**
- So the brief's first attack fails: a large common factor g lowers the budget, it never raises it (the denominator in
  Cramer's rule is a non-zero whole number, so at least 1).

**Bridge** [ran, `small.py`]: 67 matrices with orthogonal primitive rows (H at 2 to 16 points, the 4-point brick, products
and sums of blocks (a, b; b, -a)): squared row lengths multiply to det^2 exactly; entropy never above 2 log2 |det|; equality
in exactly the 5 with entries 0, +-1 (3-4-5 rotation: 1.885 against 9.288, as x-8 says). 378 random in-place moves: the
determinant is multiplied by exactly a/g every time. The proof is three lines and I agree with it.

**L3** follows from L1, L2 and the sum of the volumes being log2 of the final Delta, which is at least (N/2) log2 N. I checked
the proof line by line [derived]; it is right. The order of the wires does not matter for it: any order in which each wire
comes after its parents gives a valid ledger, and a total above (N/2) log2 N only helps the bound. What the order does change
is every sentence of the kind "this gate brings 2 units": those are facts about one listing, not about the circuit.

## 3. Is it known? Yes, in substance

- **Morgenstern 1973** (J. ACM 20, pages 305 to 306) [read through two secondary descriptions tonight, not the article]: with
  coefficients bounded in size by c, a linear circuit for A needs at least log |det A| / log(2c) gates; the proof is exactly
  the "largest determinant" potential. For the transform: (N/2) log2 N / log2(2c). L3 is this with c = 2 M^2.
- What x-0 and x-2 add is the bridge "whole-number wires of bounded height force bounded coefficients" (Cramer's rule plus
  "a non-zero whole number is at least 1"), and the remark that merges bring only log2 M, worth the additive N - 1. I did not
  find this wording in print in two searches; it is a two-line corollary and I would not call it new.
- One real merit: Ailon (arXiv 1305.4745 and 1403.1307, 2013 and 2014) [read tonight, summary] objects that Morgenstern's bound is "not scale
  insensitive": it does not see the normalised transform, of determinant 1. Taking every wire primitive removes that
  objection for whole-number circuits, which is why L3 is valid in count (G) where scalings are free.
- **What the known theory then predicts for "linear or not": nothing.** Bounded coefficients give N log N (1973). For
  unbounded coefficients no bound above linear is known for any explicit matrix (Valiant's problem, 1977; "still open" in
  Buergisser and Lotz, arXiv cs/0301016). The one general route, rigidity, is closed for H: Alman and Williams (arXiv
  1611.05558, 2017) show H is not rigid enough for it. Ailon's conjecture (arXiv 1609.03278, [read] in x-8) has the same shape
  as L3: a speed-up needs ill-conditioning exponential in the speed-up, i.e. a power of N for linear size.

## 4. Where the statements are too strong, or fail

1. **Missing hypothesis: whole numbers.** "With any coefficients" must read "with any rational coefficients". The cost model
   charges one unit for `scale (c : ℂ)`, any complex constant [read, `OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/Core.lean`]. Outside the
   whole numbers L2 is false. **Smallest failing case** [ran, exact in pairs of whole numbers, `small.py`]: 2 coordinates,
   numbers a + b*sqrt2, e = (sqrt2 - 1)^k: u = (1, 0), v = (1, e), w = (0, 1) = (v - u)/e. Every entry has size at most 1, yet
   the coefficients are (1 + sqrt2)^k: budget 2.27, 3.54, ..., 11.17 for k = 1 to 8, without limit, against log2 3 = 1.58.
   *Nearest true statement* [derived, not run]: in a number field of degree d, with "height" meaning the largest size of
   any conjugate of any entry, each coefficient is at most (2 M^2)^d. So a circuit with irrational constants and wires of
   small size is not touched by any of the height lemmas. (Numbers a + b*i with a, b whole are fine: a non-zero one has size
   at least 1 and the proof goes through [derived].) The pools searched tonight were whole-number as far as I read, so those
   searches are covered; "no bounded pool contains a linear circuit" is proved only for such pools.
2. **"A gate's volume is at most its budget" is false as written.** Smallest case [ran]: height 2, u = (1, -2), v = (1, 1),
   w = (0, 1) = (v - u)/3: (|a|+|b|)/g = 2/3, budget -0.58, volume 0. True statement: volume <= max(0, budget). A quotient
   gate never gives volume back. It does not touch L3 (which uses only the upper cap), but "the determinant needs a budget of
   (N/2) log2 N" must be read with negative budgets counted as 0.
3. **"A constant fraction of gates on wires of height about N^(1/(4C))" is not what is proved.** What is proved [derived]:
   sum over the gates of log2(4 M_j^2) >= (N/2) log2 N, M_j the tallest of the gate's three wires; so with C*N gates the
   AVERAGE number of binary digits log2 M_j per gate is at least (log2 N)/(4C) - 1. A constant fraction follows only with a
   ceiling: if no wire is taller than N^c, at least about N/(8c) gates touch a wire of height N^(1/(8C)) or more. Without a
   ceiling the lemmas do not exclude a circuit in which a few gates on enormously tall wires carry everything. x-0's own
   wording (its consequence (c), "if the tallest wire has height N^c") is correct; the shortened version is not.
4. **The lemma is silent at every size anyone ran.** [ran, `small.py`] L3 exceeds the trivial 2N - 2 only when
   log2 N > 4 + 6 log2 M: from N = 2^10 for M = 2, 2^14 for M = 3, 2^16 for M = 4, 2^28 for M = 16. At 8 points it gives 15
   for forms (19 known), 9 for height 2 and 8 for height 3 (trivial bound 14). And it is empty from M = sqrt(N) on: there the
   N - 1 merges alone may carry all the volume. So it explains none of tonight's "no 18 found with dirty wires" and none of
   the "clean at the floor" regularities; x-0 says this too.
5. x-0 (d), "the published program's wires have unbounded height", needs that program's constants to be rational, which I
   did not check; and with a saving of 8.7625e-4 it bites at no size one can write down (x-0 says so too).
6. x-0 and x-2 define height the same way (x-2: a whole-number representative with entries at most M; x-0: the primitive
   one; the primitive one is never taller). x-2's guess "every second Fibonacci number" for the exact maxima is wrong from
   M = 4 (43, not 55); x-0's formula is the right one.

## 5. Growth laws

- Per gate: at most 4 M^2 - 6 M + 3, about 2 log2 M + 2 units [ran to M = 32; proved bound 4 M^2].
- Per circuit, fixed height M: at least about N log2 N / (4 + 4 log2 M) gates: order N log N, constant falling like
  1 / log M.
- For C*N gates in whole numbers [derived from L1 to L3]: the tallest wire holds at least ((log2 N)/2 - 2C + 2)/(2C - 1)
  binary digits, about (log2 N)/(4C - 2), so height about N^(1/(4C-2)) (x-0's 1/(4C) drops the merge term); and the average
  over the gates is at least (log2 N)/(4C) - 1 digits.

## 6. In the user's terms

Nothing yet: no formula for a circuit size, no saving exponent. What it gives is a map of where a linear-size program can
and cannot be. It cannot be a whole-number circuit whose wires hold a bounded number of binary digits: that class is
N log N for ever (known since 1973). It can only be (a) a whole-number circuit whose typical gate works on numbers of about
(log2 N)/(4C) digits, which the cost model does not charge for, or (b) a circuit with irrational constants, about which
these lemmas say nothing. Neither is excluded by any known theorem, and neither has been searched tonight.
[derived, two lines] If digits were charged, the same sum says a linear-size whole-number circuit writes at least about
(N/4) log2 N digits in all: N log N would come back as a bit count.
