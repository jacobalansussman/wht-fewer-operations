# x-3 boxrelations: when can a transform used as a box be shared?

11 October 2026, 03:16 to 04:02 EDT, 60-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing reviewed, nothing in Lean, nothing published.
Marks: [ran exact] = run of mine tonight in fractions; [ran floats] = least-squares search in floating point from random starts
(a hit is a candidate that was also checked on 10 fresh boxes; "none" is not a proof); [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by
nobody; [read] = from tonight's reports; [guess].
Gate count: this report counts CALLS to a box, not gates. Where gates are mentioned it is count (G) (a gate is any a*u + b*v,
scalings free). Count (O) is not used.

**No circuit below a known count and no growth law below N log N. Nothing here changes the order, or any constant.**
What it does give: three short lemmas that say exactly which "relations of the box" can lower the number of calls and by how
much as K grows, the table r(2), r(4) the brief asked for, and one family (a box known only up to an address map) where the
lemmas leave room at every size.

## Words

K = 2^k points for the box, K x K table X, N = K^2 points for the target. *Box* A: a K x K matrix the program may apply to any
K-vector it has built. *Call program* with r calls: c_j = A (L_j x + sum over i < j of M_ji c_i), output = Q x + sum of P_j c_j,
with L, M, P, Q fixed matrices (the *glue*). *Free glue*: the glue is not priced, only calls are counted. *Family*: a set of
boxes; the program must give A X A^T (the matrix A (x) A applied to the table) for every box of the family. *Sealed* = the family
of all matrices. *Textbook*: K calls on the columns, K on the rows, 2K. *Reflection*: A = 1 - 2P with P an orthogonal
projection (so A = A^T and A^2 = 1); the transform divided by sqrt(K) is one, with P of rank K/2 (plain pairing). *Address map*:
a relabelling of the K addresses by an invertible bit matrix g, as a permutation matrix P_g. *Rank* of a matrix: the dimension
of its image.

## 0. In twelve lines

1. [derived] **Telescoping lemma.** For ANY two boxes A, A' and any call program with r calls and output F(A):
   rank(F(A) - F(A')) <= r x rank(A - A'). So a program right on a family needs
   **r >= rank(A (x) A - A' (x) A') / rank(A - A')** for every pair in the family. Three lines of proof (section 1).
2. [derived] **Scale lemma.** A program that is right for every scalar multiple t*A0 of ONE invertible matrix A0 needs r >= 2K.
   So s-cross's sealed-box lemma uses nothing but "the program does not know the scale of the box". Every family closed under
   scaling (all matrices, all symmetric matrices) is exactly at 2K.
3. [ran exact, K = 2, 4, 6, 8; derived for all K] What each relation leaves, by line 1 with the closest pair of the family:
   symmetric >= 2K - 1 (and 2K by line 2); A^2 = 1 >= 2K - 2; rotations and reflections >= 2K - 3.
   **So "symmetric", "involution", "orthogonal", "reflection" can save at most 3 calls at every size: a = r/K >= 2 - 3/K.**
   The saving s-cross found at K = 2 (2 calls instead of 4) is that "3"; it does not grow.
4. [ran floats + exact formulas] r(2): all matrices 4, symmetric 4, involutions 4, reflections 2, rotations 2, both components
   of the orthogonal group together 4 (weak: 5 starts at 3). The two 2-call programs are replayed exactly (400 cases); each fails on the other
   component (398 of 400 cases).
5. [ran floats] r(4), reflections 1 - 2P with P of rank 2 (the class of the 4-point transform): not found below the textbook's 8 at the
   full table (none at 6 in 4 starts, none at 7 in 3 starts; the control at 8 hits in 2 of 3 starts). Exact lower bound 5;
   the symmetric part alone needs 6. So r(4) is 6, 7 or 8, most likely 8.
6. [ran floats] r(4) by parts of the table: skew part (6 dimensions) 5 calls for reflections against 7 for all matrices;
   symmetric part 6 for reflections (9 dimensions, trace removed) against 8 for all matrices (10 dimensions).
7. [derived] Case (iv), one fixed box: with free glue the count is 0 (put the whole answer in Q), and line 1 says why no
   argument of this kind can ever bind: a family of one box has no pair. **For the transform itself the call count is a
   statement about the PRICE of the glue and nothing else.**
8. [derived] Priced glue, two statements. (a) If the glue is only permutations and scalings, 2K calls are needed (support
   count; sketch). (b) If the glue has b*K^2 gates of the kinds u + v, u - v, u + 2v, u - 2v, then
   r >= 2K (log2 K - 1.585 b) / (log2 K + 1): a tends to 2. This is Morgenstern's volume count with a call charged
   (K/2)(log2 K + 1). So a < 2 - eps needs glue that carries (eps/2b) log2 K binary digits per gate and is not a permutation.
9. [ran exact, K = 4, 8, 16] **The one family where line 1 leaves room at every size: boxes known only up to an address map.**
   The bound is exactly K + K/2 = 1.5 K (6, 12, 24 against 8, 16, 32), reached by the maps that fix half of the addresses.
   [ran exact, `perm7.py`] At K = 4 (six maps) an explicit **7-call program, replayed in fractions on all six maps (300 cases)**;
   textbook 8, bound 6. It is a count in a family model with free glue, not a circuit for the transform.
10. Arithmetic of the brief checked [derived]: with r = a K and glue b K^2, cost per point c(K^2) = a c(K) + b; with
    K = 2^(2^j) this gives a^j = (log2 N)^(log2 a) for a > 1, log log N for a = 1, a constant for a < 1; 2^0.9 = 1.866. Right.
11. Most useful sentence for the next wave: **a relation helps only if it makes the box "isolated": any two boxes that obey
    it differ by a matrix of high rank.** Continuous relations (symmetric, orthogonal, involution) never do; a discrete
    one (address maps) does, by a factor 3/4 in the bound.
12. Skeptic first: the telescoping lemma (it is twenty minutes old and everything in lines 3, 7, 9 rests on it).

## 1. The three lemmas

**Telescoping lemma** [derived]. Take a call program and two boxes A, A'. Write c_j, w_j for the calls and their inputs with A
(w_j = L_j x + sum M_ji c_i, c_j = A w_j) and c'_j, w'_j with A'. Then
c_j - c'_j = (A - A') w_j + A' (w_j - w'_j) = (A - A') w_j + A' sum over i < j of M_ji (c_i - c'_i).
By induction c_j - c'_j = sum over i <= j of N_ji (A - A') w_i with matrices N_ji made of A' and M only. Hence
F(A) - F(A') = sum over i = 1..r of P''_i (A - A') w_i, a sum of r terms each of rank at most rank(A - A'). QED.
Remarks. It needs no smoothness, no scaling, no symmetry of the family, and it holds for any target, not only A (x) A.
Its infinitesimal form: at a point A0 of a smooth family, for every tangent direction E,
r >= rank(E (x) A0 + A0 (x) E) / rank(E). [ran floats, `tank.py`] The least number of terms of that first-order identity
(sum of P_j E L_j = E (x) A0 + A0 (x) E for all tangent E) was searched directly: all matrices 4 at K = 2 and 6 at K = 3
(= 2K, none below), symmetric 4 and 6 (= 2K), rotations 1 at K = 2 and 4 at K = 3 (none at 3 in 4 starts), involutions 2 at
K = 2, reflections 1 at K = 2 (`tan.out`; its K = 4 lines had one or two starts each and say nothing). The exact four-call formula for the rotations of 3-space is in `k2exact.py` (e) [ran exact,
100 cases]: three calls on the columns of the symmetric part of the table, one call on the vector of the skew part.

**Scale lemma** [derived]. Let the family be {t A0}, A0 invertible. Stack the P_j A0 side by side into one K^2 x rK matrix
PP, the L_j into one rK x K^2 matrix LL, and the M_ji A0 into a block-triangular MM. Comparing powers of t in the output:
degree 1 gives PP LL = 0, degree 2 gives PP MM LL = A0 (x) A0, which is invertible. So rank PP >= K^2, rank LL >= K^2, and the
image of LL lies in the kernel of PP: rK - K^2 >= K^2, r >= 2K. QED.
Consequence: a program for the transform with fewer than 2K calls must USE the constant in H^2 = K x 1. Its terms of
different lengths in the box (0, 2, 4, ... calls deep) must mix. The 2-call programs at K = 2 do exactly this: two table
directions never pass through the box at all.

**What each relation leaves** [ran exact, `rankbound.py`, `rankbound.out`, fractions; the pattern derived for all K].

| family | closest pair differs by rank | rank of A (x) A - A' (x) A' | calls needed at least | at K = 2, 4, 6, 8 | textbook |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all matrices | 1 | 2K - 1 | 2K - 1 (2K by the scale lemma) | 3, 7, 11, 15 | 4, 8, 12, 16 |
| symmetric | 1 | 2K - 1 | 2K - 1 (2K by the scale lemma) | 3, 7, 11, 15 | 4, 8, 12, 16 |
| A^2 = 1 | 1 | 2K - 2 | 2K - 2 | 2, 6, 10, 14 | 4, 8, 12, 16 |
| rotations | 2 | 4K - 6 | 2K - 3 | 1, 5, 9, 13 | 4, 8, 12, 16 |
| reflections | 2 | 4K - 6 | 2K - 3 | 1, 5, 9, 13 | 4, 8, 12, 16 |

Why 4K - 6 [derived]: two rotations that differ by a turn in one plane; A (x) A - A' (x) A' is then, up to an invertible
factor, "table minus the table turned in that plane on both sides", whose kernel is the tables that commute with the turn,
of dimension (K - 2)^2 + 2. For reflections take the same turn applied to the mirror.
Growth law of this section: **for each of the four relations r(K) >= 2K - 3, so a = r/K tends to 2 and the cost per point
stays (log N)^(1 - o(1)): no change of order, and in the limit no change of constant, from any of them** (in the model
where the program is right for the whole family).

So at K = 2 the relation that shares calls is **A A^T = 1**, not "reflection": rotations do it too, and symmetric alone or
A^2 = 1 alone do not. Exact formulas [ran exact, `k2exact.py`, 200 + 200 cases]: write X = alpha 1 + beta J + S(p, q) with J
the quarter turn and S(p, q) = [[p, q], [q, -p]]. Rotations: A X A^T = alpha 1 + beta J + S(A A (p, q)). Reflections:
A X A^T = alpha 1 - beta J + S(A sz A (p, q)), sz = diag(1, -1). The two programs differ in the glue between the calls and in
the sign of beta, and each is wrong on the other component. [derived] The mechanism: A A^T = 1 turns A (x) A into "conjugate
the table by A"; the table then splits into pieces that the box cannot mix, of dimensions 1, 1, 2 at K = 2, all at most K,
and the 2-dimensional piece is the box applied twice. At size K the pieces are 1, K(K-1)/2 (skew tables) and K(K+1)/2 - 1
(symmetric tables without trace): only the 1 is free. That is why the saving is a constant.

## 3. Case (iv): the transform itself, and what the glue must be

[derived] With free glue a single box needs no call. With priced glue the question "fewest calls of the K-point transform
with O(K^2) other gates" is well posed, and two things can be said tonight.

(a) *Glue = permutations and scalings only* (both free in count G): 2K calls. Sketch: the support of a wire (the inputs it
depends on) has size 1 at the start and at most K times larger after a call; an output row has support K^2, so every output
is made by a call whose K inputs have disjoint supports of size K, each of them made by a call. The K outputs of one such
last call agree up to sign on each of those supports, which forces the supports to be the cosets of one subspace and the
first-round calls to be one per coset: K and K. I did not check the case where supports overlap and cancel.

(b) *Glue with bounded coefficients.* In the ledger of s-cross hybrid 3 (largest minor of the wires held), one call to H_K
multiplies the largest minor by at most sqrt(binomial(K, m)) K^(m/2) <= 2^(K/2) K^(K/2) (expand a minor along the m new
rows; Cauchy-Binet gives the sum of squares of the m x m minors of m rows of H_K as K^m). The determinant of the K^2-point
transform is 2^(K^2 log2 K). So r (K/2)(log2 K + 1) + (budget of the glue) >= K^2 log2 K. With b K^2 glue gates of budget
at most 1.585 each: a = r/K >= 2 - 2 (1 + 1.585 b)/(log2 K + 1). In the recursion c(K^2) = a(K) c(K) + b this still gives
c of order log N: the factors a/2 multiply to a positive constant.
What it says the glue must be for a < 2 - eps: not a permutation (by a), and carrying about (eps / 2b) log2 K binary digits
per gate (by b). Diagonals carry digits for nothing in count (G) but are permutation-like; unit-triangular glue (running
sums, Horner steps y_i = x_i + t y_(i-1)) is dense in O(K) gates per line but has determinant 1. The cheapest glue with
both properties is "running sums between free diagonals", which is the subset-sum matrix again (H = Z D Z^T): the box model
arrives at the same door as g-4 and s-cross fact E, from a third side.

A sharper form of the question that I did not have time to search [guess that it is the right one]: **is the K^2-point
transform equal to G1 (H_K on every row) G2 with G1, G2 of O(K^2) gates each?** That is a = 1 exactly. By (a) and (b), G1 or
G2 must contain gates that add and coefficients of size about K^(1/2b).

## 4. The family that the lemma does not close: a box known up to an address map

[ran exact, `permbound.py`, `permbound.out`: cycle counting over all 5, 167, 20159 address maps other than the identity at
K = 4, 8, 16] For boxes P_g (or H P_g, the same thing with free glue) the telescoping bound for the pair (1, P_h) is
(K^2 - cycles of h on pairs) / (K - cycles of h). Its largest value is **exactly K + K/2**: 6, 12, 24. It is reached by the
maps that fix half of the addresses (x -> x + (a.x) b); maps with one fixed address give K + 1 or a little more (9 at K = 8,
17 at K = 16). [derived] For an involution with 2^f fixed addresses the value is K + 2^f.
So this is the first relation of the night for which "right for the whole family" does not force 2K - O(1): the lemma
leaves a >= 1.5 at every size, and 1.5 would be a saving of 1 - log2 1.5 = 0.415 in the exponent in the call recursion.
[ran exact, `perm7.py`, `perm7.out`] **At K = 4 the family really is below 2K: 7 calls**, for all six address maps, in
fractions, 300 cases. How: under an address map the 16 entries of the table fall into 5 numbers that do not move (free),
5 triples that move exactly as the addresses 1, 2, 3 do (row 0, column 0, the diagonal, the sums X[x,y] + X[y,x], and the
sum-zero part of the differences X[x,y] - X[y,x] after a fixed quarter turn: ONE call each), and one number that is
multiplied by the sign of the permutation, which costs a nested pair of calls (the sign is quadratic in the box: 6 linear
equations for the glue between the two calls, solved exactly). 5 + 2 = 7. [ran floats] The search agrees: skew part 4 calls
(none at 3 in 17 starts), symmetric part 3 calls (40 of 40 starts), `k4F.out`. Whether 6 is possible is open; it would need
the nested pair to ride on calls that already carry a triple.
[derived, rough] What this does as K grows. Under the address maps the K-vector is "2 fixed numbers + one piece W of
dimension K - 2", and the table is "5 fixed numbers + 6 copies of W + a rest of dimension about K^2 that is not made of
copies of W". The 6 copies cost one call each; the rest needs two boxes in a row for each K - 2 of its dimensions, so the
plain construction gives about 2K - 2: again a bounded saving. The bound 1.5 K would be met only by chains in which every
call is at once the second box for one piece of data and the first box for the next. The scale lemma forbids exactly that
for a box of unknown scale; nothing forbids it here. That chain is the object to look for.
Honest limits. (1) It is a lower bound that leaves room, not a program. (2) Free glue: the program may contain dense
matrices of K^4 entries. (3) For the transform itself g is known, so this family model says nothing directly; what it
says is which kind of unknown the engine's unit could be exploiting. It agrees with the cut-set lemma of
`wht38/tiny/filter/joint/NOTE.md` [read]: there, no unit saves a move without an address map; here, no continuous relation
saves more than 3 calls, and the address maps are the first family that is not closed.

## 5. Mutations ("yes, and")

1. The 3-call search failed for involutions, so I asked what a failure is made of: differentiate the program along the
   family. That gave the first-order problem (`tank.py`), then the rank form of it, then the telescoping lemma, which needs
   no derivative at all.
2. The lemma is empty for one box, so the idea became "price the glue": section 3 (a), (b).
3. The lemma is weak exactly when two boxes differ by a high-rank matrix, so I looked for a family of the transform's own
   world with that property: address maps, section 4.
4. Not followed: the table as a batch. The K^2-point transform is 2K instances of one linear map; for a matrix WITHOUT
   structure, K instances cost O(K^2.38) gates by fast matrix multiplication (the left factor is fixed, so every product
   in a bilinear algorithm becomes a scaling), against K x K^2 one by one. So "sharing a box" is real for boxes that cost
   more than K^1.38 gates each, and stops exactly because the transform is already cheaper than that [derived from memory
   of the literature, no source read tonight].

## 6. Criticism, last

- The telescoping lemma and the scale lemma are by hand and unreviewed. The rank table under them is exact. Check the
  induction step of the first (the matrices N_ji) and, in the second, that the degree-1 and degree-2 parts are what I say
  when a call's input mixes degrees (they are: the degree is the number of boxes passed).
- Every "none" in section 2 is a float search with 3 to 40 starts. The weak ones are marked. The lower bounds of section 1
  are the only exact negatives: at K = 4 they say 5 for reflections and rotations, 6 for involutions, 8 for symmetric.
- r(4) for reflections is therefore known only as a range.
- Section 3 (a) is a sketch with a stated gap (cancelling supports). Section 3 (b) is Morgenstern's count and gives
  nothing that the plain count for the whole circuit does not give; its use is the per-gate requirement on the glue.
- Section 4: the 7-call program is exact but it is a toy count: six boxes, K = 4, free glue, and one call saved. The rough
  count for larger K gives a bounded saving again; only the bound 1.5 K leaves room, and "leaves room" has been the status
  of many doors tonight. The full-table float search for this family did not converge in its time (`k4E.out`).
- The growth law of the brief (r(K) = aK with a < 1.866) is not reached by any family tried: for every continuous
  relation a tends to 2.
