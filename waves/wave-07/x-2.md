A search by annealing is never a proof that a count is impossible; every "none found" below is that and no more.

Words. N = 2^k *points*; an *address* is k bits; *the transform* H has entry (-1)^(common ones). *Wire*: a value the program
holds, a vector over the N inputs, taken up to a factor. *Sign vector*: a wire with entries 0, +1, -1; its *set* is where it is
not 0. *The floor*: f(N) = N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N (7, 19, 47 at 4, 8, 16 points); *excess* e = gates - f(N).
*Cut* c: one of the N - 1 ways to split the addresses into two parallel halves L, R (x is in L when it has an even number of
ones in common with c). *Cut down* a wire to a set X: keep its entries on X. A gate is **new at X** when its wire cut down to X
is not zero, not a single input, and not proportional to the cut of an earlier wire. *Hatch wire*: a wire the floor theorem
does not allow.

## 0. In ten lines

1. **One identity holds for every linear circuit, whatever its wires and coefficients** [derived; asserted by machine on the
   night's circuits, 15 of them outside the one-tree class with entries up to 16, and on every circuit found here]. For a cut into L, R: gates = n(L) + n(R) - B + E, where n(L) = gates new at L, B = gates new at BOTH
   halves, E = gates new at NEITHER half. The gates new at L are a circuit for the half-size transform on L.
2. **E >= N/2 + 1 for every circuit and every cut** [derived; asserted on all 7 to 63 cuts of 8 circuits and on every circuit
   found]: the rows come in N/2 pairs with the same two halves, and the first wire that crosses the cut is new at neither.
3. So the floor theorem's three hypotheses are used for one thing only: **B = 0** (I call it a *clean cut*). The recursion that
   replaces t(k) >= 2 t(k-1) + N/2 + 1 is **t(N) >= 2 t(N/2) + N/2 + 1 - B**. The N/2 + 1 is forced in every class. The factor
   2 is not: with no hypothesis the recursion is t(N) >= t(N/2) + N/2 + 1, which is linear (and weaker than the trivial 2N - 2).
4. **First experiment** [ran, `hatch.c`]: 8 points, 18 gates, with each hatch open: none found in 54 runs (crossing sets 9; any sets 19;
   three-quarter sets 6; dirty wires 17; entries up to 2: 3). Control: 19 gates found in 18 of 21 runs.
5. **Unexplained regularity** [ran]: all 18 circuits of 19 gates found, in every mode, are circuits of ONE tree with exactly
   one clean cut, 7 joins and 12 shifts, and contain no hatch wire, although hatch wires were allowed and proposed all the time.
   At 20 gates one circuit of 8 uses a crossing wire and a three-quarter wire and has no clean cut: its price was one gate.
6. **Growth law, sign vectors on any sets** (hatches a and c) [derived; list checked exhaustively up to 6 coordinates]: every
   gate between two sign vectors is one of five kinds with coefficients (1,1), (1,2) or (1/2,1/2). Morgenstern's determinant
   argument then gives gates >= 0.315 N log2 N. **Crossing sets and non-affine sets cannot change the order.**
7. **Growth law, dirty wires** (hatch b) [derived + ran]: with entries of size at most M the largest coefficient sum is 3, 8,
   21 for M = 1, 2, 3 and at most 4 M^2, so gates >= (N/2) log2 N / (2 + 2 log2 M). Only the number of binary digits a wire
   holds, growing with N, can change the order: about (log2 N)/(4C) digits for C N gates.
8. **What one gate CAN buy** [ran, `foreign.py`]: read through a tree that is not its own, a gate of the staircase is new at
   both halves of up to N/2 - 1 nodes, and at one parity cut all but 1.5 N of its (3/4) N log2 N gates are new at both halves.
   Every such event is repaid, one for one, by a gate new at neither half or by a half-circuit above its floor: the sum over
   the nodes is the excess e in EVERY tree. So "about s" is the capacity of a wire on s points; the net measured is 0.
9. **Modulo 4** [ran, `dirtybook.py`]: u-9's 3.2 N circuit has E = 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 17 at its root for 4 to 128 points, against
   N/2 + 1 = 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, 65. It is line (c), the pairs, that an inexact circuit escapes; and B > 0 as well.
10. No circuit under the floor, no growth law below N log N. The question is now: can gates new at both halves go unpaid?

## 1. The bookkeeping [derived; every identity asserted by the programs]

Take any circuit for H, list its wires in the order they are made, and take a cut into L and R.
- *The cuts are a circuit.* If w = a u + b v then w cut down to L is a times u's cut plus b times v's cut. If w is new at L,
  neither cut is zero and they are not proportional, and each was seen at or before its own gate. So the gates new at L, read
  on L, are a circuit of n(L) gates that holds every row of H cut down to L: the half-size transform. Hence n(L) >= least count
  at N/2. This uses nothing about the wires. For a class of wires it needs the class to be closed under cutting down: sign
  vectors on any sets are, dirty wires are, bounded entries are, and one tree cut down by any cut is again one tree.
- *The identity.* Every gate is new at L only, at R only, at both (B), or at neither (E): gates = n(L) + n(R) - B + E.
- *E >= N/2 + 1.* Rows y and y + c have the same cut on L and, up to sign, on R. The later of the two is new at neither half:
  N/2 gates. The first wire that is non-zero on both halves is made from one wire inside L and one inside R, so both its cuts
  are old; it is not the later row of a pair, because then the earlier row would cross before it. One more gate.
- *Down the tree.* For a whole cube tree, with s the size of a node X: gates = sum over the nodes of (E(X) - B(X)), and
  e = sum over the nodes of d(X), d(X) = E(X) - B(X) - s/2 - 1. Each gate is new on a subtree of nodes; E counts that
  subtree's lowest nodes ("leaves"), B its branchings, and leaves - branchings = 1.
- *What the floor theorem is* [read u-8 section 3, u-9 section 4; restated]: in its class B(X) = 0 at every node of the
  circuit's own tree, so d(X) >= 0 and e >= 0. Lines (c) and (d) of the proof are "E >= N/2 + 1" and hold for everybody;
  lines (a) and (b) are where B = 0 is obtained.
- *A consequence for any circuit under the floor* [derived]: take the smallest N at which some class (closed under cutting
  down) has a circuit with e < 0. Then for EVERY one of the N - 1 cuts, B >= 1 + (E - N/2 - 1) >= 1: no clean cut at all.
  And for linear size, least count C N up to lower-order terms: B >= 2 t(N/2) + N/2 + 1 - t(N), about N/2, for every cut. A linear circuit has no special
  direction: in every direction more than N/2 of its gates serve both half-size transforms at once.

## 2. Which line fails for which hatch [derived; the specimens are from the runs of section 3]

| hatch | line that fails | what stays true |
|---|---|---|
| (a) a set that crosses the tree | (a): a full wire can be the join of two pieces neither of which lies in L or in R (seen: wire 21 of `specimen20_noclean.txt` = join of wires on {0,1,4,5} and {2,3,6,7}); then (b): the two projections share gates, B > 0 | (c), (d): E >= N/2 + 1 |
| (b) form + 2 x form outside its set | (a) at once: such a wire is non-zero on both halves without being full, so "an L-wire" has no meaning; (b): B > 0 | (c), (d) for an exact circuit. Modulo 4 it is (c) that goes: the rows no longer pair |
| (c) three quarters of a node | nothing at the root; (a) at the node X of which it is three quarters: it crosses X's cut and is not full on X | the root step, verbatim, whenever no non-full wire crosses the root cut |

All three open the same door. The useful hypothesis is not "sign vectors", "one tree" or "affine" but "some cut is clean".

## 4. Growth laws

**4.1 Sign vectors on any sets: the order stands** [derived; `gatekinds.py` exhaustive at 3 to 6 coordinates].
If u, v, w = a u + b v are sign vectors, compare the sets: disjoint: w = u +- v (join); partly overlapping: the overlap must
cancel, w = u -+ v on the symmetric difference; one inside the other: w = u - v (cut u down) or w = u - 2v (shift); equal
sets: w = (u +- v)/2 (the part where they agree or disagree). Coefficient pairs (1,1), (1,2), (1/2,1/2); the machine finds
exactly these three. The largest determinant of N wires grows by a factor at most |a| + |b| <= 3 per gate and must reach
N^(N/2): gates >= (N/2) log2 N / log2 3 = 0.315 N log2 N. [read: h-1 quotes the same 0.315 for coefficients 1 and 2; what is
added is that sign vectors FORCE those coefficients, whatever the sets.]
Answer to the brief's question for hatches (a) and (c): one wire on s points can be new at both halves of up to s/2 - 1 nodes
(4.3), but summed over a circuit the unpaid part is at most 0.185 N log2 N + N: at most 0.585 per gate on average. Not s, not
log s: a constant, and the constant measured at 8 points is 0.

**4.2 Bounded entries: the order stands; digits are the only currency** [derived + ran].
A gate's coefficients are ratios of 2 x 2 determinants of the three vectors, so with entries of size at most M:
|a| + |b| <= 4 M^2. Exact largest values: 3, 8, 21 for M = 1, 2, 3 (3 = 2 + 1, 8 = 3 + 5, 21 = 13 + 8; every second Fibonacci
number [guess for larger M]). Hence gates >= 0.315, 0.167, 0.114 x N log2 N, and in general (N/2) log2 N / (2 + 2 log2 M).
"Form + 2 x form" is M = 2 or 3: it cannot change the order. With d binary digits per wire (M = 2^d): C N gates need
d >= (log2 N)/(4C) - 1. [The same statement as s-cross L3, reached from the gate list instead of the volume ledger.]
How weak this wall is at any real size [derived]: a bound c N log2 N excludes N (log2 N)^0.9 only once (log2 N)^0.1 > 1/c.
For sign vectors (c = 0.315) that is log2 N above about 100,000; for entries up to 2 (c = 0.167) above about 60 million.
So "the order stands" is a statement about the limit; at every size anyone will run, the determinant alone does not forbid
a saving of 0.1 even to sign vectors. What forbids it there, if anything does, is the unproved e >= 0.

**4.3 What a gate can do, read through a foreign tree** [ran, `foreign.py`, `alldirs.py`; e = sum of d asserted each time].
The night's circuits have B = 0 and one leaf per gate in their own tree. Through other trees:

| circuit | tree | root: E, B, d | most leaves of one gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 19 | own / reversed / sheared | 5, 0, 0 / 9, 5, -1 / 8, 5, -2 | 1 / 4 / 2 |
| staircase 16 (52 gates) | own / reversed / sheared | 12, 0, +3 / 26, 22, -5 / 16, 28, -21 | 1 / 8 / 2 |
| staircase 32 (128) | own / reversed / sheared | 24, 0, +7 / 64, 56, -9 / 32, 80, -65 | 1 / 16 / 2 |
| staircase 64 (304) | own / reversed / sheared | 48, 0, +15 / 152, 136, -17 / 64, 208, -177 | 1 / 32 / 2 |

- The sheared tree's top cut is the parity of all address bits. There B = 8, 28, 80, 208 = (3/4) N log2 N - 1.25 N: all but
  1.5 N gates of the staircase are new at both halves; E = N; d(root) = -(3/4) N log2 N + 1.75 N - 1. **The root term can be
  negative at the full order N log N.** It is paid below: each parity half then holds 10, 32, 88, 224 gates, about twice its
  floor (7, 19, 47, 111).
- Every one-tree circuit has exactly ONE clean cut among its N - 1 (`alldirs.out`: the 19, both 52s, the staircases, the
  textbook). In the other cuts the share B / min(n(L), n(R)) reaches 1.00: every gate of one half-circuit is also a gate of
  the other. Textbook, 8 points: B = 8, 4, 8, 0, 8, 4, 8 and E = 8 + B in every cut.
- Outside the class [ran, `outside.py`, 15 circuits: u-4's 26, 38 and 88, with entries up to 16, and h-6's 21]: both general
  claims hold in every cut; ten of the fifteen have NO clean cut, B reaches 50 of 88 gates, and all of them pay for it:
  e = 1, 7, 19, 41 for the ten, 2 and 7 for the five that keep one clean cut.
- So sharing is not rare; it is everywhere except in one direction. What never happened is sharing that is not repaid.
- If sharing could be had at a fixed rate sigma (B = sigma x t(N/2)) with half-circuits on their floor and E = N/2 + 1, the
  recursion t(N) = (2 - sigma) t(N/2) + N/2 + 1 would give about N / sigma gates: LINEAR for any fixed sigma > 0. The floor's
  rate (1/2) N log2 N is sigma = 0; a rate alpha < 1/2 is sigma = (1 - 2 alpha) / (alpha log2 N). [derived]

**4.4 The circuit that equals H modulo 4** [ran, `dirtybook.py`; u-9's construction rebuilt, congruence asserted].
Root of the cube tree, 4 to 128 points: E = 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 17 (the exact transform needs 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, 65);
B = 0, 1, 4, 11, 26, 57. So dirty wires do both things at once: they share gates between the halves, and, the outputs being
inexact, they escape the pairs. For the exact transform the pairs cannot be escaped (section 1), so the whole gain of the
modulo-4 object beyond sharing comes from not being H.

## 5. The wall, and what it is made of

- For sign vectors the wall is the determinant: three coefficient pairs, 0.315 N log2 N. Hatches (a) and (c) are closed as
  far as the ORDER goes. Whether they move the constant from 1/2 towards 0.315 is open and is a constant.
- For hatch (b) the wall is the same with log2(4 M^2) in place of log2 3; it gives way only if the entries grow with N.
- Under both sits one exact statement: e = (waste of the two half-circuits) - B + (E - N/2 - 1) for every cut, with
  E - N/2 - 1 >= 0 always. A circuit under the floor needs B to exceed the other two in EVERY one of its cuts. In every
  circuit of the night, and in every circuit the annealer returned, the three balance to e >= 0.
- Went around twice: (1) "no 18" became "what do the 19s look like": all one tree (line 5 of section 0); (2) "the term at the
  root must be >= 0" became false through a foreign tree (-177 at 64 points), which turned the per-node inequality into a
  conservation law.

## 6. Mutations (not judged)

1. **Pay E only once.** E = N/2 + 1 is forced per cut, but ONE row gate is the later row of its pair for many cuts at once
   (row number i in order of making, for i cuts). The rows alone supply N/2 for every cut. So E costs nothing extra in a
   circuit that makes the rows last; all the difficulty is in U = n(L) + n(R) - B <= t - N/2 - 1 for all N - 1 cuts together.
   Ask for a set of wires whose cuts, in every direction, fall into few classes.
2. **Isotropy as a search constraint.** Add "no clean cut" (B >= 1 in all N - 1 cuts) to the annealer's cost and look for
   the least count: at 8 points it is at most 20. Its growth with N is the price of the necessary condition.
3. **The classes of a cut are a bipartite graph** (left: classes on L; right: classes on R; an edge per crossing wire). A gate
   new at both halves starts a new component; d(root) = 1 - (components at the end) + (edges that close a cycle). The floor
   says: one component. A row pair that stays alone in its component would save one gate. I could not build one with sign
   vectors: its second row needs a second wire on one of the two parents' edges, which closes a cycle. [derived, sketch]
4. **Rich lines.** With complete sharing, the later row (h, -h) needs a parent of the form (q, -q - 2p) with h = p + q: on
   the R side that is "form + 2 x form outside its set", exactly a dirty wire. So dirty wires are what complete sharing asks
   for, at the place where the pairs are doubled. [derived, sketch; not checked by machine]
5. **Close the regularity.** Prove or refute: every 19-gate circuit at 8 points with entries of size at most 2 is a one-tree
   sign-vector circuit. It is a finite question about one-gate relations among 3,280 (or 195,312) vectors.
6. The published unit: run `alldirs.py` on one invocation of its helper circuit. As a family the published program goes
   below f(N) in the limit (count (G) is at most count (O), and (log N)^z with z < 1 falls below (1/2) log2 N), so in the
   limit it has unpaid sharing in every cut. One unit at its own size is far above f(N), so nothing is forced there; but
   B, E and the waste measured on it would show in which cuts its sharing sits and how it is paid. [guess that it is readable]

## 7. Criticism, last

- The identity of section 1 is bookkeeping: true of every circuit, it cannot by itself forbid anything. Its only content is
  E >= N/2 + 1 and "the cuts are a circuit". The unconditional recursion it gives is weaker than the trivial 2N - 2.
- "No 18" is 54 annealing runs, not a search of the space; the pool with entries up to 3 was not searched at all. The
  annealer finds 19 only late in its schedule, so its failures at 18 are moderate evidence.
- The regularity of line 5 may be an artefact of the annealer (it may simply find the commonest circuits). 18 circuits.
- 16 points gave nothing. The brief's "then at 16" is not done.
- 4.1 and 4.2 are bounds of the form c N log2 N: they fix the order in the limit and say nothing about a saving of 0.1 at
  real sizes (see 4.2). They are Morgenstern's theorem applied to a gate list; the list is hand-derived for sign vectors and machine-checked
  at 3 to 6 coordinates; for M = 2, 3 only at 3 and 4 coordinates (the coefficients are fixed by two coordinates, so more
  coordinates can only remove cases, but I did not run them).
- Mutations 3 and 4 are sketches made in minutes.
- The floor theorem itself (u-8, u-9) is still unreviewed; section 1 re-derives its lines (c), (d) in general but takes
  "B = 0 in the one-tree class" from those reports.
