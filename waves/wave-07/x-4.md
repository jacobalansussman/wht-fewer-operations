**No circuit below a known count and no growth law below N log N. Nothing here changes the order.**

Words. N = 2^k points, k even, s = sqrt(N). An *address* is k bits. Q(x) = x1 x2 + x3 x4 + ... ; *the quadric* is the set of
addresses with Q = 0 ((N + s)/2 of them). B(x, y) = Q(x + y) + Q(x) + Q(y) is the *pairing* of Q. H(x, y) = (-1)^B(x, y) is the
transform with the two bits of each pair exchanged in the output address. D = the diagonal of the signs (-1)^Q. R = D H / s.
A subspace of addresses is *singular* if Q = 0 on all of it; the largest ones have s addresses. *Half transform* F: the transform
applied to one bit of every pair only (s separate copies of the s-point transform; textbook cost (N/2) log2 N). A *flag* is a
chain of subspaces {0} = M_0 < M_1 < ... < M_k, each twice the size of the one before. A *flag kernel* is a matrix
K(x, y) = f(x + y) where f = sum over j of c_j [u in M_j] (so f is constant on each "shell" M_j minus M_(j-1)). *Flag depth*
r of a matrix: the least number of flag kernels K_1 ... K_r, on any flags, with free diagonals D_0 ... D_r, such that the matrix
is D_0 K_1 D_1 K_2 ... K_r D_r. Addition of addresses is bitwise (XOR).

## 1. What the split is [ran: `basis.py`, `basis.out`, exact, 4, 16, 64 points]

**The group.** D and H/s are two reflections whose product R has order 3 [ran: (D H)^3 = s^3 at 4, 16, 64]. They generate a
group of six elements (the symmetries of a triangle). Every representation of that group is made of three kinds of piece:
vectors fixed by both reflections (kind "+"), vectors negated by both (kind "-"), and planes on which the two act as two
mirrors at 60 degrees (kind "plane"). Since D is diagonal, kind "+" lives on Q = 0 and kind "-" on Q = 1, and each plane has
one direction on each side. That is s-cross's table. The counts [ran at 4, 16, 64; s-cross's 256-point numbers 51, 35, 85 fit]:

| | kind "+" (fixed, on Q = 0) | kind "-" (fixed, on Q = 1) | planes |
|---|---|---|---|
| formula | (s + 1)(s + 2)/6 | (s - 1)(s - 2)/6 | (N - 1)/3 |
| 4 points | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| 16 | 5 | 1 | 5 |
| 64 | 15 | 7 | 21 |

**Why these formulas** [ran: `orbits.py`, `orbits.out`, exact, 4, 16, 64 points; g-7 had the fact for R]. Write the address
as (c, b), c = the first bits of the pairs, b = the second. After the half transform F on c (call the new label a), D becomes
the relabelling (a, b) -> (a + b, b) and H/s becomes the exchange (a, b) -> (b, a), with no signs; R is their product. The
orbits counted by the run: 1 + 1 of sizes 1, 3 at 4 points; 1 + 3 + 1 of sizes 1, 3, 6 at 16; 1 + 7 + 7 at 64.
So in that basis the whole group permutes the N addresses,
and its orbits are: the address 0; for each nonzero a the three addresses (a, 0), (0, a), (a, a) (s - 1 orbits); and for each
pair a, b that span a plane, the six addresses made from it ((s - 1)(s - 2)/6 orbits). A fixed vector is a function constant
on orbits: 1 + (s - 1) + (s - 1)(s - 2)/6 = (s + 1)(s + 2)/6. Kind "-" needs an orbit of six: (s - 1)(s - 2)/6. The planes:
one per orbit of three, two per orbit of six: (s - 1) + (s - 1)(s - 2)/3 = (N - 1)/3. All three match the run.

**(i) The sparsest basis** [ran]. Singular subspaces of s addresses: 2, 6, 30 at 4, 16, 64 points. Each indicator is fixed by R.
Rank of their span: 2, 5, 15 = the whole fixed space on Q = 0. So yes, they span, and a basis needs (s + 1)(s + 2)/6 of them.
The other fixed vectors of the same shape (signs (-1)^Q on one coset of a subspace on which B vanishes but Q does not: 1, 9,
105 of them) lie in the same span. [derived] Support s is the least possible: a vector v with H v = s v has
|support of v| x |support of H v| >= N (the uncertainty inequality of the transform), so |support| >= s.
On Q = 1 the natural fixed vectors have support 3s/2 [ran, `orbits.out`: 6 at 16 points, 12 at 64]: the alternating sum over
an orbit of six, pulled back by F, lives on three cosets and vanishes on half of each. At 16 points that is all six points of
Q = 1. The orbit sums themselves pull back to fixed vectors on Q = 0 with supports s and 3s/2 [ran: 4 and 6; 8 and 12], and
these are orthogonal to each other (F is orthogonal up to a factor and orbits are disjoint).

**(ii) The projection.** [derived] In the half-transformed basis: average over each orbit (at most six entries). In the
original basis: F, average, F back. The indicators of (i) are NOT orthogonal (two of them share 1, 2, 4, ... addresses), so
"take the inner products with a sparse basis" is only half of a projection; the other half is the inverse of their table of
inner products, and that inverse is where the half transform hides. An orthogonal basis of the whole fixed space with supports
at most 3s/2 is the one of the previous paragraph [ran].

**(iii) The coupling U.** [derived from the run above] On each plane, in the orbit basis, D and H/s are two fixed 2 x 2
mirrors on at most six addresses. Choose in every plane the direction fixed by D and the direction negated by D: in those bases
U is the identity matrix of size (N - 1)/3. (Any coupling is the identity in matched bases; the content is that here BOTH
matched bases are the half transform of vectors that sit on one orbit, so nothing is left to compute between them.)
**U is not a smaller copy of anything.** In the original basis U "looks like" the block of H between the two sides of the
quadric (28 x 36 of rank 21 at 64 points [ran]) only because the two sides are written in bases that differ by a half transform.

**Gates by "project, scale, couple".** F, then a relabelling (H/s is the exchange (a, b) -> (b, a): no gate at all), then F
back. 16 points: 2 x 4 x 8 = 64 with textbook 4-point pieces, 2 x 4 x 7 = 56 with the 7-gate brick (g-7's number), against
the record 52. 64 points: 2 x 8 x 19 = 304 with the 19-gate 8-point circuit: this is exactly the "19 x 19" record circuit,
so the route ties 304 because it is the same circuit.

**Growth law of the split** [derived]. T(N) = 2 s T(s) + 0. The pieces have size sqrt(N), so the right weight of a piece is
(size/N) x (log size / log N): 2 x s x (s/N) x (1/2) = 1. Exactly 1: N log N. The split also respects adding one pair of bits:
the 4-point case is 2 x "+" and one plane, and tensoring gives the recursion t' = 2t + m, g' = 2g + m, m' = t + g + 3m for the
three counts (2, 0, 1 -> 5, 1, 5 -> 15, 7, 21 -> 51, 35, 85, matching every row). In that form the pieces are four slices of
N/4 points: 4 x 1/4 = 1 again. s-cross's rough "4/4 + 1/3" came from pricing U as a transform; U is free, and the two
projections are not two calls at N/4 but one half transform each way.

## 2. Three identities [ran exact at 4, 16, 64: `basis.out`]

- **H = s D + K - (2/s) D K^2**, K = H with its two diagonal blocks (Q = 0 with Q = 0, Q = 1 with Q = 1) set to zero. So the
  transform is two applications of "the part of H that crosses the quadric" (rank 2(N - 1)/3). Not a saving: K = (H - D H D)/2.
- **R = (1 + A)(1 - A)^-1 with A = R - R^T** (entries 0 and +-2/s, zero inside each side of the quadric): the rotation is the
  Cayley transform of its own antisymmetric part. Cute; I found no use.
- **H(x, y) = (-1)^Q(x) x (-1)^Q(x + y) x (-1)^Q(y).** Trivial to check (it is the definition of B), and it is the relation
  H D H = s D H D behind R^3 = 1. Read as an algorithm it is Bluestein's trick for the Walsh transform: change signs, convolve
  (XOR-convolution) with the fixed vector chi = (-1)^Q, change signs. **Computing H = convolving with the sign vector of a
  quadric.** One normally computes convolutions through the transform; here the arrow is turned round.

## 3. The better question: flag depth

**Why flags.** Convolution with the indicator of a subspace M is "sum over each coset of M": N - N/|M| gates, outputs are
copies. The quadric is a union of singular subspaces of s addresses, and they can be chosen to meet only in 0 (a "spread":
s/2 + 1 of them), giving H = D (2 (sum of s/2 + 1 coset-sum maps) - s - all-ones) D [derived]: N^1.5 gates, because subspaces
that meet only in 0 share no partial sum. Sharing needs NESTED subspaces, and nested subspaces are a flag. For one flag,
all the coset sums cost N - 1 gates (one merge tree), and any weighted combination sum_j c_j (coset sum at level j) comes back
down the same tree in 2N - 2 gates: **a flag kernel costs 3N - 3 gates, whatever the numbers c_j** [derived]. Diagonals are
free in count (G). So

    gates(H) <= 3 N x (flag depth of H),

and linear size would follow from bounded flag depth. In other words: a flag kernel is "diagonal in a Haar wavelet basis",
and the question is how many alternations of Haar bases (in any linear coordinates) and diagonals make a transform.
The model escapes s-cross's L2 (wires leave the one-flag class after the first diagonal) and L4 (the c_j and the diagonals
are unbounded, and a kernel with c_j growing geometrically carries O(N) x log of volume).

**Upper bound, r <= k/2** [ran exact, `flagexact.py`]. (-1)^(x1 x2) is a flag function: with M_1 = {0, 11} and M_2 = all four
addresses of the pair it is 2[u = 0] - 2[u in M_1] + [u in M_2]. So chi is a product of k/2 commuting flag kernels:
H = D K_1 ... K_(k/2) D, checked as a gate program at 4, 16, 64 points: 9, 72, 432 gates = (9/8) N log2 N (textbook 8, 64, 384).
This is the tensor structure again; the plain program is worse than the textbook and no constant is claimed.

**Lemma A (no diagonal in between): r >= k/2** [derived]. If H = D_0 K_1 ... K_r D_r with nothing between the kernels, then
in the transformed domain a product of r functions, each constant on the shells of a flag (of the dual flags), equals the sign
vector of a non-degenerate quadratic form. Each factor is constant on the complement of a hyperplane; the r complements meet
in an affine set of dimension at least k - r, where the product is constant. A non-degenerate quadratic form in k variables is
constant on no affine set of dimension above k/2. So r >= k/2: without inner diagonals the tensor arrangement is optimal.

**Lemma B: r >= 2 from 8 points on** [derived]. d_0(x) f(x + y) d_1(y) = (-1)^B(x, y) makes the block "x in M_(k-1), y
outside" of H a rank-one matrix up to scalings; that block has rank N/4 or N/2 [ran: 2 or 4, 4 or 8, 8 or 16 at 8, 16, 32
points, all hyperplanes].

**Lemma C: r >= 3 from 32 points on, over the real numbers** [derived; forced step ran]. Let m_i be the generator of the
first step of flag i and tau_i the shift x -> x + m_i. A flag kernel is K_i = (number a_i) + (1 + tau_i)(something), so
(1 - tau_1) K_1 = a_1 (1 - tau_1) and K_2 (1 - tau_2) = a_2 (1 - tau_2). Hence, from H = D_0 K_1 D_1 K_2 D_2,

    (1 - tau_1) D_0^-1 H D_2^-1 (1 - tau_2) = a_1 a_2 (1 - tau_1) D_1 (1 - tau_2).

The right side has at most four nonzero entries per row (y - x in {0, m_1, m_2, m_1 + m_2}). The left side at (x, y) is
H(x, y) / (d_0(x) d_2(y)) times 1 - p(x) chi(y) - q(y) psi(x) + e p(x) q(y) chi(y) psi(x), with p(x) = d_0(x)/d_0(x + m_1),
q(y) = d_2(y)/d_2(y + m_2), chi(y) = (-1)^B(m_1, y), psi(x) = (-1)^B(x, m_2), e = (-1)^B(m_1, m_2). For e = +1 the bracket is
(1 - p chi)(1 - q psi); it must vanish for the N - 4 values of y outside the four. From 32 points on, the sets involved
overlap (N/2 - 8 > 0) and force p = eta psi, q = -eta chi with one sign eta [ran: `flagexact.out`, exhaustive search with
pruning: exactly these two solutions at 32 and 64 points, three solutions at 16 points; and `peel8.out`: at 32 points, plain
labelling, none of the 450 pairs m_1, m_2 with B(m_1, m_2) = 0 allows a nonzero right side]. With them the bracket vanishes
everywhere, so the right side vanishes, so a_1 a_2 = 0, so one kernel has rank at most N/2: contradiction. For e = -1 the
same steps give q = A^chi, (1 - p)/(1 + p) = A psi and the same end [derived only]. Left out: m_1 = m_2 with D_1 odd under
the shift, which I closed only by a sketch (it reduces to Lemma B on N/4 points; over the complex numbers one sub-case stays
open). At 16 points the third solution of the forced step is the one the tensor arrangement uses [ran: the identity above holds
on it, `flagexact.out`], which is why depth 2 exists there and (up to the cases left out) nowhere above.

**Lemma D, every k: r >= 2^(ceil(k/r) - 2), so r >= about k / log2 k** [derived]. Cut each kernel at level t: K_i = (terms
with j <= t) + (terms with j > t). The first part is block-diagonal on the cosets of M^i_t (blocks of 2^t addresses); the
second has rank at most N/2^(t+1). The product of r such factors is (product of the block-diagonal parts) + (rank at most
r N/2^(t+1)). The product of the block-diagonal parts is zero unless x + y lies in W = M^1_t + ... + M^r_t, of dimension at
most r t. If r t < k, W lies in a hyperplane; rows in that hyperplane and columns outside it see only the low-rank part.
That block of H has rank at least N/4 (Lemma B's run). So N/4 <= r N/2^(t+1) with t = ceil(k/r) - 1.
(The same count works with a free weight on every NODE of the coset tree, with N/2^t in place of N/2^(t+1): what it needs is
that the blocks are cosets, not that the weights go by level.)

| k | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 32 | 64 | 256 | 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r at least (Lemmas B, C, D) | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 13 | 37 | 128 |
| r at most (k/2) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 32 | 128 | 512 |

**The same with any tree and a free number on every node** [derived]. Take ANY binary merge tree on the N addresses (its
nodes need not be cosets) and a number c_v on every node v, and let K = sum over the nodes of c_v x (the all-ones block
v x v), that is K(x, y) = G(lowest common node of x and y). It still costs 3N - 3 gates: up the tree, one weight per node,
down the tree. Flag kernels are the special case "coset tree, weight by level". Call the least r with
H = D_0 K_1 D_1 ... K_r D_r the *tree depth* of H. Bounded tree depth would be linear size. What I can say:
- Tree depth 1 is impossible from 8 points on [derived]: the top split A | rest gives a rank-one block of H; a rank-one
  block a x b of the transform has a b <= N (Lindsey's lemma), so A is one address, then the next split is one address too,
  and two rows of H would be proportional on N - 2 columns, while two rows agree on exactly N/2.
- **Lemma D's count dies** [derived]. For BALANCED trees that are not coset trees the block-diagonal parts multiply to a
  matrix with at most 2^(r t) nonzeros per row, and the statement left is "H = (2^(r t) nonzeros per row) + (rank r N/2^t)
  for every t". No known rigidity bound forbids that (the known bound, N^2/(4 x rank) changed entries, is met with room).
- **Lemma C survives one step of freedom, by a longer road** [derived, 15 minutes old]. Keep coset trees at the lowest level only
  (the sibling pairs of tree i are {x, x + m_i}, m_1 different from m_2, B(m_1, m_2) = 0) and free every weight. The forced
  step of Lemma C is unchanged. Its contradiction is not: now the leaf weights may vanish on half of the addresses, as they
  do in a butterfly (the 2 x 2 matrix (1, 1; 1, -1) IS a kernel with leaf weights 0 and -2). Pushing on: the leaf weights of
  K_1 vanish on a set Z, those of K_2 on its complement, and then the upper part of K_2 (a tree kernel on the N/2 pairs) is
  forced to be, after scaling rows and columns and relabelling rows, two blocks that are each an (N/4) x (N/4) matrix of
  signs with orthogonal rows cut out of H. And [derived, by cases on the top splits, with Lindsey's lemma] **no 8 x 8 block of
  any tree kernel, rows and columns chosen freely, is a scaled 8 x 8 sign matrix with orthogonal rows.** So depth 2 stops at
  16 points in this class too. The shape of the argument is the thing to keep: **two passes for N points force a one-pass
  problem at N/4 points.** If "r passes for N points force r - 1 passes at N/4 points" held in general, tree depth would be
  exactly k/2 and the pairing of bits would be optimal; I could not make the step from 3 to 2.
- What bounded depth r with balanced trees WOULD imply: with N^e changed entries per row the transform drops to rank
  N^(1 - e/r). The known non-rigidity of the transform [from memory, Alman and Williams 2017; not re-read tonight] reaches
  rank N^(1 - c e^2/log(1/e)): e^2 where this needs e. So it would be a stronger non-rigidity statement than any known one.
  That is the place a "no" could come from (a proof that the exponent cannot be linear in e).
- For PATH-like trees (one address split off at a time) not even that is implied: the part above the cut is not of low rank.
  A kernel on such a tree is "running sums along one order of the addresses, a diagonal, running sums back": S^T C S with S
  the running-sum matrix. So tree depth contains the two-chain programs of waves 5 and 6 with the two things their wall L1
  lists as not covered: weights on both sides and diagonals in between. For two such kernels the statement to decide is exact
  and finite [derived]: write M = D_0^-1 H D_2^-1 in the two orders; its mixed second difference, divided by c_1(i) c_2(j),
  must have a mixed second difference with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column.
- One tool survives [derived]: if {x, x'} is a pair of sibling leaves of tree 1 and {y, y'} a pair of sibling leaves of tree 2
  with no address in common, then for r = 2 and M = D_0^-1 H D_2^-1: M(x, y) - M(x', y) - M(x, y') + M(x', y') = 0.
  For balanced trees that is about (N/2)^2 equations for N unknown ratios: a finite, exact question at 32 points.

**What this says about growth** [derived]. In the FLAG model the transform costs at least about N log2 N / log2 log2 N kernels'
worth of work and at most (9/8) N log2 N (the pair program). **No linear size here, and not even the published exponent in the long run**
((log N)^(1 - d) is eventually below log N / log log N for every d > 0). The model is exact at 16 and 64 points (r = 2, 3).
The gap opens at 256 points: is r = 3 or 4? If inner diagonals never help (r = k/2 always) the model is a wall with a short
statement; if they help at 256 points, one kernel has done more than its two bits, and the rate of that is the growth law.
Lemma D is a rigidity count (block-diagonal plus low rank), and the transform is known NOT to be rigid, so I do not expect
Lemma D to be improvable by the same count; Lemma C's method (peel the first and the last kernel by their first step) is the
one that reached the truth at 32 and 64 points.

## 4. Flag depth 2 at 8 points: open; and what the searches did not show

## 5. Mutations (what I would do next)

1. **three passes at 256 points.** Decide r = 3 or 4 at 256 points, flags first. By Lemma C's method: peel K_1 and K_3; what is
   left is (1 - tau_1) D_0^-1 H D_3^-1 (1 - tau_3) = a_1 a_3 (1 - tau_1) D_1 K_2 D_2 (1 - tau_3), and on every shell of flag 2
   above the level of m_1 and m_3 the right side is ONE rank-one matrix times a number per shell [derived]. That is a strong
   condition on a "second difference" of H; work it out at 64 points (where r = 3 exists) to see which solutions it allows,
   then at 256. A numerical search needs a better solver than mine (section 4): fix the target, use exact algebra for the
   forced steps, and only then fit.
2. **tree depth 2 at 32 points, any trees.** The honest first case of the general model. Tools: the alternating-sum identity
   for disjoint sibling pairs; Lemma C's longer road; [guess, a sketch only] when the sibling pairs have varying differences
   the identity turns into "a table of values of B on pairs is of the form u(c) + v(c') except at about N places", and a
   sparse matrix with a nonzero in every row has rank far above 2k + 2 for large N, which would close balanced trees.
   Path-like trees (running sums along an order) are the other end and nothing here touches them.
3. **two flags at once.** A kernel f(x + y) with f = sum of two flag functions on different flags costs 6N; Lemma A's count
   then allows r >= k/4. Sums of a few flag functions are the "rectangles over two trees" of k-cross seen as convolutions.
4. **the same question for subset sums.** Z is also a convolution-like object for the lattice of sets (a product of k commuting
   "flag kernels" of the semigroup). Flag depth of Z with free diagonals: Lemma D's count needs a block of high rank, and Z
   has them, so I expect the same k / log k; nobody has asked.
5. **odd k.** One bit alone has flag depth 2 over the reals [derived: a 2 x 2 flag kernel has a positive cross-ratio, the
   butterfly has -1, and a product of two reaches it] but depth 1 over the Gaussian numbers ((-i)^Hamming distance), and
   depth 1 as a tree kernel with free leaf weights. So over the Gaussian numbers r <= k/2 rounded up for every k, odd or
   even, and Lemma C's open sub-case (both flags start with the same pair) is exactly there.

## 6. Criticism, last

- The answer to the brief is a "no" with its reason: the third-turn split is the symmetry of the half-transform factorisation,
  g-7 had the key fact (R is a relabelling after a half transform), and I only drew the consequence for U and the pieces.
  Section 1's statements (ii) and (iii) and the supports 3s/2 are derived, not run; the dimensions and (i) are run.
- The three identities of section 2 are exact and elementary. The third is one line of algebra; calling it Bluestein's trick is
  a reading, not a result.
- The flag-depth model is mine of tonight and may be known under another name (products of Haar-diagonal factors; "HODLR"
  matrices of rank one). I did not search the literature. It is a small subclass of circuits: a bound in it says nothing
  about circuits in general.
- Lemma C is forty minutes old. Its forced step is machine-checked only for a few choices of m_1, m_2 and only in the case
  B(m_1, m_2) = 0; the other case and the degenerate case m_1 = m_2 are by hand, the latter a sketch.
- Lemma D is a rank count and I am fairly sure of it; the table's small entries use Lemma C.
- The cost "3N - 3 per kernel" is an upper bound for one kernel; kernels supported on a small subspace cost less, and the
  plain depth-k/2 program is worse than the textbook. Flag depth is a way to ask a growth question, not a route to a constant.
- The float searches of section 4 are too thin to count as evidence in either direction. The exact check there is only a
  necessary condition. Whether 8 points has flag depth 2 is open.
- "Lemma C by a longer road" and "no 8 x 8 block of a tree kernel is a scaled sign matrix with orthogonal rows" were derived
  in the last quarter of the hour, by cases, with no run behind them. Treat them as claims to attack.
- The words "tree depth" hide a choice: I allow any tree and any weights but still only DIAGONALS between passes. With
  arbitrary relabellings between passes nothing changes (a relabelled tree is a tree). With a second tree on the way down
  (k-cross's rectangles) the class is larger and I said nothing about it.
- Nothing here changes the order, or any constant.
