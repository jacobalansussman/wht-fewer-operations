**Words.** N = 2^k points; an address has k bits. The *textbook* computes the transform in N·log2 N gates. Count **(G)**: a gate is any a·u + b·v, scalings free. Count **(O)**: OpenAI's, additions and scalar multiplications each charged. Numbers are (G) unless a line says (O). *Subset sums* (Z): for every set S, the sum of the data over the subsets of S; *Yates* is the classical circuit, (N/2)·log2 N gates; f(N) and T(N) are the least gates for subset sums and for the transform, and T ≤ 2f + N. A *kernel* is a 2 × 2 block; the *class* is all matrices built from a kernel the way the transform is built from 1, 1, 1, −1; a *class identity* is a circuit that works for every kernel (1, 1; 1, τ): the transform is τ = −1, subset sums are τ = 0. *Degree d at all cube points*: the N values of a polynomial of degree at most d in the k bits, computed from its coefficients; its *rank* is the number of coefficients. A *helper* is a wire that is not an output. A *relation* is a combination of outputs that is always zero; its *length* is the number of outputs in it. A *flat* wire has entries 0, +1, −1. A *halving* is a gate that divides by 2; a *deep* gate is u ± 4v or deeper. A *minor* is the determinant of a square piece of a matrix; the largest one measures *volume*.

## 1. What came out, in ten lines

1. **Nothing beats the textbook.** No circuit over the rationals below a known count, no growth law below N·log N beyond the published one, nothing of the 1e-1 kind, no linear size [read: ten reports, the recombination, three critics].
2. **Wave 9's question has its answer: one more digit is cheap, and it does not matter** (section 2). A polynomial of degree 3 at all N cube points costs N + o(N) gates [computed: built and replayed to 32,768 points by the critic `ai-0` with a program of its own; argued for every size by an explicit formula]. It first beats the known 2N at addresses of 2,060 bits, and the construction fails at degree 4. That problem was already linear. It gives nothing for the transform.
3. **One lemma was found four times** (`ag-0`, `ag-7`, `ag-4`, `ag-5`): outputs with no short relation cannot share helpers. It gives a floor of 1.67·N to 2·N for degree about log2 k and above [argued; re-derived by `ai-1`; arithmetic modulo 2 only]. Its ceiling is 2N: it can never say more than "linear".
4. **Depth is not paid per gate.** The same 19 gates at 8 points compute the third power of the kernel (1, t; t, 1) for nine values of t; they carry 12 units of depth for the transform and up to 84 for the others [computed, exact, `ag-6`]. A halving weighs nothing in the ledger of depth, and circuits with flat wires need 0.3155·N·log2 N gates besides their halvings [argued by `ag-1`; re-derived by `ai-2`, who shows it is Morgenstern's argument of 1973].
5. **Constants that are not rational and halvings: no profit anywhere** (section 3). A constant of degree d is worth at most d·d rational gates, so constants of bounded degree leave the exponent unchanged [argued, `ag-9`; run on one circuit].
6. **"Sharing is repaid" was measured in three more rooms and is exact on one more route** [computed modulo 2, `ag-2`; computed in fractions, `ag-4`]. A *cut* splits the data of a circuit for subset sums along one address bit into a lower and an upper half. On all 20,880 cuts of minimum circuits at 8 points every gate gained is paid back by exactly one. At 16 points three exhaustive searches over all 1,208 upper circuits return nothing.
7. **Every record of the night is a class identity** [computed, `ag-8`]: 7, 19, 20, 52, 128, 304 gates all work for a general τ (the 19 after renaming the addresses). Among all circuits with 8 gates at 4 points, none is special to the transform [computed modulo 13 and 17].
8. **Other objects that now exist** [computed by their authors]: the tower as a circuit (23,766 gates at 8,192 points, built by the critic); three-helper families with 29, 61, 125 classes at 32, 64, 128 points, one fewer than two helpers allow (`ag-5`; classes, not gates); a 4-point matrix where a gate u ± 4v saves a gate (`ag-6`); the largest minor of subset sums, 1, 2, 5 at 4, 8, 16 points, every minor tried (`ai-2`).

## 2. The price of a binary digit

**The dictionary** [read: `ae-cross`, computed there for k = 2 to 6]. The j-th binary digit of the transform at an output is a polynomial of degree j in that output's address bits. So the transform up to multiples of 2^(d+1) is M·D·M^T, with M the matrix "degree at most d at all cube points" and D a free diagonal. The route costs twice the gates of M, plus N, minus the rank [argued, `af-0`, `ai-0`].

- **What is exact at these sizes** [computed by `ag-7`, exhaustive modulo 2 and 3, not over the rationals]. 8 points: 7, 11, 12 for degrees 1, 2, 3, the textbook's numbers. 16 points: 15 for degree 1; between 21 and 26 for degree 2 (no 20 modulo 2); between 26 and 31 for degree 3 (nothing run; the lower end is argued); 31 or 32 for degree 4. - **No step below the textbook's has been found at any of these sizes** [read]. The two cheap constructions do not bite here: wave 9's degree-2 circuit has 29, 63, 130 gates at 16, 32, 64 points against the textbook's 26, 57, 120 [read: `af-0`'s count], and the tower has 36 at 16 points against 31 [computed, `ai-0`].
- **Every step in the table is below N, and that means nothing.** At small sizes there are few coefficients of each degree. For a fixed degree the textbook's step tends to N as k grows [mine: 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0 gates per point at k = 40]. The question is about growth.
- **Growth, degrees 2 and 3.** Both cost N + o(N) in total, so the third and fourth digits cost o(N) each [degree 2: wave 9; degree 3: computed and argued, `ag-0`, `ai-0`]. The excess for degree 3 falls like 1 over log*(k), the number of times one takes log2 of k before reaching 1. The count first passes below 2N at k = 2,060: at most 1.943·N against 2.008·N [computed as a count; nothing built]. At 8,192 points the tower costs 2.90 gates per point, the textbook 2.99, and the best route known before it 2.69.
- **Growth, degree 4 and beyond.** The tower fails at degree 4 at every size tried [computed at 16, 2,048, 8,192 points]. From degree log2 k + c on, any circuit costs at least N·(1 + 2/(1 + 2^(2^−c))) minus the rank: 1.67·N, 1.83·N, 1.91·N, 1.96·N at c = 0, 1, 2, 3 [argued; table recomputed exactly by `ai-1` for k = 16 to 256; arithmetic modulo 2]. Over the rationals the same floor arrives three degrees later, by an argument twenty minutes old [argued, `ai-1`, unreviewed]. So N + o(N) per rung cannot go on past degree about log2 k.
- **Three helpers per output** cost exactly 2N gates, since an output with h helpers costs h − 1 gates. Whether 2N + o(N) carries a growing degree is open [argued, `ah-cross`].

**Plain answer.** In the limit, yes, for two digits: after degree 1 (N − 1 gates), degree 2 and degree 3 each add only o(N). Degree 3 is this wave's, and its saving starts at about 2^2060 points. At 16, 32 and 64 points no digit costs less than the textbook charges. Beyond degree 3 nothing found prices a step below N. The transform has k digits, and 96 to 98 % of its coefficients sit in the middle ones [read: wave 9]. The ladder gives no growth law.

## 3. Constants that are not rational, and halvings

**Constants** (`ag-9`; no critic).
- **The lemma** [argued by hand; computed on one circuit: 9 gates over the numbers q0 + q1·√2 became 13 rational gates, bound 26]. A number of degree d is d rational numbers and a multiplication table. So a circuit with s gates and constants of degree d becomes a rational circuit with at most d·d·s gates in count (G), twice that in (O). The Gaussian integers, √2 and the golden ratio have d = 2: a factor of at most 4. The eighth roots of unity have d = 4: at most 16. The exponent is the same, and linear size over such numbers is linear size over the rationals.
- **4 points** [computed, exhaustive modulo each prime named]. No circuit with 6 gates modulo 17 or 41. The known argument for 7 uses nothing special to the rationals, so 7 is the least over every field that contains them [argued]. For every pair of kernels of the class the least is exactly 7 modulo 7, 13, 17 and 41 (15, 66, 120, 780 pairs), including the values that stand for roots of unity, √2 and the golden ratio.
- **8 points** [computed; a random walk, not exhaustive]. 168 runs, 504 million moves, modulo 8 primes from 3 to 73, every coefficient allowed: no circuit with 18 gates. The walk only covers the neighbourhood of the known 19: from a random start it builds no circuit at all. The exact least at 8 points stays open, between 15 and 19.
- **What is left** [argued]. Constants whose degree grows at least like √(log2 N). OpenAI's RAM model does allow that: its scalars come from one root of unity whose order the program chooses [read by `ag-9` in the Lean files]. Nobody has a construction that uses a degree.

**Halvings and deep gates** (`ag-1`, `ag-6`; the ledger re-derived by `ai-2`).
- **4 points** [computed, exhaustive, `ag-6`]. The transform costs 7 gates with coefficients 1 and 2, and 8 without a gate u ± 2v: 8 with coefficient 1 only, with 1, 4 and 8, and with odd coefficients up to 11. All 228 sets of helpers that give 7 gates, deep gates allowed, are circuits with coefficients 1 and 2. Over 223,872 classes of targets with entries 0, ±1 on 4 inputs, the coefficients 4 and 8 never save a gate and division changes no count.
- **8 points** [computed; a census and a local search, not exhaustive]. None of the 26 printed circuits with 19 gates holds a deep gate or a halving. Adding one new half to the 19-gate circuit and removing two helpers gives no 18 in 16,500 tries (`ag-1`).
- **16 points, one 8 × 8 block** (half of the matrix that gives every address the sum of the data at its four neighbours; 16 gates known over the rationals, 15 modulo 2 [read: wave 9]) [computed, `ag-1`]. No circuit with 14 gates and one halving of any depth. No circuit with 15 gates and one halving by exactly 2 (0 of 8,695 candidates). A halving by 4 is excluded in 2,538 of those 8,695; the rest were not run. Two or more halvings were not searched.
- **Why** [argued; known since 1973, `ai-2`]. Scale every wire to whole numbers with no common factor. A gate (a·u + b·v)/c then raises the largest minor by at most (|a| + |b|)/c. A gate u ± v is worth 1 unit, u ± 2v is worth 1.585, a halving 0, and the transform needs (N/2)·log2 N units. A halving carries no depth; it hands depth back.
- **A deep gate does pay elsewhere** [computed, exhaustive, `ag-6`]. The matrix with rows (1,1,1,1), (1,−3,1,−3), (1,1,−3,−3), (1,−3,−3,9) costs 7 gates with gates u ± 4v, and has no circuit with 7 gates when every gate is shallower (coefficients 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9). For the transform a gate u ± 4v would have to cross two address bits at once, and the wire it reads then belongs to one output and cannot be shared [argued, `ag-6`].

**Plain answer.** No circuit found uses a constant that is not rational, a halving, or a deep gate at a profit. For constants of bounded degree it is argued that none can change the order. For halvings between flat wires it is argued that none can.

## 4. The ideas that are alive

**A. Coupling two half-size subset sums.** Subset sums on 2n points are (Z a, Z a + Z b). So f(2n) is the cost D(n) of two independent copies, plus or minus n [argued, `ah-cross`]. Linear size for subset sums, hence for the transform, needs the coupling cost per point, (f(2n) − 2·f(n))/2n, to add up to a finite number over all doublings. Yates pays 1/2 at every doubling, and the least counts known (0, 1, 4, 12 at 1 to 8 points) give 1/2 three times [read: `d-0`; 11 at 8 points is excluded only modulo 2, 3, 5, 7]. The first open value is at 16 points: 32 or 31. Two questions hide in it that were never separated: do two copies share anything, and can the + n be avoided. *Next:* 4 and 5.

**B. Subset sums with coefficients +1 and −1.** The volume count asks at least 0.277·N·√(log2 N) − N gates of such a circuit; Yates spends (N/2)·log2 N [argued by `ai-2` from known lemmas; minors computed to 256 points]. A circuit at the lower figure would give the transform in O(N·√(log N)) by T ≤ 2f + N: an exponent of 1/2, far past the bar [guessed; no evidence for it]. Every count of the night sits at Yates, and the lower figure passes the trivial N − 1 only beyond 2^52 points. No argument known closes the gap. The same √(log N) shows up in `ah-cross`'s window: a polynomial of degree about 5·√k in the number of common ones equals the transform on 99 % of its entries [computed], and what is left over has never been priced. *Next:* 5.

**C. Relations among mixed wires.** In a cut, a wire is *mixed* when it reads the upper half of the data. A set R of mixed wires whose upper parts cancel hands the lower half a form of L terms for |R| − 1 gates: a gain of L − |R| [argued, `ag-2`; half of it tested on 20,880 cuts]. A change of order needs L/|R| to grow without bound. It is 2 at 16 points, on paper, for relations of two and of three wires [computed modulo 2]. One anomaly: in all 13,447,158 cases reached, the form handed over has exactly one term, and nobody knows why. Not searched: relations of four wires, two or more extra gates across the cut. *Next:* 1.

**D. Two gates per output.** Nothing forbids 2N + o(N) gates at a growing degree: the floor of section 2 stops at 2N, and matrices with long relations and linear cost are known to exist [read second hand by `ai-1`]. Nothing builds it either. In classes a third helper changes the law; in gates every route still costs d − 1 per point [argued, `ag-5`]. Search cannot see this below 1,024 points. Even a yes is a law for the low rungs, not for the transform. *Next:* 2.

**E. A shared deep gate in a class identity.** In a class identity each gate has a weight, its degree in τ, and the weights add up to at least (N/2)·log2 N [argued, `ah-cross`; re-derived by `ai-2`]. The textbook meets this exactly. For the kernel (1, t; t, 1) the need is N·log2 N, 24 at 8 points, and the 19 gates spend 31 [computed, `ai-2`]. Linear size inside the class needs gates of weight about log2 N that many outputs read. `ag-6` argues against it only for a gate that ends in an output. Caution: the weight depends on the letter (correction vi). *Next:* 3, 6, 7, 8.

**F. The engine's one open integer.** In the published program a *move* is one direction of the transform applied to one whole array, a *shear* adds a multiple of one array to another, the *port* is the set of directions along which the transform splits off, and an *address map* relabels the addresses inside a block for free. `ag-3` extends "n free moves need n + 1 shears" to every free step that fixes the port [argued; exact cases computed]. For an address map that moves the port only a parity survives, so "2 paid moves + 2 shears" is neither excluded nor built. Even a yes stays below 1e-1 [argued, `ag-3`]. Held over.

**G. Anomalies kept.** One gate above the least, the transform has fewer circuit shapes than its class, 464 against about 1,364 [computed modulo p, `ag-8`]. The first circuit argued to be special to the transform has 64 gates at 16 points, the textbook count; its machine test is 65 % complete. Constants of growing degree: no construction.

## 6. The next eight experiments

1. `relation-ratio`: at 32 points, the largest L/|R| a relation of two, three, four or more wires could hand over; does it grow with size?
2. `two-gates-per-output`: the largest degree reached with three helpers per output and o(N) classes; is it bounded?
3. `dying-gates`: carry τ as a letter through every record; which gates vanish at τ = −1 and at τ = 0, and does any family lose a fixed share of its gates there?
4. `direct-sum-exact`: least gates for two independent copies of subset sums on 4 and 8 points and of the transform on 4 points; is anything below twice one copy?
5. `subset-sums-every-coefficient`: subset sums at 16 points with all coefficients modulo several primes (is 31 possible?), and the least number of ±1 gates there.
6. `weight-against-gates`: at 4 points with τ a letter, the least gates when every gate's weight is at most 1, 2, 3; and a shared gate of weight 2.
7. `deform-from-Z`: how many gates must be added to a minimum circuit for subset sums to make it a class identity; is it N − 1 at 16 points (47 against the record 52)?
8. (silly) `tau-is-a-shift`: put a cyclic shift in place of τ, so the circuit computes every kernel at once with coefficients ±1; can counting then show that no class identity is linear?

## 7. What this says about linear size

Nothing found in ten waves gives linear size or moves the exponent: the one new growth law of this wave, N + o(N) for degree 3, belongs to a smaller problem that was already linear and starts at about 2^2060 points [computed and argued]. Nothing forbids it either: the walls that became exact this wave fence circuits with flat or bounded wires, low rank, or constants of bounded degree; two of them turn out to be known since 1973 and to stop, by their nature, at free coefficients or at a constant times N [argued; read]. What is left is the oldest question in a smaller form, whether two half-size subset sums can be coupled for less than one gate per pair (first open at 16 points), and one gap named for the first time tonight, subset sums with coefficients ±1 between N·√(log N) and N·log N, where every count measured so far sits at the upper end [argued; that it could be otherwise is a guess].
