# d-0-zeta: subset sums with subtraction, played on small exact cases

2026-10-10, 45-minute box. Private research, nothing published, no git, no Lean. Tags: [ran] [derived] [read] [guess].
Files: `waves/wave-01/d-0-zeta/`.

**In one paragraph.** I built the exact minimum-circuit search the brief asked for and pointed it at every target it
could exhaust. At every size it could finish, the minimum is exactly the textbook count: subset sums at 4 and 8 points
(4 and 12 gates), the 3-cube neighbour sum (12), and 2-D prefix sums on 2x3, 2x4 and 3x3 arrays (7, 10, 12). Subtraction
does not lower any of them. It does appear: 150 of the 2,772 optimal 8-point circuits over GF(3) use one signed helper,
and five of the six I replayed over the rationals hold there with 12 gates. They all have one shape, "overshoot by one
input and take it back", and it ties. The chain-decomposition experiment ties too (10 + 22 = 32 at 16 points), and
I can say why it must. What came out of the play is a different question, which I think is the better one: the cost of
moving to a Jordan basis of the cube's "add one element" operator. That is a whole family of acceptable matrices
instead of one rigid matrix, and a cheap member would give n * sqrt(log n) at once.

Words. Z: the subset-sum matrix on n = 2^k points, y_S = sum of x_T over T inside S. Gate: one value a*u + b*v from two
earlier values. Scalings are free and an output counts when a gate is proportional to it (the projective count; every
number below is in this count). Helper: a gate that is not an output. Textbook: one pass per address bit, (n/2)*k gates.

## 1. The search, and what it found [ran]

`mincirc.c`: every non-input output must be a gate, and taking an output the moment it lies on a line through two
available values never costs more, so gates = outputs + helpers and the search runs over sets of helpers. The last
helper is restricted to points that put a missing output on a line (a necessary condition, so nothing is lost). Sets of
one or two helpers are remembered exactly. "No" means exhaustive over that field. Test: it reproduces the known 4-point
values (subset sums 4; Hadamard 7 and not 6 over GF(5) and GF(7)).

| target | textbook | result | fields exhausted |
|---|---|---|---|
| subset sums, 4 points | 4 | 4, not 3 | GF(5) |
| **subset sums, 8 points** | 12 | **12, not 11** | GF(2), GF(3), GF(5), GF(7) (21.9 million last-level states at 7) |
| J - I on 4 points (half of the 3-cube neighbour sum) | 6 | 6, not 5 | GF(3), GF(5), GF(7) |
| **3-cube neighbour sum** | 12 (two copies of J - I) | **12, not 11** | GF(2), GF(3), GF(5) |
| 2-D prefix sums, 2x3 array | 7 | 7, not 6 | GF(5) |
| 2-D prefix sums, 2x4 array | 10 | 10, not 9 | GF(5) |
| 2-D prefix sums, 3x3 array | 12 | 12, not 11 | GF(3), GF(5) |
| 2-D prefix sums, 3x4 array | 17 | not finished (two 200 s runs over GF(2) ran out of time) | none |

The neighbour sum is 12, not the 14 counted by hand earlier: the 3-cube splits into even and odd corners, each half is
J - I on 4 points, and J - I costs 6 by pairing (p = x1+x2, q = x3+x4, outputs p+x3, p+x4, q+x1, q+x2). "Below 12" is no.

The 2-D prefix sums are the sibling I added: the same matrix family (subset sums of a product of chains), where the
textbook is one pass per dimension, and the cube is the extreme case of log n dimensions of side 2.

**All optimal 8-point circuits** (`mincirc_all.c`, `z8_p3_all.out`, `z8_p2_all_b.out`). Over GF(3) there are 2,772 sets of
5 helpers that give 12 gates. 2,622 use only helpers that are plain 0/1 sums. **150 use exactly one signed helper**;
none uses two. Over GF(2) there are 2,802 sets. `lift.py` replays the six printed signed examples over the rationals
with -1 for the digit 2: five give 12 gates over Q, one does not lift that way (`lift.out`). One of them in full:

- on the way to the total of the top face it makes w = x_100 + x_110 + x_101;
- helper h = y_001 - x_110 (an output minus an input that is not below it);
- output y_101 = w + h. The unwanted x_110 enters with w and leaves with h.

So the smallest "overshoot and repay" exists at 8 points and costs exactly what the plain helper x_100 + x_101 costs.
To win, one repayment has to serve several outputs. At 8 points none does.

## 2. Symmetric chains of the 4-cube: the correction, counted [ran + by hand]

`scd.py`, `scd.out`. The 6 chains (bracket matching): 0<1<12<123<1234, 2<23<234, 3<13<134, 4<14<124, 24, 34.
Prefix sums along chains cost n - (number of chains) = 10. What is left, P^-1 Z, has rows y_S - y_pred(S) = the sum over
the T inside S that contain the element b_S removed on the way down the chain (chain bottoms keep the whole sum):
seven rows of 2 terms, six of 4 terms, one of 8 terms (listed in `scd.out`). I count 22 gates for it by hand (7 for the
pairs, 2 for each 4-term row, 3 for the 8-term row), so 10 + 22 = **32, the textbook count exactly**. At 8 points the
same route gives 5 + 7 = 12.

Growth [ran]: one chain pass costs about n and leaves 0.53 of Z's non-zero entries (k = 10: 31,296 of 59,049); two
textbook passes cost n and leave 0.44. A chain pass is a worse textbook pass. Regularity: rank(P^-1 Z - I) =
rank(Z - I) = n - (number of chains) for k = 2, 3, 4, 6, and one less at k = 5 (21 against 22). Not explained.

**Why it must tie** [derived, resting on one theorem from memory]. P has 0/1 entries, the correction has 0/1 entries, and
their product has no cancellation. Circuits without cancellation need (n/2) log2 n gates for this matrix [read, from
memory: Boyar and Find, also Selezneva; not re-checked tonight]. So "sum along chains, then a 0/1 correction" can never
beat the textbook, for any chain decomposition. The first pass has to overshoot: it must add terms that are not below
S, so that the correction has negative entries.

A count for chain passes [derived]: if Z is a product of m passes, each one a weighted prefix sum along the chains of
some chain decomposition of the cube, then the top row needs 2^k sources and gets at most (k+1)^m, so
m >= k / log2(k+1). Without subtraction m >= k/2. Passes along orders that are not chains of the cube (the overshoot)
escape both counts.

## 3. What the small cases say about the idea

1. [derived] The textbook Hadamard butterfly is a shear, a scaling and a shear: H = Z D Z-transpose **is** the textbook
   program, and textbook(H) = 2 * textbook(Z) exactly in this count. Z is the half-price half of the butterfly.
2. [ran, here and in i-cousins] H is cheaper than two Z's already at 4 points (7 against 8), and so is a generic full
   2x2 kernel (7). Z is not (4 = 4, 12 = 12). Every known gain is a gain against two subset-sum passes: it lives in
   the meeting of the down pass and the up pass, not inside either one. "Attack Z alone" removes the determinant and
   also removes the place where the small gains are.
3. [derived] Cost per entry t(k) = cost(Z on 2^k points)/2^k satisfies t(a+b) <= t(a) + t(b) (do the blocks one after
   the other). Known values: t(1) = 1/2, t(2) = 1, t(3) = 3/2 [ran]. Any size with t(j) < j/2 gives a constant-factor
   gain at all sizes and no more; a change of order needs gains that meet across scales.
4. [derived from read] Z is four transforms and four scalings, and the transform is known with a saving of 8.7625e-4 in
   the exponent of the logarithm. So
   t(k) < k/2 **must** happen at some finite k. Through that identity it happens only beyond k of about 8^1141. There is a
   smallest k* where subtraction beats one-pass-per-bit for subset sums; k* > 3 [ran], and nothing else is known about it.
   The same holds for the recurrence cost(k) >= 2 cost(k-1) + 2^(k-1): equality at k = 2, 3, false eventually.

## 4. The better question: a Jordan basis of "add one element" [derived; standard representation theory; not run]

Z = exp(U), U e_T = sum of e_(T+i) over i not in T. U is nilpotent. In any basis made of strings v, Uv, U^2 v, ...
(a Jordan basis), Z is a direct sum of Pascal matrices, one per string:

- An explicit Jordan basis is the linear form of the symmetric chains. Bracket-match the coordinates; each matched pair
  (a, b) carries the difference e_a - e_b, which U kills; the d unmatched coordinates carry E_j = the sum of all j-subsets,
  and U E_j = (j+1) E_(j+1). So Z (v tensor E_j) = v tensor sum over i >= j of C(i, j) E_i.
- Strings have length d + 1 with d about sqrt(k). A Pascal matrix of side d + 1 costs d(d+1)/2 gates plainly, so the
  block part costs about n * sqrt(log n), and n * log log n with a fast polynomial shift [guess on the cost model].
- Hence **cost(Z) <= 2 * cost(B) + O(n * sqrt(log n))** for ANY change of basis B to ANY Jordan basis of U, and if
  B costs n (log n)^a then the transform costs n (log n)^max(a, 1/2).
- B is not one matrix. Each space of string heads (dimension C(k,r) - C(k,r-1) at level r) may take any basis.
- Where the n log n hides [derived]: building B by coupling coordinates in pairs, the first level alone (x_a, x_b ->
  x_a + x_b, x_a - x_b on k/2 pairs) costs k/4 per entry, and twice that (B and its inverse) is the textbook cost of Z.
  The bookkeeping of the basis change is the whole cost; the arithmetic that matters (the Pascal blocks) is small.
  This is the same shape as the recursion t(k) = 2 t(k/2) + c(k), which is bottom-heavy and gives k.

Also met on the way [derived]: Z = sum over m = 0..k of a_m (I - c_m U)^-1 for any k+1 distinct c_m (solve a
Vandermonde system for 1/j!): Z is a combination of k+1 "count all climbing paths" solves on the Hasse diagram, each
of which costs (n/2) k, the number of edges of the cube, the same number as the textbook.

## 5. Criticism, last

- "No circuit over GF(p)" is not "no circuit over Q". A rational circuit reduces modulo p unless two operands of a gate
  become proportional modulo p, which happens at finitely many primes for a given circuit. Four primes agree at 8
  points; that is strong evidence, not a proof.
- All counts are projective (scalings free, outputs up to a factor). OpenAI's count charges scalings.
- The 16-point subset sums were not searched: 15 outputs plus 16 helpers is far beyond exhaustion, and I had no good
  restricted family. The brief's "below 32" is open. My guess is 32 [guess].
- Section 4 was derived, not run. The cancellation-free theorem and the literature on the XOR complexity of this matrix
  are from memory.
- One run line was blocked by a built-in safety check on `sh -c` lines; nothing was deleted, and I reran it with the
  rows passed as a file.
