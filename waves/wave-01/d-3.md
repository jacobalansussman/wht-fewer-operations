Words. N = 2^k points, k address bits. |y| = number of 1 bits of a frequency y. *Flag step*: an operator whose spectrum depends only on the level of y in one chain of subspaces; about 3N gates whatever k is (i-hatch, idea 1). *Dot product*: x·y modulo 2.

## The short version

1. **The rule behind the 27 "modulo 4 only" identities is a hidden symmetry.** They are the 27 "pair the bits" identities carried over by one relabelling of the frequencies, y -> y + (parity of y)·1111. That map is not a permutation of the bits, yet it keeps the weight modulo 4 up to sign. [ran: rule test `True`]
2. **At 64 points the same search finds 280 identities, not the 120 the rule predicts. The 160 extra ones are new, and one invariant modulo 8 says exactly which shapes occur.** [ran + derived]
3. **The count does not move.** Every identity found, old or new, uses k/2 flag steps. I can now say why in one line, and the line covers every program built from commuting steps of this kind, with sums as well as products. [derived, and the key dimension checked by machine for k = 2..6]
4. So the idea changes shape. "Modulo 4" is a quadratic form with a large symmetry group and an invariant modulo 8. It generates identities by the hundred. None of them is cheaper, as long as the steps commute. What is left open is precise: steps that do not commute, with scalings between them that are not of the "quadratic phase" kind.

## 1. First experiment: the 27, printed and sorted [ran]

All 54 identities at 16 points use **only the top two levels** of each flag. The lower two levels carry the value 0 and are free; that freedom is the 3 x 3 in the count. So there are 6 different identities, each counted 9 times: 3 pairings on the exact side, 3 on the modulo-4 side.

| kind | values on levels 0..4 (both flags) | count |
|---|---|---|
| exact | 0, 0, 0, 2, 1 | 27 |
| only modulo 4 | 0, 0, 0, 2, 3 | 27 |

A correction to the brief: the example quoted there (flags through {e1+e3} and {e0}) is one of the **exact** 27, and no identity "uses all four levels".

The rule. Among the 20,160 invertible linear maps of the 4 frequency bits, 48 keep the dot product. Of these, 24 keep |y| modulo 4 (the bit permutations) and 24 send it to -|y| (a permutation followed by T1: y -> y + parity(y)·1111). Applying T1 to the 27 exact identities gives exactly the 27 modulo-4-only ones. The matching count asked for is therefore 27 = 27 x 1: one extra symmetry.

Orbits of the 54: under bit permutations 3, 3, 12, 12, 12, 12; under all 48 maps 6, 24, 24.

## 2. What the special point has: a quadratic form, its symmetries, its invariant

[derived] |y| modulo 4 is a quadratic form with values in Z_4: |y+z| = |y| + |z| + 2(y·z) modulo 4. For the exact weight this is false. Everything below follows from that one line.

**Hidden symmetry.** [ran, `two64.out`] Linear maps of the frequencies that keep |y| modulo 4, against plain bit permutations:

| k | keep the weight mod 4 | bit permutations | keep the dot product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| 4 | 24 | 24 | 48 |
| 5 | 120 | 120 | 720 |
| 6 | 1,440 | 720 | 23,040 |

They are generated by y -> y + (y·v)v with |v| = 2 modulo 4 [derived; the generated group passes the check]. For |v| = 2 this is a swap of two bits. For |v| = 6, 10, ... it is a relabelling that mixes many address bits, costs nothing, and commutes with the kernel (-i)^distance. A generic member of the family t^distance has only the k! bit permutations. [recalled] These groups grow like 2^(k^2/2).

**The 64-point test.** [ran, 27 s, exhaustive] All triples of flags that use their top two levels, with values in Z_4: 19,719 different step functions, every triple with sum |y| modulo 4. Result: **280 identities**. Only 15 hold for the exact weight.

| pieces (one per flag) | count | predicted by the 16-point rule |
|---|---|---|
| three pairs of weight-1 vectors (pair the bits) | 15 | yes |
| pairs of weights 1; 3, 3 | 45 | yes |
| pairs of weights 3, 3; 5 | 45 | yes |
| three pairs of weight-5 vectors (pairing moved by the |v| = 6 map) | 15 | yes |
| two "triangles" and one pair of weight-3 vectors | 10 | **no** |
| one triangle, one "hyperbolic" piece, one pair | 60 | **no** |
| two hyperbolic pieces and one pair of weight-3 vectors | 90 | **no** |

The 120 predicted ones are the orthonormal bases of the 6 bits (for the dot product), split into pairs of equal weight modulo 4. They are not built from 16-point pieces: after the |v| = 6 map each flag depends on all six bits through the total parity.

**The new brick** [ran, `brick8.out`]. The "triangle" is an identity on three bits:

    |y| = 2·[the three bits are not all equal] + 3·[|y| is odd]     modulo 4

As integers the right side is 0, 5, 2, 3. As operators, at 8 points, exactly:

    8·K = (1-i)^3 · A · B
    K = the kernel (-i)^distance
    A = 4·(2E - 1), E = average over the points of the same parity
    B = (1-i) + (1+i)·(complement the address)

A is a reflection: Grover's diffusion on the four even points and on the four odd points. Gate count 14 + 8 = 22, the same as the 7-gate four-point unit followed by one butterfly layer; the textbook is 24.

**The selection rule** [derived from the table; the invariant is recalled: E. H. Brown, 1972]. Each piece is a two-dimensional quadratic form with an invariant modulo 8: a pair of weight-1 vectors has 2, a pair of weight-3 vectors has 6, a triangle has 4, a hyperbolic piece has 0. The weight on k bits has invariant k modulo 8 (because the sum of i^|y| over all y is (1+i)^k). Every row of the table sums to 6 modulo 8, and the combinations that do not (two hyperbolic pieces and a weight-1 pair; two triangles and a weight-1 pair; ...) are absent. So the identities are the splittings of one quadratic form into planes, and the invariant modulo 8 decides which shapes exist.

**Counted a second way** [ran, `planes.py`, 1 s]. Take every way to split the k frequency bits into k/2 mutually orthogonal planes, leaving out planes of the "mixed" kind (one vector of weight 1 and one of weight 3 modulo 4; such a plane cannot be one flag step). At 16 points: 6 splittings. At 64 points: **280** splittings, with the same five shape classes and the same counts (30, 90, 60, 90, 10). The search and the rule agree exactly. So the rule is: *identity = splitting into planes*; the 16-point "symmetry" rule was its shadow.

[guess] This is where the E8 anomaly belongs. The invariant is 0 exactly when k is a multiple of 8, and that is exactly when there is a half-dimensional subspace on which |y| is 0 modulo 4: a doubly-even self-dual code, first the length-8 Hamming code, which is the code under the E8 lattice. The hidden symmetries keep weights modulo 4, so they can never turn the "two columns agree" code (weights 2 modulo 4 present) into a doubly-even one. The E8 unit may be the first unit that is not a relabelled pairing.

## 3. Why the count stays at k/2 [derived]

**Lemma (commuting floor).** Take any function built with sums, products and constants from indicators of s affine subspaces of the frequencies, where a nested chain of subspaces counts once. It is constant on some affine subspace of codimension at most s. (Take the subspaces one at a time; each either is already constant on what is left, or lies in a hyperplane of it, and you keep the other half. In a chain, leaving the largest member leaves them all.)

**But i^|y| is not constant on any affine subspace of dimension above k/2.** If it is constant on y0 + U, the quadratic law forces w·w' = 0 for all w, w' in U, and such a U has dimension at most k/2. [ran: the largest such dimension is 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 for k = 2..6]

Hence **s >= k/2**: any program made of commuting steps of this kind (flag steps, coset averages, block steps on few bits, in any linear coordinates, combined by products and sums) needs at least k/2 of them, where a flag step counts once and a block step on j bits counts j times. This matches the exhaustive counts 1, 2, 2, 3 for k = 2..5 in i-hatch, and explains them. Free characters and free linear relabellings do not help: they replace the form by another of the same kind.

The same dimension, floor(k/2), holds for the exact weight [ran]. So the special point has no advantage here. It has far more identities (280 against 15) at the same price.

**"What does the second binary digit of the weight cost when the first is free?"** [derived] The second digit is the sum of y_i·y_j over all pairs of bits: a quadratic form over F_2 of full rank. In the right coordinates it is k/2 independent two-bit products. Its price among commuting steps is k/2 four-point blocks, about (7/8)·N·k gates, and no fewer. Making the first digit free saves 2N gates, once.

## 4. Going around the wall: what the lemma's hypotheses leave open

The lemma uses two things: the steps commute (all are diagonal in the Walsh basis), and each is constant off a hyperplane. Breaking them, in order:

- **Conjugate by the hidden symmetries.** Fails as a way out: flags go to flags, the steps still commute. [derived]
- **Put quadratic-phase scalings between the steps.** [derived, partly] A flag step then becomes a combination of projectors onto nested stabilizer codes. If one can pick one stabiliser from each step so that they all commute, the product is the identity on a code of codimension r, and a rank count on the symplectic matrix gives r >= k/2 again. The argument breaks only when the chosen stabilisers anticommute.
- **Put scalings that are not quadratic phases between the steps.** [derived] Then there is in general no common subspace on which all steps are scalars. The lemma says nothing. This is the open case.
- **One dense step alone** is impossible in every form I tried, for N >= 8 [derived]. A single tree-shaped step between any two relabellings-with-scalings has a rank-one block where the tree's root splits; a rank-one block of a Hadamard matrix has at most N entries, so a balanced split (N/2 by N/2) is out, and a lopsided tree (one point split off at a time) would make two rows of the transform proportional on N - 2 columns. A single unitary chain step would make the transform a relabelling plus a matrix of rank about sqrt(N), against the bound rank >= (N - sqrt N)/2 from i-hatch, idea 7.

**The better question.** A *tree step* is any operator that is "sum up a tree, scale each node, spread back down": O(N) gates, dense kernel. Flag steps and Grover diffusions on blocks are tree steps. Write L(N) for the least number of tree steps, with free relabellings and arbitrary scalings between them, whose product is the N-point transform.

- L(N) <= log2(N)/2, rounded up (pair the bits). L(4) = 1 [ran, i-hatch]. L(8) = L(16) = 2 [upper bound ran in i-hatch; lower bound derived above].
- If the steps commute, L(N) >= log2(N)/2 (the lemma).
- If L(N) is bounded, the transform has linear size. Nothing I know excludes L(N) = 2 for N >= 32; the rigidity bounds for Hadamard matrices are far too weak to do it [derived, roughly: two steps force "sparse in blocks plus a matrix of rank of order sqrt(N)", which the known bounds allow].
- Smallest decisive case: **is L(32) = 2?** Commuting steps need 3.
- A first look at two steps [derived, unfinished, 5 minutes]. Take two points x, x' that the first step pairs at its finest level. The difference of their output rows is a combination of two rows of the second step, and so is constant on the far half of the second tree. For a quarter of the rows at once this asks for N/4 rows of the transform to have rank about N/8 on N/2 columns. A block of a Hadamard matrix with a rows and b columns has rank at least a·b/N = N/8: the requirement sits exactly on the bound. Equality happens for subgroup-shaped blocks only [guess]. So either two steps are forced, level by level, back into the commuting shape (then the lemma applies and there is a floor for non-commuting tree steps too), or there is a non-subgroup equality case, and that case is the brick. Both outcomes are worth having.

## 5. Third question: what replaces "modulo 4" for a kernel on m-bit digits [derived]

- For the Walsh kernel on m-bit digits: the same thing, per digit. The refinements of the dot product are |x| + 2(s·x) modulo 4, one for each s; the digit's hidden symmetry group has 1, 2, 6, 48, 720, 23,040 elements for m = 1..6 against m!, and the invariant is modulo 8.
- For a cyclic digit of size 2^m (the Fourier kernel): the chirp exp(2 pi i x^2 / 2^(m+1)). "Modulo 4" becomes "modulo 2^(m+1)".
- For digits of odd size nothing extra is needed: 2 is invertible and the chirp lives modulo the same number.
- [guess] A lead for the floor note: its weight F(g) is not invariant under these symmetries. Swapping two bits inside one column of a pair commutes with the kernel and moves that pair's diagonal. A floor statement that respects the symmetries would speak of "a self-dual code in the orbit of the diagonal", and would have to say what happens for doubly-even codes, which are in no such orbit.

## 6. Limits

- The 64-point search covers flags that use their top two levels, values in Z_4. Half-integer values and deeper levels were not searched at 64 points. The lemma shows deeper levels cannot lower the count; they could add identities.
- The stabiliser-code extension in section 4 is a sketch.
- The lemma counts steps, not gates. It becomes a gate bound only with "every step that is not a relabelling costs at least about N/2 gates", which I take as obvious and did not write out.
- "L(32) = 2?" was not attempted. It is a polynomial system (two flags, three scalings of 32 entries each); an exact attempt would work modulo a large prime.
- The literature on Z_4-valued quadratic forms (Brown), and on Kerdock codes and orthogonal spreads as the Z_4-linear objects attached to this form, is cited from memory. Not read tonight.
- Criticism, last: sections 1 to 3 close the idea in its original form. Identities special to t = -i exist in quantity, and they are not cheaper bricks. The value is the lemma, the selection rule, and the sharper open question.
