# d-6-excess: where the log is born

Label d-6-excess, 2026-10-10, 45-minute box. Private notes. Marks: [read], [ran], [derived], [guess], [recalled] (from memory, not re-read).
Files: `waves/wave-01/d-6-excess/` (`gray.py`, `more.py`, `star.py`, `det5.py` and their `.out`).
Gate model as in i-play: one gate computes a*u + b*v with any coefficients; scalings and permutations are free.
excess = gates - (inputs - 1) - (outputs - 1).

**Where it ended.** The idea turned into a sharper question. The cross term of a tensor product is exactly the cost of running parallel copies that share nothing. If N parallel copies of the N-point transform could share more than half of their excess, the transform would have linear size; sharing exactly half gives N log log N. No counting argument I found protects the excess from being shared. At the smallest size (two 4-point arrays) sharing is impossible: 14 gates, not 13, by a hand proof.

**Not done.** The exact minimum for any 6- or 8-wire product (the brief's first experiment) was not computed: the i-play engine does not reach 5 wires (two runs of 240 s on 5 wires printed nothing, `det32.out`, `det48.out`). What replaced it is an exact criterion for excess 0 that needs no search, checked against the engine on 165 small cases. "Two 4-point arrays in 13 gates" and "two 3-in-4-out rectangles in 9" are answered by hand proofs, not by machine. The 8-point word of zero-excess bricks under 22 gates was not searched. Nobody has checked the hand proofs.

## 1. What excess is

- **Excess counts cycles.** [derived] A circuit with s gates on n inputs is a graph with n + s vertices and 2s edges, so s - (n - 1) is its number of independent cycles. Excess = cycles - (outputs - 1).
- **Excess is at least 0 when every output depends on every input.** [derived] The first gate with full support needs n - 1 gates below it, none of them an output; the other m - 1 outputs are further gates.
- **The cross term is a grid of squares.** [derived] The product-rule circuit for A x B has n_B cycles(A) + m_A cycles(B) + (m_A - 1)(n_B - 1) cycles. The last term is the cycle count of the product of two trees: one square for each (edge of A's tree, edge of B's tree). In excess terms this is the 2 (m_A - 1)(n_B - 1) of the brief, with the intermediate width m_A x n_B (for rectangles, pick the order with the thinner middle).
- **Excess per point is per-entry cost minus 2.** [derived] f = excess / N = cost / N - 2 + 2/N. The textbook, the 7/8 brick and any linear circuit are f = k - 2, 7k/8 - 2 and O(1).

## 2. Zero excess is completely understood: Gray trees

**Lemma.** [derived; checked against exhaustive search on all 165 sets of 2 to 4 sign rows on 3 and 4 inputs, 0 disagreements: `gray.out` part 1]
m rows of signs on n inputs have excess 0 exactly when, after free input signs make one row all ones and for some binary tree summing the inputs, every other row is reached from an earlier row y by negating y on a block (a tree node or a single input) on which y is constant.

What it gives:
- **A rectangle whose cost is its perimeter.** [ran, `gray.out` part 2, n = 3..7] All 2^(n-1) sign patterns of n inputs cost (n - 1) + (2^(n-1) - 1) gates: one gate per output, exponentially many outputs. Each output is the previous one with one input flipped (a Gray code).
- **Square zero-excess matrices exist at every size.** E.g. rows (1,1,1), (1,1,-1), (-1,1,-1): 4 gates, determinant 4. Call it G3.
- **At 4 points the Hadamard matrix is the only thing that pays.** [ran, `more.out` (a)] Of the 70 sets of 4 sign rows on 4 inputs, 68 have excess 0. The other 2 are the two Hadamard matrices. Of the 56 triples, exactly the 8 mutually orthogonal ones pay. Excess at 4 points = three mutually orthogonal rows.
- **Zero excess = smallest possible determinant.** [ran, n = 4 and 5, `more.out`] 4 points: zero-excess full-rank sets all have |det| 8; Hadamard has 16. 5 points: all 2,672 sets with |det| 16 have excess 0; none of the 320 with |det| 32 and none of the 16 with |det| 48 do. [derived] Reason: subtract each row's parent; what is left is a laminar family of blocks, which is totally unimodular, so |det| = 2^(n-1) or 0.
- **Rows of the transform are never Gray neighbours beyond one pair.** [derived] Two rows of H_N differ on N/2 points; the only block of that size in a tree is a child of the root. So with all inputs live, excess 0 holds for at most 2 rows, at every N. (Matches the 4-point table of i-play.)

## 3. One helper: the star

- The 7-gate brick is a Gray tree whose root is not an output: helper s = sum, outputs s - 2 x_i. Every output is one flip from the helper.
- [ran, `star.out`] The star J - 2I on n points costs 2n - 1 (excess 1) and has |det| = 2^(n-1) (n - 2): 16 at n = 4 (Hadamard), 48 at n = 5 (the largest determinant any 5 x 5 sign matrix has). So one helper can multiply the determinant by n - 2, not by 2.
- **Bits of determinant per gate.** [ran] Gray tree 0.500 at any size; textbook transform 0.500; 4-point star 0.571; star of size 7 peaks at 0.640. A word of sign-matrix layers made of stars therefore needs at least 0.781 N log2 N gates [derived from the table]. So products of block reflections J - 2I cannot be linear. [derived] This floor is void once layers need not be sign matrices: with free scalings all (N/2) log2 N bits can be supplied by N scalings, which is why no determinant argument survives in this model.
- [guess] Numerology worth keeping: (N/2) log2 N bits = N/2 helpers at log2 N bits each. The symmetric form of the transform is a product of (N - sqrt N)/2 commuting one-helper reflections. A linear circuit would be one in which those N/2 helpers share their sums and their bypass wires.

## 4. Products: what is shared when the cross term drops

- [ran, lemma, `gray.out` part 3] No product tested has excess 0: H2 x G3, G3 x H2, H2 x R, R x H2 (R = 3 inputs to 4 outputs). Largest zero-excess row set: 4 of the 8 rows of R x H2, 3 of the 6 rows of H2 x G3. So the true cross term is at least 1 for these pairs.
- [ran, exact replay] **H2 x G3 in 13 gates; the product rule gives 14.** Cross term at most 3 against 4. The circuit: fold a0 + a1 and b0 + b1, run the 4-point star on (a0 + a1, a2, b0 + b1, b2) for four rows, then two flips by a0 + b0 and a0 - b0.
- **What is shared.** In both known cases (H2 x H2 and H2 x G3) it is one helper that is not a product of a form on A and a form on B: sign pattern (-1)^(x1 x2), the bent function. Every output is one flip from it. A product helper cannot do this. [derived]
- [derived] Rectangles that expand (outputs far more than inputs) keep cost near one gate per output under tensor product, and rectangles that fold keep cost linear in the inputs, both by geometric series. Square full-rank products have no thin middle. So rectangles alone do not help; the gain must come from sharing across the copies in the wide middle. That is the next section.

## 5. The better question: can parallel copies share their excess?

- [derived] cost(A x B) <= cost(n_B copies of A) + cost(m_A copies of B). The product rule is this bound plus the assumption "t copies cost t times one". So the cross term is the failure of parallel copies to share.
- **Growth law.** [derived; table `more.out` (c)] Suppose N parallel copies of the N-point transform cost N (2N - 2) + (N / rho) excess(N), i.e. only a fraction 1/rho of the excess is paid per copy. Then excess per point obeys f(2k) = 2 (1 - 1/N)^2 + (2 / rho) f(k).

| rho | k = 4 | 16 | 64 | 256 | 1024 | growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (no sharing) | 1.63 | 12.0 | 54.0 | 222 | 894 | k: the log |
| 3/2 | 1.46 | 6.92 | 17.0 | 34.8 | 66.6 | k^0.42 |
| 2 | 1.38 | 5.12 | 9.12 | 13.1 | 17.1 | 2 log2 k: N log log N |
| 3 | 1.29 | 3.73 | 4.99 | 5.55 | 5.80 | bounded by 6: linear |
| 4 | 1.25 | 3.18 | 3.79 | 3.95 | 3.99 | bounded by 4: linear |

- General exponent: f grows like k^(1 - log2 rho) for rho < 2. A saving of 1e-1 in the exponent needs rho = 2^0.1 = 1.07: seven percent of the excess shared, at every size.
- Same statement in other words: N copies of H_N is "multiply a variable N x N matrix X by the fixed matrix H_N", and H_(N^2) is X -> H X H. Linear size at N^2 is "left-multiplication by H_N in O(N^2)".
- **What counting allows.** [derived, by hand] Split each gate of a two-copy circuit into its part on each copy and count it once per copy on which it does real work. A gate that works for both copies has two mixed operands; a gate with a bare input as operand works for at most one. Result: two copies of A cost at least cost(A) + m + n/2, so the saving is at most excess + n/2 - 2. The whole excess of the second copy is unprotected.
- **Smallest size: no sharing.** [derived, by hand, from cost(H4) = 7] Two 4-point arrays need 14 gates. With 13, five gates are not outputs; they must carry six units of work and admit eight inputs; the four possible splits (seeds, one-copy gates, two-copy gates) = (1,2,2), (1,1,3), (1,0,4), (2,0,3) each fail the input count, the first because the two top gates would need a pure three-input helper that no remaining gate can be. Two 3-in-4-out rectangles need 10, not 9: a saving needs at least three mixed helpers and there is room for one.
- **The mechanism exists in this gate model.** [derived from standard facts, recalled] For a generic n x n matrix one copy costs about n^2/2 or more, and n copies cost O(n^2.38) in total through fast matrix multiplication with one factor fixed: about n^1.38 per copy. So direct sums of linear maps do share, massively, for hard matrices. For the transform this route is useless as it stands (n^1.38 is far above n log n); it shows that "copies cannot share" is not a law. Cousins: tensor rank is not additive over direct sums (Shitov, 2019) [recalled]; Boolean circuits compute many copies of a hard function for about the price of one (Uhlig, 1974) [recalled].
- [read, one search, 2026-10-10] I did not find a statement of the direct-sum question for linear circuits; the hits were the bilinear and communication versions. Whether it is open for linear circuits over the rationals is [guess: open].

## 6. Regularities that will not go away

- Zero excess = Gray tree = determinant 2^(n-1). Excess is what orthogonality costs: at 4 points it appears exactly with three mutually orthogonal rows.
- Every cross term below the rule, so far, is one non-product helper one flip from many outputs.
- The log is the grid of squares in the product of two trees; equivalently, parallel copies that share nothing.
- sqrt and halves keep appearing: rho = 2 is the border between N log log N and the log; N/2 helpers carry the determinant.

## 7. Criticism, last

- The growth law is a conditional statement. Nothing here produces sharing for the transform; the one size tested by proof (two 4-point arrays) shows none.
- The hand proofs of section 5 are a session's work, unchecked. The 14 rests on cost(H4) = 7, which i-play established by search inside a finite ratio set plus a sketch.
- The Gray lemma is exact only for sign rows with full support. The machine check covers n = 3 and 4; the n = 5 and n = 6 uses rely on the lemma.
- The 0.781 floor applies to sign-matrix layers of stars only.
- A fair objection to section 5: sharing half the excess at every size is itself a statement of the strength of the result wanted. The gain is that it is a statement about two or a few copies of a small transform, which is a finite search.
