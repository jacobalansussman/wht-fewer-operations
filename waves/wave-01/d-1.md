Developer: d-1-plane, 2026-10-10. Private research. Nothing here is reviewed, nothing is in Lean.
Scripts and outputs: `waves/wave-01/d-1-plane/` (`plane.py`, `plane.out`, `polar16.py`, `polar16.out`). Both runs exact (integers, fractions, integer polynomials); about 2 seconds in total, under `light.py`.
Tags: [ran] [derived] [read] [recalled] [guess]. Counts: (O) OpenAI's, additions and scalings each charged; (G) any a*u + b*v is one gate.

Notation: N = q^2, q = 2^e, k = 2e. Rad(q) = cost of all N + q line sums of the plane over the field with q elements. T(N) = cost of the transform.

## 1. The answer to "in which coordinates do the q + 1 folds become one fold plus relabellings"

Read the N addresses as the field with N elements and take logarithms to a primitive element theta. Then:

- **[ran, N = 4, 16, 64, 256]** The N - 1 lines that miss the origin are exactly the N - 1 cyclic shifts of ONE set D of q logarithms, D = { j : Trace from F_N to F_q of theta^j equals 1 }. At 16 points D = {1, 2, 4, 8}: the 15 line sums are y_i = x_(i+1) + x_(i+2) + x_(i+4) + x_(i+8), indices modulo 15. At 64 points D = {11, 21, 22, 25, 37, 42, 44, 50} modulo 63.
- The other q + 1 lines are the rays through the origin (the cosets of the multiplicative group of F_q, plus the origin).
- So Rad(q) = one cyclic convolution of length N - 1 with a 0/1 mask of q terms, plus q + 1 ray sums. Multiplying by theta is a column-mixing relabelling, so every step of such a program is of the floor note's hatch (b).
- **[ran]** D is as far from additive structure as a set can be: every difference that is not a multiple of q + 1 occurs exactly once (two lines meet in at most one point). This is why additions alone cannot share anything.

## 2. The slice theorem is a factorisation, and the log log N is the height of a tower

- **[ran, all four sizes] Tower law.** In the group ring of the cyclic group of order N - 1, with every coefficient equal to 1:
  { Trace_(N/2) = 1 } = { Trace_(N/q) = 1 } * { s in F_q : Trace_(q/2)(s) = 1 }.   Sizes 2 = 2*1, 8 = 4*2, 32 = 8*4, 128 = 16*8.
  The left side is the transform itself: on the non-zero addresses H = (all ones) - 2 * (mask of trace one), a cyclic correlation with the maximal-length sequence. The first factor is the plane's line mask D. The second is the same object one level down, living on each ray. This IS the slice theorem, written as a product.
- [derived] For k a power of two the tower F_2 < F_4 < F_16 < F_256 < ... gives the transform as a product of log2(k) = log2 log2 N line masks, of 2, 4, 16, 256, ... terms, each acting inside cosets of its own subgroup. That is where N log log N would come from: one mask per level of the tower.
- Price list [derived]: a level with q = 2^e costs q - 1 additions per entry done naively. To tie the textbook it must cost about e per entry; for N log log N it must cost O(1).
- **[ran] Polar form.** Write an address as radius (in F_q) times angle (in the norm-one group, order q + 1). D has exactly one point on each non-trivial angle, it is symmetric under angle -> minus angle, and **the radii it uses are exactly the trace-one set of F_q**, the mask of the level below. At 16 points D = {(radius 1, angle 1), (2, 2), (2, 3), (1, 4)}. So each level's mask is the previous level's mask "wound around a circle".

## 3. Where the difficulty sits (and where it does not)

**[ran]** Split the mask by the cyclotomic factors of t^(N-1) - 1:

| N | angular only | radial only | mixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | d=1: 4; d=5: -1 | d=3: -2 | d=15: all the content, 8 dimensions |
| 64 | d=1: 8; d=3: -1; d=9: -1 | d=7: 2 * (trace-one mask of F_8) | d=21, d=63 |
| 256 | d=1: 16; d=17: -1 | d=3: 4; d=5: -4; d=15 | d=51, 85, 255 |

- On every part that depends on the angle only, Rad is multiplication by -1. [derived: its value there has absolute value 1 in every embedding, so it is a root of unity.]
- On the part that depends on the radius only, Rad is twice the level below.
- Everything else is the mixed part, where Rad multiplies by a ratio of Gauss sums of absolute value sqrt(q).
- **[ran] At 16 points the mixed part is one quadratic number.** D*D = D - 4 + 2*(circle sums) + 2*(ray sums), exactly, in the group ring. So on the 8 mixed dimensions the line-sum operator satisfies D^2 - D + 4 = 0: it is multiplication by (1 + sqrt(-15))/2. The whole 15 by 15 operator has five eigenvalues: 4, -2, -1 and that conjugate pair.

## 4. The first experiment

- **[ran]** 20 x 16 incidence matrix R: rank 16, so 4 relations among the 20 line sums (each direction's four sums add to the total). R^T R = q I + J.
- [derived] The relations alone save nothing: getting a line from "total minus the other three" costs the same 3 operations.
- **No circuit with 59 or fewer operations was found, and no machine search was run.** What I did instead was rule out three families by hand [derived]:
  1. a product of two 2-term masks with plus signs: impossible, D has no repeated difference;
  2. a recurrence (1 +- t^a) y = (two terms) x: impossible, (1 +- t^a) D has at least 6 terms because two lines share at most one point;
  3. through the 15 half-space sums: the 20 lines cost 30 operations given those sums and the total, so the 15 half-space sums would have to cost under 30; they are the transform itself (56 in count G).
- Rad at the smallest sizes, as known tonight:

| q | N | additions only (exact, = naive) | best with subtraction | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4 | 6 | 6, exact | six distinct outputs, six gates |
| 4 | 16 | 60 | <= 60 | open; 59 not found |
| 8 | 64 | 504 | <= 504 | route through transforms costs 672 (O) |
| 16 | 256 | 4080 | **<= 3408 (O), <= 2744 (G)** | 2048 + 17*64 + 272 scalings; (G): 1792 + 17*56 |

- **[derived] At 256 points subtraction provably beats the best addition-only program for the line sums** (4080 is optimal without subtraction, because no two lines share two points). The formula line sum = (1/q) * sum over the direction's frequencies was checked exactly at 4, 16, 64 [ran]; the count at 256 is arithmetic on that formula, not an instrumented run.
- [derived] Growth law: additions only, Rad = q^3 - q, about N^1.5. With subtraction, T(N) - (q+1) T(q) <= Rad(q) <= T(N) + (q+1) T(q) + N + q. So Rad is N log N at most, and Rad and T have the same order unless T is already nearly linear.

## 5. The radial matrices of idea 45 at 16 points [ran, exact in Z[zeta_5]]

Three circles of five points plus the origin. For each angular frequency the transform is a 3 x 3 matrix whose (r', r) entry depends on the product r r' only:

- frequency 0, with the origin, in the basis (value at origin, common value on each circle): [[1,5,5,5],[1,-3,1,1],[1,1,1,-3],[1,1,-3,1]]. On the circles it is "total minus 4 times one entry": rank one plus a relabelling. The sign of life idea 45 asked for is there. [derived] The entries are Kloosterman sums, so this is an accident of q = 4 and will not persist.
- frequencies 1 and 4: entries (2, 1 + sqrt 5, 1 - sqrt 5) = 2 * (1, phi, -1/phi), phi the golden ratio. Frequencies 2 and 3: the conjugate, 2 * (1, -1/phi, phi). Rows have squared length 16.
- So the 16-point transform, in polar coordinates, is two copies each of a 3 x 3 "golden" circulant and its conjugate, plus the 4 x 4 block above. Arithmetic in Q(sqrt 5), not in the rationals.

## 6. Literature, 20 minutes, two searches only

- [read, search summaries only, not the papers] Fast digital Radon / fast Hough transform: N^2 log N additions for an N x N image, but for digital lines, not lines of a finite plane. arXiv:1801.01054 (2018): that algorithm is optimal among addition-only programs. arXiv:1502.01865 (2015) uses the point-line incidence graph of a projective plane as the standard example with no 2 x 2 all-ones block. arXiv:2512.12215 (December 2025): injectivity of Radon transforms over finite fields, nothing on fast algorithms.
- [recalled, unchecked] Matus and Flusser 1993: finite Radon transform of a p x p image, p prime, p^3 additions; the fast route through Fourier slices is standard. Cohn and Lempel 1977: correlation with a maximal-length sequence through the Hadamard transform (section 2's left-hand side read backwards). The mask D as a relative difference set and as a circulant "balanced generalised weighing matrix" of order q + 1 over the cyclic group of order q - 1 is classical design theory (Jungnickel, 1980s).
- Not found: any statement on the cost of all line sums of a finite plane WITH subtraction below N log N, or any lower bound for it. Two searches is not a survey.

## 7. Criticism, last

- [derived] **With coefficients of bounded size Rad can never be linear.** det(R^T R) = q^(N-1) (q + N), so Morgenstern's argument gives at least about (N/4) log2 N operations with +-1 coefficients. Rad at that bound is exactly the transform at its own bound (N/2) log2 N. The plane does not escape the need for large coefficients; it only moves the question.
- [derived] The two-sided inequality in section 4 says Rad is the transform's equal, not an easier sibling. The idea's value is the new coordinates, not a reduction.
- The 16-point search the brief asked for was not built. The three families ruled out are narrow.
- Section 5 needs sqrt 5; section 3's components need odd roots of unity. Whether OpenAI's model lets a program pay for those was not looked at.

## 8. What I would do next (each about an hour)

See the mutations in the structured return. The one I believe in most: [guess] the cost of a level is the cost of multiplying by one algebraic integer (a Gauss-sum ratio) on the ring of (N-1)-th roots of unity. Its prime factorisation above 2 is known exactly (Stickelberger: the exponent at character a is a binary digit sum), and the digit sums add up to (N/2) log2 N [derived], which is Morgenstern's count read one character at a time. Two-term gates 1 +- t^a are units there (free of 2); three-term gates such as 1 + t + t^4 at 16 points (the field's own defining trinomial) are the candidates for the primes. Nobody in this project has tried to write the mask as (unit) x (few trinomials).
