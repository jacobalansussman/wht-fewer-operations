## 1. In five lines

1. The first experiment was run. At 16 points the atoms of the three self-dual codes are **four perpendicular copies of the 24-cell's root system (D4)**, and every count in that picture comes back to the known 64, 60 or 56 additions. No count below 56. [ran + derived]
2. The idea has an exact floor of its own: a basis of stabilizer states in which the transform is a relabelling shares at most half of its "position" directions with the standard basis. So analysis plus synthesis is never cheaper than the textbook, for any code and any basis of this kind. [derived; ran at 16 points: 39 such bases, best distance 2 of 4]
3. The idea turned into a better object. The transform, written so that its diagonal is all +1, is the **symplectic Fourier transform** S. Its eigenvectors are the N quadratic forms; the number with eigenvalue −√N is N/2 − √N/2 = 1, 6, 28, **120**. The 4-point "Householder" saving (7 for 8) is the case where that number is 1. [ran at 4, 16, 64, 256]
4. **The 120 labels of the published E8 unit are exactly the index set of that eigenbasis at 256 points** (the 120 elliptic quadratic forms on 8 bits = the 120 roots of E8 up to sign). [derived; the counts ran; the relevance to the engine is a guess]
5. S and a free sign diagonal T satisfy (TS)³ = 1: together they generate the group of order 6. Consequence: **on a subspace of dimension (N+2)/3 the transform equals a free sign change.** [ran: (TS)³ = 1 at 4, 16, 64; the dimension and "S = T there" at 4 and 16; derived beyond]

## 2. The first experiment, as asked (N = 16)

Three self-dual codes of length 4: the three ways to pair the four address bits. 48 atoms, all distinct, spanning everything. [ran]

- H sends every atom to ±4 times an atom **of the same code**: 12 atoms fixed up to sign (6 with +4, 6 with −4), 18 swapped pairs. [ran]
- Transfer matrix between any two of the three bases: 4 non-zeros per row, entries ±1/2, and it falls into **four 4 × 4 blocks**. [ran]
- The 48 atoms split into four mutually perpendicular families of 12, each of rank 4, inner products 0 and ±2 at square length 4: that is the root system D4 (the 24-cell) four times; each family holds the three codes as its three perpendicular frames. H keeps two of the families and swaps the other two. [ran]
- Why [derived]: all three codes contain 1111. One layer of 8 butterflies along 1111 (16 additions) turns every atom into a 2-term sum. What is left is four independent 4-point problems.
- **Hybrid bases**: 81 orthogonal bases of 16 atoms can be picked from the 48 (3 pure, 78 mixing codes), 27 of them closed under H. These are new bases in which the transform is a relabelling and which are not stabilizer bases of one code. [ran] Their analysis costs 32 additions each, the same as a pure one. [derived: 16 for the pair layer, then one addition per atom]
- Counts with free scaling: pair layer 16 + four 4-point transforms (8 each, or 7 by the reflection formula) + pair layer 16 = **64 or 60**. Two-sided atom analysis and synthesis: 32 + 32 = 64, or 28 + 28 = **56**. Target "under 56" not reached. [derived]

Second part of the experiment (256 points, Hamming code): the code's 1,344 coordinate symmetries act on the 16 cosets with orbits 1 + 8 + 7 [derived, not run]. Two cosets hold 32 independent numbers, so "two coset transforms in one call" is the direct-sum question for the 16-point transform, which is open in general for linear circuits and is the "batched calls" hatch of the floor note. Not run.

## 3. The floor inside the idea

[derived] A stabilizer basis is given by a maximal commuting set L of "position" and "frequency" shifts. The transform is a relabelling of the basis exactly when L is kept by the swap position ↔ frequency. If L contains the position shifts A, it contains the frequency shifts A, and they must commute, so A is self-orthogonal: dim A ≤ k/2. The basis is therefore at distance at least k/2 from the standard one, and one pays k/2 layers going in and k/2 coming out.

[ran, 16 points] Of the 2,295 stabilizer bases, 39 are kept by the swap (14 of them real). Their overlap with the standard basis: dimension 0 (20 bases), 1 (16 bases), 2 (3 bases: exactly the three codes). So the codes are the *closest* such bases, and they tie with the textbook.

This is the code version of "one array under Clifford moves needs k Hadamards". It closes "one array, one basis at a time". It does not speak about several arrays, nor about bases that are not stabilizer bases (the 78 hybrids above are the first examples; they tie too).

## 4. What it turned into: the symmetric form of the transform

Write the address as two halves and use the kernel (−1)^(p1·y2 + p2·y1). This is the Walsh-Hadamard matrix with its columns relabelled; call it S.

- S is symmetric and its diagonal is all +1; this is the form with the largest possible trace. [ran]
- For every quadratic form Q on the address bits whose "mixed part" is that kernel, the sign vector (−1)^Q is an eigenvector of S. Eigenvalue +√N for the N/2 + √N/2 forms of the first kind (hyperbolic), −√N for the N/2 − √N/2 of the second kind (elliptic). They form an orthogonal eigenbasis. [ran at 4, 16, 64, 256; one line of algebra: Q(p+y) = Q(p) + Q(y) + mixed part]
- Hence **S = √N · (I − (2/N) · Σ over elliptic Q of v_Q v_Qᵀ)**: √N times the product of N/2 − √N/2 mutually perpendicular reflections. [ran at 4, 16, 64]
- At 4 points there is one elliptic form, v = (1, −1, −1, −1), and this is the reflection that gives 7 additions for 8. The pretty fact "H − √N·I has rank N/2 − √N/2" is the count of elliptic forms. [ran]
- No flat matrix beyond 4 points is a single reflection: I − c·(all ones) is flat only at N = 4. [derived] So the 7/8 does not improve by taking a bigger single reflection.
- Cost reading [derived]: the full transform costs at most twice the cost of the N/2 − √N/2 Walsh coefficients *on the quadric Q₀ = 1*, plus N/2 − √N/2. At 4 points that is 2·3 + 1 = 7. At 16 points my best for the 6 coefficients is 38, giving 82: worse than 56. [derived, not searched]

**The relation of order 3.** Let T = diag((−1)^Q₀), a free sign change, Q₀ hyperbolic. Then (T·S/√N)³ = I. [ran at 4, 16, 64] S/√N and T are two reflections-like involutions generating the symmetric group on three letters, and S T S = T S T.

- The space splits into: a part where S = T = +1, a part where S = T = −1, and (N−1)/3 planes where S and T are mirrors at 120°. [derived from the trace: trace(TS)/√N = 1, ran]
- **So S equals the free sign T on a subspace U of dimension (N+2)/3**: 2 of 4, 6 of 16, 22 of 64, 86 of 256. [ran at 4 and 16, `play3.out`: the projector has rank (N+2)/3 and S·P = ±T·P for every one of the N quadratic forms]
- At 16 points U is spanned by the indicators of the six lines of the 3 × 3 grid of zeros of Q₀ (rank 5) and one vector living on the six points where Q₀ = 1. [ran]
- The projector onto U is P = (I + T·S/√N + S·T/√N)/3. Its entries are 0 wherever T has different signs at the two addresses: **P never mixes the points where Q₀ = 0 with the points where Q₀ = 1**, and elsewhere its entries are ±2/(3√N) off the diagonal. [derived; ran at 16: entries 0, ±1/6, 1/2] So the block of the transform on the N/2 + √N/2 zeros of Q₀ is (√N/2)·(3P − I): up to scale and a multiple of the identity, a projector onto the span of the indicators of the quadric's lines. [derived]
- There are N such T (one per quadratic form), hence N subspaces U_a. Exact identity [ran at 4 and 16]: **S/√N = (3/N)·Σ_a ±T_a·P(U_a) − (2/√N)·I.** (My first guess, without the identity term, was wrong; the run showed two different ratios and the algebra gives the correction.) This is the idea's "atoms from several codes at once on which the transform is a relabelling", in exact form: the transform is a sum of free sign changes, each applied to one third of the space, minus a multiple of the identity.

## 5. The E8 unit read in this language

[read: `R/Work/GFrame/Engine/Letters.lean`, header and definitions] The engine's free letters on one array are an additive address permutation, a phase i^(z·x + c), and a shift, each applied to every digit column alike. All are monomial. A monomial step applied alike to two columns keeps "the data where the two digits agree". So **every cheap step keeps the same code, the diagonal, on every pair of columns; no step of the unit changes the code.** The answer to the standing question is no. E8 does not enter through the columns.

[read: `R/notes/how-the-e8-unit-was-found.md` §1] E8 enters through the labels: 84 triples and 36 complements of pairs of 9 points; each label "knows" 56 others.

[derived + ran] Taking complements, these are the even words of length 9 with weight 2 or 6: the 120 vectors a of the 8-bit space with q₀(a) = 1 (120 counted; each has even overlap with 63 others, so odd overlap with 56). And the elliptic forms at 256 points are exactly q₀ + (pairing with a) for those a. So:

> the 120 labels of the E8 unit = the 120 elliptic quadratic forms on 8 bits = the canonical eigenbasis of the (−16)-eigenspace of the 256-point transform in its symmetric form; "knows" = the two forms differ by a character that does not vanish on each other's vector.

[guess] The unit's "pairs of arrays that know each other" may be this eigenbasis in disguise, and the 8 "totals" the rank-8 lattice behind it. Caution: the E7 family has 63 labels, which is every non-zero vector of the 6-bit space, not the 28 elliptic forms there; so "roots modulo 2" is the common reading and the eigenbasis match may be special to E8. Not checked against a certificate.

## 6. Things tried on the way that tie or fail (and what they are made of)

- Reflections in 4-term vectors as gates (cost 7, mixing 4 entries): 6 gates at 16 points is excluded by parity (products of perpendicular ones have half-integer entries, the target has quarters); 7 gates is excluded by dependency (some entry would be touched once, which forces 9 gates); 8 gates is the known 56. [derived] The product of the 8 known reflections was checked, with the compact form g = I − V·S⁻¹·Vᵀ (S the upper half of the Gram matrix; V has 32 non-zeros, S has 24). [ran]
- That compact form is the shape a linear program would have in this language: a sparse V and a sparse triangular solve. For reflections at 60° or 90° only, the vectors form a root diagram of type A, D or E and the transform would be its Coxeter element. At 16 points the trace allows only 4·A2 or 8·A1 at rank 8; both cost 56 or more with 4-term vectors. [derived]
- Paley form of the 8-point matrix (Fano plane): 29 additions against 24. [derived]

## 7. Criticism, last

- Nothing here lowers a count at 16 points. Every exact count returned 64, 60 or 56.
- §4's "S = T on a third of the space" is circular as a program: projecting onto U uses S. It becomes a program only if U has a cheap projector, or if a few of the N subspaces U_a can be combined cheaply. I did not test either.
- The E8 match (§5) is a bijection of index sets and of the "knows" relation's degree (56). I did not check that the unit's fitting matrix is the one this picture gives.
- The floor of §3 is for stabilizer bases. The cost step ("distance d costs d layers") is the known Clifford count [read in the brief], not re-derived.
- The symplectic Fourier transform, the Arf count and (ST)³ = 1 are classical (Weil representation). What may be new here is their use: the reflection count as the Householder saving, the E8 labels as the eigenbasis, and the one-third subspace.
