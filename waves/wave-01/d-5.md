1. **The door exists.** There is a matrix whose border count is below its exact count in this gate model.
   On n wires: 2n-1 gates in the limit, 3n-4 gates exactly. At 4 wires 7 against 8, at 5 wires 9 against 11,
   at 6 wires 11 against 14. [derived; certificates ran exactly]
2. **For the transform's own small bricks the door is shut, and I can say why.** No 6-gate border program for

3. **What exactness costs.** A border program with s gates that cancels poles up to order d becomes an exact

## 1. The picture that made everything quick

Nodes are points of projective space; the inputs are the n coordinate points; a gate puts a new point on the
line through two existing points. [derived] In a family depending on e, each node is a moving point. In the
limit e -> 0 every gate whose two operands have different limits stays an honest gate. The only gates that
can do something new are those whose operands have THE SAME limit p: then the line through them tends to a
line through p whose direction is the difference of their first-order motions. Call such a gate a
difference-quotient gate. Two consequences:

- **A border gain needs a lost node.** Two nodes with one limit means the program has more gates than
  distinct limit points. [derived]
- **Motion is free.** A chain of gates that builds p can carry, at no extra gate, a first-order motion
  p + e*a where a is an ARBITRARY vector on the chain's inputs (each coefficient 1 + c*e contributes one free
  term). A node carries a second vector in its e-digit. Reading it out needs a second node with the same
  limit and one gate, (u - v)/e. [derived]

## 2. The door example [derived; ran: `door.py`]

n wires. Chain 1 adds the inputs in the order 1, 2, ..., n with coefficients 1 + (integer)*e; chain 2 adds
them in the order n, ..., 1. Both chain ends tend to the all-ones vector. Last gate: w = (u - v)/e.
Gates: 2(n-1) + 1 = 2n-1. Limits: the n-2 proper prefix sums, the n-2 proper suffix sums, all-ones (twice),
and w, which is a generic vector (and is exact for every e: w(e) has degree 0 in e) [ran].

Exact count of that limit set: the 2n-2 targets each need a gate. The cone of w, cut at the other targets and
the inputs, has leaves in a fixed finite set F of 3n-3 points; if w lies in the span of no n-1 points of F the
cone has at least n leaves, hence n-2 gates that are not targets. Total at least 3n-4, and 3n-4 is reached.
The condition on w was checked exactly [ran]:

| wires | border gates | exact gates | w used | (n-1)-subsets of F containing w |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 8 | (31, 14, 20, 11), seed 14 | 0 of 84 |
| 5 | 9 | 11 | (29, -22, 8, -2, 31) | 0 of 495 |
| 6 | 11 | 14 | (-29, -38, 48, -8, 20, -3) | 0 of 3003 |

(Seeds 12 and 13 at 4 wires gave a w with a zero coordinate and 3 and 6 containing subsets; those are not
certificates.) Growth law: gain n-3 on 3n-4, ratio tending to 3/2.

What the gain is made of: the exact program must hit the prefix sums exactly, so their chains cannot carry
w; the border program lets the prefix sums be slightly wrong and uses the slack to carry w. **The gain comes
from intermediate targets that are only required in the limit.** Making the outputs exact again costs one
gate per perturbed output here (5 at 4 wires), more than the gain. Two evaluations share no gate.

## 3. Why the transform's small bricks have no border program [derived]

Support lemma: the support of a limit is inside the support of the moving node. For the 4-point transform
with 6 gates, the first node of full support is gate 3 at the earliest, so gates 3 to 6 are the four outputs
and gates 1, 2 are the helpers. For gate 3 to tend to a vector with four non-zero entries, both helpers must
tend to points with their full support (2 and 2, or 2 and 3), so their limits differ from each other, from
the inputs and from the outputs. No two nodes share a limit, no difference-quotient gate exists, and the
limit is an exact 6-gate program, which does not exist ([ran] earlier, `i-play/n4full.out`, GF(5..13); I
re-did the case analysis by hand for the 2-3-4 shape only). Same argument for the 3-point Fourier matrix
with 4 gates (one helper of support 2, whose limit must keep support 2). So border = exact = 7 and 5.

General form: if a target set of t points has exact count t + h, a border program with fewer gates must
contain a pair of nodes with one limit, so at most h - 2 live helpers. With h <= 1 border = exact always.
At 16 points and above the textbook has many helpers (n log n gates, n outputs), so the lemma no longer
forbids anything; it only says where to look: two different cheap nodes with the same limit.

Random search for more doors [ran, `border.py`, 600 trials each, exact helper search modulo 13]: 4 wires
with 5 and 6 gates: every limit set with 1 or 2 lost nodes needed at most as many helpers as nodes lost
(counts in `border46.out`). The random generator loses nodes much faster than it gains lines; it did not
find the door, which needs two long chains to one point. The 3-wire run crashed on a bug and was not rerun.
In the plane (3 wires) I expect no door at all: any limit line meets a line of existing nodes, so one helper
always suffices [derived for one coincidence; guess in general].

## 4. What exactness costs, and the better question

A gate over the ring C[e]/(e^(d+1)) costs (d+1)^2 ordinary gates (order k costs 2k+1) [derived]. So a
border program with s gates and pole order d gives an exact one with (d+1)^2 * s gates; at d = 1 it is the
forward-and-backward formula: the first-order part of an s-gate family is
sum over wires of (sensitivity of the outputs to that wire) x (the form on that wire), cost at most 4s.

Reading that backwards gives the question I would carry on with [guess that it is useful]:
**is the transform the first-order digit of a cheaper family?** That is: find a matrix M0 with a program of
size s0 and a first-order change of its coefficients whose derivative is (monomial) x H. Then H costs at
most 4*s0. Linear s0 would be linear size. This is a search with a far larger target than "H exactly":
M0 is free, only the tangent direction is fixed. The door example is exactly this with M0 = two sum chains.
Natural M0 to try first: the two rank-one members of K_t (t = +-1, cost n - 1), the identity, and the
complement relabelling, since the transform is G(i) in the one-parameter group G(u) = u^W
(W = (k - A)/2, A the cube's adjacency matrix), G(i)^2 = G(-1) is a free relabelling, and the derivative of
G at u = 1 is W.

Not done: the G(u) census at k = 4 (which u cost fewer than 32 gates). [derived, partial] at 4 points K_t
minus a multiple of the identity or of the complement relabelling has rank one only at t = 0, +-1; other
signed relabellings were not tried.
