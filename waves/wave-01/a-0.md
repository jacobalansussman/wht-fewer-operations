# a-0: criticism of the object "Identity: one class, and the log-log middle"

[read] = read tonight (file or web page named), [recalled] = from memory, not opened tonight, [guess] = marked guess.

## 0. Verdict in six lines

- (a) **holds, for every k, and is elementary.** One 2 x 2 identity plus the rule (AB) tensor (AB) = (A tensor A)(B tensor B).
  The runs at k = 1..4 add nothing to the k = 1 check. Its content, "all 2 x 2 Kronecker powers are one class", is known in the
  depth-2 circuit literature (Alman and Li 2025 reduce the class to the disjointness matrix, which is Z relabelled).
- (a) can be **sharpened**: three calls, not four, and three is the least possible. Over the rationals the claim "every full
  kernel is two calls of H" is **false** (smallest case: any kernel whose invariant tau is not a rational square, e.g. tau = 2);
  the true statement is "at most six calls", which is enough for the class claim.
- (b) **holds (k = 2..10) and is known**: it is Schur-Weyl duality for k copies of a 2-point space.
- (c) **holds with two corrections.** "Any Jordan basis of U" fails for the transform at 4 points; it is true for Z, and true
  for the transform with a factor 2, or for bases adapted to the pair "add one element / remove one element". The O(m log m)
  Pascal block is a legal program only where a root of unity of order about 2m is supplied; with rational constants (or the
  number i, which is all the Walsh-Hadamard statement supplies) the price is one more log log log factor [recalled].
- In the user's terms: **no circuit and no saving for the transform.** One transfer: subset sums on N points cost at most
  3 T(N) + O(N), so the published exponent z = 1 - 8.7625e-4 holds for the zeta and Moebius transforms too.
- For "linear or not" the known theory predicts **nothing**: (c) trades the question for a harder one (a linear-size change to
  the Schur-Weyl basis would make every bit-permutation-invariant operator cheap, not only the transform).

## 1. The claim, stated precisely

Notation: N = 2^k; H = [[1,1],[1,-1]] and Z = [[1,0],[1,1]] and their k-th Kronecker powers (Z_N x at T = sum of x at the
subsets of T); D(a,b) = k-th Kronecker power of diag(a,b); X = complement of every address bit; U = "add one element";
cost = work of an exact linear program in OpenAI's count; T(N) = cost of H_N.

- (a1) Z_N = D(1/32,1/10) H_N D(3,1) H_N D(3,-2) H_N D(2,1) H_N D(1,-5/9) for every k.
- (a2) H_N = Z_N D(1,-2) Z_N-transpose, hence cost(Z) <= 4 T + 5N, T <= 2 cost(Z) + N, and "linear size" and the exponent z
  are the same for all Kronecker powers of an invertible 2 x 2 matrix that is not a relabelling with scalings.
- (a3) every 2 x 2 kernel with no zero entry is two calls of H (stated over the complex numbers).
- (b) d-0's Jordan basis of U and d-7's valence-bond basis are one basis; in it Z is a sum of Pascal blocks and H is
  (-2)^(number of pairs) times Krawtchouk blocks.
- (c) T(N) <= cost(B) + cost(B^-1) + O(N log log N) for any Jordan basis change B of U.

## 3. What is wrong or loose, and the nearest true statements

**3.1 Four calls is not the least: three is.** [ran + derived]

    Z = D(1/4,1/5) H D(3/2,-1/2) H D(2,1) H D(1,5/3)        (k = 1..5 ran; all k by the mixed-product rule)

Two calls cannot do it: H diag(a,b) H = [[a+b, a-b],[a-b, a+b]] has no zero, or two. So **cost(Z) <= 3 T(N) + 4N**, and three
is the exact minimum for words of this kind (relabellings X can be pushed through H and do not help).

**3.2 "Every full kernel is two calls of H" is false over the rationals.** [derived; grid check ran]
Two calls reach exactly the kernels whose invariant tau = m11 m22 / (m12 m21) is a square in the field of constants: tau =
((a+b)/(a-b))^2. Smallest failing case: [[1,1],[1,2]] (tau = 2). H itself has tau = -1. OpenAI's machine has no instruction for
an arbitrary complex constant [read: RAM.lean header]; a program builds rationals from 0 and 1, plus what the statement supplies
(the Walsh-Hadamard statement supplies i [read: `WHTCheck/Solution.lean` line 50]; the Fourier statement a root of unity).
Nearest true statement, over the rationals, for every invertible 2 x 2 matrix M that is not a relabelling with scalings:

    T(N) <= 4 cost(M_N) + O(N)        and        cost(M_N) <= 6 T(N) + O(N).

Proof [derived; 2000 random rational M ran]: if M has no zero and tau is not -1, M diag(1, -a^2/(bc)) M has exactly one zero, so
it is Z up to relabelling and scalings (two calls of M give Z, two of Z give H). If tau = -1, M is H with scalings. If M has one
zero it is Z with scalings. Conversely M = L D U (after a relabelling if the corner entry is zero) with L and U scaled copies
of Z and Z-transpose, and Z is three calls of H.
So the class claim (a2) **survives**, with constants 4 and 6 instead of 2.

**3.3 "Any Jordan basis of U" fails for the transform.** [ran, 4 points]
The vectors e_{} , e_0 + e_1 , e_01 and e_0 - e_1 + e_01 are a Jordan basis of U (strings of length 3 and 1). Z keeps both
strings. H does not: H(e_0 - e_1 + e_01) is not a multiple of e_0 - e_1 + e_01. Reason [derived]: Z = exp(U) is a function of U;
H is not, it also needs "remove one element" and the size grading. Nearest true statements:
- for ANY Jordan basis: cost(Z) <= cost(B) + cost(B^-1) + middle, and T(N) <= 2 cost(B) + 2 cost(B^-1) + 2 middle + O(N)
  through H = Z D X Z X;
- with the factor 1: for bases whose string heads have a single size and are killed by "remove one element" (the valence-bond
  basis is one; any basis of each head space will do). That is exactly the freedom d-0 named.
The exponent statement is unaffected (a factor 2).

**3.4 The O(m log m) Pascal block: legal, with a condition.** [read RAM.lean; the algorithms recalled]
The block is a product with ONE fixed polynomial, sum 1/n! x^n, cut at degree m, between two scalings by factorials (row 6 of
the table). That is a linear map, so it is a legal program if its constants are. Exact numbers of any size cost one unit, so
factorials up to k! are fine. The fast version needs a root of unity of order L >= 2m, a power of two:
- Fourier statement: a root is supplied; its powers give what is needed if 2m divides its order [guess: it does for OpenAI's D*].
- Walsh-Hadamard statement: only i is supplied; roots of order 8 and more are not rational in i, so the FFT version is NOT
  available. A root-free fast product with a fixed operand exists (Schoenhage-Strassen / Cantor-Kaltofen, additions, rational
  scalings and shifts only), at m log m log log m [recalled, not rerun]. Then the middle is O(N log log N log log log N).
Either way the middle is below N (log N)^e for every e > 0, which is all the conditional uses.

## 4. Is it known?

- **(a), the class.** Known in spirit. Alman and Li, "Kronecker Powers, Orthogonal Vectors, and the Asymptotic Spectrum",
  arXiv:2509.14489 (17 September 2025): smaller depth-2 circuits for the disjointness matrix, and "smaller circuits for many
  families of matrices using reductions to disjointness" [read: abstract page]. The disjointness matrix [[1,1],[1,0]] is Z
  with a relabelling. Alman, Guan and Padaki, arXiv:2211.05217, treat "Kronecker powers of any 2 x 2 matrix" as one family at
  depth 2 [read: search summary]. H = Z D Z-transpose is the lower-diagonal-upper factorisation of a 2 x 2 matrix; I found
  nobody who writes the three-call word, and nobody who would be surprised by it.
- **(b).** Known: Schur-Weyl duality. Any matrix g on the 2-point space acts on each block as det(g)^pairs times the
  symmetric power of g; the symmetric power of H is the Krawtchouk matrix (Feinsilver and Kocik, arXiv:quant-ph/0702073 and
  quant-ph/0702173 [read: search summaries]), of Z the Pascal matrix. The bracket basis is the symmetric-chain / valence-bond
  basis (Greene and Kleitman 1976; Rumer-Pauling; Srinivasan arXiv:1001.0280; Go 2002 and Schrijver 2005 for the block form of
  the cube's Terwilliger algebra) [all recalled, not opened tonight].
- **(c).** B is a (non-unitary) Schur transform. The quantum literature (Bacon, Chuang and Harrow, quant-ph/0407082 [recalled])
  builds it one position at a time; as a classical linear program each position costs at least a constant times N, so the
  known constructions are N log N or worse, in line with d-7's count (k - 1.6 sqrt(k) per entry each way). I know of no
  linear-circuit study of it and found none tonight (two searches; not a survey).
- **The transfer to subset sums.** Yates's k 2^(k-1) additions is what the exact-algorithms literature uses; for programs
  with additions only it is optimal [read: `wht33/.../lit-other-models.md`, Kennes, claimed there]. I found no statement that
  subset sums over the rationals cost below N log N (one search, nothing from 2026 returned). It follows in one line from
  OpenAI's theorem and 3.1, so I would call it "probably unstated, certainly not deep".

**What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": nothing.** With bounded coefficients every member of the class with
|det| > 1 needs order N log N (Morgenstern; Z itself has determinant 1, so that bound says nothing about Z), and here the whole
determinant N^(N/2) sits in the middle (the factors (-2)^pairs and the blocks' own scalings), which is legal in OpenAI's count. With unbounded coefficients no superlinear lower bound is known for any member. The class
statement says only that no fact special to H can be NEEDED for the truth; it gives no method.

## 5. Does it scale? Growth laws observed [ran]

- (a): exact at every k; constants do not grow.
- (b): true through N = 1024. The basis matrix B is **not sparse**: non-zeros per column 1.50, 2.00, 3.00, 4.25, 6.38, 9.25,
  13.88, 20.38, 30.56 for k = 2..10, a factor about 1.5 per bit, so about N^0.585 per column. B must be computed by reuse of
  partial sums; no counting of entries will make it cheap.
- (c), the middle per entry for H, with a real operation count (two fixed-vector products per block, three scalings per entry;
  FFT of length L at 3 operations per butterfly):

| k | dense blocks | i-cross's "m log2 m" model | FFT count | share of entries in blocks where FFT wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 8.48 | 2.55 | 79.12 | 0 |
| 256 | 27.56 | 4.53 | 114.38 | 0 |
| 1024 | 53.08 | 5.53 | 131.71 | 0.004 |
| 4096 | 104.14 | 6.53 | 149.05 | 0.17 |
| 16384 | 206.26 | 7.53 | 166.39 | 0.67 |
| 65536 | 410.52 | 8.53 | 183.73 | 0.94 |

  Law: dense = 1.6 sqrt(k); FFT = 8.67 log2 k + 45. The log-log law is real, with a constant seventeen times the model's, and
  it overtakes the dense blocks only between N = 2^4096 and N = 2^16384. Below that the middle is the dense one, which is
  already under the textbook's k per entry from k = 16 on. None of this matters while B costs N log N.

## 6. What it gives in the user's terms

- **For the transform: nothing yet.** No circuit, no saving exponent. (c) is a conditional: if B and B^-1 cost N (log N)^a,
  then T(N) = O(N (log N)^a) for a > 0, and O(N log log N) (with a supplied root) for linear B. The best B anyone has costs
  about twice the textbook.
- **For another transform: a formula.** cost(subset sums on N points) <= 3 T(N) + 4N + table work = O(N (log N)^z),
  z = 1 - 8.7625e-4, in OpenAI's count over the rationals; the same for the Moebius transform (inverse of Z, same class) and for
  every Kronecker power of a rational 2 x 2 matrix (six calls). The order does not change.
- **A caution about (c) as a road to linear size** [derived]. B does not depend on the kernel. A linear-size pair B, B^-1 would
  give N log log N at once for every Kronecker power AND for every operator that commutes with permutations of the bits (the
  cube's adjacency and distance matrices, all of the Terwilliger algebra), at N sqrt(log N) at worst. That is a much stronger
  statement than "the transform is linear". A harder target is not evidence against; it does mean the reduction loses
  information, and a linear transform need not come this way.
- **What survives as a tool.** (i) Lower-bound and search work may be done on Z, three calls from H, where d-0 found no
  small-size gain at all. (ii) The freedom in B (any basis of each head space, non-unitary) is real and unexamined.

## 7. If I had another half hour

The one finite experiment that would say something about (c): the exact least cost of ANY adapted basis change at 8 points
(heads: one vector of size 0, two of size 1 killed by "remove one"; the freedom is GL(2) on the size-1 heads), against the
12-gate textbook. d-0 proposed it; nobody ran it. A B with cost(B) + cost(B^-1) + middle < 12 at 8 points would be the first
sign that the freedom buys anything; a tie would be the sixth tie of the night.
