# r-1: criticism of the two hand proofs behind "one form per node" (p-1's floor, p-8's lemma)

11 October 2026, about 01:22 to 01:43 EDT, 30-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing in Lean, nothing published.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight, whole numbers only, my own programs (`r-1/`); [derived] = by hand tonight, read by
nobody else; [read] = from p-1.md, p-8.md; [guess]. Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free.
Nothing here is in count (O).

Words. N = 2^k points, H the transform (entries +1, -1). *Merge tree*: repeatedly replace two held sums by one weighted sum; its
*nodes* are the sets of points under each sum; a *cherry* is a node of two points. *Tree rectangle*: (a node of a tree on the rows)
x (a node of a tree on the columns) with one number on it. *One letter*: H, after multiplying every row and every column by a
non-zero number (the *scalings*), written as a sum of tau tree rectangles; it is a circuit of N - 2 + tau gates. *Dual chains*: row
nodes = blocks of addresses with the same high bits; column nodes = blocks with the same low bits. *Row pair*: a row cherry
{y, y+1}. *Strip*: a rectangle one row high or one column wide; *cell*: a rectangle of one entry.
*t'(f)* for a vector f on the leaves of a tree: the least of (nodes of two or more points used) + (1/2)(single points used) over
all ways to write f as a sum of numbers placed on nodes.

## 0. Verdict

1. **Both statements hold.** I found no error in either proof and no failing case. p-1's four numbers reproduce exactly with my
   own code: **tau >= 4, 11, 32, 108 at 4, 8, 16, 32 points** [ran]. p-8's lemma (tau >= N^2/512) reads correctly line by line
   [derived]; its two countable ingredients check out exactly at 16 and 32 points [ran].
2. **Neither is in the literature that I could find** (two searches only; section 5). They are statements about a model made up
   tonight, so "new" here is cheap: the model is new, not a known open problem.
3. **The floor scales, and I can now prove it** [derived, section 4]: if L(n) is the half-floor on n = N/2 points, then
   **L(2n) >= 4 L(n) - 2n**. With L(16) = 54 this gives **tau >= N + (19/256) N^2 for every N >= 32** on dual chains. p-1 had this
   as a guess beyond 32 points. At 64 points: tau >= 368, gates >= 430, against 384 for the textbook and 304 for the best known.
   The true value of p-1's bound at 64 points is between 368 and 384 [ran: search, upper end].
4. **Three small corrections, all in the proofs' favour** (section 3): the floor also holds for complex scalings and numbers; at
   8 points the bound function itself never goes below 12 for sign scalings (p-1 printed 11, which is a relaxation); p-8's
   "fewer than 8" is 2 at the sizes I could enumerate.
5. **What they do not do**: at 16 points the floor is 32 and cannot be pushed higher by this argument, because it counts only
   strips and cells. One letter at 16 points with any numbers is still open between 32 and 45; a record needs 37. And both proofs
   cover chain trees only: a tree whose cherries point in different directions, or a tree with few cherries, is covered by
   neither (for those there is only p-8's exhaustive run at 8 points modulo 3).
6. In the user's terms: **nothing gained, one door closed with a proof.** One letter on dual chains costs between
   2N - 2 + (19/256) N^2 and N - 2 + (N^2 + 14)/6 gates: quadratic. It says nothing about circuits that are not one letter.

## 1. The two claims, stated exactly

**p-1 (floor).** Let both trees be the dual chains. Let r, c be non-zero real scalings and M = diag(r) H diag(c) = a sum of tau
tree rectangles with any real numbers. Then

    tau >= B(r, c) = sum over row pairs of t'(row y - row y+1, on the column tree)
                   + sum over column pairs of t'(column difference, on the row tree),

and B(r, c) >= 2 L(N/2) for all r, c, where L(n) = min over two sign vectors c0, c1 on n points of
sum over the n characters chi of min( t'(c0 chi), t'(c1 chi) ). Values claimed: 2L = 4, 11, 32, 108.

**p-8 (lemma).** Let the column tree pair all inputs into cherries {x, x+d} with one common direction d (every chain of subgroups
does), the row tree be anything, scalings and numbers anything non-zero. Then tau >= N^2/512.

## 3. Reading the proofs

**p-1, step by step** [derived].
(a) A row node that holds one row of a pair and not the other is that single row. So row y minus row y+1 is produced only by the
    strips and cells in those two rows. Correct for any row tree whose leaves all sit in cherries, and for ANY column tree.
(b) Cells are counted from both sides, so they get weight 1/2. Correct: tau >= strips lying + strips standing + cells.
(c) In t' the running sums may be taken among the values of f and 0. Correct (a node whose running sum is no value below it can be
    dropped at no cost); and confirmed by the brute force above.
(d) With sign scalings the difference is zero on one half of the columns, and that half is a node of the column tree. Correct for
    dual chains, and this is the only place where "dual" is used.
(e) Real scalings: replace running sums by their signs. Correct. The step "restricting to a half never costs more" needs one more
    line than p-1 gives: if the root is used with value o, each half starts from o instead of 0, which saves at most 1 per half
    and costs 1 at the root; so the full cost is at least the cost of either half alone. It holds.

Corrections, all strengthening:
- **Complex scalings and numbers too** [derived]. On a half the difference is (one complex number) x c x character. Dividing by
  that number does not change t'. Then map every running sum s to the sign of the real part of e^(i theta) s, with one angle
  theta chosen so that no entry of c becomes purely imaginary. Equal sums stay equal, so no node is added, and c becomes a sign
  vector that is the same for all row pairs. So the floor holds over the Gaussian rationals. p-8's invariant has the same
  property (two rows have equal value on a wrong cherry only if their scalings agree up to sign).
- **At 8 points the relaxation loses one** [ran]. 2L = 11 comes from letting each pair choose its half freely. Real sign scalings
  cannot make all those choices at once: B(r,c) >= 12 for every pair of sign scalings. So for sign scalings and any numbers
  tau >= 12 at 8 points (the truth modulo 3 is 13 [read: p-8]). For non-sign scalings only 11 is proved.
- **At 16 points the floor is reached, but p-1's "random" count is not of uniform pairs** [ran]. p-1's design `bound32` has
  B = 32 with my code too (rows 16, columns 16; `b32.out`), so 32 is the true least value of the bound. But in my 20,000
  uniformly random sign pairs the least B is 34 and none has 32. p-1's sample (2,021 of 829,610 at B = 32, "about 1 in 180 with
  B <= 37") was half quadratic forms, half arbitrary signs [read: `lbdesign.py`]; the low values come from the structured half.
  This matters only for p-1's mutation 3: "take the sign pairs with B <= 37" is a far smaller and more structured set than
  1 in 180 of all pairs, which makes that experiment cheaper than p-1 thought, not harder.

**p-8, step by step** [derived].
(a) phi_j(v) = v(a) c'(b) - v(b) c'(a) on a cherry {a, b} is changed only by the two single-input steps: correct, and it needs
    only that every form of the tree is the centre c' restricted to a node. That is automatic for a merge tree (by induction each
    held sum is proportional to the root's sum on its node), so "one centre" is not an extra hypothesis.
(b) Every cherry is wrong for at least one of the two classes of rows (y.d = 0 or 1): correct; one class has w >= N/4 wrong ones.
(c) Two rows of a class differ on at least (w - |character sum|)/2 wrong cherries: correct.
(d) Parseval: fewer than 2N/w <= 8 shifts have a sum above w/2: correct, and generous (2 at the sizes enumerated).
(e) Greedy choice of N/16 rows pairwise w/4 apart, then a walk around the row tree that passes each rectangle twice: correct.
    Result (N/16)(N/16)/2 = N^2/512. The constant is weak (if the observed 2 in place of 8 held at every size, which I did
    not prove, the same argument would give N^2/128), but the statement is right.
What the lemma does not say: anything useful below about 8,000 points (N^2/512 passes the textbook's N log2 N - N + 2 only from
N = 8,192). It is a statement about the order, not about 16 or 32 points.

## 4. One size further: 64 points [ran + derived; `r-1/search32.py`]

Enumerating L(32) is out of reach (2^31 sign vectors per half). Instead:

**Lemma [derived].** L(2n) >= 4 L(n) - 2n.
Proof. For f on 2n points with halves f', f'': t'(f) >= t'(f') + t'(f'') - 1 (the top node, if used, saves at most 1 in each
half and costs 1). A character of 2n points restricted to a half is, up to sign, a character of n points, and each one arises
twice. So the sum over the 2n characters of min(t'(c0 chi), t'(c1 chi)) is at least 2 L(n) + 2 L(n) - 2n.
Check on the exact values [ran]: 5.5 >= 4, 16 >= 14, 54 >= 48.

So L(n) - n at least quadruples at every doubling: **L(n) >= n + (38/256) n^2 for n >= 16**, that is

    tau >= 2 L(N/2) >= N + (19/256) N^2      for every N >= 32, dual chains, any numbers, any non-zero scalings.

| points | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| floor for tau | 4 | 11 (12 for signs) | 32 | 108 | **368 to 384** | at least 1,344 |
| how known | exact | exact | exact | exact | 368 derived; 384 = best pair of halves found by descent, an upper end for the bound, not for tau | derived |
| floor per point | 1 | 1.4 | 2 | 3.4 | 5.75 to 6 | 10.5 |
| the family (N^2 + 14)/6 [read] | 5 | 13 | 45 | 173 | 685 | 2,733 |
| tau that ties the textbook, N log2 N - N + 2 | 6 | 18 | 50 | 130 | 322 | 770 |
| tau of the best known circuit, were it a letter [read] | 5 | 13 | 38 | 98 | 242 | |

**Growth law.** The floor is quadratic, proved: between (19/256) N^2 + N and (N^2 + 14)/6, that is between 0.074 N^2 and
0.167 N^2. Observed ratios of successive floors: 2.75, 2.9, 3.4, then 3.4 to 3.6, tending to 4. From 64 points on, one letter on
dual chains is worse than the textbook, with a proof; at 32 points it is worse than the best known circuit (138 against 128)
but not yet worse than the textbook (160).

## 5. Known?

I did not find either statement, nor the model (a sum of rectangles taken from two merge trees), in two searches tonight. That is
thin: read it as "not found", not as "new". The nearest known theory is about circuits of depth two (inputs, one middle layer,
outputs), which a letter is, with tau middle gates and both layers forced to follow a tree:
- Alon, Karchmer, Wigderson, "Linear circuits over GF(2)", SIAM J. Computing 1990: a depth-two circuit for any Hadamard matrix
  needs of the order of N log N wires. It counts wires, not gates, and gives N log N, not N^2.
- Alman, "Kronecker products, low-depth circuits, and matrix rigidity", 2021, arXiv 2102.11992, and later work by Alman with
  Guan and Padaki (2022, arXiv 2211.05217) and with Li (2025, arXiv 2509.14489, seen tonight in search results only): depth-two
  circuits for this transform with about N^1.44 wires. The exponent is quoted from memory; check before citing.
What this predicts for "linear or not": nothing. Those results bound wires in unrestricted depth-two circuits; tonight's wall is
about a tree-shaped depth-two circuit and comes from the tree, not from the depth. The textbook circuit is itself not one letter.

## 6. In the user's terms

- Circuit size for one letter on dual chains at N points, count (G): at least 2N - 2 + (19/256) N^2 (N >= 32), at most
  N - 2 + (N^2 + 14)/6. Saving exponent: none; it is worse than N log N from 64 points on.
- For linear size: these proofs close this one shape and say why (a tree holds one form per node; half of the matrix needs the
  other form of each pair). They are not evidence about circuits in general.

## 7. What stays open, and what I did not do

- **16 points is not decided.** Floor 32, best letter 45, a record needs 37. The argument cannot give more: B counts strips and
  cells only and never a rectangle of two rows by two columns, and a design with B = 32 exists (checked). Closing it needs either a
  count of the large rectangles on top of B, or p-8's exact shortest-tree search at 16 points (too large tonight).
- **Trees that are not chains.** p-1 needs dual chains (step (d)); p-8 needs every input in a cherry with one direction.
  Caterpillars (one cherry) and trees with mixed directions are outside both. p-8's "order N^1.5 for every tree" is a guess.
- I did not rerun p-1's solver results (45 as the exact-cover minimum), p-8's runs modulo 3 and 5, or the 32-point replay; this
  report is about the two proofs only.
- My lemma of section 4 and the complex-scaling remark are tonight's derivations, read by nobody.
- The upper end 384 at 64 points is a descent from 64 structured seeds and random starts for five minutes, and it stopped
  improving after 8 seconds; the true value of the bound may be lower, but not below 368.
