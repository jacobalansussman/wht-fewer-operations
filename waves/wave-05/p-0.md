# p-0 classtest: at 4 points no saving belongs to the transform alone; one belongs to other kernels; the transform's own show on weight-only data only; even the 52 with four-bit wires moves to other kernels

**FIRST LINES. No circuit below the best known counts (7, 19, 52 in count G) and no growth law below N log N.** What came out:
the question "is any saving the transform's own?" has different answers at different places, each exact or exhaustive modulo primes.
(1) Full 4-point problem: **no** (all 7-gate circuits enumerated, 28 wiring shapes, none special to the transform).
(2) Same enumeration, the other way round: **one shape exists only for other kernels** (tau = 2, 1/2 and the sixth roots of unity) and
not for the transform; it is a 7-gate circuit with no hub at all.
(2b) Full 16-point problem: **no again, after a scare.** h-1's second 52-gate circuit (wires entangled on all four address bits)
did not move to other kernels in a first sample of 500 renamings of the inputs; in a second sample one renaming moved, and in that
one the circuit was carried EXACTLY to tau = -2 and tau = -3 (16 of 16 outputs). So it is a class identity too. Section 4b.
(3) Data that depend only on the number of 1-bits of the address: **yes**, the transform costs 4 and 7 gates (exact circuits) where
every other member of the class costs 5 (exact circuit; "no 4" modulo five primes) and 8 (modulo two primes). This is a corner of
the transform with k + 1 dimensions; it has not saved a gate of the full transform.

**Count (G) throughout**: a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free, an output counts when a wire is proportional to its row.

Words. N = 2^k points; addresses are k-bit strings. *Kernel*: a 2 x 2 matrix. *K_tau* = [[1,1],[1,tau]]; row w of its k-th Kronecker
power has entry tau^(number of common 1-bits of w and x) at column x. tau = -1 is the transform (Walsh-Hadamard); tau = 1 has rank
one; tau = 0 is subset sums. *The class*: all tau except 0 and 1. *Helper*: a wire that is not an output. *Support*: the inputs a wire
reads with a non-zero coefficient. *Hub*: a helper whose support is everything. *Wiring*: which two wires each gate reads.
*Shape*: a wiring with the names forgotten (inputs, helpers, outputs may be renamed within their kind). *Placement*: a shape with the
names put back. *Address map*: renaming the addresses by an invertible bit matrix. *Weight-only data*: data that depend only on the
number of 1-bits of the address.

## 1. Non-symmetric kernels  (brief, part a)

[derived] For a, b, c non-zero: [[a,b],[c,d]] = diag(1, c/a) . [[1,1],[1,tau]] . diag(a, b) with tau = ad/(bc). Kronecker powers of
diagonal matrices are diagonal, so the k-th power is (row scalings) . (power of K_tau) . (column scalings). In count (G) both are free.
So **the class has one parameter**, and tau and 1/tau are the same problem (swap the two rows of the kernel, which only renames
the outputs, and rescale) [derived; the census below shows equal counts at tau and 1/tau at every prime, ran]. The three kernels of the brief are tau = 2/3, 6/35, 1/100.

## 5. Where the transform is alone: weight-only data

If the data depend only on the number of 1-bits of the address, so do the outputs, and the k-th power becomes the (k+1) x (k+1)
matrix with entry (i, j) = sum over m of C(i,m) C(k-i,j-m) tau^m. At tau = -1 this is the Krawtchouk matrix (named so in coding
theory). k = 2: S_tau = [[1,2,1],[1,1+tau,tau],[1,2tau,tau^2]].

| k | size | the transform | every other tau | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | |
| 2 | 3 | **4** | **5** | 4: exact [read g-5; replayed]. 5: exact circuit for all tau [ran]. No 4 at any tau other than -1 modulo 7, 11, 13, 17 and 61 (58 of 58 values there) [ran, mod p] |
| 3 | 4 | **7** | **8** | 7: exact, lifted by hand from GF(13), replayed [ran]. 8 and "no 7": every tau other than -1 modulo 11 and 13 [ran, mod p]; not lifted. Modulo 7 every tau gives 7 (every tau modulo 7 is special) |
| 4 | 5 | 10 | at least 10 | modulo 11 only: no 9 for tau = -1 and for tau = 3; a 10 for tau = -1 (candidate, not lifted); tau = 3 with 10 gates undecided (search cut at 420 s, see the last line of section 10) |

The circuits [ran, exact, `weightonly_exact.py`], inputs a, b, c = the three weight classes:

    every tau, 5 gates:   h1 = a + 2b    h2 = h1 + tau c    r0 = h1 + c    r1 = h2 + (tau - 1) b    r2 = tau h2 + (1 - tau) a
    tau = -1, 4 gates:    s = a + c      r1 = a - c         r0 = s + 2b    r2 = s - 2b
    tau = -1, k = 3, 7 gates on (x0..x3):   g4 = 3 x1 + x3   g5 = x0 - x2   g6 = 3 g5 - g4
                          y0 = 4 x0 - g6   y2 = g6 + 4 x3   y3 = y0 - 2 g4   y1 = y2 - 6 g5

Is "5 at every tau modulo p" one circuit or a different accident at each tau? One circuit: `sym3_count.py` counts 18 or 22 helper
sets at most tau modulo 43 (69 at tau = 2 and 1/2; 3,556 at tau = -1, where any second helper will do), and the formula above holds
for every tau.

What the transform uses here [derived]: the sum of the rows 01 and 10 of K (x) K is (2, 1+tau, 1+tau, 2tau). It is sparse only when
1 + tau = 0. Their difference (tau - 1)(0,1,-1,0) is sparse for every tau, which is why the full 4-point problem shows no gap.

Size of this: the weight-only problem has k + 1 dimensions out of 2^k. It is a statement about one corner of the transform, and
g-5's pair route, which is this corner used inside an 8-point circuit, gives 22 gates against the 19 [read g-5]. It answers the
brief's question (a saving that exists only for the transform) and it does not yet save a gate of the full transform.

## 6. The smallest statement: level sets of a whole number

[derived] Rows w and w' of the k-th power have ratio tau^e(x), e(x) = (ones of x inside w but not w') - (ones of x inside w' but
not w), a whole number between -b and a (a, b the sizes of those two sets, d = a + b the number of bits in which w and w' differ).
A gate can join two wires cheaply only where they are proportional. For a generic tau that is one level set of e; for tau a primitive
n-th root of unity, one level set of e modulo n. The transform is n = 2.

[ran, `agree.py`, exact counting, no arithmetic with tau] Largest fraction of the N points on which two rows are proportional:

| d | generic tau | the transform | cube root | tau = i | sixth root |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| 3, 4 | 3/8 | 1/2 | 3/8 | 3/8 | 3/8 |
| 5, 6 | 5/16 | 1/2 | 11/32 | 5/16 | 5/16 |
| 7, 8 | 35/128 | 1/2 | 43/128 | 9/32 | 35/128 |

It depends on d only. Pairs of rows at 8, 16, 32, 64 points with fraction 1/2 for a generic tau: 24 of 28, 80 of 120, 240 of 496,
672 of 2,016; mean fraction 0.482, 0.458, 0.434, 0.410, against 1/2 for every pair of the transform. And even at d = 2 the
transform has two level sets of size N/2 where the class has one of N/2 and two of N/4: that second half is the gap of section 5.

Consequences [derived, none run except where said].
- Two rows whose addresses differ in d bits cost N gates for the transform (sum each half, then two gates) and N + d - 1 by the
  same method for the class (d + 1 level sets). The class pays for width.
- k-cross 1 D ("two rows agree on at most N/2 columns, so the rectangles have total area at least N(N-1)/4") becomes stronger for
  the class, not weaker: the class is the WORSE place to look for a centre close to many rows once addresses differ in 3 bits.
- The brick, the 19 and the staircase use pairs at d at most 2 [read: j-cross section on two adjacent bits; h-1 line 1]. For those
  pairs the table has no gap, which is a reason for them to be class identities. It is not the only way to be one: the second 52
  of section 4b has four-bit wires and moves to other tau all the same. h-6's finding that the staircase works with
  a different kernel on every bit says the same from the other side.
- h-5: wires entangled on at most b bits need N log2 N / b gates, so linear size needs width about log2 N [read]. In the table that
  is d up to log2 N, where the class has agreement of order 1/sqrt(d) and the transform 1/2.
- The symmetry behind it [derived]: for a generic tau the only renamings (with scalings) that keep the kernel power are the bit
  permutations and "complement bit j of the row address and of the column address together", 2^k k! in all (8, 48). The transform
  has N^2 |GL(k,2)| (96, 10,752, the number k-cross counted). **Address maps are symmetries at tau = -1 only.** The tiny-units note
  of the same night says every saving in the published engine's small game needs an address map [read, `wht38/tiny/next/cutf3.md`].
  [guess] Then the engine's savings are the transform's own, and the count-(G) savings of tonight are the class's: two different
  mechanisms. Experiment 6 tests this.

## 8. Growth law

r_K(k) = gates / (N log2 N) for the staircase, k = 2..6:

| kernel | k=2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | coefficient ratios |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the transform [read e2-0] | 0.875 | 0.8333 | 0.8125 | 0.80 | 0.7917 | 1, 2 |
| [[1,2],[3,4]], [[2,7],[5,3]], [[1,100],[1,1]] [ran] | 0.875 | 0.8333 | 0.8125 | 0.80 | 0.7917 | six values, fixed from k = 4 on |
| K_tau, tau = 2, 1/3, 3, -2, 5/7, i, roots of unity, tau a symbol [read h-6] | the same | | | | | fixed from k = 4 on |

All agree to the gate: (3/4) N log2 N + N/4. This is the class law. No kernel's curve falls faster than the transform's and no
kernel needs coefficients that grow with k. The two places where a member differs from the others are not on this curve: the
hub-free 7 at tau = 2 (same count, different wires) and the weight-only blocks (the transform cheaper by one gate at k = 2 and 3).

## 9. The question to keep asking, answered as far as it goes; and what it turned into

*What is the smallest statement about 2 x 2 matrices that a saving uses?* The level-set statement of section 6 for one pair of rows.
The brick is its d = 2 case said for all pairs at once: K (x) K = (rank one) + (tau - 1)(a relabelling with scalings), with the two
centres (1,1,1,tau) and (1,tau,tau,tau).
*Is the list at second powers finite?* Yes, and it is now written down [ran, mod p]: 27 shapes for every tau, 1 more on the short
orbits, 0 for the transform alone.
*At third and fourth powers?* The statements available are finite in number: one for each form e, at most (3^k - 1)/2 of them, read
as whole numbers by the class and modulo 2 by the transform. Which of them a 19-gate or 52-gate circuit can use is not known; no
enumeration exists at 8 points.

The better question. The transform's own asset is that e is read modulo 2, equivalently that address maps are symmetries. A sum of
rows over an orbit of a group of address maps is sparse or structured for the transform and is not for the class. Section 5 is the
smallest case (the group that swaps two bits). **Which groups G of address maps give an orbit problem that is cheap for its size,
and for which the rest of the transform is again a transform of smaller size?** A recursion of that kind is closed to the class by
construction, so the class identities of tonight say nothing against it.

Next experiments, cheapest first.
1. **Weight-only ladder.** Exact minima of the (k+1) x (k+1) block for k = 4, 5, 6, transform against class. Is the gap 1, or does it
   grow with k? (`symk.sh`; k = 4 needs about 10 minutes per case modulo 11.)
2. **Other orbit problems at 8 and 16 points.** For each subgroup G of GL(3,2) and GL(4,2) (up to conjugacy), the matrix "transform
   on G-invariant data": exact minimum with `mincirc`, against the same matrix for a generic tau where G consists of bit
   permutations, and against its own size. Look for one whose cost per dimension is below the transform's.
3. **What do the four-bit wires of the second 52 read, in the class?** Section 6 says two rows that differ in 3 or 4 bits are
   proportional on less than half the points for a generic tau, yet a circuit with four-bit wires exists there. Print the wires of
   `solve52.out`'s program at tau = -2 as "tau^(table of whole numbers) on a support" (h-6's reading) and see which level sets they
   use. Then the same exact test on the 1,237 states with 19 gates that g-1's annealer visited (not saved; rerun with a dump). A
   circuit that is rigid in EVERY frame would still be the object sought; none is known.

   brick's hubs are what the staircase has to pay for)? One annealer run with the target changed; p-5 has the 19 for this kernel

6. **The engine with another kernel.** In `wht38/tiny`, change the kernel to tau = 2 and ask whether the published unit's saving

## 10. Criticism, last

- "No shape of the transform's own at 4 points" is exhaustive modulo 7, 11, 13 and 17. The counts are identical at all four primes,
  which is what one expects of circuits that exist over the rationals, but I lifted and replayed only the two hub-free circuits;
  the 27 common shapes rest on h-6's exact staircase and brick and on the modular census.
- A "shape" is a wiring up to renaming. A helper set with extra collinear points gives several wirings; the counts 550 and 1,404
  count all acyclic choices. Another definition of shape would give other numbers; the comparison between tau is not affected.
- Every lower bound for the class (no 4, no 7, no 6 at 4 points) is modulo primes. "None modulo p" is not "none".
- Section 5 is a sub-problem of dimension k + 1. I have no route from it to a gate of the full transform, and g-5's attempt at one
  loses to the 19. Calling it "the transform's own saving" is true for the question asked and says nothing yet about the order.
- Section 6 is elementary counting. Its conclusion, that width 3 and more is where the transform and the class part, is supported by
  the table and by the absence of any wide saving in the class, and by no example of a wide saving that the transform has for the
  whole problem. It is a direction, not a result.
- The statement about the engine (address maps, section 6) joins a note I read in summary to a symmetry count done by hand. [guess]
- I did not test the other annealer shapes at 8 points myself (experiment 3).
- Section 4b: I announced a candidate after 500 frames and it was wrong. Only the second sample and the exact replay settled it.
  The lesson for experiment 3: "rigid in the frames tried" is not evidence at 16 points, where a circuit can move in fewer than 1
  frame in 1,000; decide by all frames or by an exact solve. The exact replays at tau = -2 and -3 are in one frame, two values of
  tau; "for every tau" is a guess from the form of the ratios.
- Section 6's reading (wide wires are where the transform parts from the class) lost its one test: the widest record circuit is
  a class identity. The table of section 6 stands, and so does the weight-only gap of section 5, which is where a sum over an orbit
  is taken; but I no longer have any evidence that width by itself separates the transform from the class.
- One run was lost to the memory cap (modulo 101 and 163, `sym3_big.out`); modulo 61 replaced it. The k = 4 row is modulo 11 only.
- k = 4, tau = 3, 10 gates: **not decided.** The search hit its 420 s cap without finding a 10; the transform's 10 was found after
  412 s of the same search, so most of the space was covered, but "no 10 for the class at k = 4" is NOT established. The gap at
  k = 4 is therefore 0 or more, unknown; the transform's sequence so far is 2, 4, 7, 10 and the class's 2, 5, 8, at least 10.
