# p-1 letter16: one letter at 16 points, outside the family that gave 49

11 October 2026, 00:00 to 01:00 EDT, 60-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing reviewed, nothing in Lean, nothing published.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (whole numbers or fractions; files in `p-1-letter16/`); [ran, solver] = output of a
floating-point integer programme (scipy HiGHS), a candidate only, unless the same line says "replayed", which means the rectangle
list was then checked with exact fractions and its circuit rebuilt and run on all basis vectors (`replay.py`);
[derived] = by hand tonight, checked by nobody; [read] = from tonight's reports; [guess].
Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free.

Words. N = 2^k points; *the transform* H, entries +1 and -1, H(y,x) = (-1)^(y.x) with y.x the number of common 1-bits of the two
addresses. *Merge tree*: start from the N inputs and repeatedly replace two held sums by one weighted sum; its *nodes* are the sets
of inputs under each sum. *Tree rectangle*: a node of a tree on the rows times a node of a tree on the columns, with one number on
it. *Letter*: the transform, after multiplying each row y by r(y) and each column x by c(x) (the *scalings*; here always signs unless
said), written as a sum of tau tree rectangles; it costs N - 2 + tau gates [read: k-cross 1 A]. *Dual chains*: the row nodes are the
cosets (shifted copies) of a chain of subgroups V1 < V2 < ... of the addresses, the column nodes the cosets of the perpendicular
subgroups, in the opposite order. *Row pair, column pair*: the nodes of two points. *Tile*: a tree rectangle on which the rescaled
transform is constant. *Exact-cover model*: the root rectangle with +1 and tiles with -2 that cover the set E of entries equal
to -1 once each (no overlap). *General model*: any numbers, overlaps allowed.

## 1. What the 49 is [derived; agrees with the two lists in `k-cross/rect2.out`]

The 4-point transform, rows and columns rescaled by signs, is J - 2P: all ones, minus twice a permutation matrix P (5 rectangles:
the brick). So the 16-point transform, rescaled, is

    (J - 2P) (x) (J - 2P') = J  -  2 P (x) J  -  2 J (x) P'  +  4 P (x) P'.

Write an address as (high pair of bits, low pair of bits). P (x) J is four blocks "rows with high part p(h), all low parts" x
"columns with high part h, all low parts". J (x) P' is four blocks with the roles of high and low exchanged. A tree cannot have both
"high part fixed" and "low part fixed" among its nodes (they cross). The dual chains take "high fixed" as row nodes and "low fixed"
as column nodes, so each block of P (x) J splits into 4 rectangles 4x1, each block of J (x) P' into 4 rectangles 1x4, and P (x) P'
is 16 single cells with the number (-2)(-2) = +4. Total 1 + 16 + 16 + 16 = 49: exactly the lists the solver returned
(16 of shape 4x1, 16 of shape 1x4, 16 cells of +4).
So the +4 cells are not repairs. They are the product term of a tensor square, and the "double covering" is J(x)P' meeting P(x)J.

By-product [derived]: for row and column set families that are NOT trees, the same construction costs
**gates = C(columns) + C(rows) + tau - N**, where C(family) is the number of additions needed to form all the sums of the family
(N - 1 for a tree). With "high fixed" and "low fixed" both allowed on both sides the brick's square has only 25 rectangles, but
C = 27 on each side, and 27 + 27 + 25 - 16 = 63 again. Nobody has checked whether e2-0's 52 gates are a letter over two
non-tree families; this formula is what to test it with.

## 2. Why a bent centre cannot tile [derived]

For a bent quadratic centre the rescaled matrix is M(z,x) = (-1)^Q(x+z) after relabelling the rows [read: k-cross hybrid 1], so the
set E of -1 entries is {(z,x) : x + z in S}, S = the 6 points where Q = 1. S is a difference set: every non-zero address is the
difference of exactly one pair of points of S. A tile inside E with rows z0 + U and columns x0 + W needs the whole shifted subgroup
z0 + x0 + (U + W) inside S, so U + W has at most 2 elements: tiles are 1x1, 1x2, 2x1 or 2x2, never a strip of four. With one pair
direction per tree at most 32 + 32 of the 96 cells of E lie in dominoes, the rest are single cells: at least 64 tiles.
[ran, solver] One bent design on the standard chains (x0x3 + x1x2 on both sides): exact-cover minimum 73 (optimal); with overlap
the best found in 300 s is also 73 (solver bound 58, not closed), and my exact floor for it is 48 (section 6). The 73 is
the root, 8 tiles of 2x2 and 64 single cells [replayed], which is the count the difference-set argument gives when the two pair
directions coincide. k-cross's two bent
designs, on chains picked for them, reach 49 with overlap: for a bent centre the chain matters a great deal.
So in the bent family overlap is the only way, and section 3 says what it must contain.

## 3. The block law [derived, ran `blocks.py`]

Let v be the direction of the row pairs and w that of the column pairs; in dual chains v.w = 0, so each block
{y, y+v} x {x, x+w} of H has rank one, and so does each block of M = r H c. Put R(pair of y) = r(y) r(y+v) and
C(pair of x) = c(x) c(x+w). The two rows of a block are equal exactly when R = (-1)^(x.v), the two columns exactly when
C = (-1)^(y.w). So a block is flat when both hold, a checkerboard when both fail, a vertical domino (one column of the block is -1)
when only the first holds, horizontal when only the second.

**Law.** #flat - #checkerboard = (1/2) [ (sum of R)(sum over column pairs of (-1)^(x.v)) + (sum of C)(sum over row pairs of (-1)^(y.w)) ] = 0,
because each character sum is 0. Hence also #vertical = #horizontal. It holds for every sign scaling, and for any two trees whose
pair directions are perpendicular.
[ran] 4,000 random sign pairs at 8 and at 16 points, 300 at 32 and at 64: no exception. Checkerboards seen: 0 to 8 of 16 blocks at 8
points, 4 to 25 of 64 at 16 points. Named designs at 16 points: the bent centre x0x1 + x2x3 with its transform 16 / 16 / 16 / 16; all-ones 16 each;
the 45-design 0 flat, 32 vertical, 32 horizontal, 0 checkerboard; the 13-design at 8 points 0 / 8 / 8 / 0.

**What a block costs** [derived]. Take the mixed second difference m11 - m12 - m21 + m22 of a block. A rectangle that contains the
row pair, or contains the column pair, or misses the block, contributes 0. Only a single-cell rectangle contributes. A checkerboard
has second difference +-4. So **every checkerboard block contains a single-cell rectangle, in the general model, whatever the
numbers** (two of them in the exact-cover model). And a rectangle with at least two rows and two columns is made of whole blocks,
which must be flat in the exact-cover model. Together with the law: **every 2x2 block you may cover by a large tile is paid for by a
checkerboard block somewhere else.** This is the letter's version of j-cross's "every gate saved is paid by one wire entangled on
two adjacent address bits" [read], here with a proof.
[ran, `blocks2.py`] k-cross's two bent designs, on their own chains: 16 flat, 16 vertical, 16 horizontal, 16 checkerboard. The 49
has exactly 16 single cells: the bound "one single cell per checkerboard" is met with equality. Of the 28 bent quadratic forms
without linear part, taken with their transforms on the standard chains, 24 have 16 checkerboards and 4 have 32 (those whose form
pairs the two pair directions; x0x3 + x1x2 is one, and its exact cover is 73 = root + 8 tiles of 2x2 + 64 single cells).
The answer to the brief's question: **yes, the overlap at 16 points is forced, by the 16 checkerboards of a bent centre with its
transform, and no, it is not forced by the transform**: sign vectors with no checkerboard at all exist. They are exactly those for
which the pair swap changes c by one sign on each half of the columns and r by one sign on each half of the rows, in the crossed
way [derived: zero checkerboards iff the 2 x 2 tables of counts are (4,0;0,4) against (0,4;4,0)]. Then every block is a domino,
no tile is 2x2 or larger, and each quarter of the matrix is a sign scaling rho of the transform on N/4 points done by strips
(section 4). The cheapest rho is a character, 11 strips; then r and c are "a character on each half", quadratic forms of rank 2.
A bent rho costs 12: the zero-checkerboard design of section 6 with exact cover 49 = 1 + 4 x 12 is of that kind [ran].

## 4. The design without repairs, what it is, and its growth law

**The object** [ran, solver, replayed; `pwchar.out`]. Dual chains: row nodes = cosets of <1> < <1,2> < <1,2,4>, column nodes =
cosets of <8> < <8,4> < <8,4,2>. r(y) = (-1)^(y0 (1 + y3)), c(x) = (-1)^(x0 x3). Exact-cover minimum for these signs: 45 (solver
optimal in under a second). Replay: the 45 rectangles sum to r H c exactly; the circuit built from them has 59 gates and returns
every row of H on the 16 basis vectors. Shapes: 8x1 twice, 4x1 four times, 2x1 sixteen times, 1x2 sixteen times, 1x4 four times,
1x8 twice, and the root. Largest number 2. E has 128 cells (half the matrix), against 96 for a bent centre: more cells, fewer tiles.
General model for the same signs, numbers up to 4 [ran, solver, 150 s, not closed]: best found 46 or 47 rectangles, solver lower
bound 41; my exact floor for these signs, any numbers, is 36 (section 6). So a record (37) is not excluded for this design by
anything exact, and is excluded by the solver's bound only for numbers up to 4.

**What it is** [derived]. Split rows by the top bit y3 and columns by the bottom bit x0 into four quarters of 8 x 8. In a quarter,
M = sign * (-1)^((y+b).(x+a)) with shifts a, b that depend on the quarter. Each quarter is the transform on N/4 points (bits 1 and 2)
with every entry blown up to a 2x2 block that is a vertical domino in two quarters and a horizontal domino in the other two. In a
"vertical" quarter the two columns of every column pair are opposite, so no tile is two columns wide: the quarter is covered by
vertical strips only, and their number is sum over the N/4 characters chi of p(rho chi), p = the number of constant pieces along the
row tree, rho the row signs seen by that quarter. In circuit words: a vertical quarter is "N/4 differences of column pairs, then
the transform on N/4 points", and the letter has to do that inner transform by brute force, one strip per run of equal signs,
because the column tree holds the SUM of each pair (one form per node) and this quarter needs the DIFFERENCE.
[ran, exhaustive, `pn.py`] P(n) = min over all sign vectors rho of that sum = 3, 11, 43, 171 at n = 2, 4, 8, 16 = (2n^2 + 1)/3,
reached exactly at the characters (8 of 128 and 16 of 32,768 sign vectors at n = 8 and 16).
[derived] The row subtree inside a half serves only one quarter, so it may be any tree there; with rho a character the count
1 + 2 + 4 + 4 = 11 at n = 4 cannot be lowered by another subtree (three non-trivial characters on four pair-points, a tree can
follow one of them at the top and none of the other two). So trees that are not chains do not help this design.

32-point circuit has 203 gates and returns every row of H]. tiles = 4 P(N/4) = (N^2 + 8)/6, tau = (N^2 + 14)/6:

## 6. Two more things derived

**Large numbers need overlap** [derived]. Suppose a letter has no overlaps: the rectangles partition the matrix. Then with real scalings r, c and numbers v_j the matrix v_(j(y,x)) H(y,x) = r(y) c(x) has rank one, so the signs of the
v_j form a sign scaling of H that is constant on every rectangle, and the sizes |v_j| = |r(y)| |c(x)| play no part. A partition
letter with real numbers is a partition letter with signs. Whatever large numbers buy (the Potts matrix: N + 1 rectangles with one
number of size N [read]) must come through rectangles that overlap and cancel.

**A floor for one letter on the dual chains, any numbers, any non-zero real scalings** [derived; `lb.py`, `lb16.py`, exact].
Take a row pair {y, y+v}. A tree node that contains one row of the pair and not the other is that single row. So the difference
of the two rows of the rescaled matrix is produced by the single-row rectangles of that pair alone, and their number is at least
the fewest column-tree nodes whose numbers sum to that difference. With sign scalings the difference is 0 on the half of the
columns where the pair is "equal" and twice the row, c * character, on the other half. The same for column pairs. Single cells
are counted on both sides, so they get weight 1/2. Write t'(f) = the least of (nodes of two or more points) + (1/2)(single points)
over all ways to write f as a sum of numbers on tree nodes. Then

    tau >= sum over row pairs of t'(their difference) + sum over column pairs of t'(their difference) = B(r, c).

Real scalings: replacing every partial sum by its sign turns a way of writing g into a way of writing sign(g) with no more nodes,
so t'(g) >= t'(sign g), and restricting to a half never costs more; the floor below therefore holds for all non-zero real r, c.
Minimising over what the argument does not control (the two halves of c, and which half each pair uses), exhaustively:

| points | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| floor 2L for tau | 4 | 11 | 32 | 108 |
| true or best known tau of a letter on dual chains | 5 | 13 | 45 (exact cover: minimum) | 173 |
| gates N - 2 + floor | 6 | 17 | 46 | 138 |
| best known circuit | 7 | 19 | 52 | 128 |

Per point 1, 1.375, 2, 3.375: the steps 0.375, 0.625, 1.375 grow, so the floor rises faster than N log N on these four sizes
[beyond 32 points: guess; heuristic: each pair of points alternates for half of the characters, which alone gives n^2/8 for one
half]. At 32 points the floor is above the best known circuit.
On actual designs at 16 points [ran, exact, `lbdesign.out`]: B = 36 for the 45-design (so its true tau, overlap allowed, is between
36 and 45; the solver says at least 41); B = 48 for two bent designs on the standard chains (k-cross found 49 for bent designs:
the bent family is settled to within one rectangle if those designs have the same B, which I did not check); B = 42 for all-ones.
Among 829,610 random sign pairs 2,021 have B = 32, the floor itself. For those the bound proves nothing, and one of them is run in
section 5. **So at 16 points the general model is open between 32 and 45, and a record needs 37.**

## 7. The four directions of the brief

(a) Centres that are not bent: done, they are better (45 against 49), because bent means 16 checkerboards (section 3).
(b) Output signs that are not the centre's transform: done inside (a) and inside the free-sign runs; for a rank-2 centre the
    transform has zeros and the good output signs choose a character on each half.
(c) Chains perpendicular at some levels only [derived]: a block (row node A) x (column node B) of H has rank one exactly when the
    differences inside A are perpendicular to the differences inside B, so tiles exist only on pairs of nodes that ARE perpendicular;
    dropping perpendicularity at a level removes tile shapes and adds none. Run: section 5.
(d) Trees that are not subgroup chains [derived]: for the same reason every tile lies inside (a coset of some subgroup V) x (a coset
    of its perpendicular); a node of 3, 5 or 6 points only gives tiles that are parts of those. For the 45-design the subtree
    inside a half is already free and does not help (section 4). Run: section 5.
Bound on the numbers raised from 4 to 64: at 8 points with free real rows, 13, optimal (above). At 16 points I did not get a closed run;
but the floor of section 6 holds for any numbers and any real scalings, which answers the question the bound was raised for at the
sizes computed: on the dual chains no numbers and no real scalings bring one letter under 1, 1.4, 2, 3.4 rectangles per point at
4, 8, 16, 32 points. Whether the floor keeps rising beyond 32 points is a guess: I have no proof in k.

## 8. Walls I can name, and mutations

**Wall (exact-cover letters on dual chains): one form per node.** Each column pair is "equal" on one half of the rows and "opposite"
on the other (it has to be: the two columns of H differ by a character of the rows). A tree gives the pair one form. On the half
where the pair is opposite the letter can only use single columns, and symmetrically for rows; where both are opposite it pays single
cells; and the blocks where both are equal are as many as those (the law). Zero checkerboards forces all strips, and strips are an
inner transform done by brute force: quadratic. With overlap and any numbers the same cause gives the floor of section 6 (the
difference of the two rows of a pair must be written with single-row rectangles): 4, 11, 32, 108.
What the wall does NOT cover: trees other than dual chains in the general model (their floor B is computable by the same program,
not done), letters over families that are not trees (section 1), and products of two letters (brief 8 of this wave).

Mutations.
1. **Two forms per node.** Give every column pair both its sum and its difference (N/2 more gates), and grow a second tree on the
   differences where a quarter needs it. In the count this is a letter over a forest: N - 2f + tau [read: WAVE5 note 2]. Done
   naively at every level it is the textbook recursion T(N) = 2N + 4 T(N/4). The question that is new: the letter saved 5 gates at
   8 points and 1 at 16 over that recursion by SHARING one tree across all quarters; how much sharing survives if the second tree is
   grown only on the quarters that need it (two of four)? Count it at 32 points, where the plain letter (173 rectangles) already
   loses to the textbook (130 in the same count).
2. **The law at every level.** The mixed second difference over (children of a row node) x (children of a column node) is the Haar
   coefficient of the letter at that pair of nodes; a rectangle contributes only to pairs of nodes strictly above it. So tau is at
   least the fewest "chain x chain" patterns that produce the two-tree Haar coefficients of r H c. Section 3 is the bottom level
   only. Worked out at all levels this is a floor for ONE letter with any numbers and any scalings, the first that would not need
   bounded coefficients. Small exact computation at 8 and 16 points first.
3. **Decide 16 points in the general model.** The floor says 32, the best letter is 45, a record needs 37. Take the sign pairs
   with B <= 37 (`lbdesign.py` finds them at once: about 1 in 180 random pairs) and run the overlapping model on each class
   (`rfix.py`, mode gen, an hour each rather than five minutes). If all come back above 37, one letter on dual chains is dead at
   16 points as well as at 32.
4. **Is e2-0's 52 a letter over two non-tree families?** Formula in section 1. If yes, the right object is not a tree but a family
   with a few crossings, and the cost C(family) of the crossings is the thing to minimise.
5. **Overlap with large numbers, on purpose** (section 6): the only place real scalings can act. Start from the 45-design's quarters
   and allow one rank-one correction per quarter with a free number, as the Potts matrix does.
