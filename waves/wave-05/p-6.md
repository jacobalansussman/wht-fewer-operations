Words. N = 2^k points. *The transform*: the Walsh-Hadamard matrix H. *Merge tree*: start from the N inputs, repeatedly
replace two held sums by one weighted sum (N - 1 gates); its *nodes* are the sets of inputs under each sum (2N - 1 of them,
single inputs and the whole set, the *root*, included). *Cherry*: two inputs merged with each other first. *Chain* (caterpillar):
the tree that adds one input at a time, so its nodes are the prefixes of an order. *Matching*: every node is used at most
once, either squared or multiplied with one partner node. N' = number of nodes used. Cost of such an identity: N - 2 + N'.

## 1. What the idea says, exactly

[derived] In a merge tree with free scalings every form is, up to a factor, the restriction of ONE weight vector w to a node:
f_A = sum over x in A of w(x) x. So H' = F^T M F with F a merge tree and M a matching means

    S(x,y) / (u(x) u(y))  =  sum over squared nodes A of c_A [x,y in A]  +  sum over pairs {A,B} of c_AB ([x in A, y in B] + [x in B, y in A]),

S a symmetric matrix with entries +-1 and orthogonal rows (at 4 and 8 points: any symmetric signed relabelling of the
transform, because all such matrices of those orders are relabellings of H [recalled]), u = 1/w. The number of rectangles of
k-cross is N' here, and its formula N - 2 + tau is the brief's 2c + N' - N. A symmetric S of order 8 has trace 0 (its
eigenvalues are +-sqrt 8), so exactly four diagonal entries are -1 [derived]; at order 4 the trace can be 0, +-4.
Row view, used everywhere below: with R_A = c_A * (indicator of the partner of A), **row x of the matrix is the sum of R_A
over the nodes A that contain x.** Two inputs in a cherry have rows that differ by two node indicators only.

(section 5) demands for every cherry. I did not turn this into a proof; the run is the evidence.

## 4. Why g-1's two trees cannot simply be made equal (d)

[derived from `g-1-tightwires/h8_19gates.txt`] Read as output node x input node, the 13 rectangles are: root x root;
{1,2,4,7}x{7}, {0,3,5,6}x{5}; {1,7}x{6}, {0,6}x{4}, {3,5}x{3}, {2,4}x{1}; {7}x{3,4}, {1}x{2,5}, {6}x{1,6}, {0}x{0,7};
{5}x{0,1,6,7}, {4}x{2,3,4,5}. Each of 13 output nodes and each of 13 input nodes occurs once: it is a matching, and the two
trees have the same shape (balanced). So the brief's hope is exactly: find a map s from inputs to outputs, carrying tree to
tree, under which the rectangle O x I goes to s(I) x s^-1(O) inside the set. Take the two (four x one) blocks. Their mirror
images must be the two (one x four) blocks. Either s(7) = 5 with s{0,1,6,7} = {1,2,4,7}, impossible since 7 is in {0,1,6,7}
and 5 is not in {1,2,4,7}; or s(7) = 4, s{2,3,4,5} = {1,2,4,7}, s(5) = 5, impossible since 5 would have to land in {1,2,4,7}.
So no s exists: not a count of parameters but an incidence that has the wrong parity. Trees CAN be made equal with S
symmetric (a symmetric bit matrix L with L(7) = 6, L(1) = 5 exists [derived]); what cannot be made symmetric is the matching.
Section 3 says no other matching repairs it in model C.

## 5. The partner lemma: matchings force chains

[derived] Let x, x' be two inputs and Q their first common node. By the row view, row x minus row x' is a combination of the
indicators of the partners of the nodes on the two paths from x and from x' up to (not including) Q. On the other side, two
rows of S agree in exactly N/2 places and differ in N/2, so u(x) S(x,.) - u(x') S(x',.) is non-zero in at least N/2 places
whatever the scalings (u(x) - u(x') and u(x) + u(x') cannot both vanish). Hence

    sum over the nodes A on the path between x and x' of |partner(A)|  >=  N/2.

Pair the inputs up so that the N/2 paths share no node (possible in every binary tree: each subtree hands up at most one
unpaired input). Summing: the partners have total size at least N^2/4. Partners are distinct nodes, so the sizes of all nodes
add up to at least N^2/4; that sum is the sum over inputs of (depth + 1). **Average depth of an input >= N/4 - 1.**
- Holds for one tree or two (g-1's kind of matching: then for both trees), real or complex scalings, any coefficients.
- Balanced tree: total size N (k + 1) < N^2/4 from 32 points; at 16 points the sharper count is 8 cherries needing 8 different
  partners of size >= 4, and there are 7 such nodes. So **the 19-gate pattern (balanced trees, each node once) has no
  16-point member**, which is why k-cross found single-cell repairs and 49 rectangles there [read: k-cross hybrid 1].
- Answer to the brief's standing question: the number of disjoint pairs is not the limit (it can be N); the limit is their
  SIZE. The pairs must carry total size N^2/4, and only a tree of depth of order N has that much.
- Check at 4 points: s^2 - 4 x1(x2+x3) - 4 x2x3 on ((01)(23)): cherry (0,1) partners 0 + 2, cherry (2,3) partners 1 + 1; total 4 = N^2/4.

## 6. Yes, and: the chain

A chain has N/2 nodes of size >= N/2, so the lemma does not touch it. [derived] For a chain in the order 1..N the nodes are
single inputs and prefixes, and the blocks are corners [1..i]x[1..j], row pieces {i}x[1..j] and single cells. Take second
differences: D(x,y) = G(x,y) - G(x+1,y) - G(x,y+1) + G(x+1,y+1). A corner gives one non-zero cell of D, a row piece two, a
cell four. So **D has at most 4 N' <= 8N non-zero cells.** With sign scalings D(x,y) = 0 exactly when rows x, x+1 agree in
columns y, y+1, or columns y, y+1 agree in rows x, x+1. For S(x,y) = (-1)^(p_x^T C p_y), p the order of visiting the addresses,
C a symmetric invertible bit matrix, and steps c_x = p_x + p_(x+1): D(x,y) is non-zero when c_x^T C c_y = 1. If the order is
of Gray-code type (k independent step vectors, used N/2, N/4, ... times) the number of such cells is
sum over b, b' of Gamma(b,b') N^2 / 2^(b+b'+2), Gamma the k x k table of c_b^T C c_b'. Gamma is invertible, so it contains a
permutation, and the sum over a permutation of 2^-(b + s(b)) is at least k 2^-(k-1) (arithmetic and geometric mean). So
**at least (N/2) log2 N non-zero cells**, and N' >= (N/8) log2 N. The sequency (Walsh) order, where row j has j sign changes,
gives about N log2 N [derived]. This is the conjugation wall once more, with constant 1/8: chains of this family die only
beyond 2^16 points by this count; the true first failure is surely much earlier and at 8 points model C already has none.
Not covered: orders that are not of Gray type, matrices S not of bit-linear type, scalings that are not signs.

## 7. What to do next (mutations)

- **Weights that are not signs.** [derived] If u(x+1)/u(x) = r is not +-1, a second difference vanishes only if the
  neighbouring ratio is +-(1 - r)/(1 + r) or its inverse. A ratio that reproduces itself solves r^2 + 2r - 1 = 0:
  r = sqrt 2 - 1 (tan of 22.5 degrees, the eigenvector of the 2 x 2 kernel), or r^2 = -1. So the only chains with free weights
  that keep many zero cells have weights (sqrt 2 - 1)^e(x): not rational. Run `general.py` over the field with sqrt 2 on the chain at 8 points.
- **Free the root.** Root unused or paired; one more loop in `general.py`.
- **Two chains, a matching** (not symmetric): the lemma allows it; it is the staircase of wave 4 seen as rectangles.
- **Drop "each node once" for the big nodes only**: the lemma says which nodes are short (size >= N/4); let those be used twice.
- **Turn section 3 into a proof** from the diagonal count plus the lemma; it would be the first exact impossibility at 8 points.
