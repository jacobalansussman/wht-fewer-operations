Words. *Kernel* K_tau = [[1,1],[1,tau]]; its second Kronecker power is the 4-point matrix with entry tau^(common 1-bits of row
and column address); tau = -1 is the transform. *Helper*: a wire that is not an output. *Hub*: a helper that reads all four inputs.
*Wiring*: which two wires each gate reads. *Shape*: a wiring with the names of inputs, helpers and outputs forgotten.
*Naming*: a shape with the names put back. *Weight-only block*: the kernel power on data that depend only on the number of 1-bits
of the address, a (k+1) x (k+1) matrix (at tau = -1 the Krawtchouk matrix). *Bad reduction*: a rational circuit read modulo p in which
two different wires become the same point, so that it stops being a circuit modulo p.

## 1. The claim, stated precisely

C1. For every tau other than 0 and 1 the 4-point kernel power needs exactly 7 gates.
C2. Its 7-gate circuits fall into 27 shapes that exist for every tau; one more shape (three helpers reading 2, 2 and 3 inputs, no hub)
    exists exactly when tau = 2, tau = 1/2 or tau^2 - tau + 1 = 0; no shape exists for tau = -1 only.
C3. The weight-only block costs 4 gates (k = 2) and 7 gates (k = 3) at tau = -1, and 5 and 8 at every other tau.
p-0 gave C1, C2 and the lower bounds of C3 as exhaustive modulo 7, 11, 13, 17 (C3: also 61 at k = 2; 11 and 13 at k = 3), and said so.

### 2a. Is the helper-set enumeration in `mincirc_sets.c` complete?  I found no hole.

Reading [read, the whole file]. The search rests on: (i) gates = outputs + helpers when all wires are different points;
(ii) adding an output the moment it lies on a line through two available points never hurts; (iii) helpers are added in an order
in which each lies on a line through two available points. All three are sound. The two shortcuts where an error could hide:
- *the last helper* is taken only among points on a line through a missing output and an available point. Sound: all outputs
  buildable without it are already in, so a last helper that puts no output on a line with an available point is useless.
- *sets of one or two helpers are not visited twice.* Sound: the available set after two helpers is the least set closed under
  "add a buildable output", which does not depend on the order. The key packs two point numbers in 64 bits; the filler 0xFFFFFF for
  one-element sets is larger than any point number used (at most 17^4 = 83,521), so no collision. If the table of 4 million slots
  filled up the program would loop, not answer wrongly.
- One thing to know, not an error: a set is recorded only if all m helpers are used. That is the right count when m is the minimum.
- `mincirc_sets.c` needs an even number of inputs; the weight-only runs used g-5's `mincirc_odd.c` [read: only the table strides differ].

### 2b. "None modulo p is not none": what is now exact

**Theorem A (no 6 gates at 4 points).** [derived] Let tau be neither 0 nor 1, in any field. Facts [ran, exact, `r2_exact.out` E1]:
every entry of the four rows is non-zero; the determinant is (tau - 1)^4; no two rows are proportional on three columns.
Suppose 2 helpers. The first wire built reads two inputs, so it reads 2 inputs and is a helper h1. The second reads two of
{inputs, h1}, so it reads at most 3 inputs and is the helper h2. From then on only outputs are built. An output is never
(input, output) (two rows would agree on three columns) and never (input, input). So every output is built from (helper, helper), (helper, output),
(output, output), or (helper, input); the last can give a wire with four inputs only if h2 reads three inputs and the input is
the fourth one, e_l. Hence all four outputs lie in the span of h1, h2 and e_l, which has dimension at most 3 (h2 = h1 + b e_k in
that case). Four independent rows do not fit. So 7 is the minimum for every member, with no prime in the argument.

**Theorem B (weight-only, k = 2).** [derived] S_tau = [[1,2,1],[1,1+tau,tau],[1,2 tau,tau^2]]. Its nine 2 x 2 minors are
tau - 1 (four times), tau(tau - 1), tau^2(tau - 1), 2(tau - 1), 2 tau(tau - 1) and **(tau - 1)(tau + 1)**
[ran, exact, E1b]. For tau not in {0, 1, -1} and 2 not zero: every entry and every 2 x 2 minor is non-zero, so no two rows agree
on two columns. With one helper h (the first wire built reads two inputs and no row has a zero, so it is a helper), an output
is never (input, output) (two rows would agree on two columns) and never (input, input); so every output is built from h, the one
input e_k that h does not read, and outputs: all three rows would lie in the span of h and e_k, of dimension 2. So at least 2
helpers, 5 gates. p-0's 5-gate circuit works for every tau [ran, exact, my replay, 8 values]. At tau = -1 two things happen, both
the factor tau + 1: the entry 1 + tau is zero (row 1 is built from two inputs and is an output at once), and rows 0 and 2 agree
on columns 0 and 2. That is the 4-gate circuit. **The gap at k = 2 is the factor tau + 1, in one entry and in one minor.**

**The 27 shapes for tau a symbol.** [ran, exact, `r2_lift.py`, `r2_lift.out`] For each shape I took one naming found modulo 13,
solved for the three helper coefficients as rational functions of tau (Groebner basis over the field Q(tau)), and replayed all 7
gates with tau a symbol and with fractions at tau = 3, 5/7, -2, -1, 2, 1/2: 27 of 27, no degeneration at any of the six values.
The coefficients are 1, tau, 1/(tau - 1), -tau/(tau - 1) and their like.

**The 28th shape.** [ran, exact, `r2_exact.py` E2] All 24 x 24 namings of its wiring, tau a symbol, tau eliminated last: a
solution can exist only if tau = 2 (8 namings), 2 tau = 1 (8) or tau^2 - tau + 1 = 0 (16); the numbers 8, 8, 16 are p-0's
counts of wirings. So "not at tau = -1" is exact in characteristic 0. (The helper "equal to its second parent" is left out of the
parametrisation; that would be a 6-gate circuit, excluded by Theorem A.)

**The transform's 228 sets are rational.** [ran, exact, `r2_small.py`] Over the rationals, among the 40 points with coordinates
0, 1, -1, exactly 228 sets of three helpers work, every one with a hub, and no set of two. Their reductions are 228 different sets
modulo every prime from 5 up, and the search modulo p finds 228: modulo p there is nothing but these.

### 2c. What is still modulo primes, and what a counterexample would have to look like

Not proved: that no further shape exists over the rationals or over a number field (for tau = -1, for a generic tau, or at a
further special tau), and that the weight-only block at k = 3 needs 8 gates when tau is not -1.
A circuit over the rationals survives modulo every prime at which it has no bad reduction, and a circuit over a number field
survives modulo every prime that has a factor of degree one there. So a missed 7-gate shape would have to fail at each of 7, 11,
13, 17 and 19. The circuits that do exist have coefficients 1, -1, 2, tau, tau - 1: they reduce well at every prime from 5 up, and
the counts are the same five times. I rate a missed shape as very unlikely; it is not excluded.
Two cautions on the wording "generic tau modulo p". (i) Every residue is a root of unity of order dividing p - 1 (modulo 11: order
2, 5 or 10), so a search modulo p never sees a tau that is generic in the strict sense; the exact lifts above are what makes "for
every tau" true. (ii) A lower bound for "every tau modulo p" is a statement about those residues only.

## 3. Is it known?

From memory, not re-read tonight; numbers to be checked before any of this is quoted.
- Exhaustive minimum linear circuits at small size are a known genre over GF(2) (Boyar, Matthews, Peralta, "Logic minimization
  techniques with applications to cryptology", J. Cryptology 2013: the shortest linear program problem, hard in general). I know no
  published census over the rationals with free scalings for a 4 x 4 Hadamard matrix or for the family K_tau. [guess: new as a table,
  elementary as mathematics]
- The 7-gate circuit itself is the smallest case of "low rank plus sparse" (matrix non-rigidity): rows = hub + one input. That
  route is Alman and Rao, "Faster Walsh-Hadamard and discrete Fourier transforms from matrix non-rigidity", STOC 2023,
  arXiv:2211.06459, and what the staircase of tonight does at larger size.
- That savings belong to Kronecker powers of ANY fixed small matrix, not to the transform, is the frame of the literature:
  Alman and Williams, STOC 2017, arXiv:1611.05558 (the transform is not rigid); Dvir and Liu, 2019, arXiv:1902.07334; Alman,
  "Kronecker products, low-depth circuits, and matrix rigidity", STOC 2021, arXiv:2102.11992; Kivva, 2021, arXiv:2103.05631;
  Alman, Guan, Padaki, "Smaller low-depth circuits for Kronecker powers", SODA 2023, arXiv:2211.05217. p-0's "class identity" is
  the 4-point shadow of that. In those papers the transform differs from other 2 x 2 kernels in constants, never in order.
- The weight-only block is the Krawtchouk matrix, the k-th symmetric power of the 2 x 2 kernel (Feinsilver and Kocik, 2005);
  the cut into blocks by permuting address bits is standard representation theory. Known.
- Lower bounds: with coefficients of bounded size, (1/2) N log2 N (Morgenstern 1973). With free coefficients, nothing above
  linear is known for any explicit matrix (Valiant 1977; Lokam's survey 2009). The census uses free coefficients.
**What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": nothing, in either direction.** No known lower bound applies to count (G),
and no known upper bound for any member of the class is below N log N in order.

## 4. Does it scale?

- The census cannot be pushed to 8 points by this method: 11 helpers in a 7-dimensional projective space. p-0 did not claim it.
- Weight-only blocks, one size further (k = 4, 5 x 5) [ran, mod 11, g-5's `mincirc_odd`]: no 9 gates at tau = -2 and tau = -1/2
  either (p-0 had tau = -1 and 3). **10 gates for another member: not decided** (runs of 20 and 10 minutes on the rented
  machine, tau = 3 and tau = -2 modulo 11, neither finished; section 8). The transform has a 10 modulo 11 [read p-0, not lifted].
- A structural reason to expect the gap NOT to be the transform's alone from k = 4 on [ran, exact, `r2_small.out`]: the number of
  vanishing 2 x 2 minors of the block, which is what Theorem B says a saving is made of:

  | k | tau = -1 | tau = -2 | tau = 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3 | 8 (no zero entry) | 0 (2 zero entries) | 0 |
  | 4 | 16 (4 zero entries) | **14** | 0 |
  | 5 | 44 | 6 | 0 |

  So at k = 4 the member tau = -2 (and 1/2 its mirror, -1/2) has almost as many coincidences as the transform. [guess] "the
  transform is alone on weight-only data" is true at k = 2 (exact), true at k = 3 modulo three primes, and may become "the
  transform and a few special members" at k = 4.
- Growth law observed for the gap: 0, 1, 1 at k = 1, 2, 3; k = 4 open. Ceiling [derived]: the block has (k+1)^2 entries, so the
  whole weight-only block costs at most (k+1)^2 gates for anybody, and no gap on it can exceed about (log2 N)^2 gates.

## 5. In the user's terms

**Nothing yet: no circuit for N points, no saving exponent.** At 4 points the minimum is 7 for every member (now a theorem),
which is the known rate 7/8 at that size. The weight-only gap is one gate in a corner of dimension log2 N + 1 and can never be
worth more than (log2 N)^2 gates by itself.

On the two things said to rest on it.
- *The answer to the class question* ("is any saving the transform's own?"): at 4 points, no, and that is now half exact (every
  shape the transform has, every member has; the transform has more namings of each, 2 or 3 times). Whether a shape was missed is
  modulo five primes.
- *q-cross's reading, "everything the transform has that its class lacks is worth at most about N sqrt(log N)"*: [derived] this
  does not follow. The argument is: one change of basis B, the same for every member, cuts every member into weight-type blocks,
  and the blocks are cheap. That bounds every member by cost(B) + cost(B inverse) + blocks. It bounds the DIFFERENCE between the
  transform and another member only if B itself is cheap, and nobody has a cheap B: a circuit for the transform that does not go
  through B is not touched by the argument. As a statement about minimum circuits it is a guess.
- What does follow [derived from two facts in the brief, not run]: K_tau = [[1,0],[1,1]] diag(1, tau - 1) [[1,1],[0,1]], so the
  k-th power of K_tau is (subset sums, transposed) x (a diagonal) x (subset sums); and subset sums = four transforms and five
  diagonals (i-cross W5 [read]). So **every member costs at most 8 transforms in count (G), plus at most 11 N scalings in count
  (O)**. If the transform had linear size, every member would. For the ORDER the class is one problem; no member is an easier or
  a harder target by more than that factor. (The converse direction, a member cheap implies the transform cheap, I did not derive.)
  This is the statement to keep; it needs no census.
