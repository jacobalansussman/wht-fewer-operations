Words. N = 2^k points. *The transform*: the Walsh-Hadamard matrix H. *Merge tree*: start from wires, repeatedly replace two
held sums by one weighted sum; its *nodes* are the sets of wires under each sum. *Rectangle*: a set of rows times a set of
columns with one number on it. **A letter** is a matrix that, after rescaling its rows and its columns once, is a sum of
rectangles (row node of one tree) x (column node of another tree). As a circuit a letter is: merges going up a tree on its
input wires, then additions going down a tree to its output wires. *Middle wires*: the outputs of the first letter, which
are the inputs of the second. *Character*: a row of the transform; *on a coset*: restricted to an affine subspace of addresses.

## 0. In ten lines

1. [ran] I wrote a program that takes any gate list, replays it exactly, and finds the fewest letters it can be cut into
   (`letters.py`). Result: g-1's 19 is ONE letter. e2-0's family (7, 20, 52, 128, 304 gates) comes out as **1, 2, 3, 4, 5
   letters**, one more at every doubling (proved for the first three, the 52 by hand; for 128 and 304 the search for
   fewer letters stopped at its node limit). Bricks (56), "pairs then the 19 twice" (54), and two gate lists I built by
   tensoring known pieces and replayed exactly, **132 at 32 points and 304 at 64 points**, are two letters.
2. [ran + derived] e2-0's 52 is not two letters, and I can say exactly why: four merges in its middle are each used by two
   different trees. Compute those four merges twice (on copied wires, copies are free) and it IS two letters: 56 gates,
   middle 24 wide. **So each of the four gates between that 56 and the 52 is one merge shared by two trees.**
   (The cheapest two-letter list I have at 16 points is the pair cut, 54, middle 16 wide.)
3. [derived] A product of two letters is the same thing as ONE letter whose table of numbers is not sparse but is
   "how much two families of nested sets of middle wires overlap". Equivalently: the rescaled transform is a sum over the
   middle wires j of (a staircase over the output tree) x (a staircase over the input tree). Section 3.
4. [derived] Ranks do not forbid a linear number of rectangles for two letters: N middle wires placed like a net (one wire
   in every tree box of area N) meet every rank inequality with room to spare. Section 4, Lemma 1.
5. [derived] **Cell lemma.** In the block shape where the trees are perpendicular subgroup chains, a cell with one active
   middle wire forces that wire to be "a character plus a constant" in BOTH letters. Then each letter contains transforms
   of size about sqrt(N), and two letters cost what one letter costs at the square-root size. Section 4, Lemma 2.
6. Growth law, best two-letter circuits built: **7, 19, 54, 132, 304** gates at 4 to 64 points, ratio to N log2 N:
   0.875, 0.79, 0.84, 0.83, 0.79; middle width exactly N each time. From 128 points on it is governed by one letter at
   16 points, which is poor (63 gates), so the ratio climbs back: 0.90 at 128, 0.98 at 256. Section 5.
7. The way out that the cell lemma leaves: cells that carry TWO or more middle wires. e2-0's 20 at 8 points is exactly
   that [read off `slp_8.txt`]: two wires per cell, for example (1,1,1,1) and (1,1,0,0), which together span two characters;
   the second is half the sum of the two. It is the one two-letter object here that is not a tensor cut.
8. [ran, solver] The planned solver search over wide middles produced no number: on the rented machine (load 17 to 23)
   the integer programme did not finish 8-point cases with a wide middle. Its calibration did (13 rectangles, 19 gates,
   replayed exactly), and so did one later run at lower load (line 10).
9. Wall, named: **a letter cannot use its own outputs again.** Inside one letter a merge belongs to one tree; the 3/4 family
   saves by re-merging, and pays one letter per level. Two letters cannot do it at any middle width I tried.
10. Better question it turned into: **is one letter enough for the 8-point map "four whole characters and four half
    characters" in 16 gates?** Yes would give 52 at 16 points in two letters. [ran, solver] First answer: never below 18 in

## 1. The count for a letter, and the tool

[derived] Cost of a letter = (merges needed to build every column node that carries a rectangle) + (rectangles) -
(number of places where a stream starts). For one tree on each side this is k-cross's N - 2 + tau.
[ran] `letters.py` checks this the other way round, without rectangles: it assigns every gate to (letter, up or down) so that
- the up gates of a letter add forms on DISJOINT sets of that letter's input wires, and those sets are nested or disjoint;
- a down gate adds, to at most one down gate of the same letter (its stream), forms that are up forms or input wires;
- a wire made in an earlier letter can be an input wire of any later letter for free.
It searches depth-first for the fewest letters and prints "EXHAUSTIVE" when a failed search was complete.
Calibration: g-1's 19 comes out as one letter (7 up, 12 down); the 7-gate brick as one letter (3 up, 4 down).

## 3. What a product of two letters is  [derived]

Write the rescaled transform as R1 D R2: R2 = sum over r of (number) x (wire set A_r) x (input set B_r), R1 = sum over s of
(number) x (output set C_s) x (wire set E_s), D the scalings of the m middle wires (m may exceed N).
- **One letter with a dense table.** R1 D R2 = sum over all pairs (s, r) of [number_s x number_r x d(E_s meet A_r)] on the
  rectangle C_s x B_r, where d(set) adds the scalings of the wires in the set. So two letters = one letter over the output
  tree and the input tree whose table of numbers is "sparse x overlaps of two nested families x sparse".
- **Staircases.** Column j of R1 is u_j = the sum of the steps (number_s on C_s) of all rectangles above wire j in its
  tree; row j of R2 is w_j likewise. The rescaled transform = sum over wires of d_j x u_j x w_j. Each wire is one rank-one
  piece whose two sides are staircases, and two wires share every step that sits above both in the middle tree.
- **Symmetric case.** Same trees and numbers on both sides gives F D F^T, which is p-6's "selftranspose"; with one middle
  tree it is a signed Cholesky factorisation, and H = Z D Z^T (Z = subset sums) is that factorisation of the transform.
One letter of k-cross is the special case "every wire has one step on each side" (wire = rectangle, m = tau).

## 4. Three lemmas  [derived tonight, a few lines each, checked by no program]

Call wire j *active inside* an output node C if u_j is not constant on C (some rectangle above j has its output set
strictly inside C); same for input nodes B and w_j. The *cell* of (C, B) is the set of wires active inside both.
**Lemma 1 (ranks).** rank H[C, B] <= 2 + (wires in the cell of (C, B)), for every output node C and input node B.
Proof: wires not active in C give one common column pattern, wires active in C but not in B one common row pattern.
This is k-cross's rank lemma and g-6's Lemma A in one statement (g-6: every wire sits in one node per level).
It forbids nothing at linear size: for trees that are perpendicular subgroup chains the transform needs exactly
|C||B|/N (g-6's Theorem 1), and N wires, each living at one output leaf and one input leaf, placed so that every box
C x B of area N holds exactly one of them (a "net"; bit reversal is one) give |C||B|/N wires in every larger box.
**Lemma 2 (cells; "two calls" for any number of wires).** Let C be a coset of a subgroup W and B a coset of W-perp,
so that the block H[C, B] has rank one: (character eta on C) x (character xi on B). If neither side is constant after
the rescaling, then **the character eta on C lies in the span of the constant and the u_j of the cell's wires, and the
character xi on B in the span of the constant and their w_j.** With ONE wire in the cell: u_j = (number) x character +
constant, on both sides. So block C of the second letter is the |W|-point transform with scaled columns plus one
row-constant term, and it must itself be a sum of that letter's rectangles strictly inside C. Consequence: with one wire
per cell, **rectangles(L1) >= (N/|W|) x [one-letter count of the |W|-point transform, given its row-constant term free]**,
and rectangles(L2) >= |W| x [the same count for the (N/|W|)-point transform]. At |W| = 4 the free term is the brick's hub
and 4 single cells remain: the brick layer.
Two letters are then two tensor factors, each a forest of one-letter transforms.
**What goes around it.** (i) Cells with r >= 2 wires: the lemma only asks that r staircases SPAN the character, a free
r x r change of basis per cell. This needs blocks with |C||B| = rN, so the middle stays N wide; to have two wires in a
rank-one cell needs m = 2N. (ii) Wires active in several blocks at once (g-6 and p-3 exclude them by definition).
(iii) Trees that are not subgroup chains (p-3's "door that is not periodic").
**Lemma 3 (the last step).** For two outputs y, y' that are the two leaves under one node of the output tree, (row y) -
(row y') of the rescaled transform = (forms added at leaf y) - (forms added at leaf y'). With sign rescalings the left
side is plus or minus twice a character on a hyperplane coset, N/2 inputs. (For two outputs further apart: the same with
"forms added on the two paths below their meeting node".) So when the output tree ends in N/2 pairs, the down part of
the last letter must be handed N/2 half-size characters as merged forms. [checked on g-1's gate list by hand] In the 19
they are g1 - g2, g1 + g2 on {0,1,6,7} and g3 + g4, g3 - g4 on {2,3,4,5}: two characters of each half, each a node or a
sum of two nodes of the input tree. [guess] At 16 points a half has 8 points and a tree has only two 8-nodes; I think
that is where k-cross's 49 rectangles come from.

## 5. Growth law

T_L(k) = fewest gates with L letters at 2^k points. Upper bounds, all exact gate lists [ran] unless marked:

| points | one letter | two letters (middle width) | e2-0's family (letters) | textbook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 7 | 7 (1) | 8 |
| 8 | 19 | 19 | 20 (2) | 24 |
| 16 | 63 [read, k-cross, solver] | 54 (16) | 52 (3) | 64 |
| 32 | | 132 (32) | 128 (4) | 160 |
| 64 | | 304 (64) | 304 (5) | 384 |
| 128 | | 808 [derived: 16 x 19 + 8 x 63] | 704 (6) [read, formula] | 896 |
| 256 | | 2016 [derived: 32 x 63] | 1600 (7) [read, formula] | 2048 |

- Two letters by tensor cut: T_2(a + b) <= 2^b T_1(a) + 2^a T_1(b). By Lemma 2 this is forced in the one-wire-per-cell shape.
- **Two letters tie five letters at 64 points (304 = 304), and lose from 128 on** because one letter at 16 points costs 63.
  If one letter at 16 points cost 50, two letters would tie the family at 256 (1600). A file of another agent
  (`p-1-letter16/pwchar.out`, a solver line, not yet reported) shows 45 rectangles, that is 59 gates: 776 and 1888.
- The middle width at every optimum I have is exactly N. The one wider object (24) costs more (56), not less.
- So T_2(k)/N is NOT bounded on this evidence: 3.4, 4.1, 4.75 at 16, 32, 64 points and then it follows T_1 at sqrt(N).
- The family's letters grow like k - 1. Nothing I have separates "rate 3/4 needs log N letters" from "needs 3".

## 7. The question to keep asking: what must a middle wire look like?

[derived] A middle wire is one rank-one piece d_j x u_j x w_j of the rescaled transform. As a sum of inputs it is
(the one fixed input rescaling) x (a staircase on the input tree); as it is spread over the outputs it is (the one fixed
output rescaling) x (a staircase on the output tree). A character on a coset is a one-step staircase on BOTH sides, but
only if the fixed rescaling agrees with that character on that coset, and on a coset of s points just one of its s
characters can agree. That is the whole budget, and it is p-3's sentence "a rank-one direction can be proportional to
only one character" seen from the middle. Large rectangles in both letters need many wires that share a step, so their
forms must differ only outside a large node, on both sides. Characters of one coset never do. Mixes of characters do:
e2-0's wires (1,1,0,0) and (0,0,1,-1) are each half the sum or difference of two characters, and they share a step.
**The better question.** Take blocks with two wires per cell (r = 2). At 16 points that is: first letter = two copies of
an 8-point map M8 whose outputs are, for each of the four 4-point characters, the character on the whole 8-block and the
character on one 4-block (any choice of which, any signs); second letter = four 5-gate pieces (e2-0's top level, which
IS one letter). Total 2 x cost(M8) + 20. e2-0 does M8 in 16 gates with two letters. **Is M8 one letter in 16 gates?**
[derived] By hand I get 18 (e2-0's trees; p4 starts a stream, the stream p4 - p9 costs two gates because p9 is a down
form and cannot be read as one form), 19 (one 8-point centre, two single-cell corrections per whole character) and 20
(centres that are characters). 16 would be 52 in two letters; 17 would be 54, a tie; 18 is 56, the copied 52 again.
[ran, solver, `m8.py`, `m8_1.out`, `m8_2.out`; every gate list replayed exactly] The one run that finished properly on the
rented machine: M8 as one letter, gate count as objective, 40 s per design, 23 different designs. e2-0's own trees and
signs: **18**, and the solver reports that 18 is the optimum for that design. The 22 others (other choices of which
half each half character lives on, other signs of the whole characters, other pairings and centres): 18 once, 19 seven
times, 20 six times, 21 five times, 22 three times; four of them reported optimal (18, 19, 19, 20), the rest stopped at
the time limit. **Nothing below 18.**
So in this shape two letters give 2 x 18 + 20 = 56 at 16 points: the copied 52 again, by a second route.
Limits: the output tree was my clustering, not searched; 23 designs; a float solver's "optimal".

## 8. Mutations ("yes, and")

1. The solver would not run. Yes, and: count letters exactly instead of rectangles approximately. That gave the table.
2. A wider middle (copies) made two letters out of the 52 but cost 4 gates. Yes, and: the width that matters is not
   "N plus a few" but "two wires per cell"; with bigger blocks that needs no extra width at all (r = 2 above).
3. Two letters reduce to one letter at sqrt(N). Yes, and: then the two-letter record at 256 points is decided by ONE
   number, the one-letter count at 16 points (63 today, 50 needed to tie). That is p-1's and p-8's number.
4. "Letters" measure how many times a merged value is merged again. Yes, and: ask for T_3. Three letters, sizes
   4 x 4 x 4 at 64 points, is 3 x 16 x 7 = 336 by bricks; e2-0 has 304 with 5; is there a 3-letter list under 304?
5. Lemma 3 says the last step consumes half-size characters. Yes, and: by transposition the first step produces them.
   A circuit is "N/2 half characters in, N/2 half characters out"; the count of the middle is what is unknown.

## 9. The wall

**Inside a letter, a merge is used by one tree.** Every saving beyond two letters that I can account for is a merge
shared by two trees (4 of them at 16 points, exactly the 4 gates between 56 and 52), and sharing costs a letter. With two
letters and one wire per cell the cell lemma turns the problem into one letter at the square-root size, where the only
known numbers are 7, 19 and 63. With a bounded number of letters this is still N log N with a worse constant than the
family; nothing here bends the order. What would: a one-letter count that stays linear (p-8's question), or cells with
several wires whose staircases share steps at every scale at once.

## 10. Criticism, last

- Lemmas 1 to 3 and the "one letter with a dense table" identity are derived by hand in the hour. No program checked them.
  Lemma 2's consequence for rectangle counts leans on "given its row-constant term free", which I did not quantify beyond 4 points.
- The letter counts are for the gate lists AS WRITTEN. Another list with the same number of gates may need fewer letters.
  "Not two letters" is proved only for the 52 (by hand); for 128 and 304 the program stopped at its node limit.
- The table's two-letter column is upper bounds by tensor cuts. I ran no search over two-letter products; experiment (b),
  (c), (d) of the brief were not carried out as written.
- The row for 128 and 256 points uses k-cross's solver figure 63 and e2-0's formula; I replayed neither.
- My definition of a letter lets wires pass through a letter for free and lets a wire be copied for free. Both are
  right in count (G); a stricter definition would give more letters, not fewer.
- "M8 needs 18 gates as one letter" is 23 designs of a float solver with a fixed, clustered output tree. It is not
  a minimum over trees. An exact search over all trees on 8 leaves (p-8-fibonacci has a tool for that) would settle it.
- "Each of the four gates is a shared merge" compares the 52 with ONE two-letter rewriting of it (56). The best
  two-letter list at 16 points is 54, so the honest gap between two letters and three is 2 gates, not 4.
- I read no literature tonight. Whether "letters" is a known depth measure for linear circuits (it resembles the
  number of alternations between fan-in and fan-out phases) I do not know.
