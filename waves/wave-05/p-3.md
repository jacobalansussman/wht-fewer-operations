   blocks. g-6's "12 available" counted 4 exceptions of the other kind (kind "u"), and those never help: when one is used, the
   block's outgoing direction is itself a character, which pins the coset the good columns must lie in and takes back the column
   it gave. [derived; the count checked exhaustively by machine: all distributions of wires, all trees, all matchings]
2. **The tie moves to 16 x 2**: every tree on 16 leaves needs 8 exceptions of kind q per block, 16 are available, 2 blocks. Tight again.
3. **16 x 2 is empty too**, by one more equation: tightness forces the first 4-leaf node of the tree to be a plane whose four
   wires sit in four cells with the SAME character on that plane, and then the four rows of the first step on that plane span

   the rank bound run on the skeptic's explicit 16-leaf candidate: rank at most 2 on its plane]
4. **So, under (P) alone (with 2 <= w <= N/2, as c-2 rightly adds), two tree steps make the transform at 16 points and below

## 1. Words

N = 2^k points, H the transform with entries (-1)^(x.y). *Tree*, *node*, *tree step* as in g-6: a matrix A with a tree on
its rows and a matching of rows to columns (the columns are the *middle wires*) such that for every node R, with R' its
matched wires, the block A[R, wires outside R'] has rank at most 1 and so has A[rows outside R, R'].
*Two-step word*: H = A.B, A a tree step on the outputs, B a tree step on the inputs.
*(P)*, the periodic shape: for a subgroup W of w elements, the cosets of W are nodes of the first tree and the cosets of the
perpendicular subgroup (c = N/w elements each) are nodes of the second. "Shape w x c".
*(G)*, grid: every block of the first kind meets every block of the second kind in exactly one middle wire.
*Block xi*: a coset of W with its w wires S'. *Block eta*: a coset of the perpendicular with its c wires U'.
*Cell* (xi, eta): the wires common to block xi and block eta. Under (G) every cell holds one wire; in general 0, 1, 2, ...
chi_eta: the character of W belonging to eta (a column of H restricted to a W-coset); chi_xi likewise on the other side.
On the block (xi, eta) the transform is the rank-one matrix chi_eta x chi_xi (g-6).
u_xi: the one column direction of A[block xi, wires outside the block]. q_eta: the one row direction of
B[wires outside block eta, block eta]. rho(R): the dimension of the smallest affine subspace containing R.

## 2. The rule for a cell, with (G) dropped [derived]

Split the product over the wires. For a node R inside block xi (R = the whole block included) and block eta:

    H[R, block eta] = sum over wires z in R' and in block eta of (column z of A on R) x (row z of B on block eta)
                      + c_R x (something) + (something) x q_eta,

where c_R is the one direction of A[R, wires outside R'] (for the whole block, c_R = u_xi). This is g-6's block law with the
single wire replaced by a sum over the cell.

**Exceptional cells.** Call the cell (xi, eta) exceptional if u_xi is proportional to chi_eta (kind u) or q_eta is proportional
to chi_xi (kind q). A vector is proportional to at most one character, so there is **at most one kind-u cell per xi and at most
one kind-q cell per eta: at most c of kind u and at most w of kind q in the whole word.**
In a cell that is not exceptional, reduce modulo u_xi on the left and q_eta on the right: chi_eta x chi_xi does not vanish, so
- the cell is not empty (g-6: "an empty cell is an exceptional pair"), and
- **chi_eta lies in the span of the cell's columns of A and u_xi.**

**The rule at a node R.** If a non-exceptional cell has NO wire in R', all its columns restricted to R are multiples of c_R,
and so is u_xi restricted to R; hence chi_eta restricted to R is a multiple of c_R. Characters proportional on R differ by an
element perpendicular to R - R. So the non-exceptional cells with no wire in R' lie in ONE coset of (R - R)-perp, which has
w / 2^rho(R) elements. And if u_xi is itself a character chi_e (the only case in which the block has a kind-u cell), that coset
is the coset of e, and e is exceptional, so it holds at most w / 2^rho(R) - 1 such cells.

**The node inequality.** The w cells of a block are: exceptional ones; ones with a wire in R' (at most |R|); and
non-exceptional ones with no wire in R'. With n_q = the number of kind-q cells of the block:

    w  <=  w / 2^rho(R)  +  n_q  +  |R|        for every node R inside the block.

The kind-u cell does not appear: it adds 1 to the exceptional cells and removes 1 from the coset. **Kind u never helps.**
(g-6's text states the pinned coset; `emin8.py` implemented the weaker "pairwise proportional" rule and so allowed 3 arbitrary
exceptional columns per block, 12 = 4 + 8 in all.)

**Lemma F (one-sided).** [derived] Nothing above used the inside of B (by transposing the word, the same holds with the
two factors exchanged). Let H = A.B with A a tree step whose tree has the
cosets of W as nodes and B ANY invertible matrix for which each coset of W-perp has c wires outside which its column block
has rank at most 1. Then for every block and every node R inside it, n_q >= w - |R| - w / 2^rho(R), and the n_q of all c
blocks add up to at most w. With blocks of rank r in B instead of 1, the n_q add up to at most r w.

## 3. 8 x 4: closed

For w = 8 the inequality reads n_q >= 2 for a pair, 3 for a 3-leaf node, 2 for a 4-leaf plane, 3 for a 4-leaf node that is
not a plane. A tree on 8 leaves has a node with 3 or 4 leaves whose children have at most 2. So with n_q = 2 that node is a
plane made of two pairs R1, R2 of the same direction d, and the pair inequality is tight for both: the two kind-q cells and
the (at most two) cells holding the wires of R1' fill one coset of d-perp, and the same for R2' with the same coset (the
kind-q cells are in both). So R1' and R2' sit in the same two cells, the plane's four wires touch only 2 cells, and the plane's
own inequality fails: 8 <= 2 + 2 + 2. **Every block of 8 needs n_q >= 3.** [derived]
Four blocks need 12; the word has at most 8. **No two-step word of shape 8 x 4 (or, transposed, 4 x 8), with any meeting
pattern of the blocks, any trees, any matchings, any scalings.**

[ran, `cells8.py`, `cells8.out`, 18 s, exhaustive over all distributions of the 8 wires over the 8 cells, all trees, all matchings]
| exceptional cells of the block | result |
|---|---|
| calibration: grid, weak rule, 3 arbitrary exceptional columns / 2 | feasible / none: agrees with g-6's `emin8.out` |
| 2 of kind q, u not a character (weak rule) | **none** (36 distributions) |
| 2 of kind q, 1 of kind u (pinned coset) | **none** (120 distributions); grid only: none |
| 3 of kind q, with or without kind u | feasible (caterpillar trees; without (G) one cell takes 3 or 4 wires) |
| 4 of kind q (what 8 x 2 at 16 points can afford) | feasible |

Which equation kills g-6's fully specified hatch ("one E1 column and two E2 columns per block, caterpillar"): at the 3-leaf
node of the caterpillar, the two good columns outside must lie in the 2-element coset of the E1 column's own character; one
of the two places is the E1 column itself. 5 columns outside, room for 3 + 1.

## 4. 16 x 2: tight, then closed

For w = 16: a 3-leaf node needs n_q >= 9, a 4-leaf plane 8, a 4-leaf non-plane 10. Two blocks, 16 available: **n_q = 8 in
both blocks, exactly tight**; every q_eta is a character, f(eta) = the block in which eta's kind-q cell lies, 8 and 8.
Tightness at the first plane P = R1 + R2 (direction d; L = the 4 cells perpendicular to P - P):
- the 4 wires of P' lie in 4 different non-exceptional cells Y, and Y is exactly one coset of L; the non-exceptional cells
  with no wire in P' form the other coset of L inside the same coset K of d-perp (minus e if u is a character);
- so all leaf pairs of the block have direction d, and the non-exceptional cells of the two blocks are the two cosets of d-perp.
[ran, `planes16.py`, exhaustive over all sets of 8 (and 7) kind-q cells and all placements of the plane's wires: with 8,
24 solutions (weak) and 6 (pinned), all with exactly this structure; with 7, none.]

**The equation that kills it.** [derived] Because c = 2 and q_eta is the character chi_f(eta), the combination of the two
columns of H in block eta that kills q_eta is, on one side, a combination of the columns of A at the two wires of block eta,
and on the other side it is "chi_eta on the block xi = f(eta) + 1, zero on the other block":

    2 * (chi_eta on block f(eta)+1, zero on the other block)  =  beta * (column z of A) + beta' * (column z' of A),
                                                                  {z, z'} = the two wires of block eta.
(checked as C5 on the 16-point words of section 6, 71 combinations)

Take eta in Y, z its wire in P', z' the partner. z' is not in P' (four different cells), so column z' restricted to P is a
multiple of c_P, and c_P is proportional to the common character of the cells with no wire in P'. beta = 0 is impossible
(it would make chi_eta on P a multiple of c_P, and Y is the other coset). So column z of A restricted to P lies in the span
of (the character of Y on P) and c_P: the same 2-dimensional space for all four wires of P'. Every other column of A,
restricted to P, is a multiple of c_P. **The four rows of A on P have rank at most 2.** A is invertible. Contradiction.
So **no two-step word of shape 16 x 2 or 2 x 16 either, with or without (G).**
Under (G) the span identity is not even needed: the four wires of P' are then good columns in g-6's sense, lambda chi_eta + s u,
with the same character on P, and u on P is a multiple of c_P. In general this is a second rule, which neither g-6 nor c-2 nor
`cells8.py` uses: **the inside rule: at every node R, the columns of R' restricted to R, together with c_R, must span all of
R.** Good columns contribute only as many dimensions as their cells have different characters on R.
Under (G) the tight structure dies even earlier, at a leaf pair: all good columns of the block have the same character on a
pair of direction d, so a pair whose two wires are good columns has two proportional rows.
[ran, `inside8.py`, second half] On the skeptic's explicit 16-leaf tree (`c-2/c2_e16.py`, 8 exceptional columns, passes the
outside rule at every node): the rows of the step have rank at most 1 on each of its four leaf pairs, at most 2 on its plane,
3 on its 6-leaf node, 5 on its 8-leaf node. Not invertible.
[ran, `inside8.py`, first half, exhaustive] The inside rule is not a universal killer: for a block of 8 under (G) with 3, 4, 5
or 6 kind-q cells (eleven cases up to symmetry, with and without a kind-u cell) a tree passing both rules exists each time.
So 8 x 2 at 16 points under (G) stays "not excluded" by counting.

## 5. The table again, and the growth law

Need per block: 0 for w <= 4; 3 for w = 8 [ran]; at least 3w/4 - 4 for w >= 16 [derived]. Available: w in all.

| shape | needed (kind q) | available | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 x 4 (16 points) | 0 | 4 | the brick word |
| 8 x 2 (16 points) | 2 x 3 | 8 | not excluded; the brick word read this way uses 4 + 4, without (G) [ran] |
| 8 x 4, 4 x 8 (32 points) | 4 x 3 = 12 | 8 | **empty** |
| 16 x 2, 2 x 16 (32 points) | 2 x 8 = 16 | 16 | tight, then **empty** by the rank of the plane rows |
| every shape at 64 points and beyond | c (3w/4 - 4) or 3c | w | empty (w >= 16 forces c = 1; w = 8 forces c <= 2; transposed: the same for c) |

**N(2) = 16 under (P).** Let N(s) be the largest size s tree steps can make. In the periodic class the first term is 16, as
the brick law 4^s says. The brief's hope (N(2) = 32 breaking the pattern) is refuted for periodic shapes. What is NOT decided:
N(2) over all tree pairs (section 7), and N(3), where this method gives nothing: with a dense middle step the condition "the
cell has a wire in R'" becomes "the middle step connects R' to the block", which a tree step does for every pair [derived, in
agreement with g-6 section 5].
If B's blocks have rank r and A is still a rank-one step, Lemma F gives c <= r / (3/4 - 4/w) [derived]. With both steps of
rank r the node inequality changes (nodes of at most r leaves become free) and I did not work it out; my rough estimate is
that two wide steps reach N of order r^2, which is the trivial split H_N = (H_sqrtN x I)(I x H_sqrtN) [guess]. No gain in
order from widening is visible.

## 7. What is left at 32 points, and a tool for it

**The quadric pair one level down.** [ran, `quad2.py`, `quad3.py`; ranks modulo 2^31 - 1 for rejections (a lower bound, so
rigorous), exact fractions for acceptances] S = {x0 x1 + x2 = 0}, U = {y1 y3 + y4 = 0}, nu(S, U) = 4 as g-6 found.
g-6's Lemma A must hold for the children too:
- 8-point subsets S1 of S with nu(S1, U) <= 4: 874 of 12,870. Splits S = S1 + S2 with both halves passing: **13** of 6,435.
  The same numbers for U against S.
- The 13: three splits into two 8-point cosets (stabilisers 8 and 8) of two DIFFERENT subgroups (for example x0 = 0 gives
  {x0 = 0, x2 = 0} and x0 = 1 gives {x0 = 1, x2 = x1}), and ten splits into halves with a stabiliser of 2 elements.
- Pairs (split of S, split of U) in which all four 8 x 8 node pairs have nu <= 4: **69 of 169**.
So Lemma A does not kill the quadric top at the second level. It names 69 candidates (times the same for the complements).
This answers the brief's last question in part: the shape between "periodic" and "quadric" is **piecewise periodic**, every
node a coset but of a subgroup that changes from branch to branch; the quadric is its smallest member. Whether its margin
grows was not measured.

**The cut inequality.** [derived, not run] For any node R of the first tree and U of the second,
H[R, U] minus c_R x (something) minus (something) x q_U has rank at most |R' and U' in common|, with c_R depending on R only
and q_U on U only. Cut both trees anywhere (a partition of the outputs into nodes R_i, of the inputs into nodes U_j):

    sum over i, j of rank H[R_i, U_j]   <=   N  +  #{(i, j): c_i lies in the column space of H[R_i, U_j]}
                                                +  #{(i, j): q_j lies in the row space of H[R_i, U_j]}.

The 2 x 2 cut is Lemma A (N + 2 nu <= N + 4). Blocks against blocks plus the nodes inside one block is sections 3 and 4.
It bites where blocks have low rank, because a direction then lies in few column spaces; for sets in general position at 32
points it says nothing. On piecewise periodic trees the blocks have rank 1 or 2 and it should bite.

   the complements), counting kind-q and kind-u cells with the subgroup allowed to change per branch. If the count is tight

   First count [derived, by hand, not run]: split S by x0. The half x0 = 0 gives two cosets of W1 = {x0 = 0, x2 = 0}; the

6. **The inside rule as a search filter** (section 4): add it to `cells8.py` and to c-2's explicit 16-leaf tree, and ask
   again which trees survive at 8 x 2 (16 points), where a word is known only without (G).
2. **A census instead of one pair.** All pairs (S, U) of 16-point sets at 32 points with nu <= 4 up to affine maps (g-6 has
   the periodic ones, one quadric pair and one descent with stabilisers 4 and 2), then `quad3.py` under each. Finite.
3. **Lemma F as a peeling test for three steps.** If the first of three steps has a W-coset level with w >= 16, the product
   of the other two must have rank at least c (3/4 - 4/w) on average across the perpendicular cosets, outside any c wires
   (2 of 4 for w = 16, c = 4). The three brick layers at 64 points have 3 of 4 there [derived, not run]. Which pairs of tree
   steps can have that much rank across a foreign partition is a question about the last two steps only.
4. **Limits.** The count uses "at most one character per direction" and invertibility; neither survives a limit of words
   with growing terms (g-6 section 6). A limit word of shape 8 x 4 is not excluded by this report.
5. **Kind u can be made to help only by leaving rank one**: a block whose outgoing space has dimension r may contain r
   characters, but the pinned coset then becomes "the cells whose characters on R lie in an r-dimensional space". Worth one
   exact count at r = 2, w = 16, where pairs become free.

## 9. Criticism, last

- Sections 2 to 4 are hand derivations made inside one hour. Machine support: the combinatorial counts (`cells8.py`,
  `planes16.py`, exhaustive given the rule) and the identities on five words at 16 points (`validate.py`). The last step of
  section 4 (rank at most 2 on the plane) has no instance to be tested on and is three lines by hand.

  (it is tight only in blocks of 4).
- "At most one kind-q cell per eta" needs q_eta to be one direction, that is, hypothesis (P) for single cosets. Trees whose
  nodes are unions of cosets but never single cosets are not covered, and nothing non-periodic is.
- The 16-leaf count was done by the inequality, not by an exhaustive tree search (601 million pairs of subsets); only the
  bottom plane was enumerated.
- Section 7's numbers use Lemma A only, for one quadric pair, one level, the S and U sides (the complements are translates).
  The cut inequality was derived and not run. "Piecewise periodic" is a name for 3 of 13 splits, not a theory.
- The rank-r remark in section 5 is rough.
- I did not run a solver at 32 points. The decision is by counting and one rank argument; a referee should attack section 2's
  "kind u never helps" and section 4's span identity first.
