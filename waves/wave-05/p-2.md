# p-2 mirror64class: the mirror route on subset sums. The obstruction is gone, the count is not: 5/4 of Yates at every size reached; and everything except one table is linear

2026-10-11, 00:00 to about 00:50 EDT, 60-minute box. Private research: nothing published, no git, no Lean, nothing reviewed.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `p-2-mirror64class/`), [ran, search] = a heuristic search whose result was
replayed exactly (an upper bound, not a minimum), [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by nobody, [read] = from a report named,
[guess]. Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free. Nothing here is in count (O).

Words. N = 4^m points; an address is m *digits*, each 0, 1, 2 or 3 (two address bits). *Subset-sum kernel*: K = [[1,1],[1,0]];
its power on 2m bits is the subset-sum matrix Z up to relabelling. *Yates*: the textbook for subset sums, (N/2) log2 N gates
(4, 32, 192, 1024 at 4, 16, 64, 256 points). *R*: K (x) K times the relabelling-with-signs of k-cross hybrid 3; R R = I.
*Mirror matrix*: I - R^(x)m, twice the projection onto the *mirror*, a space of dimension r = (N - sqrt N)/2 (1, 6, 28, 120).
*Read point* of an input: its column of the mirror matrix, up to scale. *Write point* of an output: its row. An entry is zero
exactly when that write point is *perpendicular* to that read point. *Secant*: the line through two read points.
*Reduced model* (g-0): start from r points, a gate adds a point on the line through two points already there, all read points
must appear; points used that are not read points are *helpers*, x of them. *Three boxes*: read the mirror coordinates,
apply a small *middle* table, write back, add the input.

## 1. The object in one digit [derived; the runs agree]

K (x) K on addresses 0..3 times P (exchange addresses 0 and 3 with sign -1, det K = -1) is R = I - u w^T, u = (1,1,1,0),
w = (0,1,1,-1), w.u = 2. As a program: s = x1 + x2 - x3 (2 gates), then x0 - s, x1 - s, x2 - s (3 gates), x3 untouched:
**5 gates against Yates's 4.** R fixes e0 and sends eps_j to eps_j - u for eps_1 = e1, eps_2 = e2, eps_3 = -e3.
Consequences at m digits, all seen in `struct.out` for m = 1, 2, 3:
- a digit 0 in an input passes through, so the read point of an input is "e0 on its zero digits, the full point q_J on the rest",
  q_J = eps_J - v_J (tensor products, v_j = eps_j - u); for one non-zero digit q = u whatever the digit: three inputs, one point;
- on the write side digit 3 plays the part of digit 0; the map tau(x0,x1,x2,x3) = (-x3, x1, x2, -x0) carries the read
  configuration to the write configuration, so every count below is the same on both sides [derived; the machine counts agree];
- non-zeros of the mirror matrix: 9, 89, 777; perpendicular pairs among non-zero rows and columns: 0 of 9, 136 of 225,
  3,192 of 3,969 [ran].

Cost formula used [derived, extends g-0's to points that vanish or coincide]: read = N' + D - 2r + x, write = D' - r + x',
final additions N'' (N', N'' = non-zero columns, rows; D, D' = distinct points). Checked on two cases where the forward circuit
can be counted by hand: 4 points (2 gates for s) and the 7-coordinate frame at 16 points (14 gates: 2 + 2 for the two
one-digit sums, 6 for three column sums and 4 for two row sums of the 3 x 3 array of inputs with both digits non-zero).

## 2. First experiment, 16 points [ran: `mir.py search16`, `mid16.py`; outputs `search16_zeta.out`, `mid16_zeta.out`]

(a) 15 non-zero columns, 11 distinct read points in dimension 6: A = e0 (x) u (inputs 1, 2, 3), B = u (x) e0 (inputs 4, 8, 12)
and a 3 x 3 grid p_jj' = eps_j (x) u + u (x) eps_j' - u (x) u. No three on a line. Eleven four-term relations: nine
"rectangles" of the grid, and A + B + p12 + p21 = 0, A + B + p11 + p22 = 0. Points on two or more secants (the pool): 19,
of which 7 on three secants. Write side: the same numbers. The table of perpendicular pairs is printed in `search16_zeta.out`.
(b) Helpers. x = 0, 1, 2: none (1, 19, 171 helper sets, every start set). x = 3: 50,280 (helper set, start set) pairs.
Why the pool suffices for the lower bound [derived]: no three targets are on a line, so every useful line holds a helper;
a helper lies on at most 3 secants; with two helpers at most 3 + 3 + 1 gates are available and 7 are needed, with equality only
if both lie on three secants, and those are in the pool. So **x = 3 for helpers that are any rational points**.
Read = 15 + 11 - 12 + 3 = **17** (floor 14), write = 11 - 6 + 3 = **8** (floor 5).
Middle [ran, all 138.9 million pairs of the 11,786 distinct start sets on each side]: never free; least one row with two
non-zeros, 7 non-zeros in all: **1 gate**. Histogram of "rows needing a gate": 1: 2,620 pairs; 2: 143,436; 3: 2.7 million;
4: 21 million; 5: 64 million; 6: 51 million.
**Three boxes: 15 + 17 + 1 + 8 = 41.** Yates 32. k-cross's rough floor 36 was too kind: the floor is 34 and the truth 41.
The 4-point route used on each digit in turn is 8 blocks x 5 = 40, one better than the three boxes.
(c) is section 3. (d) Other kernels [ran, `struct.out`]:
- [[0,1],[1,1]] (the zero in the other diagonal place): every number identical to the subset-sum kernel. It is its mirror image.
- [[1,1],[1,2]]: nothing vanishes or coincides (4, 16, 64 distinct points), floors 7, 46, 172 as for the transform; no zero
  entry at 4 and 64 points, and **four zero entries at 16 points, all on the diagonal** (addresses 0, 3, 12, 15): a small
  exception to k-cross's "a zero needs a zero entry in the kernel". No two different addresses are perpendicular.
  I did not run the helper search for it; g-0's 24 and 56 presumably carry over [guess].
- A zero off the diagonal ([[1,1],[0,1]]): k-cross's relabelling needs b*c non-zero, so that mirror does not exist; instead
  K diag(1,-1) = [[1,-1],[0,-1]] is already a reflection at 2 points, with a mirror of dimension N/2 [derived, not run].

## 3. 64 points [ran on the Linux machine: `m64.py`, `pool.py`, `m64c.py`; outputs `m64_*.out`, `c64_*.out`]

57 distinct read points, r = 28, 29 + x gates in the reduced model. No three on a line. **Only 33 four-term relations and 57
points on two secants: 3 x 11 and 3 x 19, the three embedded 16-point copies and nothing else.** So the 27 points with three
non-zero digits lie on no line with a pool point at all: with the pool of g-0's kind the search cannot even reach them.
Their structure [ran, `type3.py`, exact]: q_J - q_J' = (eps_a - eps_a') (x) q_rest when J and J' differ in one digit; so the
difference of two mirror points along a digit is a fixed direction times a mirror point of the next smaller size. The 27
span 19 dimensions; the relations among them are the mixed differences, a product of three "sum zero" codes of length 3, so
the shortest has exactly 2 x 2 x 2 = 8 terms [derived], and 2^m = sqrt N at m digits. From 19 start points they are reached in 30
gates with 22 helpers (two copies of the 9-point grid, and four product points).
Search [ran, search]: pool of 637 structured candidates (`pool.py`: every plus-direction times the smaller minus pool, u
times the smaller plus pool, at every digit position; all checked to lie in the mirror), 694 points, 2,582 lines; start set =
28 independent read points; add one helper at a time, then prune. Calibrated at 16 points (finds 3). At 64 points:
92, 87, 81, 77, 75, 71, 70, 67, 66, **63 (replayed exactly: start set of rank 28, every one of the 92 gates a collinearity;
`c64_s3.out`; 41 tries in four runs, 2 of them failed to reach all points)**. Hand scheme about 53 [derived, not replayed]. No lower bound beyond x >= 1 except this
[derived]: at least 8 of the 27 full points are gate outputs, and the helper on such a gate's line lies on no second secant,
so the sharing that makes 16 points cheap (one helper on three secants) is not available for them.
Count with x = x' = 63: 63 + (64 + 63) + (29 + 63) + middle = **282 + middle**, 1.47 of Yates before the middle.
The digit-by-digit route is 48 blocks x 5 = 240. To tie it the three boxes need x + x' + middle = 84; to tie Yates, 36.

## 5. "Yes, and": allow more coordinates than the dimension [derived from replayed pieces; `type3.py`, `mid.py`, `lift16.py`]

The three boxes insist on r independent start points, and the read points of different "types" (which digits are zero) are
dependent on each other (taken type by type they need 7 and 37 start points where the mirror has dimension 6 and 28); part of
the helpers pay for that. Drop the insistence. Read each type by itself:
type with n non-zero digits, 3^n - 2^n numbers, by the scheme of section 3. Then
- read = 2N - 2 * 3^m (14, 74, 350), write = 2N - 3 * 3^m + 1 (6, 48, 270), final additions N - 1: **5 (N - 3^m) in all**;
- the middle is a table of side 4^m - 3^m (7, 37, 175) and rank r (6, 28 [ran]; 120 not run), entry = (write frame point) .
  (read frame point) / 2. Its block (write type S', read type S) is zero unless S and S' together cover all digits and share
  one: 5 of 9, 19 of 49, 65 of 225 blocks, that is 3^m - 2^m [ran: the counts, and that every non-zero block covers;
  derived from the counts: the covering blocks that vanish are the complementary pairs].
- its non-zeros: 22, 296, 3,292, per row 3.1, 8.0, 18.8, a factor near 9/4 per digit: as dense as subset sums itself.
  Greedy pair sharing (exact, no cancellation): 10, 121, 971 gates. Lower bracket (one gate per distinct row with two or more
  non-zeros): 5, 34, 171. Two other frame choices give the same picture (`mid_b2.out`, `mid_b3.out`).
- 16 points, frames whose start points are read points plus two pool points (the least possible; helpers only as start
  points) [ran, `lift16.out`]: 7 coordinates, all 306 x 306 pairs: read 14, write 6, middle of the pair with the best lower
  end between 4 and 6: **total 39 to 41**; 8 coordinates, a sample of 1,200 x 1,200 of the 2,366 x 2,366 pairs: read 12,
  write 5, middle 6 to 10: **38 to 42**. For the 7-coordinate pair printed there the middle is exactly 6 (two rows with three
  non-zeros, two with two, and no wire can serve two of them) [derived]: 41. Other pairs are not settled.
Reading: the helpers of the three boxes and the gates of the middle are the same cost in two places. At 16 points
(read + write + middle) is 26 for the three boxes, 25 digit by digit, 24..26 and 23..27 for the redundant frames.

## 6. The question that came out, and the one I was told to keep asking

**Better question.** Subset sums at N = 4^m points = 5 (N - 3^m) gates + one table of side N - 3^m and rank (N - sqrt N)/2,
whose rows and columns are indexed by two frames one may choose. Linear size for subset sums (and then, by H = Z D Z^T
[read: g-4], for the transform at twice the cost) is exactly: **frames, each generated in linear size, with a middle of
linear cost.** The frames I built give a middle as dense as the problem. What is not known: whether the middle, for the best
frames, is again a kernel power in disguise (then nothing is gained), or something with a shorter description. Its block
pattern (non-zero exactly on pairs of digit sets that cover and meet) is a statement about two set systems, not about lines.

**Kept question.** Which member has the most perpendicular pairs? Only kernels with a zero entry have any between different
addresses, and up to relabelling there is one such member, subset sums; the count is "pairs not in the subset relation",
corrected on the diagonal: N^2 - 3^k - N + 2 sqrt N zero entries [derived; 7, 167, 3,319 ran]. Can the count be carried through the three-call word Z = D H D H D H D [read:
g-4]? No: the mirror matrix of H has no zero at 16 and 64 points [ran], so all of it is lost at the first call. In the other
direction (H = Z D Z^T) nothing needs carrying: a count for Z is a count for H at twice the count; a mirror route for Z at
rho times Yates gives the transform at rho N log2 N, and the best known is 3/4. The floor allows rho < 3/4 only from 256
points on; everything measured is rho >= 1.25.

## 7. Regularities and anomalies to keep

1. **The mirror route never beat its own 4-point brick.** Transform at 16 points: floor 56 = 8 x 7 (g-0). Subset sums at 16
   points: 41 against 8 x 5 = 40. Two members, same law, the second by exhaustive count. [guess: a theorem, "three boxes at
   4^m points cost at least m 4^(m-1) times the 4-point route"; it would settle the route for the whole class.]
2. Total minus floor is conserved across models at 16 points: 7 (three boxes), 6 (digit by digit), 5 to 7 and 4 to 8
   (redundant frames with 7 and 8 coordinates). Cost moves between helpers and middle and does not leave.
3. sqrt N comes back: the full points' shortest relation has 2^m terms although the kernel has a zero. The zero entry
   removes g-0's obstruction in the middle and leaves the one in the helpers.
4. One helper per secant at 64 points against one per three secants at 16.
5. Per type the cost is linear with constant 2 (2 * 3^n - 3 * 2^n gates for 3^n points).
6. Complementary types pair to zero in the middle.

## 9. Criticism, last

- Nothing here is below a known count and no growth law is below N log N. On subset sums the mirror route is a worse constant
  than Yates at every size where I have a circuit, and Yates itself was never in danger at 16 points (floor 34 > 32).
- The brief's premise "the mirror gets sparse fast" is true as a fraction and false for the purpose: (9/4)^m non-zeros per row.
- "41" is exact for helpers from the pool and minimal helper counts; a start set with a helper outside the pool and x = 3 is
  not excluded by my argument (only x <= 2 is).
- The 64-point helper counts are upper bounds from a weak greedy on a loaded machine; I have no lower bound; the hand scheme
  (53) was not replayed. The write side at 64 points was not searched (same configuration by tau [derived]).
- Section 5's circuit was not assembled and replayed end to end; its three parts were counted separately (type schemes replayed
  exactly, the middle as an exact table, the cost formula checked on two small cases). The middle's cost is a bracket.
- The cost formula with vanishing and coinciding points is mine tonight.
- I read no literature. Whether "subset sums = 5 (N - 3^m) + a middle table" is a known decomposition I do not know.
