**Verdict: the claim holds at 16 points. It changes one entry of a table of upper bounds. It does not change the order,
and it does not carry to 32 points by the obvious route.**

Words. *The transform*: the 16 x 16 Walsh-Hadamard matrix H. *Merge*: a gate that adds two sums of disjoint sets of
wires. *Nested family*: sets of which any two are either disjoint or one inside the other (the nodes of a tree).
*Rectangle*: a set of rows times a set of columns with one number on it. *A letter*: a matrix that, after rescaling its
rows once and its columns once, is a sum of rectangles whose row sets form one nested family and whose column sets
form another. As a circuit: merges going up, then additions going down, each down gate continuing at most one earlier
down gate (its *stream*). *Middle wires*: the outputs of the first letter, which are the inputs of the second.
*Character*: a row of the transform; *half-character*: a row restricted to x0..x7 or to x8..x15.

## 1. The claim, exactly

For the gate list `h-1-staircase3/slp16_52_from54.txt` (52 gates, 16 points):
(a) there is an assignment of every gate to (letter 1 or 2, up or down) that satisfies the rules coded in
`p-7-twoletters/letters.py`, with 30 gates in letter 1 and 22 in letter 2 and 19 middle wires;
(b) there is no such assignment with one letter;
(c) hence the fewest gates of a two-letter circuit at 16 points is at most 52, where p-7's table shows 54.

## 2. My own check  [ran, `r-0/check.py`, output `r-0/main.out`]

I did not reuse p-7's program. Mine has its own parser, replay and search, and one thing p-7's does not have: a
**certificate that does not trust the search**. From an assignment it builds each letter as an explicit matrix,
(row scalings) x (sum of rectangles) x (column scalings), checks that the row sets and the column sets are nested
families, checks each letter against the exact replay, and multiplies the letters.

- Replay: all 16 rows of the transform are wires. 52 gates.
- One letter: none, search exhaustive, 1,983 nodes (the same node count as q-cross's run).
- Two letters: found. **Letter 1 is a 19 x 16 matrix, 30 gates (14 up, 16 down), 28 rectangles. Letter 2 is a 16 x 19
  matrix, 22 gates (10 up, 12 down), 25 rectangles. Product of the two matrices = H_16 exactly.**
- Same tool on the calibration cases: g-1's 19 is one letter; e2-0's 20 and h-8's 21 at 8 points are two, not one.

So (a), (b), (c) are true. Parts (a) and (b) also have short proofs by hand.

**Why not one letter** [derived, 5 lines]. h12 = h11 - 2 x15 cannot be a merge (h11 already contains x15, or h11 is a
down gate), so it is a down gate; then h14 = h12 + 2 x9 is a down gate. In the same way h20 = h17 + 2 x6 and
h22 = -h20 + 2 x0 are down gates. h31 = h22 + h14 adds two down gates. A down gate may continue only one. End.

**The two letters by hand** [derived from the printed assignment, `main.out`]. Letter 1 is everything that lives on one
half of the inputs: h1 to h28, h30, h45. Its merges are the pairs {i, i xor 7}, the fours {0,1,6,7}, {2,3,4,5},
{8,9,14,15}, {10,..,13} and the two halves: nested. Its 16 down gates each add 2 x (one input or one pair) to one
stream; the streams start at the two half centres h11 and h17. Letter 2 is the 22 gates that mix the halves. Its
merges are on the sets of middle wires {h24,h25}, {h14,h22}, {h21,h27}, {h26,h28}, {h18,h45}, each then enlarged by
one more wire (h13, h9, h16, h6, h5): ten sets, nested. Its 12 down gates have at most one down operand each.

## 3. The three attacks asked for

**Is the coded definition the one p-7's lemmas are about?** Yes for the matrix class. The certificate shows that an
assignment accepted by the code IS a rescaled sum of tree rectangles, which is p-7's definition in words. [ran]
But p-7's cell lemma (its Lemma 2) says nothing about this circuit, for a reason worth seeing: **the second letter has
almost no down tree.** Only three sets of outputs with more than one member exist in it ({y4,y12}, {y5,y13},
{y0,y8}); the other outputs hang alone, and five of them (y1, y2, y3, y6, y7) are merges read off directly. The cell
lemma speaks about blocks (output node) x (input node) where both trees are chains of subgroups. Here only 6 such
blocks exist. So the lemma is not contradicted; its hypothesis is simply not forced. [ran: the cell table in
`main.out`; p-7's Lemma 1, rank of a block at most 2 + wires in its cell, holds on all 60 pairs of nodes.]

**Does the free pass-through hide a third letter?** No. No input x skips letter 1: all 19 middle wires are made in
letter 1 (8 are merges that leave directly, 11 are down gates). What the tool prints as "pass-throughs" are rectangles
with a single row: a merge that is itself an output of the letter. That is a rectangle like any other. [ran]

**Fairness to p-7.** Its 54 was "the cheapest two-letter list I have", an upper bound, never a minimum. Its sentence
"the honest gap between two letters and three is 2 gates" overstated that bound, and q-cross is right to correct it.
q-cross's own "the gap is 0" has the same defect: it compares two upper bounds. The fair sentence is in section 7.

## 4. What the circuit actually does  [derived by reading the gate list; its vectors are printed in the file]

It is the cut "8 points twice, then the top address bit", with some work postponed.
- Each half builds g-1's centre (7 merges) and then only some of its 8 half-characters: 16 gates on the upper half,
  14 on the lower, where g-1's finished circuit costs 19 each. That is letter 1: 30 instead of 38.
- The mixing step costs 22 instead of 16. That is letter 2.
- The saving of 2 comes from two groups of four outputs, {y2, y4, y10, y12} and {y3, y5, y11, y13}. Each group is
  made from ONE half-character per half in 5 gates: hub = (lower half-character) + (upper half-character); two
  outputs = hub plus or minus 2 x (a merge of four inputs); two more by subtracting 2 x a half-character. The plain
  way needs four half-characters and four gates.
- It works when two characters differ, on a half, exactly on a merged set of four inputs, with the merge's signs equal
  to the character there. A merge has one sign pattern, so each of the four fours serves one group: two groups, two
  gates. The other eight outputs cost 3 gates a pair, no saving.

So the wall is the same one as everywhere tonight: **a node offers one form.** The saving is a bounded number of
gates per block, not a change of rate.

## 5. Does it scale?  [ran, `r-0/check.py push`, output `r-0/push.out`]

q-cross stopped at 16 points. I built two 32-point lists from the second 52 and replayed them exactly:
- pairs first, then the second 52 on the sums and on the differences: 136 gates;
- the second 52 on each half, pairs last: 136 gates.
Both: three letters found. Two letters: none found, but the search stopped at 400,000 nodes, **not a proof**.

tensoring, and the best two-letter list at 32 points stays p-7's 132 [read, not replayed by me].

Growth law observed, best two-letter lists: **7, 19, 52, 132, 304** gates at 4 to 64 points; divided by N log2 N:
0.875, 0.79, 0.81, 0.83, 0.79. Only the 16-point entry moved (54 to 52). Nothing here is below (3/4) N log2 N, and
p-7's reading for 128 points and beyond (two letters follow the one-letter count at the square-root size) is untouched.

## 6. Is it known?

The fact about this one gate list is new only inside tonight's folder. The structure is old under other names:
- A letter is a matrix with nested bases of rank one. In numerical analysis this is an H2-matrix (Hackbusch,
  Khoromskij and Sauter 2000 [recalled]; Hackbusch and Borm 2002 [read, abstract]): multiply by going up the column
  tree, applying the couplings, going down the row tree, in time linear in N plus the number of couplings. That is
  k-cross's "gates = N - 2 + rectangles" [derived comparison, mine]. The fast multipole method has the same shape.
- For Fourier-type matrices that theory is known NOT to give bounded rank; the structure used instead is the
  butterfly: every block (row node) x (column node) of area N has small rank, which for the transform is g-6's
  Theorem 1. It is factorised into about log N sparse layers: N log N operations (Li, Yang, Martin, Ho and Ying,
  "Butterfly factorization", arXiv:1502.01379, 2015 [read, abstract]).
- In complexity theory the nearest measure is the depth of a linear circuit. Alman, "Kronecker products, low-depth
  circuits, and matrix rigidity", arXiv:2102.11992, 2021 [read, abstract]: depth d gives size O(d N^(1 + 0.96/d)) for
  the transform in a count of wires with unbounded fan-in, not count (G).
What the known theory predicts: a bounded number of layers gives N log N or worse in every known construction, and no
lower bound excludes linear size for unrestricted circuits. Whether the number of letters of the transform has been
studied as such, I did not find in three searches.
