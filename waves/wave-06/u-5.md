has read this. **No circuit below any known count, no growth law below N log N. Nothing here changes the order.**
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (programs and outputs in `u-5-unit2adic/`, fractions and whole numbers only);
[read] = in a file I opened tonight; [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by nobody; [guess].
Gate count: **(G)** everywhere (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings and copies free). **Moves** = the engine's own unit (one
direction applied to one whole array); a move is 2^h gates (G) at one digit column.

Words (m-cross's, repeated). *Wire*: a value the program holds, as a vector over the inputs. *Primitive form*: the wire scaled so
that its entries have odd denominators and are not all even. *Class*: the primitive form modulo 2, a set of inputs. *Survivor*: a
gate whose class is new; *dead gate*: any other. *Kinds* by the classes of the two parents and the result: **J** three different
classes; **S** the result has a parent's class; **Q** the parents share a class and the result has another (the result is a
difference of two wires that agree modulo 2, divided by 2); **E** all equal. I split J into *J disjoint* (the parents' classes do
not meet: a merge), *J nested* (one inside the other; the result is the difference) and *J cross* (they overlap, neither inside
the other; the result is the symmetric difference, so something cancels modulo 2). *Order* of a dead gate: the largest m with
result = odd x (an earlier wire) modulo 2^m. *One merge tree modulo 2*: at most 2n - 1 classes, any two nested or disjoint.
*The unit* [read: h-7, R/README section 6]: 120 *sources* x, 120 *targets* y, 783 *helpers*; one *invocation* of its helper circuit
is what the certificate file describes; the unit is five invocations. *Label* (h-7 says frame): the subspace of the 9 address bits
along which an array has been transformed so far. *Port* p_t: the 9-bit word of pair t (120 of them, odd weight).

## 5. Where the Q gates are, and what they mean [ran + derived]

- **Moves down: 72 layers, 36,864 gates.** They are the scratch copies of the 8 totals coming from label dimension 9 to 0.
  8,176 of them are survivors: the classes (56 sources) x (coset of an intermediate label) are new. m-cross's control said a
  transform run backwards is all Q gates; here the backwards run is applied to a *mixture*, which is new.
- **Last additions: 120 layers, 61,440 gates.** After the scatter a target's row is x_t + y_t + (1/2)(+-56 sources): primitive
  form 2 x_t + 2 y_t + (odd mixture). Modulo 2 the wanted term is invisible. The helpers build the same odd mixture (in the
  end one helper holds what is left of it, a class of 8 to 19 sources, and the target holds the same class), and the last addition
  subtracts it: a difference of two wires equal modulo 2, and the factor 2 is the scaling that was free.
- [derived] As matrices on the 120 sources: the scatter is M = (1/2) P, P = 2M with entries 2 on the diagonal and 0, +-1 off it
  (rank 8; M M = 15 M [read: h-7]); phase B is A = P - 2 I, entries 0, +-1, full rank. **I = (P - A) / 2 and P = A modulo 2.**
  The engine pays moves for P (rank 8, 9 moves each: the 72) and none for A (additions between arrays at equal labels).
- In the *helpers full* reading these 120 additions are not Q gates: the helpers' own values sit in the classes and blur them.
  That reading has Q gates only in the moves down.
- m-cross's hybrid 3 found that a difference quotient of two OUTPUTS serves only those two. Here both parents are over-limit
  mixtures and the quotient is an output: the case that hybrid left open.

**The conflict.** [read: h-8 section 6] One unit makes 56,715 moves where the plain schedule makes 56,835; a-2's ledger wants
56,835 f n of rise for W independent transforms at f digit columns; moves give 1 each and h-8 argued additions give at most

**What gives way** [derived; the algebra checked exactly on a toy with 8 addresses, `algebra.py`, `algebra.out`].
- A move along z is D_z = (1+i)/2 + (1-i)/2 (shift by z) [read: h-7]. D_z D_z = shift, so the inverse move is a move followed by
  a relabelling [ran].
- The certificate says: source array at label <p>, target at label 0; afterwards source at the full label, target at label
  p-perp (8 dimensions, p outside it [ran: true for all 120]) holding y + x. Write the source ARRAY as X = D_p (content). Then,
  on the arrays, the invocation is: **X and Y each make the 8 moves of p-perp, and Y receives D_p^-1 X** [ran on the toy:
  both identities hold exactly]. The source array has made 8 moves, not 9; what the target receives from it has the support
  of all 9 directions (rows of 8 non-zeros against 4 in the toy).
- Three shears, Y += D^-1 X, X -= D Y, Y += D^-1 X, give (X, Y) -> (-D Y, D^-1 X) [ran]: the pair is exchanged and both contents
  have moved along p, with no move along p. This is the README's "a bank pair is two data arrays that exchange their contents
  through additions; the exchanges are what saves moves" [read], in two lines. The unit's bank comment says every data array
  makes five blocks of rank 8 and one block of rank 4, that is 44 moves for 45 directions [read: `tools/gen/foldlib.py`, "every
  bank role 5 x (h-1), 4"], and the unit is "3 forward + 2 backward invocations" [read: `tools/whatif_copies.py`]: of its five
  port directions every data array moves along four, and one is never moved along.
- So h-7's dictionary is exact for the contents and off by one direction for an array that enters as "label <p>" without
  having made that move. In the array's own coordinates the scratch copy at label 0 holds a sum over t of w_t D_(p_t)^-1 X_t:
  each term has 2^f non-zeros at f digit columns, and the target it is added to has one. **The scatter additions of those
  invocations are the gates h-8 asked for: two inputs whose sizes differ by a factor that grows with f; rise f + log2 57.**
  4v unpaid port moves per unit times f n is the missing 480 f n [derived; the count matches, the assignment of which
  invocations enter unpaid I did not read from the engine].
- Consequence for the meter: in array coordinates the class of a mixture is no longer (set of sources) x (one coset). The
  term of source t sits on a coset that depends on t through its port. Batch and address are entangled there, in the wires
  themselves, and h-7's "never entangled wire by wire" holds only in content coordinates. I did not run the meter in array
  coordinates; it needs the engine's exact frame operators.

## 7. Growth laws [ran for E8; second result: see the box at the end of this section]

- **Q gates per invocation = (cst + q) 2^h** at one digit column: cst = the moves of the scratch copies, q = the Q
  additions; q = v in E8 (one per target), 2.25 v in the second unit. At f columns: cst f n + q n. As a share of all gates it
  tends to cst / moves = 72 / 9,039 = 0.8 %.
- **Order: 1 at every gate** (E8, full meter). The unit never needs a wire that agrees with an earlier one modulo 4 without
  being a multiple of it.
- **Crossing gates: the scatter**, 400 of 736 in E8 and nothing else; 22,800 of 29,040 in the second unit and 1,753 others.
- **The scatter is as thin as its shadow allows** [ran + derived]. A matrix of whole numbers has rank at least its rank
  modulo 2 (an odd minor is not zero). Scale each target's row after the scatter to primitive form: the rank modulo 2 of the
  120 classes is 8 and there are 8 totals; in the second unit 22 and 22. So **with the same pattern of odd entries no
  circuit can scatter from fewer totals**, in either unit. Fewer scratch copies need another pattern modulo 2, or copies that
  come down less far. (For the README's open direction "the scratch copies"; a necessary condition only.)
- **Depth adds under nesting** [derived; one line of arithmetic checked, `algebra.out` (f)]. A block of the outer unit on a
  target is done by the inner unit while the target holds 2A + U (A wanted, U the odd mixture; every target makes all its 8
  moves in that state [read: h-7]). The inner targets hold 2a + u. The wire is (2A + U) x (2a + u) = U x u modulo 2, and the
  doubly wanted A x a carries the factor 4. So **inside d nested units the answer sits up to d binary digits below the class
  of the wire that carries it, and coming out costs d difference quotients, one per level.** The small circuits of four
  waves work at depth 0 with no quotient at all [read: m-cross]. This is the same axis as m-cross's experiment 8 (the
  transform modulo 4, 8, ...), reached from the other end.
- With h-7's numbers: the saving multiplies by 1 - 2.1e-3 per level, the excess adds 2.8 bits per level, and now the 2-adic
  depth adds at most 1 per level. A program that halves the textbook this way (h-7: about 328 levels) may hold answers up to
  about 330 binary digits deep [derived from h-7's count; rough].

## 8. What this specifies, and the better question

An order-changing step, as the one known example does it, in the meter's words:
1. contents held along flags that cross (more ports than label dimensions; this is what an address map is for);
2. sums of many sources formed where labels agree, then **a few of them brought down** (Q gates on the address side);
3. a scatter whose gates join crossing classes, leaving each wanted term one binary digit under an odd mixture;
4. removal of the mixture by additions only, ending in **one difference quotient per target** (Q gates on the batch side);
5. order 1 throughout; depth, not order, grows with nesting.
None of 1 to 4 occurs in any small circuit of the night [read: m-cross line 1]. 2 and 4 are the two halves of "no Q gate".

**The better question: the shear problem.** Given v pairs of arrays and v directions p_1..p_v in a space of h bits, add to
each target its source moved along its own direction, Y_t += D_(p_t)^-1 X_t, while every array makes the moves it owes anyway.
The plain way costs v moves (one per pair). With the three-shear exchange a unit of the five-invocation kind saves
4v - 5 (cost of one batch of v shears) [read: README section 7, D = 4v - 5 cst]. The published solutions cost
(number of totals) x (dimensions each copy comes down): 8 x 9 = 72 for v = 120, and 22 x 20 = 440 for v = 1,320. Everything the unit does beyond that identity is a solution of this one problem, and it is a
problem about a matrix: **P with 2 on the diagonal, zero wherever port t is not orthogonal to port s, of small rank, and
equal modulo 2 to a matrix A that additions alone can build.**
- Ceiling [derived; agrees with `wht37/avenues/NOTE.md` line 12, "it skips one move per data array"]: one direction per array per
  unit, so at most 1/(5h) of the moves whatever the helper circuit. This mechanism with one-dimensional ports cannot reach
  a saving of 1e-1. Ports of dimension r (that note's "fat labels") turn the shear into Y += F_P^-1 X and the count into
  4 v r - 5 (rank) h; the same three-shear identity holds for any invertible F [ran for D].
- What linear size would need in this language [derived, a recursion]: if a unit takes W arrays through m directions with
  a W moves and b W additions, the cost per point obeys c(m F) = a c(F) + b. a = m is the textbook; the published unit has
  a = 44.905 for m = 45. **O(n) needs a < 1 (fewer than one move per array for all m directions); a = 1 gives n log log n.**
  The ledger does not forbid it (a-2: it never forbids linear size); it says where the rise must then come from: additions
  between wires whose sizes differ by 2^(f r), that is shears through blocks of rank r close to m.

Mutations:
1. `shear-small`: the shear problem at h = 3 and 4 with all odd-weight ports, in (G) with additions counted: least number of
   gates for v shears against v (2^h + 2^h). It is the tiny-unit hunt with a cleaner target (one batch of shears, not a unit).
2. `quotient-P`: for which families of ports is there a P = A modulo 2 with rank(P) h < (4/5) v r? Section 7 gives
   rank(P) >= rank of (A modulo 2), met with equality by both published units. This is not yet a floor for a family of
   ports, because A may be chosen. Two regimes [derived]: wanted term at depth 0 (odd diagonal): the number of totals is at
   least the min-rank of the "knows" graph over the two-element field, which is index coding's number; wanted term one
   digit down (both published units): the shadow has ZERO diagonal, the two-element bound is nearly empty, and the
   constraint moves to modulo 4. So the published scatters are index codes over the rationals whose reduction modulo 2 is
   not an index code. Question for a reader of that literature: is the min-rank of the E8 "knows" graph over the two-element
   field larger than 8? If yes, "one digit down" is what the rationals buy here. Claim nothing until computed.
3. `meter-in-array-coordinates`: redo section 4 with the classes written in the arrays' own inputs (section 6, last item);
   it needs the frame operators of `R/Work/GFrame`. Prediction: classes that are unions of cosets of different subspaces,
   one per port, and Q gates at every x-to-helper addition.
4. `depth-2`: a two-level toy of the mechanism of section 7 (a quotient inside a quotient) at 16 points, to see whether a
   small circuit can use depth 2 at all; hybrid 3 of m-cross says quotients of outputs are circular, so the parents must be
   mixtures.
5. `helpers-between`: read how the engine treats what the helpers hold between invocations (section 1's anomaly: 7,808
   source terms left in the helpers; 45 to 81 helper values in each target if helpers are inputs).
6. `one-move`: is a = 1 excluded for units of this kind by a count? Every array must leave label 0; the three-shear
   exchange gives one direction for free only against three shears. A floor on a in terms of the cost of a shear would be
   the first statement that separates n log log n from the present design.

## 9. What failed, and what I changed

- First try: meter with every register as an input. It showed no Q addition at all and a tree that breaks at the second gate.
  The failure was made of the helpers' own values sitting in every class. Changed the reading to the checker's own (helpers
  empty): the 120 quotients appeared, and the crossing gates turned out to be the scatter and nothing else.
- Second: "the saving must show inside the invocation". It does not (9,039 moves against 8,967 plain). The failure is made
  of the question: the invocation is a shear, not a transform. Going around it gave section 6.
- A statement I wrote and then found FALSE on the toy and withdrew: "at the full label every move D_z is diagonal, so the 120
  directions become free scalings there". The frame operator commutes with D_z; it does not diagonalise it. What is true is
  only that the nine moves down undo, for each source at once, the eight moves it made and leave the inverse of the ninth.
- The second result (p = 11) was too large for the full meter (first 1,500 MB, then 600 seconds; a run on the rented
  machine was too slow under its load and I stopped it); a batch-side counter replaced it and finished in 34 seconds.

## 10. Criticism, last

- The classes rest on h-7's dictionary (row a of a frame is supported on a + U with entries of one modulus). I read h-7's
  statement, not the Lean. If some frame row has entries of two moduli, classes are smaller than cosets and every count changes.
- Intermediate labels inside a block are my choice (the frame's stored basis, in order). Other flags give other intermediate
  classes; the counts of crossing pairs depend on it, the kinds of gates and the Q count do not.
- "Order" compares only wires at the same label and address; a wire at another address of the same coset counts as order 1.
  Orders 2 to 5 could hide there only if two different rows of one frame agreed modulo 4 up to a unit; I did not check that.
- The prologue assumes each source makes its port move before the invocation; section 6 says some do not. The five numbers
  are for the dictionary's coordinates, which is what the brief asked for; in array coordinates they would differ.
- Section 6 is derived from labels and counts. I did not read the engine's five-stage word; "4v unpaid port moves" matches
  the unit's own counts (44 moves per array for 45 directions, D = 4v - 5 cst, the gap 480; these are one fact counted three
  ways, not three confirmations) and the invocation-by-invocation assignment is not checked. The toy uses plain products of D for frames, the engine uses one operator per subspace.
- "Depth adds" is a statement about a nested program I did not expand. One level is measured; two levels are arithmetic.
- (n/2) log2 n is not the right yardstick for 1,023 small transforms; I report it because the brief asks.
- The "exact repeat" test and the lean mode of the meter compare 64-bit hashes of exact rational vectors, not the vectors.
- Nothing here is a circuit, a bound on gates, or a change of order. It is a reading of two published objects.
