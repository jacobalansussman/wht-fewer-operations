# u-1 corners-theory: the corners of the subset-sum picture grow like a power of N above 1. A wall, with its reason.

11 October 2026, about 01:42 to 02:40 EDT, 60-minute box. Private. One agent; nothing reviewed, nothing in Lean, nothing published.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (whole numbers; files in `u-1-cornerstheory/`); [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by
nobody else; [read] = from tonight's reports; [guess]. Gate count: **(G)** throughout (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free).

**No circuit below a known count, no growth law below N log N. The result is a lower bound in a model: it closes the two-chain
route to linear size, and says what a route would have to give up.**

Words (as in WAVE6-PICKS). N = 2^k points; an address is a subset of {1..k}. Z is the subset-sum matrix (1 where input T is
inside output S). *Down-set of S*: the inputs inside S, 2^|S| of them. *Picture*: Z with outputs in one order (rows), inputs in
another (columns), zeros after the last row and column. *Corner*: a non-zero of the second difference M. The two-chain program
costs N - 2 + corners gates. *Step*: two neighbouring outputs (or the last output and the added zero row); the non-zeros of its
row of M are the places where (row S - row S') changes value along the inputs. *Run*: consecutive inputs.
New words here. *Top* of a step: the larger of its two outputs (it is never inside the other). *R+ of a step*: the inputs inside
the top and not inside the other output; it has at least half of the top's down-set, 2^(s-1) inputs if the top has size s.
*Window*: L consecutive inputs.

## 0. In ten lines

1. [ran, exhaustive] **5 and 13 are the exact minima at 4 and 8 points** (every input order, exact dynamic programme over the
   output orders). 8 optimal pairs at 4 points; **168 optimal pairs at 8 points, on 72 input orders, 28 classes** up to
   permuting the address bits. The three reformulations of the brief hold on every one of them.
2. [ran] The rule "every step is one run" is NOT what the optimum is: among the 168 there are pictures with a step of 3 non-zeros
   and with a step of 4, paid for by more prefix steps (d = 3, 4 or 5; corners = 2N - d + extras). So 2N - 3 is an accident of
   bookkeeping at 8 points, not a law.
3. [derived; ingredients checked by run on 482 pictures] **The window lemma.** A window of L inputs that lies inside the down-set
   of S forces S to contain the union W of the window, and |W| >= log2 L. So a long run of inputs can serve only the few
   outputs just above its union; there are only N windows; and every step needs half of a down-set covered by its runs.
4. [derived] Consequence: the number of steps whose top has size s and whose R+ is at most r runs is at most
   **2 (N - L + 1) C(k - lam, s - lam) / (l - L + 1)**, l = 2^(s-1)/r, any L <= l, lam = log2 L rounded up.
5. [derived] **Corners >= N^(1.10 - o(1)).** About a quarter of all steps have a top of size k/2 or more, and all but a vanishing
   share of those need more than 2^(0.10 k) runs. So corners per point are unbounded, **and they outgrow log N**: the two-chain
   count is not N log N, it is a power of N above 1. Upper bound known: 3^k = N^1.585 (both orders Gray). Truth: between.
6. [ran, `windowbound.py`] The explicit bound is useless at the sizes anyone can run: it first exceeds N at k = 19, is 3.7 N at
   k = 64, 28.5 N at k = 96, and **passes Yates' own (N/2) log2 N + 1 only near k = 128** (1.05e41 against 2.18e40). So the
   numbers 5, 13, 30, 80 neither confirm nor contradict it; they are simply too small. Their ratios (2.6, 2.3, 2.7) are all above
   the 2.14 = 2^1.10 the bound asks for in the long run.
7. [derived] Two cheap theorems on the way: at most 2k + 1 steps have a single non-zero, so **corners >= 2N - 2k - 1 at every
   size** (9 at 8 points where the truth is 13); and every input lies in the "top run" of at most 4(k + 1) steps (a Helly
   argument), which already kills "one run per step" for large k and gives corners >= 3N - O(N/k).
8. [derived] The doubling rule: if the 2N-point orders are block orders (first all addresses without the new element, then all
   with it, on both sides), the count is a sum of THREE N-point counts plus a seam. Tripling is forced; any rule better than 3
   must interleave; and by line 5 no rule with 2 c(N) + O(N) exists at all.
9. What nesting forbids: section 4. In one sentence: **nested rows are sub-cubes, and a line through the cube has only N windows,
   each of which fits inside few sub-cubes of nearly its own size.**
10. [derived, rough] The variation "single inputs and single outputs as extra nodes" is covered (constant 1/4). Signed or scaled
    pictures: covered by a rougher count. Three chains (a free middle order): NOT covered, and section 5 says why that is the
    door: each extra letter should halve the excess exponent, and Yates is the end of that road.

## 1. First experiment: every optimal pair at 4 and 8 points [ran]

where row S minus row S' changes value along the inputs (right end counted), with the zero row as a ninth city where the path
must end, runs the exact dynamic programme over subsets, counts the optimal output orders and lists them (`opt4.txt`, `opt8.txt`).

| points | least corners | optimal pairs | input orders that reach it | classes up to bit permutations | prefix steps d |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 5 (exact) | 8 | 6 of 24 | 4 (3 up to the dual) | 3 in all |
| 8 | **13 (exact)** | 168 | 72 of 40,320 | 28 | 3 (48 pictures), 4 (114), 5 (6) |

Histogram at 8 points of "best count for a fixed input order": 13 (72 orders), 14 (408), 15 (1,218), ... up to 26 (162).
`look.py` then checks on all 176 optimal pairs: the two-salesmen formula, the bonds formula and "all one-run steps implies
2N - d" all hold [ran]. The dual (complement every address and exchange the two orders) has the same count on every class [ran].

In words. The six middle addresses of the 3-cube form a hexagon (sizes 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 2, each step adding or removing one
element). In 84 of the 168 optimal pairs the middle outputs, read with the empty and the full set skipped, are one walk along
that hexagon, and likewise the middle inputs in 84; in the others the walk is cut where the path passes through the empty or
the full set. Only 12 steps in all 168 pictures join two middle outputs that are not hexagon neighbours [ran, `lastchecks.py`].
A hexagon step from {f} to {e, f} changes the down-set by exactly the pair {e}, {e, f}, and that
pair is a neighbouring pair on the hexagon: this is why one run suffices. The profile lists show what the 13 is made of:
(2,1,2,2,2,1,2,1) is eight one-run steps with d = 3, but (2,1,1,1,4,2,1,1) is also 13, with one step of FOUR non-zeros and
five prefix steps (6 pictures); 114 of the 168 have a step of three; only 48 are one run at every step. The honest formula is corners = 2N - d + (sum over steps of
non-zeros beyond 2). In two of the eight 4-point optima M has an entry 2: a step between two outputs neither inside the
other ("a prefix goes out, the adjacent run comes in"); so "a step of two non-zeros joins S inside S'" is true only when the two
non-zeros are +1 and -1.
The 8-point trick does not continue for a structural reason: for k > 3 a step from S to S plus e changes the down-set by a
sub-cube of 2^|S| inputs, and section 2 shows such sub-cubes cannot be runs for more than O(k) outputs S per element e.

## 2. Three lemmas [derived], each checked by run where a check is possible

Setting: any two orders, plain picture. For a step with outputs S (top, size s) and S' (or nothing), d = row S - row S' is +1 on
R+ = {T inside S, not inside S'}, -1 on {T inside S', not inside S}, 0 elsewhere. Every run of R+ ends at a place where d
changes, so **non-zeros of the step >= runs of R+**. And |R+| = 2^s - 2^|S and S'| >= 2^(s-1).

**Lemma 1 (prefix chain).** A step with a single non-zero has d = +1 or -1 on a beginning of the input order, so R+ is a prefix
and the other side is empty. Prefixes are nested. R+(S1, S1') inside R+(S2, S2') holds exactly when S1 is inside S2 and
(S1 minus S1') is inside (S2 minus S2') [test with the singletons of S1 minus S1' and with T = S1]. Two chains of subsets, at
least one strictly growing per step: at most 2k + 1 such steps. **Corners >= 2N - 2k - 1.** (Seen: at most 5 at 8 to 64 points.)

**Lemma 2 (three runs through one input).** Among three intervals of a line that share a point, one lies inside the union of
the other two. Give each step its *top run*: the run of R+ that holds the input whose address IS the top S (it is in R+). If the
top runs of three steps share an input, one top is an input inside another step's run, hence inside that step's top. So the tops
of the steps whose top run passes through a given input contain no three mutually incomparable sets: two chains, at most
2(k + 1) tops, each the top of at most two steps. **Every input lies in at most 4(k + 1) top runs**; the top runs have total
length at most 4(k + 1) N. A step that is one run has a top run of 2^(s-1) inputs or more, so one-run steps with a top of size s
number at most 8(k + 1) N / 2^s: almost none above size 2 log2 k. Hence all but O(N/k) steps have three non-zeros or more.

**Lemma 3 (windows). The main one.** Let a step have top S of size s and R+ in at most r runs. One run has length
l >= 2^(s-1)/r. Take any window of L <= l consecutive inputs inside it. Its L members are different subsets of S, so their union W
is inside S and 2^|W| >= L. For a fixed window, the outputs of size s that contain W number at most C(k - lam, s - lam),
lam = log2 L rounded up; each is the top of at most two steps; the input order has N - L + 1 windows of length L; and the run
holds l - L + 1 of them. Therefore

  #(steps with top size s and at most r runs) <= 2 (N - L + 1) C(k - lam, s - lam) / (l - L + 1).

With r = 2^rho and L = 2^(s - 2 - rho) this is at most N 2^(rho + 3) C(k - s + rho + 2, rho + 2) / 2^s.

**Theorem (two chains).** At least half of the outputs of size >= k/2 are tops, so at least N/4 - o(N) steps have a top of
size >= k/2. For s >= k/2 and rho = alpha k the bound above is N^(1/2 + alpha + (1/2 + alpha) h(alpha / (1/2 + alpha)) + o(1)),
h the binary entropy; it is o(N) as long as alpha + (1/2 + alpha) h(alpha/(1/2 + alpha)) < 1/2, which holds up to alpha = 0.10
(0.100 gives 0.490; 0.105 gives 0.508) [derived, by hand calculator]. So N/4 - o(N) steps have more than 2^(0.10 k) runs:
**corners >= N^(1.10 - o(1)), gates of the two-chain program likewise.**

Checks [ran, `checklemmas.py`, `checklemmas.out`, `checklemmas_runs.out`]: on q-cross's best pictures at 8, 16, 32, 64 points, on
all 168 optimal 8-point pairs and on 310 random or half-regular pictures at 4 to 128 points, every inequality used holds: non-zeros
>= runs of R+; |R+| >= 2^(s-1); single-non-zero steps <= 2k + 1; top runs through one input <= 4(k + 1) (seen: 5, 6, 8, 11);
and for EVERY window length L and top size s the number of (step, window inside a run of R+) pairs is within its cap
2 (N - L + 1) C(k - lam, s - lam). These runs test the bricks; they cannot test the conclusion, which only starts at k = 19.
What the data do show is the mechanism at work, already at 64 points (best picture, 261 corners): runs of R+ by top size are
1 to 2 (size 2), 1 to 3 (size 3), 1 to 4 (size 4), 1 to 5 (size 5): the steps between large outputs are the expensive ones.

**Extensions** [derived, rougher, unchecked].
- *Single inputs and single outputs as extra nodes* (the letter on chains in full). A step's difference is then a combination of
  t prefixes and single columns, t = rectangles ending at that row plus single-row rectangles of its two rows; each adds at most
  two places of change, so 2t >= runs of R+, and each rectangle is counted at most twice: rectangles >= (1/4) sum of runs.
  Same exponent.
- *Signs or any scalings of rows and columns.* Runs of constant value of d may now join R+ to R-, so a run lies inside the union
  of the two down-sets. Half of a window is then inside one of them; outputs holding half of a fixed window pairwise share most
  of their elements (Cauchy-Schwarz on the window), which bounds their number by 2^(rho + 5) C(k, rho + 5)^2. A power of N above
  1 again, with a smaller exponent. The same sketch covers the count modulo 2 (s-pick's "odd corners").

## 3. The doubling rule [derived]

Z at 2N points: outputs S or S + e, inputs T or T + e (e the new element). Count interactions (step, bond of neighbouring
inputs) by type. A step between two outputs without e meets a bond between two inputs with e in zeros only: no corner. The other
three combinations (without/without, with/without, with/with) each reproduce the N-point window. So for block orders,
rows = (order pi1 of the addresses without e, then pi2 of those with e), columns = (sigma1 without e, then sigma2 with e):

  corners(2N) = c(pi1, sigma1) + c(pi2, sigma1) + c(pi2, sigma2) + seam terms >= 3 c_min(N).

**Concatenation triples, whatever the four orders**; this is q-cross's 8, 22, 64, 190, ... and the upper bound 3^k. To do better
the with-e and without-e addresses must be interleaved on at least one side. Interleaving twins on the input side (T next to
T + e) makes the rows with e cost exactly c(N) and the rows without e cost about twice their number of ONES, which is the
N^1.5 of the down-sets. So the seam is not a boundary effect: it is either a third copy or the bulk of the down-sets. And by
section 2 no doubling rule with 2 c(N) + O(N) can exist for all N; the average ratio must be at least 2^1.10 = 2.14.

## 4. What exactly does nesting forbid? [derived]

For the transform, two rows agree on half of the columns and a node holds one form: a rank obstruction, quadratic (q-cross W-A).
For Z the rows are nested, and the obstruction is of another kind, about ORDER:
- a row is a sub-cube (the down-set of S); a step must switch on or off at least half of a sub-cube;
- the wires of the program are runs of ONE line through the cube, and a run inside a sub-cube of 2^s points, of length close to
  2^s, has a union close to S: it belongs to that sub-cube and its few neighbours (C(k, rho) of them for a run of 2^(s - rho));
- there are N windows and N/4 large steps, each wanting 2^(k/2) / r window-places. Supply and demand: r^2 k^(log r) >= 2^(k/2).
So nesting forbids **sharing long runs between many outputs**. The 8-point picture works because its sub-cubes are edges, and
six edges fit on a hexagon. The cube has no line on which many middle sub-cubes are nearly intervals. This is the brief's attempt
(i) made exact; it is a "consecutive ones" obstruction, not a rank one. It answers q-cross's "what I do not have is a reason",
with the correction that the reason gives MORE than N log N: the search numbers "sit on (N/2) log2 N" only because they are small.

## 5. Where the wall stops, and what would go around it

The proof uses: (a) every wire on the input side is a run of one fixed order; (b) each output is reached from its neighbour
directly by such runs; (c) rows are sub-cubes. Yes-and, twice:
1. *Free middle order (three chains), or several letters.* [guess, with a construction behind it] Z on k bits is (Z on the first
   half of the bits) times (Z on the second half), each a block-diagonal copy of the sqrt N-point picture. Two letters therefore
   cost about 2 sqrt N c(sqrt N): if one letter is N^(1 + c), two are N^(1 + c/2), j are about j N^(1 + c/j), and j = k letters
   is Yates. So the lemma does not move with the middle order: it WEAKENS, exponent halved per letter, and the family of
   pictures is the usual size-against-depth trade with letters as depth. Linear size would need j letters at O(N/j) corners
   each; nothing here forbids or supplies that. This is the same statement as q-cross fact D ("the records need more letters as
   N grows"), now with a growth law attached at j = 1.
2. *Other tree shapes on the input side.* A node holding L inputs inside a down-set still forces the union to have log2 L
   elements; what is lost on a balanced tree is the "N windows" count (a tree has 2N - 1 nodes, also few) and the step structure
   on the output side. I expect the lemma to survive for letters on any two trees in the form "a rectangle of width L inside the
   1-region has at most C(k, .) 2^(k - log L) rows above it", which is p-6's partner lemma for Z. Not done.
Other identities found on the way [derived; (i) also ran: 1,200 random tests at 4 to 32 points, no violation; (ii), (iii) not
run]: (i) deleting rows or columns never increases the corner count (triangle inequality place by place), so corners of any
sub-picture bound corners from below; (ii) corners is a hitting set: for any two
rows and two columns whose 2 x 2 minor has a non-zero second difference, the box they span contains a corner, so corners >= the
largest family of disjoint such boxes (the shape of the "independent rectangles" bounds for search trees); (iii) modulo 2 the
odd corners are the non-zeros of Z written in two path bases, and the inverse of M is the table of quadrant sums of the Moebius
matrix in the two orders.

## 6. Criticism, last

- Lemma 3 and the theorem are one hour old and read by nobody. The bricks are checked by run; the entropy arithmetic (0.10) is by
  hand calculator; the "N/4 steps have a large top" count is one line. A skeptic should attack: "each S is the top of at most
  two steps" (true on a path), the count C(k - lam, s - lam) when the window's union is larger than lam (then fewer outputs
  qualify: the count is decreasing in |W|), and the choice L = l/2.
- The theorem says nothing at any size one can run. Below k = 19 it is vacuous, and it passes Yates' law only near k = 128. So
  it does not decide whether a two-chain circuit can beat Yates at 64 or 128 points (targets 129 and 321 of brief 1): it only
  says such a win cannot persist. The constant could be improved a lot (merge with Lemma 1, count R- as well, use all sizes).
- The exponent 1.10 is a lower bound for a model with upper bound 1.585; I have no idea where the truth is, and no construction.
- The extensions (singles, signs, modulo 2) are sketches. The j-letter law in section 5 is a guess from one construction.
- Not done from the brief: the exact minimum at 16 points; the seam of the 8-point picture; the literature check on off-line
  range sums (Chazelle); three chains by experiment. The 168 optimal pairs are listed but only summarised, not classified by hand.
- It is a lower bound in a model. It does not touch general circuits for subset sums, where nothing above linear is known.
