## 1. The three claims, stated exactly

**(a)** x is a table of K^2 numbers. A program makes calls c_j = A (L_j x + sum over i < j of M_ji c_i), j = 1..r, and
outputs Q x + sum of P_j c_j. The matrices L_j (K by K^2), M_ji (K by K), P_j (K^2 by K), Q are fixed: they do not depend on
A. If the output equals (A (x) A) x, that is the table A X A^T, for EVERY K x K matrix A, then r >= 2K.

**(b)** For A = [[c, s], [s, -c]] with c^2 + s^2 = 1, write X = alpha 1 + beta J + [[p, q], [q, -p]] (J the quarter turn).
Then A X A^T = alpha 1 - beta J + [[t, u], [u, -t]] with (t, u) = A diag(1, -1) A (p, q). Two calls where (a) needs four.
The report adds: this is not a saving, because the glue costs as much as the box.

**(c)** Every circuit for subset sums at N = 2^k points whose gates are u + v and u - v has at least N (0.277 sqrt(k) - 1)
gates [g-4 section 6.3]; hence every two-chain picture has at least 0.277 sqrt(k) - 2 corners per point, and at least
0.17 sqrt(k) - 2 if some steps carry a coefficient 2 [s-cross hybrid 4].

## 2. (a) The box lemma

**The proof, written out** [derived]. Put t*A in place of A. The output becomes a polynomial in t whose part of degree d is
the sum, over all chains j_1 > j_2 > ... > j_d of calls, of P_j1 A M_j1j2 A ... A L_jd. The wanted answer t^2 (A (x) A) has
degree 2 only, and the identity holds for every t, so:
- degree 1: sum over j of P_j E L_j = 0 for every matrix E. That says the tensor sum of P_j (x) L_j is zero. If the L_j span
  s dimensions, write each L_j in a basis of that span: the tensor being zero gives s independent linear relations among the
  P_j, so the P_j span at most r - s dimensions.
- degree 2 at A = 1: sum over i < j of P_j M_ji L_i = the identity on K^2 numbers, of rank K^2. Its rows lie in the rows of
  the L_i, which lie in the rows of s basis matrices of K rows each: K^2 <= s K. Its columns lie in the columns of the P_j,
  which lie in the columns of at most r - s basis matrices of K columns each: K^2 <= (r - s) K.
- So s >= K and r - s >= K, and r >= 2K.

**The attack "a call's input mixes degrees".** A call's input L_j x + sum M_ji c_i does mix a part of degree 0 with parts of
degree 1 and more. It does not matter: nothing is argued call by call. The whole output is one polynomial in t and the
identity is used coefficient by coefficient. I see no gap.

**What the proof really needs** [derived]. Three things about the family of boxes: (i) it is closed under scaling, or at
least the identity extends to t*A (true for any family whose multiples it contains, and for any family dense among all
matrices); (ii) the degree-1 identity is LINEAR in E, so it is enough that the family spans all K x K matrices; (iii) one
invertible member. So the lemma holds as it stands for "all multiples of orthogonal matrices", and it says nothing for
(1) a family that does not span (symmetric matrices, multiples of reflections) and (2) a family that is not closed under
scaling (orthogonal matrices, reflections, involutions). The report names (2) as the hatch; (1) is a second one.

**Exact checks** [ran, `exact.py`, fractions]. The textbook program (K calls on columns, K on rows) written as L, M, P, Q
at K = 2, 3, 4: equal to A (x) A on random whole-number boxes; degree-1 tensor exactly zero; dim span L = K, dim span P = K,
sum = r = 2K. So the textbook meets both inequalities with equality: the lemma is tight.

**Known?** One search did not find the lemma. Its two ingredients are standard: splitting a computation into parts of equal
degree, and a rank count. The nearest literature asks a different question: how many products "matrix times vector" are
needed to learn a property of an unknown matrix (Meyer, Swartworth, Woodruff, arXiv:2502.08029, 2025, for vectors of
Kronecker form [read: search summary only]). Note the contrast [derived]: if the glue may MULTIPLY call results, K calls
always suffice (call the box on the K unit vectors, then A is known). The 2K is a statement about linear glue.

**What it gives.** A count of calls, not of gates. In the recursion T(K^2) = r T(K) + glue it forces r = 2K for a program
that does not look inside the box, which is N log2 N. **Scope, which the report should state more loudly:** for ONE fixed
box, such as the transform itself, "number of calls" means nothing (the glue alone can compute anything), so the lemma is
not a lower bound for the transform; it is a statement about programs that are uniform over a family.
The remark in the report that m directions should need m K^(m-1) calls is not proved by this argument: the same two counts
give only 2 K^(m-1) [derived].

## 3. (b) Two calls for a 2 x 2 reflection

**Exact** [ran, `exact.py`]. I checked the formula as an identity between polynomials in c and s, reduced by s^2 = 1 - c^2,
for the four unit tables. That proves it for every table and every reflection over any field, where the report's `refl2.py`
tested 13 rational points (which, as it happens, is also a proof: entries of degree 2 that agree at more than 4 points of a
circle agree on the circle). A diag(1, -1) A = [[2c^2 - 1, 2cs], [2cs, 1 - 2c^2]]: the reflection at twice the angle.

**The correction** [ran exact, `rot.py`]. For a rotation R = [[c, -s], [s, c]], R X R^T = alpha 1 + beta J + [[t, u], [u, -t]]
with (t, u) = R R (p, q): the same two nested calls, without the diag(1, -1). A rotation is not symmetric and is not its own
inverse. So the report's sentence "the relation that lets calls be shared is symmetric and an involution together" is not the
right one. What both cases use is A A^T = 1: it frees the identity part, makes the quarter-turn part a sign, and leaves a
2-dimensional part on which A X A^T turns by twice the angle. In floats, multiples of reflections (symmetric, A^2 a multiple
of 1, but closed under scaling) found NO 3-call program [ran, table above]: the saving goes away the moment the family is
closed under scaling, as the lemma's proof suggests it should.

**Known.** Classical: the tensor square of the 2-dimensional representation of the group of 2 x 2 orthogonal matrices is
"trivial + determinant + angle doubled", and doubling an angle is applying the matrix twice. The rotations are the circle
group, where every such decomposition is into 1-dimensional pieces.

**Does it scale?** Not by the same mechanism; not excluded otherwise. [derived from standard facts about orthogonal
matrices, from memory, not run] For K >= 3 the pieces of the table that every orthogonal box maps to themselves are: the
identity (1 number, free), the antisymmetric tables (K (K - 1)/2 numbers; at K = 4 with determinant +1 they split 3 + 3) and
the symmetric trace-free tables (K (K + 1)/2 - 1 numbers). The mechanism of K = 2 is a piece of exactly K numbers on which
A X A^T is a word in A. A piece of exactly K numbers exists only at K = 2 (symmetric trace-free) and K = 3 (antisymmetric,
where an orthogonal box acts as plus or minus itself: one call for 3 of the 9 numbers).
**That is not a proof that reflections need 2K calls at larger K, and I could not get one.** What I can prove [derived; ranks
ran exact, `eig.py`] is a rank count: a program with r calls to A = 1 - 2P (P a projection of rank m) is a program with r
calls to P, each call adds a term of rank at most m to a fixed matrix, so r >= rank(A (x) A - A' (x) A') / (2m) for any two
members A, A' of the family. That gives:

| K | rank of the mirror m | r at least | sealed box 2K |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| 4 | 1 | 5 | 8 |
| 4 | 2 (the transform's own kind) | 3 | 8 |
| 8 | 4 (the transform's own kind) | 7 | 16 |

For reflections with a mirror of half the dimension, which is what H / sqrt(K) is, the count is K - 1: about half the
sealed box's 2K, and the "one call per line" that K = 2 shows. (The count is weak: at K = 2 it gives 1 where the truth is 2.) **So between K - 1 and 2K nothing is proved for the
transform's own family at K >= 4.** If K calls with glue of O(K^2) gates existed there, the recursion would read
T(K^2) = K T(K) + O(K^2), which is N log log N [read: u-5; derived]. I have no evidence that it exists: at K = 2 the glue costs
a whole transform, and at K = 3 the searches found no program with 3, 4 or 5 calls in 6, 4 and 1 starts (silence, not proof; the rank count there says at least 3). s-cross's experiment 4 (K = 4, mirror of
rank 2) is the test, and it should count glue from the start.

**Glue** [derived]. Count (G): 4 gates before the calls (alpha, beta, p, q are two butterflies), 2 calls of 2 gates, 4 gates
after: 12, against 8 for four calls and 7 for the record. For the transform's own kernel A diag(1, -1) A is the exchange of
p and q, and the formula is the textbook's four butterflies. "Not a saving" stands.

## 4. (c) The volume bound for subset sums (bounded coefficients)

**The chain of steps, each checked.**
1. [derived + ran] Z is the k-fold Kronecker power of [[1, 0], [1, 1]], whose singular values are the golden ratio phi and
   1/phi. So Z has singular values phi^(k - 2j), each C(k, j) times.
2. [ran exact at 4 and 8 points, `exact.py`] The sum of the squares of all r x r minors equals the r-th elementary symmetric
   function of the eigenvalues of Z^T Z (Cauchy-Binet): 9, 16, 9, 1 at 4 points and 27, 193, 594, 870, 594, 193, 27, 1 at
   8 points, equal to the enumerated sums for every r. That function is at least the product of the r largest eigenvalues.
3. [derived] **One large minor from the sum: sound.** There are C(N, r)^2 <= 4^N minors of size r, so the largest squared
   minor is at least the sum divided by 4^N, and the largest minor at least (product of the r largest singular values) / 2^N.
   With r = the number of singular values above 1: log2 of the largest minor >= N (log2(phi) E|k - 2J| / 2 - 1), where J
   is the number of heads in k fair coins and log2(phi) = 0.694.
4. [derived] Morgenstern's count: when every gate is u + v or u - v, the largest minor of the matrix of all wires at most
   doubles per gate, and starts at 1. So gates >= log2(largest minor). **It does not need the wires primitive** (the report
   asks): it needs each gate's |a| + |b| in whatever scaling the circuit is written in, and the outputs to be the rows of Z
   themselves. With a gate u + c v the factor is 1 + |c| per gate.

**The constant** [ran, `bounds.py`; exact E|k - 2J|, floats only for log2]. E|k - 2J| is BELOW sqrt(2k/pi) for even k (ratio
0.940, 0.985, 0.996 at k = 4, 16, 64), and 0.694 x 0.798 / 2 = 0.27696, so g-4's own count gives slightly less than its
printed formula: 1.2071 N at k = 64 where 0.277 sqrt(k) - 1 says 1.2160. Harmless, and repaired with room to spare by the
standard form (next paragraph). Thresholds: the count is positive from k = 13, passes N at k = 53 and 2N at k = 117;
the report's "near 52 and 117" is right.

**Known, and slightly stronger as known.** Buergisser and Lotz, "Lower bounds on the bounded coefficient complexity of
bilinear maps", arXiv:cs/0301016 (2003), Proposition 3.1 [read tonight, statement only]: gates >= log2 of the "r-mean square
volume" minus (number of rows)/2, where that volume is exactly the square root of the sum of squared r x r minors. Their
model: additions, subtractions, multiplications by numbers of size at most 2. It measures r wires against ALL inputs
(a volume, not a square minor), so only the choice of rows is paid for: N/2 instead of N. Applied to Z:

    bounded-coefficient gates for subset sums  >=  N (0.347 E|k - 2J| - 1/2)   ~   N (0.277 sqrt(k) - 0.5).

This is above g-4's printed formula at every k I tabulated (2 to 1000), so **claim (c) is true as printed**. I did not find
the bound applied to the subset-sum, disjointness or Sierpinski matrix in two searches (g-4 reports the same). The method
goes back to Morgenstern (1973) and Raz (2002). Their paper also keeps the bound when a limited number of gates with
unbounded coefficients are allowed [read: abstract summary; not applied to Z here].

**The ceiling** [derived; not in g-4 or s-cross]. The singular values of a piece of a matrix are at most those of the whole
matrix, so every r x r minor of Z is at most the product of the r largest singular values of Z:

    N (0.347 E|k - 2J| - 1)  <=  log2(largest minor of Z)  <=  0.347 N E|k - 2J|  <  0.277 N sqrt(k).

Volume arguments for subset sums are therefore exhausted at N sqrt(log N): they cannot reach N log N, the size of Yates'
method. Numbers per point (`bounds.out`):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.52 | -0.08 | 0.14 | -0.45 | 2 |
| 8 | 0.76 | -0.01 | 0.30 | -0.22 | 4 |
| 16 | 1.09 | 0.12 | 0.60 | 0.11 | 8 |
| 30 | 1.50 | 0.52 | 1.01 | 0.52 | 15 |

| 128 | 3.13 | 2.13 | 2.63 | 2.13 | 64 |
| 1000 | 8.76 | 7.76 | 8.26 | 7.76 | 500 |

Exact small cases [ran, exhaustive]: the largest minor of Z is 1 at 4 points and 2 at 8 points (sizes 3, 4, 5), as the
report says. I did not redo 16 points (the report's search found 5; the ceiling there is phi^12 = 321).

**Corners.** gates = N - 2 + corners, so corners per point >= (gate bound)/N - 1: with g-4's formula 0.277 sqrt(k) - 2, as
the report says. **The coefficient 2**: in a plain picture (entries 0 and 1) a corner is a second difference, of size at most
2, one gate "running value plus or minus 2 x a running sum" with |a| + |b| = 3, so dividing by log2 3 = 1.585 is right, and
0.17 sqrt(k) - 2 follows (I get 0.175 sqrt(k) - 1.63). **Not handled:** the report says "plain or signed". With signs on
both sides the entries are 0, +1, -1 and a second difference can be 3 or 4; the divisor is then log2 5 = 2.32 and the bound
0.119 sqrt(k) - 1.43. Same growth law, smaller constant. With free weights there is no bound at all, as the report says.
The bound is empty at every size anyone can run: 0.277 sqrt(k) - 2 is positive only from k = 53, and reaches the 2.5
corners per point measured at 32 points only near k = 264.

## 5. In the user's terms

- **Circuit size for n points: none. Saving exponent: none.**
- (a) says where not to look: a composition that is uniform over all boxes pays 2K calls, the textbook. Any saving must use a
  relation of the transform, and by section 3 the relation that helped at K = 2 is orthogonality, in the one dimension where
  the orthogonal group is a circle.
- (c) is about bounded coefficients. In count (G) scalings are free, and in count (O) a multiplication by any rational number
  is one operation, so (c) does not bound the user's cost at all. What it does say for linear size: a circuit of C N gates
  for subset sums needs gates whose coefficients (in whole-number scaling) have average log2 size about 0.277 sqrt(k)/C,
  and the method cannot ask for more than that. A saving of 1/2 in the exponent (N sqrt(log N)) is not excluded even with
  coefficients +1 and -1; linear size is.
- For the transform itself the same count gives the full (N/2) log2 N (Morgenstern), so any program below N log N must use
  large or small coefficients; the published one does.

## 6. What failed, and the nearest true statements

- Nothing in (a), (b), (c) fails at any size I ran.
- s-cross hybrid 2, "the relation that lets calls be shared is symmetric and an involution together": not the right
  relation. Smallest counterexample: any 2 x 2 rotation, two calls. Nearest true statement: at K = 2, every orthogonal box
  of a given determinant sign needs two nested calls, A A for rotations and A diag(1, -1) A for reflections.
- g-4 6.3, "Z_bc(N) >= N (0.277 sqrt(k) - 1)" is true but its stated derivation falls short of it by up to 0.01 N once k is 52 or more.
  Nearest statement with a complete derivation: N (0.347 E|k - 2J| - 1) by g-4's count, N (0.347 E|k - 2J| - 1/2) by
  Buergisser and Lotz.
- s-cross hybrid 4, "plain or signed ... at least 0.17 sqrt(k) - 2 when some have [a coefficient 2]": true for plain
  pictures; for signed ones 0.119 sqrt(k) - 1.43.
