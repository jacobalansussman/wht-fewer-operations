Words (as in WAVE6-PICKS). N = 2^k points. *Wire*: a value the program holds, a vector over the N inputs. *Class* of a wire: the
wire scaled to odd denominators and reduced modulo 2. *Survivor*: a gate whose result has a new class; *dead*: any other gate.
*Excess*: e = gates - (N - 1) - (N/2) log2 N. *Brick*: the 7-gate circuit at 4 points. *The 19*: the 19-gate circuit at 8 points.
*Staircase*: e2-0's family, 7, 20, 52, 128, 304 gates. *Tensor product* A x B: A inside each of n blocks, then B across the blocks,
once for each of A's m outputs. *Join*: a gate adding two wires on disjoint sets (kind J of m-cross). *Shift*: a gate
w -> w - 2v that changes the sign of w on the support of v (kind S). *Merge tree*: the nested family of sets on which wires live.
New here. *Sign vector*: a wire with entries 0, +1, -1 (up to one free factor). *Affine tree*: a merge tree whose every node is
a set of addresses cut out by fixing some linear conditions on the address bits, and is split into two parallel halves (the
bit-order tree and every relabelling of it by an invertible bit matrix; the split direction may differ from branch to branch).
*Half-restriction*: a full wire restricted to one half of its node, up to sign.

## 0. In ten lines

1. [derived, section 3] **Theorem (hand proof, unreviewed). A circuit in which every wire is a sign vector supported on a node of
   one affine merge tree has at least N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N gates.** So m-cross's floor is a theorem in that class at every N:
   e >= 0 always, the 19 is optimal there, 16 points need at least 47, and the rate of any family of this kind is at least 1/2.
   [ran, `delta.out`] All 24 circuits I metered lie in this class (every wire a sign vector, every support an affine set,
   supports nested or disjoint), so no circuit of this kind can change the order or go below rate 1/2.
2. [derived] The proof is itself the composition law the brief asks for, in exact form:
   **e(circuit) = e(left projection) + e(right projection) + delta, with delta >= 0** (section 3). The cross term is a sum of
   such deltas. **It is never negative in this class.** [ran, `recurse.out`, 24 circuits] e is exactly the sum of delta over all nodes.
   The brick and the 19 have delta = 0 at every node and nothing else does. Tensor product: delta = m - 1 at each of the n - 1
   outer nodes, which is the (m - 1)(n - 1). Staircase: delta = s/4 - 1 at a node of s points (its joins there, less one).
3. [ran] The tensor law e(A x B) = n e(A) + m e(B) + (m - 1)(n - 1) holds exactly on 19 products built and replayed here
   (4 to 64 points, factors on the floor and above it). `compose.out`, `ways.out`.
4. [ran] The staircase read as a composition has **cross(m, n) = (3/4) m n - m - n + 1** exactly, for all six ways of splitting
   16, 32, 64 points. The tensor product has m n - m - n + 1. Only the coefficient of m n moved, from 1 to 3/4.
5. [derived] Growth law for any composition rule: rate = 1/2 + (e0/N0 + kappa) / k0, where the base has N0 points, k0 bits,
   excess e0, and kappa is the limit of cross(M, N0) / (M N0) as M grows. Tensor: kappa = (N0 - 1)/N0. Staircase: 1/2.
   Rate 1/2 needs kappa = 0 (adding a level costs o(N) above the floor). Below 1/2 needs kappa < 0, which line 1 forbids here.
6. [ran] In the 23 circuits of `ways.out`, gates = joins + shifts, no other kind, and **shifts never exceed (N/2) log2 N**;
   e = (duplicate joins) + (shifts - (N/2) log2 N). Every circuit with a full set of shifts has e = number of duplicate joins.
7. [ran] 19 x 19 at 64 points has 304 gates: it ties the staircase exactly (e = 49 both).
8. [ran] Without the agreement rule (partners free), the least number of shifts that join all rows from one centre is exactly

9. [derived] Answer to "what would the outer circuit read so that one inner saving serves all blocks": a partner supported on

    two full parents, and two sign vectors admit no such gate. The to-do list is the three hypotheses: sign vectors, one tree,

Where the excess sits [ran, `cutrank.out`; derived identity]. (N/2) log2 N is the sum of |t|/2 over the N - 1 internal nodes t, so
**e = sum over nodes of (wires on t - 1 - |t|/2)**. Per node size, summed:
brick: pairs -2, root +2. The 19: pairs -4, fours -4, root +8. Staircase 16: pairs -8, fours 0, eights +2, root +11.
Brick x brick: pairs -8, fours +8, eights -2, root +11. The root is always in debt by at least N/2 - 1; only thin nodes below
can pay it. In the tensor product each of the n - 1 outer nodes repeats its first join m times: that is the (m - 1)(n - 1).

Cut-rank [ran, `cutrank.py`]. Lay a wire out as a table (some address bits down, the rest across) and take the largest rank over
all such splits. Product wires have rank 1. In the brick, the staircase (8, 16, 32 points) and brick x brick, the wires of
rank 2 are exactly the over-limit wires, and their number is exactly the gates saved against the textbook (1, 4, 12, 32, 8).
h-1's second 52 has four wires of rank 3 and saves no more. No wire of rank 4 anywhere. (The measure depends on the choice of
address bits: the 19, whose tree is a relabelled one, shows 11 wires of rank 2 for 5 gates saved.)

## 2. Laws  [derived unless marked]

**Staircase.** e(N) = (N/4)(k - 3) + 1 [ran: 0, 1, 5, 17, 49]. Then cross(m, n) = e(mn) - n e(m) - m e(n) = (3/4) m n - m - n + 1
[ran: 5; 13, 13; 29, 33, 29].
**Any rule of the form cross = g m n - m - n + 1** has the solutions e(N) = c N log2 N - g N + 1, with c fixed by the base:
c = (e0 + g N0 - 1) / (N0 k0). Tensor (g = 1): c = (N0 - 1 + e0)/(N0 k0), the curator's formula. Staircase on the brick: c = 1/4.
**In general** divide by mn: e/N is additive up to cross/(mn). Composing the base onto ever larger circuits gives
rate = 1/2 + (e0/N0 + kappa)/k0, kappa = lim cross(M, N0)/(M N0).
So with a fixed base: a constant below 3/4 needs kappa < 1/2; rate 1/2 needs kappa = 0, that is, a new top level that costs
o(N) more than N/2 dead gates per address bit; a change of order needs the top level to cost o(N) per bit in all.
**Joins against shifts** [ran, `ways.out`]. From the textbook (64 joins, 0 shifts) each shift removes more than one join:
1.25 per shift in brick products, 17/12 in the 19, 11/8 in the staircase; the floor would be 49/32 at 16 points and tends to 2.
Nobody has more than (N/2) log2 N shifts.

## 3. The floor is a theorem for sign-vector circuits on one affine tree  [derived; hand proof; unreviewed]

**Claim.** Let T be an affine merge tree on N = 2^k addresses. Let a circuit have every wire equal, up to a factor, to a sign
vector whose support is a node of T, and let it hold all N rows of the transform. Then gates >= f(N) = N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N.
Any gates are allowed between such wires (joins, shifts, differences of two full wires, wires made from above).

**Proof.** Induction on N; f(1) = 0. Let L, R be the two halves of the root, G_L and G_R the gates whose result lies inside L or R,
W the set of full wires. Gates = G_L + G_R + |W|.
(a) *What makes a full wire.* Two wires on nodes give a full sign vector only as: a join of an L-wire and an R-wire; or a shift of
a full wire w0 on a node s inside L or inside R by a partner equal to +-w0 on s. Two full sign vectors u, v give no third:
a u + b v has entries of sizes |a + b| and |a - b|, equal only if a b = 0.
(b) *Projection.* Keep everything inside L and add, at L, the L-restrictions of all full wires. Each restriction not already
held at L is made by one gate from earlier ones: its first carrier cannot be a join (a join's L-part is held at L) nor a shift
inside R or on all of L (same restriction up to sign), so it is a shift on a node strictly inside L, and the same shift applies
to the restrictions. A wire inside L made from full wires is a restriction of one of them, or a difference of two of them, and
the same gate works on restrictions. So the projection is a circuit of the same kind on L. Since L is half of an affine
node, the restrictions of the N rows are the N/2 rows of the transform on L, each twice. So the projection has at least
f(N/2) gates; it has at most G_L + n_L, where n_L is the number of L-restrictions of full wires not held at L. Same for R.
(c) *Counting full wires.* Give each full wire its image (L-restriction, R-restriction). The N rows fall into N/2 pairs with
equal images, so the number of distinct images is at most |W| - N/2. On the other side: the first carrier of each new
L-restriction and of each new R-restriction have n_L + n_R different images (a shift changes one coordinate; if a first
carrier of a new L-value and one of a new R-value had the same image, each would have to come no later than the other's
parent), and the first full wire is a join whose image has both coordinates held below. So distinct images >= n_L + n_R + 1.
(d) |W| >= N/2 + 1 + n_L + n_R, hence gates >= (G_L + n_L) + (G_R + n_R) + N/2 + 1 >= 2 f(N/2) + N/2 + 1 = f(N). End.

**Exact form.** e(circuit) = e(projection on L) + e(projection on R) + delta, delta = |W| - N/2 - 1 - n_L - n_R >= 0.
delta = 0 means: one join at the root, and every other full wire is either the first carrier of a new half-restriction or the
second member of one of the N/2 pairs of rows.
[ran, `delta.py`: top level] On all 24 circuits: distinct images = |W| - N/2 exactly, distinct images >= n_L + n_R + 1, delta >= 0.
[ran, `recurse.py`: every level] It rebuilds both projections as ordered lists of wires, checks that every wire of every
projection is one gate (a join or a shift with a held, agreeing partner) from earlier ones, and recurses down to pairs:
no failure in 24 circuits, delta >= 0 at every node, and e = the sum of the deltas. By node size: staircase 64: 8, 12, 14, 15 at
sizes 8 to 64 (s/4 - 1 per node); 19 x 19: 28, 14, 7 at sizes 16, 32, 64 (7 per outer node); h-1's second 52: 0, 0, 0, 5.
At 16 points the same total is reached by different splits:

|---|---|---|---|
| h-1's second 52 (projections of 19 gates, on the floor) | 0 + 0 | 5 | 5 |

So h-1's second 52 already has both halves on the floor and pays everything, 5, in the weave at the root; the staircase pays

**Consequences inside the class.** e >= 0; cross >= -(n e(A) + m e(B)), so cross >= 0 for factors on the floor; 7 and 19 are
optimal at 4 and 8 points; 16 points: at least 47 (record 52); every family has rate at least 1/2. Morgenstern's bound for
coefficients 1 and 2 gives only 0.315 N log2 N [read: h-1], so this is stronger where it applies and silent elsewhere.
**What a 16-point circuit on the floor must be** [derived from delta = 0]: two 8-point floor circuits, one on each half (their
trees may differ), woven so that every shift at the root is a step of exactly one of them. With one wire per half this is a
set of 25 images in a 13 x 13 grid that touches every row and column and is connected by single steps, plus the 8 repeats.
That is a search over pairs of 19-gate circuits and paths in a 13 x 13 grid, far smaller than h-6's 32,768-vertex problem.

**Free partners** [ran, `freesteiner.py`, exact dynamic programme over all 8 and 128 sign vectors]. Drop the agreement rule: a
move changes the sign on any node. The least number of moves in a tree joining all rows is 4 and 12 at 4 and 8 points, equal
to the spanning tree of the rows. So at these sizes the agreement rule costs nothing and extra vertices gain nothing; step (c)
above explains why (it never uses the agreement rule). h-6's 46 shifts at 16 points (one centre) against 32 is then the first
size at which agreement has a price, if 32 cannot be reached.

## 4. The question to keep asking

*What would the outer circuit have to read so that ONE inner saving serves ALL blocks?* [derived]
In a product the outer join at an outer node is made once per inner coordinate: m times (tensor) or once per 4 points
(staircase, through a hub with two different inner characters on its two halves). To make it once, every other wire on that
outer node must come from the first by shifts. Changing the inner coordinate of a wire that spans B blocks means changing sign
on the same inner node in every block: B shifts with B partners, one per block, because the tree's nodes lie inside blocks.
One partner supported on (that inner node) x (all B blocks) would do it in one gate. That support is a node of the tree with
the inner and outer bits exchanged: an address map. It crosses the nodes of the circuit's own tree, so the circuit would have
more than N - 1 survivors and leave the class of section 3, which is where it would have to go to beat the floor.
What that costs is u-6-twotrees' measurement (its outputs were still arriving; I did not use them).
[guess] "Saving multiplies" in the published program is a statement about rank, not about gates: a level that passes fewer
numbers through a cut lets everything beyond the cut run on fewer coordinates, so the saving is proportional to the outer
cost. In every circuit here each block passes all m of its coordinates, and no wire has cut-rank above 3.

## 5. The wall, and what it is made of

One gate, one half-restriction. Step (c) is the whole (N/2) per level: N rows have N/2 images, and every other image is paid.
The hypotheses, as a to-do list:
1. *Sign vectors.* With entries of two sizes (1 and 3, say) a gate may have two full parents and change both halves at once.
   That is m-cross's Q gate or a step of order 2; u-7 reports what one such quotient can finish (at most 2 outputs).
2. *One tree.* A partner on a crossing support (section 4). Step (b) fails: the restriction of the circuit to L is no longer
   a circuit on L, because a crossing partner restricted to L is a new wire nobody paid for.
3. *Affine nodes.* If L is not half of an affine node, the N rows have N different L-restrictions, not N/2 twice; the pairs of
   step (c) disappear and the count has to be redone. I did not try a tree of this kind at 8 points.
The published unit breaks 2 at its centre [read: m-cross hybrid 4]. Which it breaks first at the level of gates is u-5's question.

## 6. Mutations (not judged)

- **a ledger for crossing wires** [guess, read off the proof's bookkeeping, not proved, not tested]. Fix any affine tree T and
  allow sign-vector wires on affine sets that cross T's nodes. In step (b) such a wire becomes two wires in the projections
  (one gate there for each half), and in step (c) a shift by such a partner changes both half-restrictions at once. Each
  costs the bound at most one gate per level of T at which the wire is cut. So the candidate law is
  **gates + (sum over wires of the number of levels of T that cut the wire) >= N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N, for every affine tree T.**
  If true it says how often a crossing wire can pay: once per level it crosses, at most log2 N times. Linear size would need
  about N wires that are each cut at a constant fraction of the levels and pay every time; the bound alone allows that
  (it only gives gates >= about N/2). First test: u-6's two-tree circuits at 16 points, and any 8-point circuit built with a
  partner on (inner node) x (all blocks). This is "one inner saving serves all blocks" as a count.
- **weave16**: decide 47 at 16 points in the class by the delta = 0 conditions (section 3): pairs of 8-point floor circuits and
  a connected set of images. If no weave exists, compute the least delta: that is cross(8, 2) for floor factors, exactly.
- **delta as a search objective**: give the annealer of h-1 (`sa.c`) the sum of deltas as its energy instead of the gate count.
  It says at which node a circuit wastes, and a move that lowers delta at the root of the second 52 from 5 is a 51.
- **non-affine tree at 8 points**: split the 8 addresses into two halves that are not a hyperplane and its complement; count
  what the proof's step (c) gives and search the sign-vector circuits on that tree. It is the cheapest hypothesis to drop.
- **two-size wires at the root only**: allow entries 1 and 3 on full wires, keep one tree; step (a) then admits gates with
  two full parents. How many images can such a gate reach, and does |W| >= N/2 + 1 + n_L + n_R survive?
- **what the wall does not cover: subset sums.** [derived] The rows of the subset-sum matrix have supports that cross (the
  sets "inside S" for different S overlap without nesting), so no circuit for subset sums has all its wires on one tree, and
  the transform built as two subset-sum circuits and a scaling (H = Z D Z^T) is outside the class by construction. The
  theorem puts no obstacle on q-cross's corner count. A floor for subset sums would need its own argument.
- **rank instead of gates**: look for a way to write the transform on a block as a sum of pieces that each pass fewer than m
  numbers through the block's boundary, and price the recombination. This is the non-rigidity route of the literature.

## 7. Criticism, last

- The theorem is a hand proof written in the last twenty minutes. The steps most likely to hide a gap: (b), that every wire
  inside L made from full wires is derivable in the projection with one gate; (c), the claim that first carriers have different
  images. Its test on data: steps (b), (c), (d) hold at every node of 24 circuits, all of which were built by people or
  programs that never tried to violate them, and none of which has a gate made from above or a difference of two full wires,
  the two cases of step (b) that are argued by hand only.
- The class is narrower than m-cross's "one tree modulo 2": it needs entries 0, +1, -1 and supports that are nodes, not only
  classes that are nested. m-cross's question about whole-number coefficients at 8 points is not answered.

  bit-order tree at 4 and 8 points only.
