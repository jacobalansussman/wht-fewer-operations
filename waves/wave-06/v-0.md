**Verdict: the floor holds. It changes nothing for the order: it is a floor for one narrow class, and the fact that this class
cannot be linear was already a theorem of 1973. What is sharp and (as far as I looked) new is the exact number.**

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

Words. N = 2^k points; an address is k bits. A *wire* is a value the program holds, a vector over the N inputs. A *sign vector*
is a wire whose entries are 0, +1, -1 after one scaling; its *set* is where it is not 0. An *affine tree* is a nested family
of sets: the whole cube, split in two parallel halves, each half split again in two parallel halves, down to single addresses.
A *flip* (u-8: shift) changes the sign of a wire on one node of the tree; in the class it is the gate u - 2v and needs a held
*partner* v equal to +-u on that node (the *agreement rule*).

Claim. If every wire of a circuit is a sign vector whose set is a node of ONE affine tree, and the circuit holds all N rows of
the transform, then it has at least f(N) = N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N gates (2, 7, 19, 47, 111, 255).

## 2. Are the two classes the same class? No. [derived; ran]

- u-9's class: the nodes are the cosets of one chain of subspaces, so every node of a level is split in the same direction.
- u-8's class: the direction may differ from branch to branch. It contains u-9's class.
- They coincide at 2 and 4 points. At 8 points u-8's class has, up to the symmetries of the transform (address maps and
  translations), exactly ONE more tree: left half split along one bit, right half along another ("twisted"). [derived]
- Both proofs prove the larger statement: the induction only uses that the two halves of the root are parallel, so that the
  N rows restricted to a half are the N/2 rows of that half, each twice. Nothing asks the two halves to be split alike.
- No circuit of the night uses the extra freedom: the 19-gate circuit lies on a chain tree [ran, `inclass.out`: pairs
  {0,7},{1,6},{2,5},{3,4}, fours {0,1,6,7},{2,3,4,5}, one direction per level], and u-9 reports "one flag" for all 31 [read].

## 3. The proof, read line by line [derived]

I re-derived what a gate can do between two sign vectors on nodes s, t of a tree. Only the ratio b/a matters, and only
+-1, +-2, +-1/2 can give a sign vector that is not a multiple of u or v. The results are: a *join* of two sibling nodes; a
*flip* of u on a node inside its set (partner agreeing there); a *restriction* of u to a smaller node. Two wires on the whole
cube never give a third wire on the whole cube. This is u-9's list; it is complete.

u-9 section 4, steps (a), (b), (c): correct as written. The count is gates >= (wires below and on the left half, plus new
left patterns) + (same on the right) + (whole-cube gates that add no new pattern), and the last number is at least N/2 + 1
because the rows come in N/2 pairs with the same two half-patterns, and the first whole-cube wire is a join.
u-8 section 3, steps (a) to (d): correct as well; its step (c) (first carriers have different images) is the same count,
done from the other side. I found no gap in either. Both are hand proofs; mine is a third reading, not a machine check.

**Neither proof uses that the partner of a flip is held.** Every step goes through if a flip on any node is allowed with no
partner at all. So what is proved is a floor for the *partner-free game*, and (section 5) f(N) is exactly the value of that
game. The same argument can therefore never give more than f(N): any gap above it is the price of partners.

## 4. My own exact check at the sizes claimed [ran]

**4 points** (`game4.py`, `game4.out`). The true game with partners, gates found by brute force over the six ratios (no
list of move types assumed), all subsets of the 12 possible wires: no legal play with 4, 5 or 6 gates; 12 plays with 7.

**8 points: 18 gates are impossible, on both trees, even without the agreement rule.** (`floor8.c`, `close18.py`.)
Method. Any play has at least one wire on each of the 4 pair nodes, a set F of wires on the two halves (both sides non-empty),
and a set W of whole-cube wires. Each whole-cube wire is a join of two wires of F or a flip of another whole-cube wire. So
|W| is at least the least number of edges of a tree joining a root and the 8 rows, where root-to-(a, b) is a join and every
flip on a node is an edge (no partner asked). That is an exact dynamic programme (Dreyfus-Wagner) on 129 vertices; I ran it
for all 14,400 sets F with at most 6 wires (7 or more give 4 + 7 + 8 = 19 at once).

| tree | F = 2 | F = 3 | F = 4 | F = 5 | F = 6 | bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chain (cube tree) | 4+2+13 = 19 | 4+3+12 = 19 | 4+4+10 = **18** (16 sets) | 4+5+10 = 19 | 4+6+9 = 19 | 18, then closed below |
| twisted | 19 | 19 | 4+4+12 = 20 | 20 | 20 | **19** |

The 16 sets with 18 are those where the two wires of a half are two rows of that half that one pair wire per pair node cannot
both build (for example + + + + and + - - +). The first pass treated the wires of F as free. `close18.py` then exhausts
these cases in the partner-free game with the wires of F to be made as well (by a join of pair wires, a flip of a held
half-wire, or a restriction of a held whole-cube wire): 16 sets F x all pairs of extra whole-cube wires (144 hang together)
x 16 choices of pair wires: **0 legal plays with 18 wires.** So 19 is the minimum at 8 points in u-8's class and in u-9's.
This closes the search u-9 left open (its annealer found no 18 in 8 runs; that was not a proof).
Limits: the argument that only these 16 sets need a second look is mine; the programme is mine and was read by nobody.

## 5. One size further, and the growth law [derived; ran]

**The partner-free game has value exactly f(N) for every N.** Upper side, built and replayed at 2 to 128 points
(`weave.py`, `weave.out`): one wire per node below the root (N - 2), one join, and a tree of (N/2) log2 N flips that reaches
all N rows. The tree is recursive: for every flip p -> c of the half-size tree do it on the left, then on the right; then
flip the whole right half once for each row of the half. Flips: 1, 4, 12, 32, 80, 192, 448. Wires: 2, 7, 19, 47, 111, 255, 575.
Lower side: the theorem. So at 16 points the partner-free value is 47 exactly, and the question "47 or 52" is only about partners.

**What partners cost in that tree** [ran]: it asks for 2, 8, 24, 64, 160, 384 different partner patterns at 4 to 128 points,
that is (N/2)(log2 N - 1), where a circuit on the floor may hold only one wire per node (N - 2 in all). If each had to be
bought with a gate, the total would be about N log2 N: the textbook count again. The 19 shows a cleverer tree needs only 6
at 8 points. So inside the class everything between rate 1/2 and rate 1 is the price of partners, and the staircase (rate 3/4)
is one way of paying it. I did not decide 16 points (47 to 52 stands).

Growth law of the class: (1/2) N log2 N + N - 1 <= least gates <= (3/4) N log2 N + N/4 (staircase: 7, 20, 52, 128, 304 [read]).
Order N log N either way.

## 6. Is it known? [memory; no web search in this box]

- The ORDER is known. With every wire scaled to a sign vector, every gate of the class has coefficients of size at most 2.
  Morgenstern (1973, "Note on a lower bound of the linear complexity of the fast Fourier transform", J. ACM 20) proved that a
  linear circuit with bounded coefficients needs a constant times log |det| gates, and |det H| = N^(N/2). With coefficients 1
  and 2 that is about 0.315 N log2 N [read: u-8, h-1]. So this class could never have been linear, by a theorem 53 years old.
- The exact floor N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N for this class I do not know from the literature. It is a small sharp lemma in the
  family of restricted-model bounds (Morgenstern; Chazelle 1998 on spectral bounds; Ailon, arXiv 1305.4745 and 1609.03278,
  on well-conditioned circuits). I did not search tonight, so "new" means only "I cannot name a source".
- A coincidence worth one line: the number of flips forced, (N/2) log2 N, is exactly log2 |det H|, Morgenstern's bound for
  coefficients of size 1. [derived; not explained]
- What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": inside any class with bounded coefficients, not linear. Linear size, if
  it exists, needs wires whose entries are not bounded in ratio (the published unit and u-9's "dirty wires" go that way).

## 7. In the user's terms

Nothing toward the order, and no constant. A circuit of this kind for n = N points has at least N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N
gates (G); saving exponent 0. Its use is negative and exact: every record of the night (7, 19, 52, 128, 304) lives in this
class, the class is closed at 4 and 8 points, and it is capped at half the textbook count for ever. Searching further in
it can at best move a constant from 3/4 toward 1/2.

## 8. What fails, and the nearest true statements

Nothing in the claim fails. Corrections to how it is told:
1. "The two classes" are not one class: u-8's is larger from 8 points on. The floor holds for the larger one.
2. "19 is optimal in the class" was a consequence of a hand proof plus an annealer; it is now an exhaustive result at 8 points.
3. The floor is the exact value of the game WITHOUT the agreement rule (all N). u-9's guess that the 5 gates at 16 points
   are the price of that rule is therefore the right question; the proof cannot answer it.
4. Not done: 16 points with partners; the twisted tree with partners (its floor is 19; I built no 19 on it); a web search.
