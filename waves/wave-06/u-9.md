# u-9 checkerboards: every record circuit of the night belongs to one class (sign vectors on the cosets of one chain of subspaces); that class has the floor N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N; a wire that carries a 2 outside its own set breaks the floor modulo 4

Lean, nothing published. Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (whole numbers or bit vectors; files in `u-9-checkerboards/`);
[ran, annealer] = a search, so an UPPER bound, replayed exactly; [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by nobody else;
[read] = from tonight's reports or the web; [guess].
Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free. Count (O), OpenAI's, is not used.

**No circuit below a known count for the transform or for subset sums, and no growth law below N log N. Nothing here changes the
order.** What is new: three small theorems with their checks (one of them a floor for the class that contains every record
circuit of the night), one replayed object that works only modulo 4, and a sharper question.

Words (WAVE6-PICKS, repeated). N = 2^k points; an address is k bits. *The transform* H: entry (y, x) is (-1)^(number of common
ones). *Subset sums* Z: entry (S, T) is 1 if T is inside S. *Picture, second difference M, corner, two-chain program*: as in
q-cross (gates = N - 2 + corners). *Checkerboard*: a 2 x 2 window 10/01 or 01/10 of a 0/1 picture; its second difference is +-2.
*Odd corner*: a cell where M is odd. *Merge tree*: repeatedly join two held sums; its *nodes* are the sets under each sum;
the *cube tree* is the one whose nodes are the sets of addresses that agree on the first j bits. *Letter*: the matrix as a sum
of rectangles (node of an output tree) x (node of an input tree).
New words here. *Parity matrix* B: B(y, x) = 0 or 1, the parity of the number of common ones, so **H = J - 2B** (J = all ones) and
"H modulo 4" is "B modulo 2". m(y, x) = the number of common ones, as a whole number. *Hyperplane of d*: the set of inputs x with
d.x odd; row y of B is the indicator of the hyperplane of y. *Form*: a wire whose entries are 0, +1, -1 (up to one scaling);
its *set* is where it is not 0. *Dirty wire*: a wire that, scaled to have odd entries on its set, has entries equal to
2 modulo 4 somewhere outside its set. *Junction point*: a helper row that is not an output (a hub).

## 2. H modulo 4: the crossing lemma [derived; checked]

Put outputs y_0 .. y_(N-1) and inputs x_0 .. x_(N-1) in any orders. Write d_a = y_a + y_(a+1) and e_b = x_b + x_(b+1) (bitwise, the
*steps*). Away from the added zero row and column the second difference of B is odd exactly when d_a . e_b is odd. So

   odd cells >= number of pairs (a, b) with d_a . e_b odd = sum over a of c(d_a),   c(d) = number of input steps e_b with d . e_b odd,

and c(d) is the number of times the input walk crosses the hyperplane of d. Signs on inputs and outputs change nothing here.

**Lemma.** That sum is at least k 2^(k-1) = (N/2) log2 N, for every pair of orders. The same holds when each side is any
spanning tree instead of a walk (each output = an earlier output plus running sums; each running sum = an earlier one plus an input).
Proof. Pick d1 with c least; then d2 outside span(d1) with c least; and so on: d1 .. dk, with c1 <= c2 <= ... <= ck, and
V_i = span(d1 .. di). (i) The input walk visits all 2^m values of (d1.x, .., dm.x), so at least 2^m - 1 of its steps change
that value, and each such step is counted in some c_i with i <= m: c1 + .. + cm >= 2^m - 1. (ii) The output walk visits all
2^(k-i) cosets of V_i, so at least 2^(k-i) - 1 output steps lie outside V_i, and each of those has c >= c_(i+1). Summing by
parts, the total is at least sum over i of c_i 2^(k-i), and with (i) that is at least
sum over m < k of 2^(k-m-1) (2^m - 1) + (2^k - 1) = k 2^(k-1). Both (i) and (ii) only use connectedness, hence trees. QED.

Equality: a Gray order on the outputs and the bit-reversed Gray order on the inputs, both ending at address 0.

Checks. [ran, `cutlemma.py`] At 4 and 8 points, ALL chains and ALL spanning trees (by their step counts: 1,183 and 1,394
different count vectors at 8 points): the least is 4 and 12. [ran, `ann2` mode 2] Annealer over both orders: 4, 12, 32, 80 in
every restart at 4 to 32 points, 193 and 194 at 64 points (floor 192), never below. [ran, `layers.py`] In the Gray pair the
odd cells are exactly (N/2) log2 N and the checkerboards exactly (N/2)(N/2 - 1) (2, 12, 56, 240, 992); the annealer's best
pictures have the same number of checkerboards. So q-cross's remark is now exact: **the transform's two-chain count is
(N/2) log2 N cells of value 2, a theorem, plus a quadratic number of cells of value 4.**

## 3. Any tree on the inputs, and junction points [derived; ran]

Fix any merge tree T on the inputs. For a set D of inputs let c_T(D) = the least number of nodes of T whose exclusive-or is D
(the whole set free: it is an output sign). [derived] c_T(D) = the least number of edges of T across which a 0/1 labelling of
the nodes changes, the leaves being labelled by D (this is the count biologists call Fitch's). A letter modulo 2 for B is a
network joining 0 and the N rows of B in which a step from one row to the next costs c_T of their difference (u-3's network
picture, read modulo 2).

- **Spanning theorem** [derived]. Without junction points the network costs at least (N/2) log2 N, for every T. Proof: the
  lemma's proof word for word; (i) becomes "labellings for d1 .. dm together label the nodes by m bits, all 2^m values occur
  on the leaves, so at least 2^m - 1 edges change". Equality on the cube tree and on Gray chains.
  [ran, `spanning.py`] 6,000, 2,500, 500 random trees at 16, 32, 64 points: never below 32, 80, 192; cube tree and chains equal them.
- **With junction points** [derived]: at least half, (N/4) log2 N, because in any metric a network with junction points is at
  least half the best network without. [ran, `steiner4.c`, exact dynamic programme over all 256 possible junction rows]
  At 4 points: 4 on every tree. At 8 points: **12 on every one of 140,006 sampled trees** (9,245 + 9,098 trees reach 12, the
  others need 13 to 15); junction points never help. [guess] The floor with junction points is (N/2) log2 N too.
- Transfer to letters of H itself [derived]: if H = sum of c_t x rectangle_t with coefficients of odd denominator, then B
  modulo 2 is a letter with at most (even-coefficient rectangles) + (odd ones) + (pairs of odd ones that overlap) rectangles.
  When the odd rectangles do not overlap (true of every letter I know of: one full rectangle) the floor applies to H.

## 4. The form game: m-cross's formula is a theorem on the cube tree [derived; small cases ran]

**Claim.** A circuit for H in which every wire is a form whose set is a node of the cube tree has at least
t(k) = N - 1 + (N/2) log2 N gates: 2, 7, 19, 47, 111. Quotient gates are allowed.
What a gate can do between two such forms [derived by cases on the two sets]: *merge* two sibling nodes (two sign choices);
*shift* u - 2v, v on a node inside u's set and equal to +-u there: it flips u on that node; *restrict*: (u +- v)/2 or u -+ v
with v agreeing with u on part of u's set: the result is u cut down to a smaller node; or produce nothing new. Each gate
makes one wire, so gates = wires.
Proof of t(k) >= 2 t(k-1) + N/2 + 1. Let X0, X1 be the two halves. For each half let V_i = the sign patterns (up to a global
sign) on X_i of the wires held on X_i, together with the restrictions to X_i of all wires held on the whole set. (a) V_i with
everything below it is a legal play of the game on X_i: a whole-set wire is made by a merge (its halves are held), by a shift
on a node inside one half (a legal shift of the restriction), by a shift on X0, X1 or everything (restriction unchanged), and
restrictions of whole-set wires restrict further in the same way. So |V_i| + (wires strictly below X_i) >= t(k-1).
(b) Call a whole-set gate *new* if it puts a pattern into V_0 or V_1 that was not there. Then gates >= 2 t(k-1) + (whole-set
gates that are not new). (c) The outputs come in N/2 pairs (y' with top bit 0 or 1) that have the SAME pattern on X0 and the
same on X1 up to sign. The first of a pair to be made already shows both patterns, so the second is not new. And the very
first whole-set wire is a merge, not new; if it is an output, its partner is not new either. So at least N/2 + 1 gates are
not new. QED. Equality [derived]: all sums of the tree (N - 1 gates), then the spanning network of section 3 by shifts.
Checks [ran, `cleangame.py`]: exhaustive over all sets of wires at 2 and 4 points, with merge, shift, un-merge and the
quotient move: 2 and 7. At 8 points the claim says 19, the record; I could not run the game exhaustively at 8 points in the
hour (it is a finite search over 152 possible wires, the first thing a skeptic should run). h-5 left "18 gates at 8 points"
open for its pair class after an exhaustive run up to 17; if that class is inside this one (I did not check its definition),
the claim closes it: 18 is impossible, 19 is the minimum.
**At 16 points it says 47; the record is 52; so for this class the truth is between 47 and 52.** [guess] The 5 gates are the
price of the agreement rule (a shift needs a held partner equal to +-u on the node), which the proof does not charge.
Search for a counterexample at 8 points [ran, `game8.py`, an annealer over sets of wires of the game, so no proof]: with 19
wires a legal play is found in 3 of 3 runs within 35,000 moves; with 18 wires none in 8 runs (six of 400,000 moves), the
best sets always keep 2 wires that cannot be derived.
Address maps [derived]: an invertible bit matrix A on the inputs, with its inverse transpose on the outputs, keeps H. So the
claim holds for every tree whose nodes are the cosets of a chain of subspaces V_1 inside V_2 inside ... (a *coset tree*).

**The night's circuits are in this class** [ran, `formcheck.py`, `formcheck_all.py`: exact replay of every gate-list file I
could parse under h-1, h-3, h-6, g-1, e2-0, h-0, p-*, u-*; 54 files compute all N rows of H]. For each wire: is it a form; is

## 5. The hatch: dirty wires [ran, `dirty.py`, `dirty.out`]

Section 4 needs "form". Drop it: let a wire be (indicator of its node) + 2 x (indicator of ANY set), modulo 4. Then:
- all tree sums (N - 1 gates); for each address bit i one carrier wire that receives the hyperplane of bit i by shifts on
  whole nodes (2^(i-1) gates; N - 1 in all); then split the k bits in two halves, give one half to the left subtree and one
  to the right, recursively; a node responsible for a set of bits holds one wire per combination of them, and a parent makes
  each of its wires with ONE gate, left + right, because (1 + 2a)(1 + 2b) = 1 + 2(a + b) modulo 4.
- Gates: 10, 26, 54, 110, 214, 418, 814 at 4 to 256 points, 3.2 N to 3.4 N and falling. All N x N entries equal H modulo 4.
  Every wire, divided by its power of 2 and read modulo 2, is the indicator of a cube-tree node (the program checks all
  wires). 280 of the 814 wires at 256 points are dirty. No division by 2 anywhere.
- So "one merge tree modulo 2, no quotient gate" does NOT force (N/2) log2 N dead gates modulo 4 (I did not run m-cross's
  meter on this object, so I do not know the orders of its dead gates). What forces the count is "every wire is a form".
  [derived from sections 4 and 5 together]
- m-cross's precision circuit (under 4 N modulo 4) uses the k hyperplane sums as wires, and those cross each other; this one
  hides them as the even part of tree wires. Same idea, but it shows the modulo-2 meter cannot see it.
- Why it stops at modulo 4 [derived]: modulo 8 the product of two sign vectors is their sum minus 1 plus 4 x (the common part
  of their two hyperplanes). The N common parts needed at the top merge are N different sets (codimension 2), and a parent
  can no longer make a wire with one gate. That term is the carry. By the known route (subset sums cut off at level j,
  `m-cross/precision.py`) each further digit costs about 2 N more, and k digits are needed because a row is a product of k signs.

## 6. Digits, layers and the count (N/2) log2 N [derived; identities ran in `layers.py`]

- H = J - 2m + 4 floor(m/2), m = number of common ones [ran, 4 to 64 points]. m has rank k and costs about 3 N. So **the
  transform and the matrix floor(m/2) cost the same up to O(N)**. floor(m/2) has rank N - k - 1 and N^2/4 + (k - 2) N/4
  corners in the Gray pair (4, 18, 72, 280, 1088) [ran].
- The matrix m = sum over j of 2^j x (j-th binary digit of m) is cheap, and its lowest digit alone is (J - H)/2. There are
  only log2 log2 N + 1 digit matrices. Each one separately is as hard as a member of the transform's family (digit 1 needs
  the fourth roots of unity). "The digits of a cheap matrix are each expensive" is the whole difficulty, said in one line.
- The layers of H = sum of (-2)^j B_j (B_j = number of j-subsets of the common ones) have ranks C(k, j) [read: precision.out].
  Layer j reads every input of weight >= j. Sum over j of the number of such inputs = sum of the weights of all addresses =
  (N/2) log2 N [ran]. Reading and writing together: N log2 N, the textbook count. So the brief's two outcomes resolve as:
  about log2 N layers, each of full size until j passes k/2; the sizes fall like a binomial tail, not geometrically.
- Second digit in two chains [ran, `ann2` mode 3: the matrix "second binary digit of m"]: 5, 25, 126, at most 556 odd
  corners at 8 to 64 points over both orders. Per point 0.6, 1.6, 3.9, 8.7: quadratic. Reason [derived]: for the first digit
  an output step differs on one of only k hyperplanes, each reused N/2^i times; for the second digit every output step
  differs on its own quarter of the cube, and one order of the inputs cannot keep N different quarters in few runs.
- H is the only matrix with entries of absolute value below 3 that is congruent to J - 2m modulo 4. So all of the cost of H
  beyond 3 N is the cost of making the entries of a rank-(k+1) matrix small again. [derived; it is the volume wall in 2-adic dress]

   smallest piece of the Sierpinski question, and the marginal cost of a binary digit of H.

   two inputs), and the determinant gives only N log log N as a floor. I found no direct method tonight.
