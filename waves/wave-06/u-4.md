Words (all from WAVE6-PICKS, repeated so this page stands alone). N = 2^k points; an *address* is k bits, read as a subset of
{1..k}. *The transform* H: entry (S, T) is (-1)^(size of the common part). *Subset sums* Z: y_S = sum of x_T over the T inside S.
*Yates*: the usual method for Z, (N/2) log2 N gates. *Two-chain program*: running sums F_b of the inputs in a chosen order, then
the outputs one after the other, each = the next output + a few F_b; it costs N - 2 + corners gates, a *corner* being a non-zero
of the second difference M of the 0/1 picture of Z in the two orders. *Wire*: a held value as a vector over the inputs.
*Class*: the wire rescaled to have odd denominators and not all entries even, then read modulo 2. *Survivor*: a gate whose
class no earlier wire has; *dead*: any other gate. Kinds of gate by the classes of the parents u, v and the result w: *J* three
different classes; *S* (shift) w has a parent's class; *Q* the parents share a class and w has another; *E* all equal.
*Order* of a dead gate: the largest m with w = (odd number) x (an earlier wire) modulo 2^m. *One merge tree modulo 2*: exactly
2N - 1 classes, any two nested or disjoint. *Over the limit*: (non-zeros of the wire in the inputs) x (non-zeros in the outputs) > N.

## 1. The object

[derived, as in the brief] (-1)^m = (1 - 2)^m = sum over j of C(m, j) (-2)^j gives H = Z D Z^T with D = diagonal (-2)^|J|, and
"J inside T" is "complement of T inside complement of J", so Z^T is Z with every address complemented.
Program `zdz.py` [ran]: reads a pair of orders (an output file of q-cross's `cat3.py` or u-0's annealer), builds
- pass 1: running sums f_b of x_(complement of cols[b]); outputs v_J (J = complement of rows[a]) from the last row upwards;
- pass 2: running sums g_b of (-2)^|cols[b]| v_(cols[b]); outputs y_(rows[a]) from the last row upwards;
writes the gate list (`name = a * u + b * v`, inputs x0, x1, ...; files `zdz_<orders>_<assoc>_<p2>.txt`), replays it with whole
numbers on all N basis vectors and compares every output with the row of H. All replays: True.
Two choices that do not change the count: *assoc* = how a step with several terms is added (seq: output + one running sum at a
time; run: the running sums among themselves first, then the output); *p2* = same picture for pass 2, or the transposed picture
(rows' = complements of cols, cols' = complements of rows; same corners). Orders used: q-cross's `cat3_4/8/16.out` (5, 13, 30
corners), `cat3g_32.out` (80), `cat3g_64.out` (261), and u-0's `r32.out` (80), `r64.out` (239), copied at 01:57.
My meter is inside `zdz.py`; `crosscheck.py` runs m-cross's own `meter2.py` on six of the written files: same kinds, same
survivors, same dead gates, same orders, same over-limit counts [ran, `crosscheck.out`].

## 4. What the readings say, as laws

**L1 [derived; fits every run].** In a circuit of this shape the meter's split is the split into passes. Pass 1 holds whole-number
sums, so its classes are sets and almost every gate makes a new one. Pass 2 starts from wires whose classes are the N up-sets
and whose scales are 2^|J|; a wire of pass 2 has the class of its least-scaled part, and adding anything more divisible by 2 is a
shift. So survivors = (cost of the Z^T circuit) and dead = (cost of the Z circuit), up to k - 1 gates and the wasted ones.
With Yates both are (N/2) log2 N. With two chains both are N - 2 + corners.

**L2 [derived from L1; the numbers of line 3 above].** For this family "fewer dead gates than (N/2) log2 N" and "a subset-sum
circuit cheaper than Yates" are one statement. m-cross's guess, carried to circuits of the other kind, is the classical open
question about subset sums with subtraction. It is not a new obstacle and it is not a new lever.

**L3 [ran].** Q gates: zero at 8, 16, 32, 64 points when steps are added one running sum at a time. Highest order: k - 1 =
log2 N - 1 (a shift by the full address of weight k while the least weight held is 1). Neither grows in a way that costs or
saves gates: the Yates control has the same shape with no Q gate and order 1 throughout. **So "Q gates of growing order" is
not the signature of a circuit of the other kind**, at least not of this kind. The signature is: about half of the gates are
survivors, classes that are up-sets (overlapping, never nested unless comparable), shifts by 2^j with j up to k.

**L4 [derived].** In every circuit for the transform, dead >= N - 1: the N outputs are N different wires of one class (every
entry odd), and at most one of them is the first wire of that class. [ran] The bound is reached at 4 points by `hubparity4.txt`:
a = x0 + x1, b = x2 + x3, y0 = a + b, c = x1 + x3, d = x1 + x2, y1 = y0 - 2c, y2 = y0 - 2b, y3 = y0 - 2d. 8 gates, 5 survivors,
3 dead, no Q gate, order 1, 9 classes with 5 overlapping pairs, every wire tight (h-0's sum 0).
And at 8 points by `hubparity8.txt` [ran, exact replay; both meters]: the hub y0 = sum of all inputs (7 gates), the seven
0/1 sums c_S over the inputs T with |S and T| odd (12 gates, sharing pairs), y_S = y0 - 2 c_S (7 gates): 26 gates, 19 survivors,
7 dead, no Q gate, order 1, 27 classes with 84 overlapping pairs, every wire tight (h-0's sum 2).

**L5 [ran: four 4-point circuits and four 8-point circuits side by side].** (survivors, dead, gates): the brick (3, 4, 7); Yates-ZDZ (4, 4, 8);
hub-minus-twice-parity (5, 3, 8); the textbook (3, 5, 8). The only one with 7 gates is the only one with an over-limit wire
(h-0's law gates + over-limit = N log2 N holds for all four). At 8 points: the 19 is (7, 12, 19), the textbook (7, 17, 24),
Yates-ZDZ (12, 12, 24), hub-minus-twice-parity (19, 7, 26). Trading dead gates for survivors has never saved a gate in
anything I metered: each dead gate removed cost at least one survivor. Going from 12 dead gates to 7 at 8 points cost 12 more
survivors starting from the 19, and 7 more starting from the Yates control.

**L6 [ran].** The share of gates that read two over-limit wires grows: 0%, 24%, 32%, 43%, 60% (58% with u-0's order). q-cross
argued that no wall of the night covers the two-chain circuit; these numbers are that sentence measured. The circuit is

## 6. Yes-and: four mutations, each run

are taken in an order compatible with inclusion: (H in the order p) = L D L^T with L lower triangular with ones on the
diagonal; L and D are unique when no pivot vanishes. Any such L gives the transform in at most 2 cost(L) gates (G), and also in
2 cost(L^-1) because H^-1 = H / N. So: is subset sums the best L?
[ran, `ldl.py`, `ldl_kinds.py`, exact fractions, exhaustive]

## 7. What the idea has turned into

[derived] In count (G), cost(H) <= 2 cost(B) for ANY matrix B with B D B^T = H, D diagonal (run B^T, scale, run B; the second
circuit is the first one reversed, which for a square matrix has the same number of gates: the classical transposition
principle). And an O(N) transform would give an O(N) such B, namely Z itself (Z = four transforms and five diagonals,
i-cross W5 [read]). So nothing is lost by asking only this:

**Write the quadratic form q(x) = sum over S, T of (-1)^|S and T| x_S x_T as a weighted sum of N squares of linear forms,
q = sum_J d_J (l_J(x))^2, so that the N forms l_J can be computed together in few gates. How few?**

Subset sums is the answer "l_J = sum of x_T over T containing J, d_J = (-2)^|J|" (the moments of x). What tonight adds:
- [ran] all the answers that come from elimination in some order of the addresses are as sparse as subset sums or less, and
  none has a better two-chain picture at 8 points (mutations 1 and 3);
- [ran] the meter modulo 2 reads such a circuit as "half survivors, half shifts", whatever it costs (L1, L3): it cannot tell
  a cheap answer from a dear one, so the three hypotheses of m-cross are not where the order is decided for this kind;
- [derived; one step run, mutation 4] the answers that do NOT come from elimination are B = Z Q with Q any rational matrix
  that preserves the form D (Q D Q^T = D). That is a large group (it contains a reflection for every vector u with
  u^T D^-1 u not zero); one reflection never gave a sparser B at 4 and 8 points; nobody has looked further, and g-0's mirror
  (H / sqrt N = identity minus twice a projection; 24 gates for the basis at 16 points) is its cousin with N/2 squares
  instead of N.
