# e-1-vcycle: one half-size problem that is not a transform, between two cheap frames

**First line, as asked: no circuit below the known count and no growth law below N log N came out of this.** The best object is a 16-point program of the idea's shape with NO middle at all, 62 gates built and replayed exactly on all 16 basis vectors (`e-1-vcycle/exp6.out.program.json`, made by `verify.py`), 58 by the transposition principle (that circuit was counted, not built), against 56 known and 64 textbook (count G). The idea turned into three sharper questions (section 6).

Tags: [read] [ran] [derived] [guess]. Count used everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a·u + b·v, scalings free. All gate counts of frames are **greedy upper bounds** (a pair-merging heuristic, `lib.py: cse_cost`), not minima.
Files: `e-1-vcycle/lib.py`, `exp1.py … exp11.py` with `.out` files, `verify.py`. (`exp3` was stopped by its time cap before printing; its content was rerun as `exp6`.) Exact integers and fractions throughout, run through `light.py`.

Words. N = 2^k points. H is the transform, S the same transform followed by a free swap of the address bits in pairs, so that its diagonal is all +1 ("relabelled form"). The **minus space** is the set of vectors that the transform multiplies by −√N; its dimension m is N/2 for H and (N − √N)/2 = 1, 6, 28 for S. A **cheap eigenvector** is √N·f − S·f, where f is a signed indicator of a coset of a subgroup D (a "coset atom"); S·f is again a coset atom, on a coset of size N/|D|. A **frame** is a list of m independent vectors of the minus space, written as the columns of L. Its **analysis** is "compute the m inner products with x" (N numbers in, m out); its **dual frame** R is the unique list in the minus space with R·L = identity.

## 3. Three exact statements about the shape "frame, middle, frame"

**3.1 The count.** [derived] With R·L = G invertible and R killing the plus space, S/√N = I − 2·L·G⁻¹·R. A circuit for "y = x − 2·L·c" is the transpose of a circuit for "x and Lᵀx", so it costs cost(analysis of L) + m. Hence
**program = cost(R) + cost(G⁻¹) + cost(analysis of L) + m.**
Check: 4 points, m = 1, u = (1,−1,−1,−1): 3 + 0 + 3 + 1 = 7, the known 7. With R the dual frame, cost(G⁻¹) = 0.

**3.2 No free middle.** [derived] Suppose R and L have linear size and the middle G⁻¹ equals A·h·B, with h the half-size transform and A, B invertible of linear size. Then h⁻¹ = B·(R·L)·A is a product of four linear-size maps: h was already linear. So "cheap frame, ONE transform call, cheap frame" can exist only if the transform is already linear; it cannot be how linearity is first obtained. The textbook escapes this only because its middle is 4·(I + h/√N), a transform plus the identity, and its frames are not cheap.
Also: any recursion T(N) = T(N/2) + c·N sums to a linear program, so the V-cycle is a **way of organising the search for a linear circuit, not a weaker statement than one**.

**3.3 Frames are ratios.** [derived; ran at 16 and 64, `exp6.out`] In the plain form, the plus space of H_N is the graph of Γ = (2/√N)·h − I, with h the half-size transform: the vectors (u, Γu). A circuit whose kernel is exactly the plus space is [K·Γ, −K] with K invertible. So:
**a cheap frame of the minus space exists if and only if there is an invertible K of half size such that K and K·h are both cheap** (then h = K⁻¹·(K·h), a "ratio" of two cheap maps).
"h is cheap" implies it (take K = I); the converse would need K⁻¹ to be cheap too, so it is a weaker-or-equal statement, and I do not know it to be known or studied. Pick 3 of this wave ("density") is its version with K a coordinate projection of lower rank.

## 4. What the failure is made of

**4.1 A coset atom and its image pay log₂N between them.** [derived] In any tree-shaped computation of coset atoms (sums and differences along a chain of subgroups: the wavelet-packet tree), the cost of a family is the sum over its members of log₂|D|. S sends an atom on D to an atom on a coset of size N/|D|. So member by member, f and S·f cost log₂|D| + log₂(N/|D|) = log₂N: this is the uncertainty principle |supp f|·|supp S f| ≥ N in the units of gates. A frame has about N/2 members: (N/2)·log₂N for analysis, the same for synthesis, **N·log₂N in all, for every choice of scales**, coarse, fine or mixed. This may be why "every count comes back to 64" in the first wave. For whole bases the same statement is a triangle inequality: the number of butterfly layers from the standard basis to a basis B, plus from B to the transformed standard basis, is at least log₂N. [derived quickly, not checked by machine]
- Seen exactly: all 705 perpendicular frames cost (N − m) + m·log₂N = 10 + 24 = 34. [ran]

**4.2 Where it leaks.** The statement charges f and S·f separately; the frame needs only the difference √N·f − S·f.
- When the supports of f and S·f overlap and cancel, the vector is sparser than both together: 4 + 4 − 2 = 6 non-zeros. Six such vectors can be analysed in **24 gates** (800 of the 4,048 independent choices), 4 per vector instead of 5.67. [ran, `exp5.out`] That is 3 gates at 4 points and 24 at 16: (3/8)·N·log₂N both times. [two data points; guess as a law] Reason: these vectors are (signed sum of column j) ∓ (signed sum of row i) of the 4 × 4 array, and the 8 signed row and column sums cost 24 in all. [ran, `exp6.out`]
- But then the frame is as far from perpendicular as it can be: every inner product is ±2 against a square length of 6, the Gram matrix is 4·I + 2·(a ±1 matrix), its inverse has 32 or 36 non-zeros of 36, and the dual frame has 80 non-zeros and costs 44. Total 24 + 44 + 6 = 74 again. [ran]
- The two extremes (perpendicular: 34 + 34; sparsest: 24 + 44) both give 68, which looked like a conservation law. **It is not one**: a search over 6-subsets of the 166 vectors found 26 + 26 = 52 (program 58), and random frames lie between 62 and 96. [ran, `exp6.out`, `exp6b.out`, `exp8.out`, `exp8w.out`: four searches, all stop at 52]
- 64 points (m = 28) [ran, `exp4.out`, `exp9.out`; greedy, and the search is weak at this size]: the sparsest cheap eigenvectors have 12 non-zeros (2,016 of them; then 21,420 with 16). The cheapest analysis found for a frame is **192 gates = (N/2)·log₂N**, not the 144 that (3/8)·N·log₂N would give: the 48 signed single-digit sums do cost 144, but each frame vector must then combine several of them. Its Gram matrix has 564 non-zeros of 784, the inverse 720, and the dual frame costs 692: program 192 + 692 + 28 = 912 against 336. The tensor frame of perpendicular vectors costs 246 per side, program 520. Random greedy attempts to complete a perpendicular frame of 28 from subgroup sizes 2, 8, 32 stopped at 12 to 18 vectors, 200 times out of 200; whether one exists I do not know.
- **Growth, as far as it ran:** (frame + dual + m) divided by N·log₂N is 7/8 at 4 points, 58/64 = 0.91 at 16, and at least 1.35 at 64 for the two frames I could cost (no search of the pair at 64). The frames lose ground with size in everything that ran; the per-vector analysis cost is 3, 4, 6.9 at 4, 16, 64 points, which is 4.1's log₂N per vector coming back.

**4.3 The simple floor of the shape.** [derived] The columns of any analysis R of the minus space are N pairwise non-parallel vectors in m dimensions (they are the projections of the N points, which are equiangular). So the transposed circuit needs N − m gates and R needs 2(N − m); the same for L. Any program of the shape "compress to m numbers, expand" costs at least **4N − 3m** for N ≥ 16: 46 at 16 points, about 2.5·N in general. (At 4 points m = 1, all projections are parallel, and the true cost is 7.) The shape is not excluded below 56 by this argument, and not by any argument I have.

**4.4 The frame recursion is a direct-sum problem.** [derived] With u = (1,−1,−1,−1): minus space at N = (u ⊗ plus space at N/4) + (u⊥ ⊗ minus space at N/4). A frame at N is one plus-frame on the contracted array and one minus-frame on three arrays: four arrays of size N/4. Done separately, the plus and minus frames together cost about 3·N·log₄N = 1.5·N·log₂N (from ε(N) = 4·ε(N/4) + 3N); the minus frame alone ran at 34 to 38 gates at 16 points and 246 at 64. "One child" means: serve those four arrays for the price of one plus O(N). That is the published result's kind of saving, asked for at full strength.

**4.5 A first look at Question B.** [ran, `exp11.out`; a weak search: one start from the standard basis and eight random starts, 270 seconds] At 16 points, K a basis of 16 coset atoms of any subgroups, mixed freely and not necessarily perpendicular, scored by cost(K) + cost(K·S). The standard basis scores 0 + 64 (the greedy does not find the known 56 for S itself) and no single exchange improves it; random bases start between 82 and 95 and none descended below 64. So mixing chains of subgroups does not by itself beat the tree accounting of 4.1. What was not tried: rows that are not coset atoms.

## 5. The free third of d-4, located

[derived from traces; ran at 4, 16 and 64 points, `exp10.out`] On d-4's subspace U (dimension (N+2)/3) the transform equals a free sign change T. U splits into a part in the plus space and a part in the minus space of dimensions ((N+2)/3 ± √N)/2: **5 and 1 at 16 points, 15 and 7 at 64 (both ran), 51 and 35 at 256 (formula).** The minus part consists of the minus vectors supported where T = −1, that is on a quadric of m points.
- At 16 points it is one vector per quadratic form, 16 in all, and these are exactly the 16 sparsest vectors of the minus space (6 non-zeros, entries ±1). [ran, `exp7.out`: the sign function of each support is an eigenvector of S, 10 with +4 and 6 with −4; S v = 4·T v on each]
- They are 16 equiangular lines in a 6-dimensional space with cosine 1/3. [ran] (16 is the largest number of equiangular lines that 6 dimensions hold: from memory, not looked up tonight.)
- They are a tight frame: **Σ v vᵀ = 8·I − 2·S, so S = 4·I − ½·V·Vᵀ.** [ran] V is a signed incidence matrix "point lies on quadric": 6 non-zeros per row and column, rank 6. At 64 points the minus part of the free third has dimension 7 (ran for one form), so "one vector per form" and this picture are special to 16 points. What is equiangular at every size is the set of N projections of the points onto the minus space, and those need the transform.
- Why [derived after the run; it agrees with it]: write P₁ and P₂ for the projectors "u·uᵀ/4 on the first base-4 digit" and "on the second". The transform is (I − 2P₁)(I − 2P₂)·4, its minus projector is P₁(I − P₂) + (I − P₁)P₂ = **(P₁ − P₂)²**, and V is 4·(P₁ − P₂) up to the signs of its columns. The diagonals of P₁ and P₂ cancel: that is the sparsity, and the pattern of non-zeros is the 4 × 4 rook's graph (same row or same column). With more digits the minus projector is the nested square ((P₁ − P₂)² − P₃)² …, which is no longer sparse: 64 points is a different animal, as 4.2 saw.
- The classical picture behind the numbers [from memory, not looked up]: 6, 28, 120 = (N − √N)/2 are the odd theta characteristics in genus 2, 3, 4 (Weierstrass points, bitangents, tritangent planes), the same list as d-4's elliptic quadratic forms and the E8 labels; 16 lines at cosine 1/3 in 6 dimensions is the largest equiangular set there.
- The 166 cheap eigenvectors of section 2 are closed under adding or subtracting two of these 16: putting all such sums and differences into the family leaves it at 166. [ran, `exp8w.out`]
- So "the frame built around the free third" exists and is the frame of 4.2: the cheapest analysis I found and the worst middle. The plus part of U (most of it) is where the formula does nothing anyway.

## 6. The idea, transformed

**Question A (finite, checkable).** At 16 points, relabelled form: the least value of cost(frame) + cost(dual frame) over ALL frames of the 6-dimensional minus space. Below 50 beats the known 56; the floor is 40 (that is 46 − 6); searches stop at 52 inside the family of 166 cheap eigenvectors with a greedy cost. Two things were not done: frames outside that family, and exact (not greedy) costs. The best frame and its dual are printed at the end of `exp6.out`.

**Question B (the order question in this language).** Is there an invertible K with K and K·h both of linear size? Coset-atom bases give exactly N·log₂N between the two (4.1). The families that break the "log of the support" accounting are nested ones: prefix sums have supports 1, 2, …, N and cost one gate each. A K made of nested, non-perpendicular functionals whose transforms are also nested is the object to look for; a tensor power cannot be one.

**Question C (for the engine).** Section 4.4: a unit that serves "three arrays with the minus frame and one with the plus frame" below four separate frames.
