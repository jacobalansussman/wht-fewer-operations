# b-0: criticism of e2-0's mixed-hub circuit family

> Editor's note (11 October 2026): this report was written before the paper of Coppersmith, Feig and Linzer (1994) had been read. The 4-point circuit in 7 operations and the rate 7/8 are due to that paper, not to the patent filed in 2002 or to Alman and Rao (2023). See [Earlier papers](../README.md#earlier-papers). What this report says about published work is as its author marked it: read through a tool that summarises, from an abstract, second hand, or from memory. Nobody checked those statements against the sources.

**FIRST LINE. The circuits are real and the counts are right, through 4,096 points in both counts; I found no paper that has them. But they do not change the order (still N log N, saving exponent zero), and the sentence "below the textbook in OpenAI's count" is true only for the transform divided by sqrt(N), which OpenAI's statement does not accept. For the transform OpenAI's statement asks for, the same programs cost MORE than the textbook at 16 points (at least 72 against 64) and go below it only from 4,096 points on. The "wall" is confirmed at 8 points by exhaustive search (20 is the least inside the class) and remains a guess beyond.**

Marks: [read] [ran] [derived] [guess]. Unreviewed work of one night; nothing in Lean. Folder: `waves/wave-02/b-0/`.

Words. N = 2^k points. H is the Walsh-Hadamard matrix, entries +1 and -1; the transform is x -> H x. A *gate* in count (G) is any a*u + b*v (multiplying by a constant is free). In count (O), OpenAI's, an addition, a subtraction and a multiplication by a constant each cost one. The *textbook* program is the usual butterfly: N log2 N additions and subtractions, no multiplications, in either count. A *piece* is one output of the transform restricted to a block of inputs; a *hub* is a helper value made of two different pieces on the two halves of a block.

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

1. (G) For every N = 2^k, k >= 2, there is an explicit program for H x with (3/4) N log2 N + N/4 gates, all coefficients +-1 or +-2 (7, 20, 52, 128, 304 at 4 to 64 points; textbook 8, 24, 64, 160, 384).
2. (O) When k is even, the same program, with each value stored at a suitable power-of-two scale, computes H x / sqrt(N) in (7/8) N log2 N + N/4 operations (8, 60, 352 at 4, 16, 64 points).
3. This is said to improve both leading constants of Alman and Rao (additions only, and all operations).
4. (wall) Programs made only of pieces and two-piece hubs on one fixed chain of halvings cannot do better than (3/4) N log N.

## 2. What I verified myself

- [ran, `fam.py`, output `fam.out`] My own generator. It shares no code with e2-0's: I re-derived the recursion by hand and wrote the coefficients down directly (no linear solving), then replayed every program in exact integer arithmetic on all N unit inputs. Every output is exactly a row of H, each row once, for k = 2 to 12.
- [ran, `replay_files.py`, output `replay_files.out`] e2-0's own files `slp_4, 8, 16, 32, 64.txt` and `oslp_4, 16, 64.txt`, read by my own reader with exact fractions: 7, 20, 52, 128, 304 gates, outputs exactly the rows of H; 8, 60, 352 operations, outputs exactly the rows of H/2, H/4, H/8.
- [derived] The recursion is a proof, not a pattern: a "half problem" on a block of size m costs D(m) = 2 D(m/2) + 3m/4 with D(2) = 1, and the whole transform costs 2 D(N/2) + 5N/4. I checked each of the three gate shapes by hand (hub = A + c*B; piece = hub + 2e*witness; piece = tau*(2*witness - hub)) and that the witness each gate needs is the piece its grandchild already made. So claim 1 holds for every k >= 2.

## 3. Does it scale: the growth law

[ran to 4,096 points, derived for all k] Exactly, with no drift:
- (G): (3/4) N k + N/4. Ratio to the textbook 3/4 + 1/(4k): 0.875, 0.833, 0.8125, ..., 0.771 at k = 12. It tends to 3/4 and never below.
- (O), outputs H x / 2^(k/2), k even: (7/8) N k + N/4. For k odd the outputs are H x / 2^((k-1)/2) and the cost is (7/8) N k + (5/8) N (so 26 at 8 points and 160 at 32 points: not below the textbook there).
- (O), exact H x: N more multiplications, (7/8) N k + (5/4) N for k even, (7/8) N k + (13/8) N for k odd. Below the textbook only when k > 10 (even) or k > 13 (odd).
The growth is N log N with a smaller constant. It is not a power saving and not linear.

## 4. OpenAI's statement and count

- [read, `R/OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/Core.lean`] OpenAI's circuit count is exactly count (O): the gates are `add`, `sub` and `scale c` for any complex constant c, one unit each; outputs may be named in any order for free. **So a halving is one operation. That part of the claim is correct.** [read, `RAM.lean` lines 163, 188] In the machine model the `scale` atom is also billed one, but the number 1/2 has to be made first (one, one plus one, inverse: three units, once).
- [read, same file] `Circuit.Computes C A` demands `C.eval x = A.mulVec x` exactly, and the matrix is the transform with no division. [read, `Goal.lean`] `DFTProgram` demands `result.val = dft n x`. [read, `R/WHTCheck/Solution.lean` lines 37 to 53] the Walsh-Hadamard statement demands `result.val = wht k x`, entries +1 and -1. **None of the statements accepts H x / sqrt(N).** I found no divided version anywhere in `R/OAI/`.
- [read] All three goals ask for less than every constant times n log n (`MainStatement`: for every c > 0; `TimeBounds` and `WHTTimeBounds`: little-o of n log n), and the machine model says constant factors are "immaterial". A program with (7/8) N log N operations does not meet these goals and a constant is invisible to them.
- [ran, `scale_ilp.py`, output `scale_ilp.out`] Could a cleverer choice of scales rescue exact H x at small size? I let an integer program choose a power-of-two scale (from 4 down to 1/16; powers of two are enough [derived], the range is my restriction) for every value of the family's program, sharing each scaled copy among all its users. At 16 points the least number of multiplications for exact H x is at least 20 (the solver's proved bound) and at most 24, so the total is between 72 and 76: above the textbook's 64. For H x / 4 the least is exactly 8 (proved), total 60: e2-0's count is the best this program allows.

**Smallest failing case of "below the textbook in OpenAI's count": 16 points, for the transform OpenAI asks for (72 or more against 64). Nearest true statements: (a) H x / 4 at 16 points in 60 operations; a fair comparison for that divided transform is also 64 (two rounds of the known 8-operation program for H/2 on 4 points), so 60 is a real gain there; (b) exact H x below the textbook from 4,096 points on (48,128 against 49,152), for even k.**

## 5. Is it known? (searched 2026-10-10)

- [read, arXiv:2211.06459, J. Alman and K. Rao, "Faster Walsh-Hadamard and Discrete Fourier Transforms From Matrix Non-Rigidity", first version 11 November 2022, second version 14 June 2023; I read it through an automatic page summary, and the numbers agree with e2-0's direct reading of pages 1 to 3] Theorem 1.1: exact H x in (23/24) N log N + (13/12) N operations, counting additions, subtractions and multiplications by constants; they say no algorithm with fewer operations than the textbook was known before. Their Theorem 6.1, from the 4-point trick (the 4-point matrix is "all ones minus twice the identity", up to order and signs): (7/8) N log N additions, (1/8) N log N halvings, N - 1 multiplications by a power of two. Their Theorem 6.2, from an 8-point trick: (22/24) N log N additions and (1/24) N log N halvings. Both are applied level by level; the summary found no sharing between adjacent levels.
- So the 4-point 7-gate program is known (it is their Theorem 6.1). **What e2-0 adds is the interleaving of levels, which turns 7/8 into 3/4 additions with the same (1/8) N log N halvings.** For exact H x, all operations counted: this family (7/8) N log2 N + (5/4) N (k even), Alman and Rao (23/24) N log2 N + (13/12) N. Leading constant 7/8 against 23/24; additions alone 3/4 against 7/8. Claim 3 holds on my reading.
- [read, search results only] A seminar announcement of 19 November 2024 (J. Alman, University of Chicago) still gives 23/24 as the best constant. Five searches for a later improvement (2024 to 2026, for 7/8, 3/4, "improves Alman and Rao") found nothing. I could not consult a citation index, so "new" means "not found by me tonight".
- Related results in other counts, not comparable [read, search results only]: Alman, "Kronecker products, low-depth circuits, and matrix rigidity" (2021), (1.81 + o(1)) N log2 N wires in layered circuits; Alman, Guan and Padaki, arXiv:2211.05217 (2022), depth-2 circuits of size N^1.443; Alman and Li, arXiv:2509.14489 (2025), a conditional lower bound for depth 2.
- One correction of perspective: OpenAI's paper and the published result of this project already give fewer than c N log N operations for EVERY c > 0 at large enough N. So as a statement about the limit, "the best leading constant" is already zero. The family is a statement about sizes people actually use: from 4,096 points (8,192 when k is odd, section 8) up to astronomically large N it is the smallest exact count for H x that I know of. Below 4,096 points the textbook is still the smallest for exact H x.

## 6. What known theory says about "linear or not"

- [derived, from Morgenstern 1973] If every gate has |a| + |b| <= c, a circuit for H x has at least (N/2) log2 N / log2 c gates (each gate can multiply the largest sub-determinant by at most c, and det H = N^(N/2)). The family has c = 3: at least 0.3155 N log2 N. It sits at 0.75, within a factor 2.4 of the best possible for its kind.
- [derived, a little more than e2-0 wrote] The same floor holds for EVERY program whose values are all vectors with entries 0, +1, -1 (up to a free scaling), whatever their shape: for three such vectors with p = a*u + b*v one always has |a| + |b| <= 3 (if the supports are not nested both coefficients are +-1; if one support is strictly inside the other the coefficients are +-1 and at most 2 in size; if the supports are equal they are +-1/2). So pieces, hubs, e2-0's "quadric pieces" (leak 1), the one-flip hub (leak 2) and the whole stabiliser pool are all walled at 0.3155 N log N. **No refinement of this family that keeps 0, +-1 values can be linear.** A program with C*N gates needs coefficients of size at least N^(1/(2C)).
- [read, search results only] Ailon (arXiv:1305.4745, 2013; arXiv:1403.1307, 2014): programs whose intermediate values stay well conditioned need a constant times n log n steps. The (O) form of the family is of that kind (every stored value has bounded size).
- So known theory predicts exactly what is seen: a better constant, the same order. A linear program must use large coefficients AND badly conditioned intermediate values. The family uses neither.

## 7. The wall lemma (claim 4)

The class: every value of the program is a piece on a block of ONE fixed chain of halvings, or a hub (one output of the left half of a block plus or minus a DIFFERENT output of the right half). The claim: such programs cannot go below (3/4) N log N.

- [derived, re-checked by hand] The one-gate part is right. A piece P of a block and a hub of that block whose two characters both differ from P's: P minus any multiple of the hub lives on half of the left half AND half of the right half, which is not a block, or is not a 0, +-1 vector at all. If the hub contains P's character on one half, the partner is twice that character on a quarter, and that quarter is a block of the chain exactly when the hub's two characters differ by the split direction of the level below. One such hub then gives all four outputs of its pair in one gate each. This is e2-0's mixed-hub lemma and I agree with it.
- [read] **The counting part is not a proof**, and e2-0 says so: derived only for programs "in which every piece comes from a mixed hub", "a guess for mixtures with textbook steps".
- [ran, `wall_ilp.py`] The class is richer than the argument uses. At 8 points it has 48 possible helper values (16 pieces, 32 hubs) and **568 one-gate derivations**; the family uses 20 of them. The ones the argument does not consider: a half piece as half the sum of two outputs, or as a hub minus a half piece; a hub as an output minus twice a pair piece; a hub from another hub.
- [ran, `wall_ilp.out`, `wall_ilp_cap19.out`] Test at the smallest size: the fewest gates of ANY 8-point program inside the class. An integer program over the whole class, 6 minutes on a busy machine: it found only a 21-gate program and proved "at least 18". Asked directly for 19 or fewer, 4 minutes: neither found nor excluded. So the solver left 18, 19 or 20 open. [ran, `wall_dfs.py 19`, output `wall_dfs19.out`] I then wrote an exhaustive search with no solver (pure Python: give every computed value one derivation, never more than 19 values, no value depending on itself): 6,257,809 search nodes, 224 seconds, **no program with 19 gates or fewer has all its values in the class.** With the family's 20 this gives: **at 8 points the least inside the class is exactly 20, textbook steps and all the extra derivations included.** Sanity of that search [ran]: allowed 22 gates it finds a program in one second (`wall_dfs22.out`); e2-0's 20-gate program has all its values in the class and all 20 gates in my derivation table (`wall_member.py`), so the search would accept it; allowed 20 gates it did not reach one within 330 seconds (`wall_dfs20.out`), which is a matter of search order, but it means the search has not itself exhibited a 20. The "none at 19" rests on one program of mine, written tonight, not cross-checked by a second method (the solver did not finish). By hand [derived] the three natural schemes cost 20 (the family), 22 (one hub serving all four outputs of its pair) and 24 (textbook). This is the wall confirmed at the smallest size, where e2-0 had only a guess for mixtures; it says nothing yet about 16 points or about the constant 3/4 in general.
- What is certain is the floor of section 6: 0.3155 N log N for every program with 0, +-1 values. So the true statement today is: inside the class the constant is between 0.3155 and 0.75; "0.75 is the end" is a guess with a correct local lemma under it.
- What the lemma does not cover, by its own hypotheses: hubs A + t*B with t other than +-1 (the only kind that leaves the 0, +-1 world and so the only kind that could pass the floor); hubs of three or four pieces; two chains at once.

## 8. In the user's terms

- Circuit size for n points: (G) 0.75 n log2 n + n/4. (O), exact transform: 0.875 n log2 n + 1.25 n (n a power of 4), below n log2 n from n = 4,096.
- Saving exponent: zero. The published program, n (log n)^(1 - 8.7625e-4), is smaller for large enough n; the family is smaller at every size anyone will run.
- Toward linear: nothing directly, and section 6 says why nothing of this kind can. What is worth keeping is the mechanism: one helper value serves two neighbouring levels, so each pair of adjacent levels saves N/4. It is a saving by sharing between levels, the same species as the published direct-sum saving. The question it leaves is the one e2-0 lists as mutation 5: the same skeleton with coefficients that grow with the level, which is the only way out of the 0.3155 floor.
- As a small unit for the engine [guess, not checked]: a unit that returns its transform divided by a power of two can still be used if the engine carries one global scale and multiplies the N outputs once at the very end (N operations in all, below the leading term). Then the 16-point unit at 60 for 64 is a constant factor 15/16, and the family itself already does better (7/8). A constant factor in the unit changes the constant of the engine, not its exponent, unless the engine's exponent depends on the unit's cost; I did not check that, and it is the one question here that could matter to the published number.
- For odd k, exact H x [derived, not run]: one textbook level on top and the family on the two halves costs (7/8) N k + (11/8) N, slightly better than the (13/8) N of section 3; below the textbook from k = 13.
