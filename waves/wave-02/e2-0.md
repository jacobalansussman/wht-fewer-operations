**FIRST LINE. A circuit below the textbook count and below the project's best, and a growth law with a smaller constant (still N log N, not linear).**
In count (G) (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free) the 8-point Walsh-Hadamard transform costs at most **20** gates
(my brief said 22; two reports of tonight, e-2.md and e2-3.md, have 21 and say "20: not found, not excluded"; the textbook is 24),

**T(N) <= (3/4) N log2 N + N/4** for every N = 2^k. Explicit programs, coefficients only +-1 and +-2, every output EXACTLY its row of H:

## 2. The 20-gate program, read as pieces  (`slp_8.txt`; first found form in `replay_P20.out`)

Split the 8 inputs into lo = x0..x3 and hi = x4..x7. Write y'_a for a 4-point character.
1. On lo, 5 gates: two pair pieces p, q (one per pair, NOT two per pair), the 4-point hub h = p - q (a character with one entry
   flipped), and two 4-point outputs h + 2x_i, -h + 2x_j. On hi the same, 5 gates, for the two OTHER characters. Total 10.
2. Two *mixed hubs*, 1 gate each: M = (y'_a on lo) + (y'_b on hi), with a and b **different** characters that agree on one pair and are opposite on the other.
3. Eight outputs, 1 gate each: one output of b is +-M +- 2*(pair piece on lo); one output of a is +-M +- 2*(pair piece on hi); the two
   remaining ones (same character, other sign on hi) by a textbook step from those and the half pieces already held.
The textbook holds every character on both halves (8 half pieces). This program holds each character on ONE half only (4 half
pieces) and pays 2 hubs. [ran, per helper, `walk_stab_1.out`] outputs one gate away: pair pieces 1 each, half pieces 2 each,
mixed hubs 2 each (4 if the missing pair pieces were held), the two 4-point hubs 0.
This is the staircase the brief asked for (item e): each pair piece is used twice at two different stages, once as an input
of the 4-point hub and once, two levels up, as the partner ("witness") that turns a mixed hub into an output.

## 3. The family  [derived, then ran at 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 points]

A cube Q of size m has halves L, R. Its characters are (alpha, +) = (y_alpha on L, y_alpha on R) and (alpha, -) = (y_alpha on L, -y_alpha on R), alpha a character of the half.
**Mixed-hub lemma** [derived]. Let M = (y_a on L) + c*(y_b on R), with a = (gamma,+), b = (gamma,-) the two extensions of one
quarter-size character gamma. Then each of the four characters (a,+), (a,-), (b,+), (b,-) of Q is one gate from M:
the partner of the first a-output is y_gamma on one quarter of R (which quarter depends on the signs), the partner of the first
b-output is y_gamma on one quarter of L, and the second of each kind follows from the first and the half piece.
**Half problem D(m)**: for every alpha, ONE of the two extensions, plus one quarter witness per alpha.
Recursion: D(m) = 2 D(m/2) + 3 m/4 (per gamma: 1 hub + 2 pieces), D(2) = 1; top: T(N) = 2 D(N/2) + 5 N/4 (per gamma: 1 hub + 4 outputs).
The signs close up with one uniform rule (`gen.py`: left child asks for the + extensions, right child for the - extensions,
hub sign c = -(sign asked of this node)*(top bit of gamma)); the witness a node needs is exactly the half piece its grandchild already made.
Solution: D(m) = m (3 log2 m - 1)/4 and **T(N) = (3/4) N log2 N + N/4**.

| N | textbook | best before (32, 64: by tensoring the 7 and the 21) | this family (G) [ran, exact] | ratio to textbook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 0.875 |
| 8 | 24 | 21 (tonight; 22 in my brief) | **20** | 0.833 |
| 16 | 64 | 56 | **52** | 0.8125 |
| 32 | 160 | 140 | **128** | 0.800 |
| 64 | 384 | 336 | **304** | 0.792 |

Per size of support the family holds N/2 pieces and N/4 hubs, where the textbook holds N pieces. Helper supports at 64 points: 32, 48, 48, 48, 48, 16 for sizes 2..64.
Where the gain over 7/8 comes from [derived]: the non-interleaved version (quarter transforms, then hubs) obeys T(N) = 4 T(N/4) + 7N/4, which is the
old 7/8. Interleaving, so that every level's hub input is the next level's witness, makes EVERY pair of adjacent levels save N/4 instead of every second pair.

## 4. The same programs in OpenAI's count (O)  [ran, `oprice2.py`; explicit programs `oslp_4.txt`, `oslp_16.txt`, `oslp_64.txt` written by `oslp.py` and checked by the independent `ocheck.py`, output `ocheck.out`]

[read, R/OAI/Computability/FourierTransform/RAM.lean lines 163 and 188] the model has a `scale` atom billed one, so a multiplication by a constant is charged 1, as count (O) says.

Hold a point of support 2^j multiplied by 2^(-floor(j/2)) and a hub at the scale of its two pieces. Then a gate "hub +- 2*witness"
needs no multiplication at odd levels and ONE halving of the hub at even levels. No gate has the form -u-v.

| N | additions | multiplications | total (O), outputs = H x / sqrt(N) | textbook for H x | exact H x (N more multiplications) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 1 | 8 | 8 | 12 |
| 8 | 20 | 6 | 26 (outputs H x / 2) | 24 | 34 |
| 16 | 52 | 8 | **60** | 64 | 76 |
| 32 | 128 | 32 | 160 (outputs H x / 4) | 160 | 192 |
| 64 | 304 | 48 | **352** | 384 | 416 |

So for N = 4^t the orthonormal transform H/sqrt(N) costs at most (7/8) N log2 N + N/4 in count (O): 60 at 16 points, where the
project's standing fact was "16 points 64, and the count always comes back" (the textbook's 64 is for H x; for H x/4 it would pay 16 more). For H x itself add N multiplications:
(7/8) N log2 N + (5/4) N, which is above the textbook until log2 N > 10 and below it after. For odd k the top level costs N/2 more.
`ocheck.out`: oslp_16.txt has 60 lines (20 additions, 32 subtractions, 8 halvings) and its outputs are exactly the 16 rows of H/4; oslp_64.txt has 352 and gives H/8.
My first pricing by annealing (`oprice.out`) was worse and is superseded. What is NOT checked: whether OpenAI's statement wants H x itself (then 76 at 16 points, above 64) and whether the engine can use a unit that returns H x / sqrt(N).

## 5. The wall, as a lemma, and where it leaks

**Lemma (wall)** [derived by hand, case check on the three gate shapes]. Take programs whose points are (i) characters restricted to the
cubes of one fixed chain of halvings and (ii) hubs joining two such pieces on the two halves of a cube. Then a piece
(y_alpha on L, +-y_alpha on R) can be made in one gate only if y_alpha on L or y_alpha on R is held: with a hub (y_a on L, c*y_b on R),
a and b both different from alpha, the partner would live on half of L AND half of R, which is not a point of the class.
So the number of pieces of one size is at least the number of different characters among the pieces one size up.
That alone would allow a geometric fall (N, N/2, N/4, ... pieces: a LINEAR program). What forbids it [derived]: the fall needs
every hub to yield four pieces at every level; the witness of a hub with characters a on L and b on R exists only if a + b is the
split direction delta of the level below; four pieces per hub below makes the set A of characters held on L closed under adding
delta; and the set held on R is A + delta, which must not meet A. Contradiction. With two pieces per hub the count stays at
N/2 pieces and N/4 hubs per size, which is the family: **(3/4) N log N is the end of two-piece hubs on one chain** [derived for programs
in which every piece comes from a mixed hub; a guess for mixtures with textbook steps].
The contradiction has one hypothesis that can be attacked: *the witness is a piece of the same chain*.
[derived, from failproof P1] Volume: all coefficients here have |a| + |b| <= 3, and Morgenstern's bound for that is
(1/2) N log2 N / log2 3 = 0.315 N log N. So with coefficients 1 and 2 the truth lies between 0.315 and 0.75 N log N; linear needs coefficients that grow.
**Leak 1** [derived]. Drop the fixed chain: P_alpha = (y_a on L, c*y_b on R) + 2*(y_alpha on S) holds for a, b both different from
alpha, with S = (half of L along alpha+a) union (half of R along alpha+b). S is not a subcube; it is the zero set of a quadratic
form. So a hub CAN serve a character it does not contain, at the price of one piece on a quadric. Whether such pieces can be shared is untested.
**Leak 2** [derived]. The one-flip hub r = y_0 - 2e_a is one gate from ALL N outputs for every N; the partners are the N - 1 sums over
"a hyperplane through a, minus a" (at 4 points: single inputs, free; at 8 points: the seven lines of the Fano plane, 14 gates, total 26 to 29; counted by hand, not run).
So the transform equals, up to O(N) gates, multiplication by the point-hyperplane incidence matrix of projective space, a circulant
of size N - 1 (the Singer cycle). At 8 points every +-1 hub is of one of two kinds: 2 flips (4 outputs at distance 2) or 1 flip (1 output at distance 1, 7 at distance 3).

## 6. Position in the literature  [read: Alman and Rao, arXiv:2211.06459v2, pages 1 to 3, June 2023]

Their Theorem 1.1: H x in (23/24) N log N + (13/12) N field operations, of which (22/24) N log N additions. Their Section 6.1 variant:
(7/8) N log N additions and subtractions plus (1/8) N log N halvings plus N multiplications. This family: (3/4) N log N additions plus
(1/8) N log N halvings plus at most N multiplications, total (7/8) N log N + O(N). If section 4 survives an independent check, both of their
leading constants are improved; the project's notes (`lit-other-models.md`) found no later improvement of 23/24. I did not search further tonight.
In OpenAI's model a constant at N log N is below the published exponent saving asymptotically; the possible use is as a small unit.

## 7. Answers to the brief's questions

- Which pieces do the shortest programs found use, and do they halve? Subcubes of sizes 2, 4, ..., N and two-piece hubs. They halve,
  but only HALF of the textbook's pieces are needed at each size. No program found uses a piece that is not a subcube or a two-subcube hub.
- One helper serving a number of outputs that grows with N: yes, the one-flip hub serves all N, with partners that cost as much as the transform (leak 2). With cheap partners the count seen is 4.
- What replaces "perspective from a point" at 8 points: a third frame. Each mixed hub is the centre from which two pair pieces are
  projected onto two outputs (y = 2*pair - M, y' = 2*pair' + M); two such centres, and the rest by textbook steps.

## 8. Mutations

1. Quadric pieces (leak 1) at 16 points: put "y_alpha on a union of two quarters of different directions" into the pool and search below 52.
2. Check the (O) count 60 at 16 points with the project's own checker, and ask whether the engine can take the mixed hub as a unit.
3. Exact 8-point minimum in the stabiliser pool by a search in C using the symmetry group (order at least 21,504) and the 35-lines regularity: is it 20?
4. Three-level interleaving: a hub of four quarter pieces of four characters whose witnesses sit three levels down; the lemma does not cover it.
5. Growing coefficients on the same skeleton: M = A + t*B with t depending on the level (the family K_t), since Morgenstern says the constant cannot fall below 0.315 with 1 and 2.
6. The Fourier transform: the same hub with a root of unity, M = (chi_a on L) + omega*(chi_b on R).
7. The circulant of leak 2: multiplication by a fixed element of a cyclotomic field that lies in a small subfield (at 8 points it is (-1 + sqrt(-7))/2).
