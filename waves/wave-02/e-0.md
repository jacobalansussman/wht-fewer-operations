# wht39 / ideas / e-0-ring: the transform as multiplication by one number

## 0. The words used

- N = 2^k addresses, read as the field with N elements; theta a generator of its non-zero elements; n = N - 1.
- **The ring**: polynomials in t modulo t^n - 1. "t" is the relabelling "multiply the address by theta". On the non-zero addresses the transform is multiplication by M = J - 2*mask, where J is the sum of all powers of t and **mask** is the sum of t^m over the m with Trace(theta^m) = 1 (N/2 terms).
- **Piece**: the ring splits into one field per divisor d of n (the d-th roots of unity). The **mixed piece** is d = n, where everything difficult lives.
- **Letter**: an element with 2 or 3 terms. Used as a numerator it costs (terms - 1) gates per entry (G). Used as a denominator (solve around the cycle) it costs about twice that.
- **Prime above 2, valuation**: on each piece the number 2 factors into several prime ideals, one for each class of exponents j under doubling (j, 2j, 4j, ...). The valuation of an element at the prime j is how many times that prime divides its value at t = zeta^j. I compute it honestly: plug the 2-adic lift of theta^j into the element and count factors of 2 (`val.py`).
- s(j): the number of ones in the binary expansion of j.

## 1. One exact identity at 16 points [ran, exact, `verify16.py`, `unit16.py`]

    mask = -(1 + t + t^4)(1 + t^2 + t^8) + J - C3 + C5        in Z[t]/(t^15 - 1)
    M    = 2 (1 + t + t^4)(1 + t^2 + t^8) - J + 2 C3 - 2 C5

C3 = 1 + t^5 + t^10 and C5 = 1 + t^3 + t^6 + t^9 + t^12 are the sums over the subgroups of order 3 and 5: in the plane developer's language, the ray sums and the circle sums. 1 + t + t^4 is the field's own defining polynomial x^4 + x + 1 read at the shift, and the second factor is the same thing after the free relabelling t -> t^2. So **at 16 points the transform is: one three-term pass, the same pass again after squaring the addresses, and three cheap corrections (total, rays, circles).** `verify16.py` checks the polynomial identity coefficient by coefficient and checks that the matrix it describes is the 16-point Walsh-Hadamard matrix up to relabelling.

- On the mixed piece alone, all 36 pairs of letters from {1 +- t +- t^4, 1 +- t^2 +- t^8} give the mask exactly, with at most ONE extra two-term letter (1 - t^a) as numerator or denominator, and four of them with none: -(1+t+t^4)(1+t^2+t^8), (1-t-t^4)(1-t^2-t^8), -t^10 (1-t-t^4)^2, -t^5 (1-t^2-t^8)^2. So there the mask is, up to a relabelling, **the square of one three-term element**. The plane developer's quadratic D^2 - D + 4 = 0 is this fact.
- Cost: the two three-term passes alone are 2 * 2 * 15 = 60 gates (G); the best known is 56 (G), the textbook 64. **Not a cheaper circuit.** [derived] It could not have been: the 15 non-zero entries must each receive at least 4 gates on this route, and 56 leaves 3.7.
- The brief asked what a denominator buys at 16 points: nothing is needed. Zero denominators suffice.

## 2. The ledger: which letters carry anything [ran k = 4, 5, 6; derived in general]

- **[ran] Stickelberger's rule holds exactly as recalled by the plane developer**: the valuation of the mask at the prime j is k - 1 - s(j). At k = 4: (2, 1, 1, 0) at j = 1, 3, 5, 7. At k = 5: (3, 2, 2, 1, 1, 0) at j = 1, 3, 5, 7, 11, 15. At k = 6 likewise at all twelve primes. The norm of the mask on every piece is a pure power of 2.
- **[derived, and ran for coefficients +-1, +-2, +-3 at k = 4, 5, 6] Two-term letters carry nothing, whatever the coefficients, numerator or denominator.** Proof: scale a + b t^m so that a, b are 2-adic integers not both even. If one is even the value is a unit. If both are odd the value is 1 + theta^(jm) modulo the prime, which is zero only when theta^(jm) = 1, and there the letter is the plain scalar a + b. The same holds with Gaussian coefficients. So any product of two-term letters and their inverses has the SAME valuation at every prime of a piece, and the mask does not (2 against 0 at 16 points). **No number of two-term resolvents, with any coefficients, gives the transform in this ring.** This settles wild 72 in its literal form.
- **[ran] Three-term letters with coefficients +-1 carry valuation exactly 1, at the primes j where 1 + x^a + x^b vanishes at x = theta^j, and nowhere else.** Never 2 (all letters at k = 4, 5).
- **[ran] Which letters are "prime" (norm a pure power of 2):**
  - k = 4: 1 +- t +- t^4 and its twist: norm 2^4 on the mixed piece. This is why section 1 exists.
  - k = 5 (n = 31, one piece only): **none.** All 120 prime-carrying letters have norm 2^5 times 373, 683, 311 or 1489, each 30 times (373 for signs + +, 1489 for - -, the other two for mixed signs). Each of those odd primes is 1 modulo 31, so it splits into 30 primes and the 30 letters of one sign pattern, which are the images of one another under t -> t^u, sit on 30 different ones: [derived] no product or quotient of such letters cancels the odd part. **At 32 points the mask is not a product of +-1 three-term letters, two-term letters and their inverses at all.**
  - k = 6: 36 pure letters, and the only ones that touch the mixed piece are polynomials in t^9 or t^21, that is, letters of the subfields with 8 and 4 elements pulled back. Their valuation is a function of j modulo 7 (pattern 1,0,1,0,1,0 on the six mixed primes) or constant.
- [derived] Why purity dies: the norm of a generic three-term letter grows like 1.38^phi(n) (1.38 is the Mahler measure of 1 + x + y [recalled]; at k = 5 it predicts 16,000 against the observed 2^5 * 373 = 11,936), while a pure letter touching r primes has norm 2^(k r). So for large n every sparse letter drags odd primes with it.
- [derived + recalled] The true primes of this problem are the Jacobi sums (quotients of Gauss sums); as ring elements they have about N terms, as dense as the mask.

## 3. What a denominator buys [derived]

Take any identity mask * (product of denominators g) = (rational c) * t^s * (product of numerators f) on the mixed piece, integer coefficients. Write |f| for the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of f. Since |f(zeta)| <= |f| in every embedding, the average over the primes of the valuation of f is at most log2 |f|. Hence

- numerators only: the sum of log2 |f| over the letters is at least the mean of k - 1 - s(j), which is (k - 2)/2. This is Morgenstern's bound read inside the ring.
- with denominators AND the free scalar c: the scalar recentres the pattern, the numerators pay for the primes above the centre and the denominators for those below, and the bound drops to the mean absolute deviation of a binomial, **about sqrt(k / (2 pi))**:

| k | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 16 | 64 | 256 | 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| numerators only, (k-2)/2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 7 | 31 | 127 | 511 |
| with denominators and a free scalar | 0.57 | 0.87 | 1.07 | 1.22 | 1.57 | 3.18 | 6.38 | 12.76 |

**That is the answer to "what does a denominator buy that no numerator can": negative valuations. With them the size budget of the letters falls from log N to sqrt(log N).** The same sqrt(k) appeared twice in the first wave (the overlap window 2.25 sqrt k and the Schur-Weyl count 1.6 sqrt k in `i-wild.md`). It is only a budget, a lower bound that stops biting; section 4 says why this ring cannot spend it.

## 4. The wall in this ring, and what it is made of

**[ran, `roots.py`, exact] One sparse letter sees almost none of the primes.** Number of mixed-piece exponents j (out of phi(n)) at which the best three-term letter not coming from a subfield vanishes: 4 of 8 (k = 4), 6 of 36 (k = 6), 16 of 128 (k = 8), 20 of 600 (k = 10). In primes: 1, 1, 2, 2 out of 2, 6, 16, 60. The average letter sees 1.6 to 1.8 exponents. [recalled, unchecked: Kelley 2016, a polynomial with T terms over the field with q elements has at most about 2 q^(1 - 1/(T-1)) roots outside the cosets where it vanishes identically; the run agrees with sqrt(N) for T = 3.]

The mask has a valuation different from any chosen centre at all but about 0.8/sqrt(k) of the primes. So:

**Lemma (candidate, [derived] from the run and the recalled bound).** In Z[t]/(t^n - 1), if the mask equals (rational) * (monomial) * (a product of letters and inverses of letters, each with at most T terms, any integer coefficients) on the mixed piece, then the number of letters is at least about N^(1/(T-1)) / 2; for three-term letters at least sqrt(N)/2.

- Consequence: with three-term letters the cost is at least N^1.5, the cost of applying the plane's line mask D directly. Letters with more terms see more primes (the Frobenius orbit t + t^2 + t^4 + ..., k terms, vanishes at half of them), but the lemma's cost bound, (T - 1) * N^(1/(T-1)) / 2 gates per entry, is smallest near T = 0.7 k, where it is about k per entry. **Inside this ring the bound never falls below the textbook's order.**
- The tower does not rescue it. [derived] The level mask D of the plane (trace to the field with q = sqrt(N) elements equal to 1) has valuation e - (number of carries when the two halves of j are added), e = k/2: again different at almost every prime. "One mask per level at O(1)" would need a sparse element whose valuations count carries. Subfield letters only give functions of j modulo q - 1, and the number of carries is not such a function.
- [derived] **The other commutative ring fails for a different reason.** In the ring of digit flips (the additive group) the transform is, up to free diagonal phases, the element with eigenvalue i^(weight of a). A letter with T terms has an eigenvalue that depends on at most T - 1 parities of a, and a product of letters and inverses on the span of all of them; if the span is not everything there is a non-zero a it cannot see, and i^weight changes under adding it. So the sum of (T - 1) over the letters is at least k: **the textbook is exactly optimal among products of sparse elements and their inverses in the additive ring**, denominators included. (There a denominator buys nothing at all: the inverse of 1 + c*flip is again two-term.)
- What the two failures are made of: in the multiplicative ring a sparse letter has too few roots; in the additive ring it has too small a span. Both use commutativity (eigenvalues multiply). Neither says anything about a product that alternates the two kinds of relabelling.

## 5. The Hankel anomaly: the guess is refuted, and the anomaly is bigger than reported [ran, `hankel.py`, `hankel.out` to `hankel4.out`, `hankel_exact.py`]

The Hankel matrix of one period (size N/2, entry c[start + i + j]) depends on where the period starts. Scanning every starting point and every primitive polynomial, modulo one large prime (full rank modulo a prime is full rank exactly; a lost rank is a candidate until redone exactly):

| k | n | singular (start, polynomial) pairs | largest lost rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 7 | 0 of 14 | |
| 4 | 15 | 14 of 30 | 2 |
| 5 | 31 | 0 of 186 | |
| 6 | 63 | 144 of 378 | 4 |
| 7 | 127 | 0 of 2286 | |
| 8 | 255 | 170 of 4080 | 11 |

- **The curator's guess (a repeated prime factor of n) is refuted**: 15 and 255 have none and are singular at many starting points. The first wave saw "31 of 32 at k = 6 only" because it looked at one starting point per k.
- **What is true in the data: never singular for k = 3, 5, 7; often singular for k = 4, 6, 8.** The singular starting points come in runs with lost rank 1, 2, ..., r, r, ..., 2, 1 (r up to 11 at k = 8), the signature of a square block of zeros in the table of Hankel determinants.
- [derived] What a lost rank means for us: the Hankel matrix is the block of the transform between an arc of N/2 consecutive logarithms and another such arc. Lost rank r = an r-dimensional space of inputs supported on half the multiplicative circle whose transform vanishes on half the circle. At 256 points there is an 11-dimensional such space. [guess] This is subfield structure (even k has the field with 4 elements, or the plane over the field with sqrt(N) elements); see the next lines for what k = 9 and k = 10 say.
- **[ran, exact rationals, `hankel_exact.py`] At k = 4 and k = 6 the singular starting points and the lost ranks are confirmed exactly**, the same list as modulo the prime (7 of 15 and 27 of 63 for the first polynomial; none at k = 3 and 5).
- **k = 9 (odd, but composite): 0 singular of 1022** (two polynomials, every starting point). So "composite k" is not the cause. **k = 10: 0 singular among 256 sampled starting points** (every fourth one, one polynomial; a run of four or more would have been caught). So the reading "even k" rests on k = 4, 6, 8 only, where the share of singular starting points falls fast (47%, 38%, 4%); at 1024 points it is rare or absent. An honest statement of the anomaly: singular half-arc blocks exist at 16, 64 and 256 points and at no odd k tried (3, 5, 7, 9).
- k = 12 did not finish inside the ten-minute limit.
- [derived] The same fact read as a fraction: a lost rank r at some starting point means M * F(t) = t^a * G(t) modulo t^n - 1 with degree(F) + degree(G) at most n - 1 - r, where every element allows n - 1. For even k the transform's element is "a ratio of two polynomials" of unexpectedly low total degree (lower by up to 11 at 256 points). F and G are dense, so this is not a cheap circuit; it is a regularity nobody has explained.

## 6. The questions the brief said to keep asking

- **Which sparse elements are prime for this ring?** At 16 points the defining trinomial and its twists. At 32 points none. At 64 only letters lifted from subfields. Beyond, [derived] none: norms grow like 1.38^phi(n).
- **How many does the transform need: k, or log k?** Neither, in this ring: 2 at k = 4 (an accident: the mixed piece has only two primes), then at least about sqrt(N) three-term letters, or one letter per bit if letters may have k terms. [recalled] The Gross-Koblitz formula writes each eigenvalue as a product of k factors, one per binary digit of j (a power of -2 times a value of the 2-adic Gamma function): that is "k of them, the textbook in disguise", and the single factor behind it has valuation 1 exactly on the half-interval j > n/2, so [derived, by the argument that a run of n/2 consecutive roots forces n/2 terms] it is dense.
- **What does a denominator buy?** Section 3: negative valuations, hence a budget of sqrt(log N) in place of log N; and nothing more in a commutative ring. [derived] In the cost model itself a denominator is not a new letter: it is a chain of N gates each feeding the next. What is new is the search space: every construction in this project is shallow, and a resolvent is a circuit of depth N.

## 7. Yes, and: where the idea goes

1. **Chains.** [derived] Up to free scalings and one rank-one term, the inverse of any cyclic two-term letter IS the prefix-sum matrix (lower triangle of ones) along the cycle. So the honest form of this idea is: write the transform as a short word in prefix sums along DIFFERENT orderings of the addresses, with free relabellings and scalings between. Each letter costs N - 1 gates (G) and has full support at once, so the "support doubles per layer" reason for log N layers is gone; orderings that interleave the two halves of any cut have full rank across it. Not run. First experiment: 8 points, all words of three chain letters (21 gates against the textbook 24), solved as polynomial equations in the weights.
2. **Alternate the two rings.** The two walls of section 4 are each tied to one commutative ring. A product (multiplicative letter)(digit-flip letter)(multiplicative letter)... lives in the group ring of the maps x -> u x + s, which has no eigenvalue calculus. The only general constraint I know there is the 2-adic one (the transform has N-choose-i-many elementary divisors 2^i), and with large coefficients it is weak.
3. **The product formula.** Besides the tower law (Hasse-Davenport lifting, which the plane developer found), Gauss sums satisfy the Hasse-Davenport product formula [recalled], and it holds here: **[ran, `hd.py`, double precision, error 1e-14 relative, k = 4, 6, 8, 10] g(j) * g(j + n/3) * g(j + 2n/3) = N * g(3j) for every j**, g(j) the eigenvalue of M at the exponent j. In the ring, w a cube root of unity:

       M(t) * M(w t) * M(w^2 t) = N * ( -J3 + 4 K(t^3) )

   where "t -> w t" is a free diagonal twist of the data by the cubic character, J3 is the sum of all powers of t^3, and K is the set of u modulo n/3 with Trace to the field with 4 elements of theta^u equal to 0: the hyperplane of the projective space over the field with 4 elements (the classical Singer difference set). **[ran, EXACT, `hd_exact.py`, arithmetic in Z[w], 16, 64 and 256 points] the displayed identity holds coefficient by coefficient.** Read as operators, with B = (the transform's element) after (the cubic twist): **B^3 = N * (-J3 + 4 K(t^3))**. **At 16 points the right side is 16 * (4 - C5): three twisted transforms in series equal four times the identity minus the circle sums, a map that costs O(N).** At 64 points K is a line of the projective plane of order 4 (5 points of 21: {3, 6, 7, 12, 14}); at 256 points 21 of 85. It is the order-three cousin of H*H = N (and of the fact that "phase gate then Hadamard" has order three on one bit), and nobody in this project has used it. It does not by itself give a recursion (solving for one M costs two others); it needs cube roots of unity (pairs of rationals). [recalled] The same formula exists for every divisor m of n in place of 3. [guess] The words to try are those in which the three twisted copies act on DIFFERENT arrays of a direct sum, since the published saving is a direct-sum saving.
4. **The half-arc null spaces** of section 5 are explicit inputs with half-size support and half-size transform support in multiplicative order. Explain them; they are the kind of object the floor note's hatch "blocks run only on known zeros" asks for.
5. **A restricted model where a bound might be provable.** Bounded-coefficient letters with denominators and free scalings: the norm argument of section 3 gives sqrt(log N) per entry and no more. Either a construction meets it or the argument can be sharpened; both would be new.

## 8. Criticism, last

- Sections 2 to 4 are about ONE model: products inside the cyclic ring. They do not bound circuits. The lemma of section 4 leans on a recalled root bound that I confirmed only for three terms up to k = 10.
- The identity of section 1 is exact and checked, and it is not a saving.
- "No cancellation at 32 points" is derived from the four observed odd primes being 1 modulo 31; I did not run a lattice search over letters with coefficients other than +-1, nor over four-term letters. Experiment (b) of the brief was done on the mixed piece at 16 points only; experiment (c), 64 points, was replaced by the ledger of section 2, which shows why a short product cannot exist there with +-1 three-term letters.
- Hankel ranks are modulo a prime. The singular cases are candidates until one is redone in exact arithmetic; the pattern (runs with triangular profiles, three even k) is not noise.
- Recalled and unchecked: the Mahler measure 1.38, the Gross-Koblitz formula, Kelley's bound, "Jacobi sums generate the Stickelberger ideal", the product formula for divisors other than 3. (For the divisor 3 it is checked exactly at 16, 64 and 256 points.)
- The product formula of section 7 is a classical identity in new clothes; I have no circuit from it. It is in the report because it is exact, it is a relation among transforms in series that the project has not used, and its right side is cheap at 16 points.
