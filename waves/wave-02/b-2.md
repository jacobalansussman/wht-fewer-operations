# b-2: criticism of e-7's slack law (Lemma 4) and its "door"

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The law is true: I re-derived it, checked both of its ingredients on every subset at 4 and 16 points, and reproduced its three headline numbers. It is a lower bound in a framework that is already in the literature (Paturi and Pudlak 2004, "outer dimension"; the counting step is the Donoho-Stark uncertainty count). The "door" at a half-length child plus a quarter-length child is real only in this sense: this one argument stops proving anything there. It is not evidence that a linear program exists. The only matrices A that I can actually build for that case are the textbook's tensor structure in disguise, and their row weight grows at least like 0.75 * N^(1/4). In the user's terms it gives nothing yet: no circuit, no saving exponent.

## Words

N = 2^k points, H the plain transform (entries +1 and -1, H*H = N*I). The identity under test is H = S + A*C*B, where C is the child (m = rho*N numbers pass through it), A has N rows and m columns, and S is the remainder. *Weight* of a vector: how many of its entries are not zero. *nnz*: number of non-zero entries of a matrix. *E_minus*: the vectors w with M w = -sqrt(N) w, where M = H Q^T and Q is the relabelling in S; for S = sqrt(N) * identity it is the eigenspace of H itself, of dimension N/2. *Outer dimension* (Paturi and Pudlak): the smallest dimension of a space that contains a given space and is spanned by vectors of weight at most s.

## 1. The claim, stated precisely

[derived] **Theorem (what Lemma 4 really proves).** Let S = sqrt(N) * Q with Q a signed relabelling and M = H Q^T symmetric. Let d = rank(H - S) (this is the dimension of E_minus) and r = m - d (the *slack*). If H = S + A*C*B with A of m columns, then for every t between r + 1 and m, the t lightest columns of A together touch a set T of rows with

    t - r  <=  |T|^2 / N .

Consequently nnz(A) / N >= rho * sqrt(j * N) / (r + j) for every j >= 1, and choosing j = r gives e-7's formula

    nnz(A) / N  >=  rho / (2 * sqrt(rho - 1/2 + sigma/N)),      sigma = N/2 - d  (0 for the identity, sqrt(N)/2 for the best relabelling).

Proof, three lines. (a) The columns of H - S span exactly E_minus, so E_minus sits inside the column space of A; any t columns of A therefore span a space that meets E_minus in dimension at least t - r (this needs no assumption that A's columns are independent). (b) Vectors of E_minus that live on a set T of rows form a space of dimension at most |T|^2/N: on that space the T-by-T corner G of M acts as -sqrt(N), so N * dim <= sum of squares of G's entries = |T|^2. (c) The t lightest of m columns carry at most t/m of all entries.

Three corrections to the wording in `e-7.md`, none of which hurts it:
- The bound is on the **average** number of entries per row of A, which is stronger than "each row needs".
- At rho = 1/2 with the identity the formula's "unbounded" is, exactly, **sqrt(N)/2 per row** (take j = 1; r = 0). It is the floor note's statement with a constant.
- The hypothesis "H Q^T symmetric" is needed in step (b). Symmetric relabellings do reach the smallest possible rank of H - S, (N - sqrt N)/2 [derived: the trace of H Q^T is at most N]; for a relabelling that is not symmetric the law says nothing.

## 3. A sharper form, and it changes nothing [derived; ran on every subset]

Applying step (b) to the complement of T as well gives dimension <= d - s + s^2/N with s = N - |T|. No subset violates it at 4 and 16 points, and at 16 points with the identity it is attained for every |T| >= 10. With this cap the chain forces, at rho = 3/4: 1.06, 1.05, 1.03, 1.03, 1.03 entries per row at N = 16 ... 4096. Still a constant. **So counting plus the uncertainty principle cannot close the door, in either form.** That part of e-7's reading stands.

## 4. Is it known?

- **The framework is known.** [read, the paper itself] R. Paturi and P. Pudlak, "Circuit lower bounds and linear codes", Zapiski Nauchnykh Seminarov POMI 316 (2004) 188-204; J. Math. Sci. 134 (2006) 2425-2434. They define D(s, V) = the least dimension of a space that contains V and is spanned by vectors of weight at most s. Lemma 4 is a lower bound on D(s, E_minus): in their terms it says D(s, E_minus) >= N/2 + N/(4 s^2) for the identity remainder [derived: t columns of weight s touch at most t*s rows; take t = N/(2 s^2)]. Their Corollary 5: **if a space of dimension n/2 is produced by a circuit of size c1 * n and depth c2 * log n, then D(n^eps, V) <= n/2 + c3 * n / log log n.** They also conjecture, for Fourier-type matrices, that D(n^eps, V) >= (1 - eta) n, which would rule such circuits out.
- **Step (b) is known.** [from memory, not opened tonight] Donoho and Stark, "Uncertainty principles and signal recovery", SIAM J. Appl. Math. 49 (1989): functions living on T whose transform lives on W form a space of dimension at most |T| |W| / N.
- **The rank side is known.** Changing N entries brings H to rank N/2 (that is H - sqrt(N) I; elementary). The lower bound N^2/(4 * rank) on the number of changes is [read, abstracts only] G. Midrijanis, arXiv cs/0506081 (2005), and R. de Wolf, arXiv quant-ph/0505188 (2005). That H is not rigid in the sense that would block linear circuits: J. Alman and R. Williams, arXiv 1611.05558 (2016).
- **The identity H = sparse + (low rank), used recursively with a fixed small kernel, is known** and is where this whole line starts: [read, abstract] J. Alman and K. Rao, arXiv 2211.06459 (November 2022, STOC 2023), 23/24 * N log N. e-7's Lemma 5 (a fixed identity pays the remainder's rank in calls) is why that route gives a constant factor only.
- The exact formula rho / (2 sqrt(rho - 1/2 + ...)) for the transform's eigenspace I did **not** find in print in a short search. It is a two-line consequence of the two known pieces.

**What the known theory predicts for "linear or not".** Nothing decides it. But Corollary 5 turns the door into a sharp test. A program with T(N) = T(N/2) + T(N/4) + cN and bounded row weights has depth O(log N), so if it exists then E_minus must sit inside a space of dimension N/2 + O(N / log log N) spanned by vectors of weight N^eps, for every eps > 0 [derived from Corollary 5, applied to H - sqrt(N) I, which costs N more gates]. Lemma 4 allows that (it asks for weight about sqrt(log log N) there). Nobody knows such a space, and the authors of the framework conjecture that for Fourier-type matrices none exists.

## 5. Does it scale? The door one size further [ran, exact, 16, 64 and 256 points]

e-7 had no example of a suitable A beyond 16 points. I built one family and checked it exactly at 16, 64 and 256 points.

[derived] Write N = n1 * n2. The space V = E_minus(n1) (x) everything + everything (x) E_minus(n2) has dimension 3N/4, contains E_minus(H_N), and is spanned by vectors that are "an eigenvector of the short transform in one block". Its orthogonal complement is K = E_plus(n1) (x) E_plus(n2). A basis: every sparse basis vector of E_minus(n1) placed in each of the n2 positions, plus, for n1/2 chosen blocks, every sparse basis vector of E_minus(n2).

[ran] K inside the +sqrt(N) eigenspace exactly; A^T K = 0 exactly; rank(A) = 3N/4.

| N | built as | columns of A | nnz(A) | per row, average | per row, largest | Lemma 4 allows | rho = 1/2 would force |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 4 x 4 | 12 | 30 | 1.88 | 4 | 0.75 | 2.00 |
| 64 | 4 x 16 | 48 | 164 | 2.56 | 10 | 0.75 | 4.00 |
| 256 | 16 x 16 | 192 | 1008 | 3.94 | 13 | 0.75 | 8.00 |

Two things follow.
- **The drop is real.** A quarter more middle does buy a much sparser A than the half-length case allows at all (3.94 against at least 8 at 256 points). Lemma 4 is not merely silent there.
- **But this family is not sparse, and it is the textbook.** [derived] Its average is exactly 1.5 * s(n)/n for N = n^2, where s(n) is the size of the sparsest basis of E_minus(n) (5 at 4 points, 42 at 16). Section 1 forces s(n) >= n * sqrt(n)/2, so this family has **at least 0.75 * N^(1/4) entries per row**, and at most 0.75 * N^(0.33) (bases made of 4-point pieces). In general a middle of (1 - 2^-j) N numbers is reached with weight about N^(1/(2j)): bounded weight only when the middle is the whole of N, which is the textbook.

**Growth law observed:** lower bound constant (0.75, or 1.03 with section 3); best construction 1.88, 2.56, 3.94 at 16, 64, 256, law between N^0.25 and N^0.33. The gap between "constant" and "N^(1/4)" at rho = 3/4 is the open question, and it is the Paturi-Pudlak question for this one space.

**Why small sizes cannot answer it** [derived, a dimension count, not a proof]. Fix which entries of A may be non-zero, w per column. "The column space contains E_minus" is N^2/8 equations; A has (w - 1) * 3N/4 free numbers. A generic pattern therefore needs w >= N/6 + 1: 4 at 16 points, 12 at 64, 44 at 256. At 16 points "4 per column" looks sparse, which is why searches there succeed; from 64 points on, only a structured A can work, and the one structure known is the table above. e-7's remark "no run below 5,184 points can see the wall" is true for the wall and equally true for the door.

## 6. What it gives in the user's terms

**Nothing yet.** No circuit, no formula for n points, no saving exponent.
- If the door identity existed with bounded weights at every N, the size would be T(N) = T(N/2) + T(N/4) + cN = O(N). That "if" has no example and no supporting computation beyond 16 points, where everything looks sparse.
- With the only A that exists (section 5), the A layer alone costs at least 0.75 * N^1.25 entries: more entries than the textbook has gates (N log2 N) from about a million points on, and no better than what the half-length family already gave e-7 (N^1.25 to N^1.5).
- The B side was not examined by e-7 or by me: B must equal (inverse of the child) * (the unique Y with A*Y = H - sqrt(N) I) and be sparse too. That is a second condition of the same kind, and no one has looked at whether the family of section 5 meets it.

## 7. Does anything fail?

The law does not fail. What fails is the reading "the only door of the wave":
- *Smallest case where the reading misleads:* 16 points. A suitable A exists there with 30 entries (section 5), so Lemma 4's condition, and every condition on A alone, is met at 16 points. Whether the full identity exists there is still undecided: e-7's search left 7 entries over, and that search fails its own calibration at this size. If it does not exist, the block is on the B and child side, about which Lemma 4 is silent.
- *Nearest true statement:* "For a remainder sqrt(N) times a symmetric relabelling, the uncertainty count forces sqrt(N)/2 entries per row of A at a half-length middle and nothing at any longer middle; the half-plus-quarter case is the question whether the transform's eigenspace has outer dimension at most 3N/4 with bounded weight, which is open, has a known tensor construction at weight N^(1/4) to N^(1/3), and is conjectured false for neighbouring matrices by the people who posed it."

## 8. If someone continues

1. **The B side at 16 and 64 points for the family of section 5** (exact linear algebra, no search): compute Y, try the natural assignments of columns to the two children, count B. One short run; it decides whether this family is a half-touch identity at all.
2. **A structured A that is not a tensor product.** The dimension count says it must be special. Raw material: the eigenvectors of smallest weight. [ran] At 16 points those of weight 4 span exactly 3 of the 8 dimensions of each eigenspace, and nothing of weight 5 adds to them. [derived, not run] They are the signed indicator vectors of the cosets of the three self-dual codes of length 4, six in each eigenspace.
3. **Corollary 5 as a test of any future linear candidate:** it must exhibit the space of dimension N/2 + o(N) with weight N^eps. Cheaper to check than a circuit.
