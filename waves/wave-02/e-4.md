# e-4 `passes`: pay in running sums, not butterflies

**First line.** No circuit below the textbook count and no growth law below N log N came out of this. Passes lose to
butterflies at every size I could solve: 4 points need exactly 3 passes (butterflies: 2 layers), 8 points and above need
at least 4, and every form I could analyse in general needs at least log2 d + 1 passes or leaves at least
(d/2) log2 d non-zeros behind. What did come out is one clean theorem (the "corner count", section 4), three exact
small-case facts, and a precise statement of the only form the idea still has room in (section 7).

Nothing here is in Lean. Marks: [ran] = exact computation of tonight, [derived] = argued by hand tonight and checked by
nobody else, [guess], [read]. Folder: `waves/wave-02/e-4-passes/`.

**Words.** *d* = 2^m: number of points of one digit. *H*: the Walsh-Hadamard matrix on d points, entry (-1)^(a.x)
for row label a and column label x (bit strings, a.x = number of common ones). A row of H is a *character*.
*Pass*: y_i = x_i + c_i * y_(i-1) along the points in some order; its matrix is a full triangle. *Difference*: the
inverse of a pass, y_i = x_i - c_i * x_(i-1); its matrix has two non-zeros per row (one in the first row). Both cost
d - 1 gates in count **(G)** (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free); every gate count below is (G). I write **P** for
a pass and **B** for a difference; a product of h such letters, with free relabellings and scalings between them, is
"h passes". *h(d)*: the least h for which the product can be H up to relabelling and scaling. A win over the best known
(G) count (7/8 of d*m) needs h <= m - 1 at d >= 32; at d = 8 it needs h = 3 (21 gates against 22), at d = 16 h = 3.
*Window*: the 2 by 2 block on two adjacent rows and two adjacent columns of a matrix written in a chosen order.

## 1. The small cases, solved

| d | h(d) | how |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 | [read, i-fields] H_2 = T diag(1,-2) T^t |
| 4 | **3** | lower bound [derived], example [ran, replayed exactly] |
| 8 | **>= 4** (and no P B B P found) | [derived] + [ran, exhaustive inside the derived reductions] |
| >= 16 | >= 4 | [derived] |

- **Two passes never suffice for d >= 4** [derived]. BB: a product of two differences has a row with at most 2
  non-zeros, H has d. PP: invert, same thing (the inverse of H is H/d). PB: moving the pass across gives
  B1 * H = B2; the first row of B1 picks one whole row of H (d non-zeros) and a row of B2 has two. BP: same on columns.
- **4 points, three passes** [ran: `pbp.py pbp 4`, 8 solutions]. Rows in the order (0,3,1,2), columns (0,1,2,3):
  H = (running sum down the rows) * E * (running sum along the columns) with
  E = [[1,0,0,0],[0,-2,0,2],[0,0,2,-4],[1,0,-2,0]], whose non-zeros lie on one path, so E is one difference-type pass.
  Replayed on the four unit vectors in exact arithmetic: equal to H. Cost 8 gates here (one coefficient is 0),
  9 in general, against 7 known. So at 4 points the pass is the worse letter.
- **8 points, three passes: none** [ran: `pbp.py pbp 8`, output `pbp8.out`]. Of the eight words of length 3 only PBP
  (and its inverse BPB) survives the non-zero count at d >= 8; in PBP the coefficients are forced (section 2); the
  exhaustive search over all column orders up to symmetry (30) and all row orders finds no row order that even passes
  the row test. The same hand count excludes PBP for every d >= 16 (at most two different half-spaces can be
  "interval-shaped" in one order, which caps the row order at 9 labels).
- **Any word with all passes first and all differences after (or the reverse) needs h >= log2 d + 1** [derived].
  P^a B^b means (a differences) * H = (b differences). On the left there is a row that combines at most 2^(a-1)
  characters; a combination of k characters is non-zero on at least d/k points (the uncertainty principle of the
  group). On the right every row has at most 2^b non-zeros. So a + b >= m + 1. This covers all-P and all-B.
- **8 points, four passes in the word P B B P: none in the samples** [ran: `four.py 8 30`, `four_signs.py`]. Here the
  middle E must be a product of two differences. With plain running sums outside: all 4,237 arrangements whose E has
  at most 30 non-zeros (a product of two differences cannot have more), none factors. With every sign pattern
  (coefficients +-1 outside) on the best arrangement: 16,384 patterns, none factors. At 4 points the same test finds
  a factorisation at once, so the test works. Limits: the factor test follows the coefficients that cancel an entry
  and one step of a free ratio, not a free ratio carried over several steps, so "none" is "none found" here; other
  outer coefficients and the alternating words were not run.
- **Wrap-around, 4 points: a near miss** [derived]. With cyclic passes the supports work out at d = 4: a cyclic
  difference of the four characters in the order 0,1,3,2 has the support of a single cycle of length 8, as required.
  But the four coefficients are forced to have product exactly 1, and a cyclic pass is singular exactly when the
  product of its coefficients is 1. The wrap-around form exists only on the locus where it stops being invertible.
  (This is the same fact as the hand computation in the brief: tridiagonal-circulant inverse only at t = 0.) A cyclic
  pass also costs 2(d-1) gates as a running sum, so it was never cheaper.

## 2. What free coefficients buy: nothing in this form, and why

Take the form pass * middle * pass, i.e. E = B1 * H' * B3 with B1 a difference on rows, B3 on columns, H' = H up to
scaling. An inner entry of E is s - c' r - c q + c c' p, where (p q / r s) is the window of H' and c, c' the two
coefficients.

- If the window has an even number of minus signs it factors: p (c - r/p)(c' - q/p). It vanishes when c or c' is the
  matching sign.
- If the number is odd, it vanishes exactly when c' = +-(1 + c)/(1 - c) or the same with c replaced by -c [derived].
  The map c -> (1+c)/(1-c) has order 4: 0 -> 1 -> infinity -> -1 -> 0, and it fixes i and -i.

So the coefficients that can kill windows consistently are 0, 1, -1, infinity, i, -i: the six corners of an
octahedron on the Riemann sphere; any other value sits in an orbit of 8 and is over-determined after two rows.
[derived, the last clause is a sketch.] In the three-pass form it is sharper: after normalising one row and one column
of H to all ones, every coefficient of both outer passes is 1 except at most one per pass, which is 0 or -1
[derived; this is what makes the 8-point search finite]. **Answer to the standing question: a pass with free
coefficients reaches nothing here that plain sums with signs do not, and no large coefficient appears** (the middle has
entries 0, +-2, +-4). Allowing i and -i as well changed no count [ran: `anneal4.py`, d = 4, 8, 16, 32: 7, 23, 73, 252,
the best arrangements found were real].

## 4. The corner count (the one theorem)

Write H' as a picture of +-1 in the chosen row and column orders and draw a wall between unequal neighbours.

1. [derived, checked on every arrangement in `corners.out`] An inner entry of E = D H' D^t is zero exactly when the two
   rows agree on its window or the two columns agree on it. So the non-zeros are the **corners** of the walls (windows
   with an odd number of minus signs) and the **crossings** (checkerboard windows), plus a few on the border.
   Running sums on both sides price a matrix by the corners of its picture: the summed-area-table idea, exactly.
2. [derived, checked] corners = G := the number of pairs (i, j) with w_i . delta_j = 1, where w_i is the sum of the
   two consecutive row labels and delta_j of the two consecutive column labels.
3. [derived, checked] crossings = (constant windows) + (d - 1) - b/2, b = number of wall ends on the border. So
   crossings are at least the number of flat windows minus d: the picture must be stripes almost everywhere.
4. **Theorem** [derived; exhaustive at d = 4 and 8: minimum 4 and 12, `gmin.py`]. In every arrangement of H_d the number
   of corners is at least **(d/2) log2 d**, the number of butterflies, with equality for Gray code against bit-reversed
   Gray code.
   *Proof.* For a direction delta let f(delta) = number of row steps with w_i . delta = 1. (a) For k independent
   directions the f's add up to at least 2^k - 1: project the row order onto those k bits; it visits all 2^k values,
   so it moves at least 2^k - 1 times. (b) Pick a basis b_1, ..., b_m among the column steps greedily by increasing f,
   f_k = f(b_k). The column order visits every class modulo span(b_1..b_k), so at least 2^(m-k) - 1 column steps lie
   outside that span, and each of them has f >= f_(k+1). Summing by parts twice, G >= sum_k 2^(m-k) f_k
   = F_m + sum_(k<m) 2^(m-k-1) F_k with F_k = f_1 + ... + f_k >= 2^k - 1, which is m 2^(m-1). QED.
5. **Counts** [ran]. Least number of non-zeros of E over all orders: d = 4: **7**, d = 8: **23** (exhaustive, signs +1;
   annealing with signs finds 23 again). d = 16: 72 found, d = 32: 252 found, d = 64: 911 found (annealing, upper
   bounds only, the last one from a short run). 7 and 23 are d*m - 1, and I expected 63 at d = 16; it did not come.
   Corners in the best arrangements at d = 4 to 32: 4, 13, 34, 86, hugging the bound 4, 12, 32, 80; the excess is
   crossings: 2, 9, 26, 143 (the rest are entries on the border).
6. [derived, sketch, constants not checked] Crossings must grow like d^(3/2): a region of straight walls is a block of
   H of rank one, such a block has area at most d (Lindsey's lemma), so the picture needs about d regions of
   perimeter about sqrt(d) each, and by 3 every boundary costs. So pass * sparse middle * pass costs at least about
   d^(3/2): **worse than butterflies as d grows.** The found values 7, 23, 72, 252, 911 grow by factors 3.3, 3.1,
   3.5, 3.6 per doubling, between 2.83 (d^1.5) and 4 (d^2); the textbook count 8, 24, 64, 160, 384 is passed at d = 16.

What the failure is made of: one order per side. A single order sees only blocks of rank one, and H has none larger
than d entries. This is the same fact that defeats any fixed-order banded or "low rank off the diagonal" format.

## 5. What does not forbid a constant number of passes

[derived] The rank argument that kills one order does not kill several. A pass has rank at most 1 across any cut of its
own order. If the wires keep their identity through h passes with orders pi_1..pi_h, then for every half A of the
wires, rank(H restricted to A by not-A) <= total number of times the h orders cross between A and not-A, and that rank
is at least d/4. So the only demand is: **the union of the h paths must be an expander** (every half cut at least d/4
times). Three random paths do that [guess: the usual bound on the bisection width of random 6-regular graphs,
quoted from memory; two paths are borderline]. So no rank count excludes three or four passes in incompatible orders; sections
1 and 4 exclude them for other reasons (supports, and forced coefficients), and only for the words P^a B^b and
P B^b P. This is Valiant's old remark that graph shape alone never forces more than linear size, seen for paths.

## 6. Answers to the questions of the brief

- h(4) = 3, h(8) >= 4, h(16) >= 4. For every word I could treat in general, h >= log2 d + 1 (monotone words) or
  h >= (1/2) log2 d + O(1) (one pass each side, from 4.6). Nothing suggests that h stops.
- A pass with free coefficients reaches nothing beyond signs in the forms solved; the useful values are the six
  octahedron points; no large coefficients.
- The floor note's count is right that one pass moves a diagonal by d - 1 dimensions at cost N; what it does not see is
  that the pass leaves (d/2) log2 d corners behind, each of which still has to be paid.

## 7. Where the idea still has room, and mutations

- **Alternating words** P B P B ... with four or more letters in different orders are not covered by anything above:
  the uncertainty count needs the passes on one side, the corner count needs one pass per side. Smallest decisive
  case: d = 8, h = 4 in the words P B P B and B P B P (the word P B B P is already empty in the samples of section 1;
  28 gates, no win, but it would settle whether h(8) is 4 or more). First size where a win is arithmetically
  possible: d = 32 with h = 4.
- **Second pair of passes in other orders on the middle E.** Rows of E are sparse and sometimes proportional on their
  overlap (at d = 8, rows (0,0,0,0,2,-4,0,4) and (0,0,0,0,2,-4,4,-4) differ in two places), so a further difference
  with a free order does remove entries. This is greedy sparsification of E and is a real search, not run.
- **Corner theorem as a tool elsewhere.** Its proof uses only that row labels and column labels each visit a whole
  group and that the window parity is bilinear. The same count for the cyclic group (Fourier matrix), and for the
  published engine's units, would say how many "corners" any two-sided scheme must pay there.
- **The octahedron.** The six admissible coefficients are the six single-qubit stabiliser states; the order-4 map
  is a Clifford element. This ties the pass picture to the `spin` pick; I did not follow it.
