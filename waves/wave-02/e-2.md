## 1. What the idea turned into: an exact formula for the cost of any set of outputs

Terms. S = a set of m outputs (frequencies) of the N-point transform. r = its affine dimension (the dimension of the smallest shifted subspace that contains S). f(S) = least number of gates, count (G), computing those m outputs.

**Reversed form [derived; transposition principle, checked by the replay above].** Reversing a linear circuit changes its gate count by (outputs minus inputs). So f(S) = N - m + g(S), where g(S) is the least number of gates that build, from the m unit vectors, the "sign vectors" t_x = ((-1)^(s.x)) for s in S, one for each address x. There are exactly 2^r different ones (up to a factor), each needs its own gate, so

  f(S) = N - m + 2^r + helpers(S),

and helpers(S) depends on S only, not on N. The reversed problem has m coordinates instead of N, which is why an exhaustive search reaches it. In words: the cost of a set of outputs = the cost of evaluating, at every address, a signal whose spectrum lives on S.

**Lemma A [derived, both directions; confirmed by the search in 6 cases].** If p lies outside the affine span of S, then f(S + p) = f(S) + 2^r exactly, count (G), any field of characteristic not 2. (Upper: feed e_p into one input, then flip its sign on each of the 2^r vectors. Lower: drop the new coordinate; of each pair (t, +1), (t, -1) one gate becomes a duplicate, and so does the first gate that touches e_p.)

**Corollary [derived].** f(S) >= N + 2^r - 2 for every S, with equality for r + 1 points in general position. So the whole transform needs at least 2N - 2, and everything above that is the cost of the DEPENDENT outputs (those inside the span of earlier ones): call it the excess E. Linear size is exactly: E = O(N), a constant number of gates per dependent output. At 4 points E = 1; at 8 points E <= 7 (was 8); at 16 points E <= 26.

## 2. Part (c): translates lose, completion wins

The best set that is not a subspace: 3-space minus a line, 6 outputs, 24 gates. To cover 16 outputs it needs 4 translates. Two translates inside one 3-space share the fold (8 gates) and nothing else I could show: 8 + 16 + 16 = 40 per 3-space, 80 in all, against 56. Completing the SAME circuit to the missing line cost 5 gates instead of a second translate's 16. So the half of the idea that works is "a dense set that is cheap, then a cheap completion", not "cover by translates". [ran / derived]

## 3. Part (b): the four encoder sizes 30, 34, 39, 60, explained

[ran, `forest.py`, all 8008 six-subsets, exact] In the bent form the 16 addresses are a 4 x 4 grid (rows = cosets of {0,7,11,12}, columns = cosets of {0,3,13,14}), the transform is "4 x + total - 2 (row sum) - 2 (column sum)", and the eigenspace of dimension 6 is all tables a(row) + b(column) with a and b summing to zero. Read 6 cells as 6 edges between 4 row-nodes and 4 column-nodes. **A six-set is an information set exactly when its edges form two trees covering all 8 nodes and the first tree does not have as many rows as columns.** Count: 3456 (one isolated node) + 576 (shapes 2+1 and 2+3) + 16 (shapes 3+1 and 1+3) = 4048. [derived, then ran]

Why four values: each column of the encoder is the codeword that is 1 on one edge and 0 on the other five; removing that edge leaves three pieces, and the weight of the codeword depends only on how the pieces split rows and columns. Only the weights 6, 8 and 11 occur, in only four combinations: 6x6 gives 30; four 6s and two 8s give 34; (6,6,6,8,8,11) gives 39; 6x11 gives 60 (the 16 sets made of two stars). Weight 6 is the least possible (one row line minus one column line), so 30 is a floor.

Growth [derived]: any eigenvector of a symmetric Hadamard form has at least sqrt(N) non-zero entries (apply the matrix and compare the largest entry), so every such encoder has at least about N^1.5 / 2 entries. "Cheap set and sparse encoder together" cannot mean few entries; it could only mean an encoder with a small circuit, which is the transform problem again.

## 4. What the failures are made of, and the way around each

1. Scattered sets fail because each new direction of span doubles the number of sign vectors (Lemma A, exact). Yes, and: stay inside one subspace and ask for density there.
2. Tree-shaped circuits (split the set by a hyperplane, recurse) cost at least the sum of the leaf depths, so at least m log2 m, with equality only for subspaces [derived]. With bounded coefficients Morgenstern's bound gives the same order for every set [derived from read]. Yes, and: every gain seen here is a non-tree part, a hub: in reversed counts, 5 gates against the tree's 6 for three outputs, 7 against 8 for a plane, 12 against 15 for five outputs, 14 against 18 for six [ran, `dens_m3-6.out`].
3. Translates share only the fold. Yes, and: complete instead of translating. That step gave the 21.
4. The line-sum route (cover by the sqrt(N) + 1 subspaces of a spread, share all folds) is where "union of subspaces sharing their folds" leads; it is d-1-plane's subject and I left it there.
