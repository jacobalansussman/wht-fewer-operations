# h-4 zetafloor: subset sums, a meter that is an identity, and the exact place where the floor proof stops

2026-10-10 23:38 to 2026-10-11 00:38 EDT, 60-minute box. Private research: nothing published, no git, no Lean, nothing reviewed.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `h-4-zetafloor/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, [read] = from a report named,
[guess] = marked guess. Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v with a, b non-zero, scalings free, an output counts
when a wire is proportional to it. Nothing here is in count (O).

Words. N = 2^k points, an address is k bits. **Subset sums** Z: y_S = sum of x_T over the T inside S. **Yates**: one pass per bit,
k 2^(k-1) gates. A **wire** is a linear form in the inputs, written as a vector with one entry per address. Per bit the 2-vectors
e = (1,0), f = (0,1), j = (1,1): an input is a tensor product of e's and f's, the output y_S has j on the bits of S and e elsewhere
(I write eje for "e on bit 0, j on bit 1, e on bit 2"). **Product wire**: a tensor product of one 2-vector per bit.
**Entangled**: not a product. **Symmetric along bit i**: the wire does not change when bit i of the address is flipped (its factor
on bit i is j). For a gate w = a*u + b*v, the gate **kills** bit i when w is symmetric along i and u, v are not;
kappa = the number of bits it kills. **Idle gate**: kappa = 0. **Excess** of a gate: kappa - 1 when kappa is at least 2.
**Surplus**: kills of a bit beyond the N/2 that are needed. **Hub**: an entangled wire that several gates use; here mostly
M x R, a full-rank 2 x 2 array M on one pair of bits times a product R (its **context**) on the other bits.

## 0. In ten lines

1. **A meter that is an identity** [derived]. For every circuit for Z, over any field, every bit is killed by at least N/2
   gates. Hence **gates = k 2^(k-1) + idle - excess + surplus**. Yates has kappa = 1 at every gate. Subset sums fall below Yates
   exactly when some circuit has **excess > idle + surplus**. In a circuit with c N gates the average gate kills at least
   (log2 N)/(2c) bits at once.
2. **Why the signed helper ties** (task b) [ran, all 2,772 optimal 8-point circuits of d-0 re-enumerated; 2,748 replay over the
   rationals]. Only 24 of them are Yates-like (kappa = 1 everywhere). The other 2,724 contain entangled wires and gates that
   kill two bits at once; 1,542 contain a gate that kills all three. The signed helper is an entangled wire made by an idle
   gate that buys one double kill. In 2,742 circuits idle = excess exactly and surplus = 0.
3. **An anomaly that did not go away** [ran]: in 6 optimal 8-point circuits **excess = 4 > idle = 3**, with one surplus kill.
   So "every multiple kill is paid by an idle gate" is false already at 8 points; what holds there is only the sum.
4. **The proof with entangled wires** (task c) [derived; pieces checked by `lemmas.py`]. Product wires only: Yates. ONE entangled
   wire: still k 2^(k-1). ANY number of entangled wires, as long as no gate touches two of them: still k 2^(k-1).
   **The proof stops at the first gate that touches two entangled wires.**
5. **The object at which it stops: the triangle gate** [ran]. Three hubs on the three pairs of three bits, on one line, the
   gate kills a bit and its output is a hub: a hub born by a useful gate, not by an idle one. Gates of this kind occur inside
   optimal 8-point circuits. I built the cleanest one by hand: two idle gates and **three** double kills on offer (section 4).
   Every circuit containing it needs 13 gates (11 and 12 excluded exhaustively over the field with 3 elements).
6. **Why it still loses at 8 points, in a form a proof can use** [derived]: the wires symmetric along a bit form a space of
   dimension N/2 and the outputs are a basis of it. A hub born by a kill takes one of the N/2 useful kills of that bit away from
   an output, and that output must then be made inside the space. At 8 points that costs at least what the triangle won.
7. **What the transform has at 4 points that subset sums lack, in one sentence** [derived + ran]: on each bit the transform has
   two new output directions, so its hub (a swap of the two input directions with the two output directions) gives each of four
   consumers two births; for subset sums one output direction IS an input direction, the swap must fix it, only one consumer
   gets two births, and the hub (1,1,1,0) costs 5 against 4.
8. **16 points** (task a): not settled. No 31 found by two local searches over the fields with 2 and 3 elements (section 7);
   heuristic, weak evidence. What the searches did give: 1,064 sampled 32-gate circuits replayed over the rationals, with up to
   14 entangled wires and gates that kill all four bits; in 1,059 idle = excess, **in one excess exceeds idle by 2** (2 surplus kills).
   At 8 points the largest such gap is 1. Nothing here beats any known count.
9. The exact minimum at 16 points is now a statement about one object: Z on 8 points computed **from a changed basis** (the 8
   kill outputs of one bit), plus what it costs outside to produce that basis. With the standard basis both parts are Yates.
10. Better question: **is there a basis B of an 8-dimensional symmetric space, containing hubs born by triangle gates, from which
    the 8 outputs cost less than 12 minus what B cost outside?** That is a finite search at 16 points, and its analogue at every
    size is the recursion a floor proof has to close.

## 1. The kill count [derived]

Fix a bit i. Sym_i = the wires symmetric along i, a space of dimension N/2. No input is in it. The outputs y_S with i in S are
N/2 independent members of it, so they are a basis of it. (For every set I of bits the outputs y_S with S containing I are a
basis of the wires symmetric along all of I.)
In a gate w = a u + b v with a, b non-zero, each of the three wires is a combination of the other two. So if two of them are in
Sym_i, so is the third: a gate has all three wires in Sym_i, or none, or exactly one. "Exactly one, and it is the output" is a
**kill of i**. Every wire in Sym_i is the output of a kill of i or is made from two wires of Sym_i, so all of Sym_i's wires lie
in the span of the kill outputs. The outputs need dimension N/2. **At least N/2 gates kill bit i.** QED.

Summing over bits, sum of kappa = k N/2 + surplus; splitting the gates by kappa:

    gates = k 2^(k-1) + idle - excess + surplus .

Nothing was assumed about the field or the coefficients. It is a-2's level count with "tight" removed: level = number of
symmetric bits, and a-2's gate lemma (levels differ by at most one) is exactly "kappa at most 1", which is what tightness bought.

Consequences [derived]:
- The exact minimum is k 2^(k-1) - max(excess - idle - surplus). By d-0's exhaustive runs the maximum is 0 at 4 and 8 points over
  the fields with 2, 3, 5, 7 elements [read]; by g-4's three-call identity it is positive at astronomical size [read].
- **Linear size**: c N gates and kills summing to at least k N/2 force average kappa at least (log2 N)/(2c), so a constant
  fraction of the gates kill a constant fraction of log N bits at once. In point coordinates (section 6) such a gate adds two wires that are
  proportional on all but N^(1-c) points of the cube. This is j-cross's "gates saved per over-limit wire must grow like
  (1/2) log2 N", as an identity instead of a guessed law.
- **The exact split of any circuit along one bit**: gates outside Sym_i, then N/2 + surplus kills whose outputs are a spanning
  set B of Sym_i, then gates inside Sym_i that make the N/2 outputs from B. Yates with bit i first has B = {j x input}, inside
  cost = Yates one bit smaller. Yates with bit i last has B = the outputs, inside cost 0, outside cost twice Yates one bit
  smaller. Every "cut" in between ties, because the two copies outside share nothing [derived, sketch]. A saving is a basis B
  that is cheap to reach from outside AND from which the outputs are cheap: not a cut of one circuit.

## 3. The proof, and where it stops (task c)

**P1** [derived; ran: 34,560 gates at 4 points and 6,030 at 8 points, largest kappa 1]. Two product inputs and a product output:
the inputs differ in exactly one tensor factor, so kappa is at most 1. With all wires products this gives exactly Yates.

**P2** [derived; ran: 259,200 and 533,970 gates, largest kappa 0]. Two product inputs and an entangled output: kappa = 0.
So **an entangled wire made from two product wires always costs an idle gate.**

**P3** [from P1, P2]. A gate with kappa at least 2 has an entangled input.

**P4, one entangled wire** [derived; ran on 496 hubs with entries -2..2]. Let e be the only entangled wire. Every gate that uses
it has a product partner and a product result, so e is a combination of two products. If they differ in three or more factors
that decomposition is unique (the standard uniqueness of a rank-two decomposition, recalled), it is the pair e was made from, and
no gate uses e. If they differ in two factors, e = M x R is a hub and every partner is x x y x R. The result of the gate is the
product psi(y) x phi(x) x R, where phi is the projective map x -> (the direction orthogonal to M^-1 x) of the line of 2-vectors
and psi its inverse: **a hub acts on product wires as a swap of its two bits composed with a projective map of the line.**
The result is symmetric along both bits only for x = phi^-1(j), y = phi(j): **at most one double kill per hub** (none when
j^T adj(M) j = 0). The run: one partner for 376 hubs, none for 120 (no partner exists, or its entries are outside the 16
directions tried), never two. Excess at most 1, idle at least 1. QED.

**P5, any number of isolated entangled wires** [derived]. If every entangled wire is made from two product wires and every gate
that uses it has a product partner and a product result, P4 applies wire by wire: excess <= number of entangled wires <= idle.
**The count k 2^(k-1) holds for every circuit in which no gate touches two entangled wires.**

**Past that point**, for hubs on two bits [derived, by rank across a cut; not machine-checked]:
- (hub, hub) -> product happens only for two hubs on the same pair in the same context, and then both aim at the same output.
- (hub, product) -> hub happens only on the same pair in the same context (a change of M, idle).
- **Transport**: M x R' + c M x R'' = M x (R' + c R'') carries a hub to a new context and can be a useful kill. The contexts where
  M exists by transport lie in the span of the contexts where it was born, and a double kill needs the hub at its output's
  context. To reach context j of a bit by transport one needs the hub at contexts e and f, and context f carries no output.
- What is left is (hub, hub) -> hub across different pairs. That is the triangle gate.

So: **the proof survives any number of entangled wires that stay apart; it stops at the first gate with two entangled wires on it,
and among hubs on two bits the only such gate that is not accounted for is the triangle gate.** Wires entangled on three or more
bits (they are in most optimal 8-point circuits) are not covered by anything above except the exhaustion at 8 points.

## 4. The triangle gate, built and priced [ran]

General form with all three contexts equal to j [derived; `lemmas2.py` checks one instance]:
j0 x (a x j - c j x f)12 + j1 x (a' x j + c j x f)02 = (j x a + a' x j)01 x j2. Its hubs have no double-kill partner at all
[derived: j x b + a' x j minus a multiple of j x j has rank one only if b or a' is j; for ee - ff the determinant is -1 throughout]. The useful version has the two parents at context e and the child at context j [derived]:

    p = e0 x (ee + fe + ff)12 = x000 + x010 + x011         (address written bit 0, bit 1, bit 2)
    q = e1 x (ef + fe + ff)02 = x001 + x100 + x101
    w = p + q = (ee + ef + fe)01 x j2                        the gate kills bit 2; three hubs on three pairs
    p + x001 = ejj,   q + x000 = jej,   w + (x110 + x111) = jjj      three double kills

p is one idle gate from an output (p = eje + x011). q is not: it needs a stepping stone (x001 + x101 = jef, a kill of bit 0,
then + x100, idle), or else q = jej - x000 after jej was made the ordinary way, and then its own double kill is gone.
So the tally by hand is: **2 idle gates, 3 double kills, excess > idle by design, and 2 surplus kills** (the stepping stone, and
a fifth kill of bit 2), 13 gates: eje, p, ejj, jef, q, jej, w, x110 + x111, jjj, eej, jee, and two for jje.
With parents at context e, the child at context j and partners that are products of e, f, j, this triangle and its mirror image
are the only ones [derived: the child must be (1,1,1,0) for its partner to be ff x j].

Priced exactly (`tri8.out`, `mincirc` over the field with 3 elements, the three hubs added to the targets): **no circuit with 11
or 12 gates contains p, q and w; 13 is reached.** Where the second surplus kill comes from [derived]: w = ejj + jej - eej.
The child hub lies in the span of three outputs of Sym_2 (it has to: the outputs are a basis of Sym_2). Its birth by a kill
uses one of the four useful kills of bit 2, so one of eej, ejj, jej, jjj must either be killed into Sym_2 as a fifth kill or be
made inside Sym_2, and from {w, ejj, jej} the wire eej is a three-term sum: two gates that kill nothing new.
**A hub born by a kill always displaces an output of its space; the triangle wins only if the displaced output comes back for
free**, that is by a single gate that is itself a useful kill of another bit. At 8 points it never does (exhaustion).

This is a hub in the inclusion-exclusion sense: (1,1,1,0) = y_0 + y_1 - y_empty on its pair, "everything strictly below the top".
The same wire on a set S of bits (the sum of x_T over all proper subsets T of S) is one gate from y_S and kills all of S at once.
A linear-size circuit would need such wires for most S at O(1) gates each.

## 5. What the transform has that subset sums lack [derived + ran]

A hub on two bits sends product wires (x, y) to (psi(y), phi(x)), phi a projective map of the line (P4); such a map is fixed by
three values.
- Kernel [[1,1],[1,tau]], rows a = (1,1), b = (1,tau), tau not 0 or 1: four distinct directions e, f, a, b. The involution
  exchanging e with b and f with a has the hub (1,1,1,tau); it sends the four inputs to the four outputs, one gate each, two births
  each: 3 + 4 = 7 against 8 [ran for tau = -1, 2, 1/3, 7, `lemmas.out`]. This is j-cross Hybrid 4 read as a map of the line.
- Subset sums: output directions e and j, and e is an input direction. Three directions. The involution fixes e and exchanges f
  with j; its hub is M = (1,1,1,0), d-0's overshoot helper. M - ef = je, M - fe = ej, M + ff = jj [ran]: one birth, one birth,
  two births, and the fourth output ee is an input. 2 + 3 = 5 against Yates's 4.

**The sentence: a kernel with a zero entry has an output direction equal to an input direction; each bit then needs N/2 births
instead of N, Yates already collects that, and the hub's map has a fixed point, so a hub does two births for one consumer only.**
Along tau -> infinity nothing dies gradually: 7 holds for every finite tau; at the limit the target drops to 4 and the hub to 5.

The mirror image [derived]: in H = Z D Z^T (4 + 4 gates at 4 points) the transform's hub x00 + x01 + x10 - x11 is t_00 - 2 t_11
in the middle variables t = Z^T x. It joins the top and the bottom of the middle layer: the saved gate lives where the two passes
meet (d-0's remark, with the wire named), and it is not a wire of a circuit for Z alone.

## 6. Point coordinates [derived; ran `lemmas2.py`, k = 2, 3, 4, 8,551 random wires, 0 mismatches]

Read a wire w = sum c_T x_T as the function p -> sum over T of c_T * product over i in T of (-p_i) on the cube {0,1}^k. Then
inputs are signed indicators of the up-sets {p contains T}; the output y_S is the indicator of the sub-cube {p_S = 0};
**w is symmetric along i exactly when the function vanishes on the face p_i = 1**; a gate kills the set K exactly when its two
inputs are proportional on the union of the faces p_i = 1, i in K, and neither vanishes on any of them. Yates halves a sub-cube
per gate. The hub (1,1,1,0) is delta_00 - delta_11: it vanishes on both faces except at the corner where they meet.
