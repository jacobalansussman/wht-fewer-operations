# h-0 onepergate: can one over-limit wire pay for more than one saved gate?

2026-10-10 23:37 to 2026-10-11 00:20 EDT, inside a 60-minute box. Private research: nothing published, no git, no Lean, nothing reviewed.
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `h-0-onepergate/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, unchecked by anyone,
[read] = from tonight's reports, [guess] = marked guess.
Gate count: **(G) throughout** (a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free). Nothing here is in count (O), OpenAI's.
Words. N = 2^k points. *Wire*: a linear form a program holds, up to a scalar. s = its non-zeros written in the inputs,
s' = its non-zeros written in the outputs; always s*s' >= N. *Tight*: s*s' = N (plus and minus signs following a parity rule on a
sub-block of the addresses; its *level* d is log2 s, so inputs are level 0 and outputs level k). *Over the limit*: s*s' > N.
V = number of gate wires over the limit, T = number of tight gate wires (the N outputs included). **Q = gates + V - N log2 N.**
The guessed law L1 of `j-cross.md` says Q >= 0. A *hit* would be Q < 0.

**No circuit below the best known counts and no growth law below N log N. No hit: nothing found with Q < 0.**

## 0. In nine lines

- (a) The 19-gate circuit is on the law exactly: 19 gates, 5 over-limit wires, **19 + 5 = 24** [ran]. So are all the records
  of tonight (7 at 4 points; 19 and 20 at 8; 52 and 54 at 16 [ran]; 128 and 304 [read, derived]), and so are the mirror
  images (the transposed circuits) of the four I transposed (19, 20, 52, 54) [ran].
- L1 in plainer words [derived]: **an over-limit wire lets a program do without at most two tight wires.** The textbook holds
  N log2 N tight wires; every record holds exactly two fewer per over-limit wire.
- (b) In the pool of all 304 vectors with entries 0, 1, -1 whose support is a sub-block (it contains the 128 tight wires, the 240
  stabiliser vectors and all 128 sign vectors): 3.0 million neighbours of the 19 and the 20 tested exactly, 6,787 circuits
  visited by a weighted annealer in four runs, **least value of gates + V = 24, never 23** [ran]. Local, not exhaustive.
- **The anomaly to keep: a floor.** At every gate count from 19 to 24 the least value of gates + V that the search meets is
  exactly 24 (19 + 5, 20 + 4, 21 + 3, 22 + 2, 23 + 1, 24 + 0) [ran]. The exchange rate is one for one at every point of the line.
- **Any coefficients, exact** [ran]: a wire that is made from tight wires and read with tight partners is one gate from at most
  **4** outputs, at 8 points and at 16 points alike, and the partners get dearer with N (single inputs at 4 points, pairs at 8,
  blocks of four at 16: always N/4 addresses each). So between "4 outputs, free partners" and "N outputs, dear partners"
  there is nothing, as long as the partners are tight. Entries 1 and 3 do occur among these wires and do not help.
- So a wire that pays for two gates cannot be a lone helper among tight wires. It has to sit in a *cluster* of over-limit wires
  that feed one another. The 19 is the only circuit of tonight with such a cluster (5 wires, 6 outputs come out), and it is
  again exactly one for one.
- New lens [ran]: at 8 points the 240 stabiliser vectors are **two copies of the E8 root system, and the transform swaps
  them.** The textbook only adds wires at right angles. In the two law-abiding circuits the gates whose inputs are neither at
  90 nor at 45 degrees (they are at 60 degrees inside one copy, or touch a wire outside both copies) number exactly twice the gates saved.
- Not done: an exhaustive search; any search at 16 points; chains of wires with unbounded entries beyond one such wire.
- L1 at 8 points is tied to an open number [derived]: any circuit with 15 gates, or with 16 gates and one tight helper, breaks it.

*Transposed circuit*: reverse every gate (a wire read by f gates becomes a sum of f terms); it has the same number of gates and
computes the transposed matrix, which here is the transform again. Its wires are different forms, so V can change.
Three facts:

1. **Q is not the same for a circuit and its mirror image** (8 against 2, 3 against 2), but on the records it is 0 on both sides.
2. **Lemma (Q adds up under tensor products)** [derived]. If A computes the m-point transform and B the n-point transform, the
   two-layer circuit (n copies of A, then m copies of B) has Q = n*Q(A) + m*Q(B). Reason: a first-layer wire is (wire of A) on
   one block, so its s is unchanged and its s' is multiplied by n; a second-layer wire is (an output of A) times (a wire of B), so
   its s is multiplied by m and its s' unchanged; either way it is tight exactly when the small wire is. Checked on the 54 [ran]. So no tensor product of
   circuits on the law leaves the law, and an L1-breaker at any size must contain a part that is not a tensor product.
3. **L1 restated** [derived]. gates = T + V, so "saved <= V" is the same as **T + 2V >= N log2 N**: count a tight wire once and
   an over-limit wire twice. Equivalently: (tight wires the textbook holds and the program does not) <= 2V. On the records, per
   level: the 20 lacks 4 pairs and 4 blocks of four and has 4 hubs; the 19 lacks 4 pairs and 6 blocks of four and has 5 wires.

**Why the two 21s waste** [ran, read off `meters8.out`]. Law-abiding circuits have every over-limit wire read by exactly two
gates (four in the brick). The 21s spend over-limit wires on jobs that a tight wire does at the same price:
- e-2's 21 (Q = 3): three of its six over-limit wires are read by one gate only, each to deliver a single output
  (g17, g25 with s = 3, and g27 with entries 1 and 3 and s' = 3). Three wires read once, Q = 3.
- e2-3's 21 (Q = 8): it builds the centre by chains, so the nodes of its merge tree are over-limit (two partial sums with
  s*s' = 3N, two half-hubs with 4N) where the 19 has tight pairs and blocks; with no tight pieces to flip, it leaves the centre
  by four single-input flips (a star of four wires with 8N) and needs two more. Its mirror image has only 5 over-limit wires.
Both searches ran over a prime field with all coefficients free and landed on sign vectors plus one wire with a 3.

## 4. The census of hubs, any coefficients [ran: `census.py`, `census_out.py`; `census_3.out`, `census_out_3.out`, `census_out_4.out`]

The brief's question: what does a wire look like that is read by many gates, each landing on an output or a tight wire?
If the partners are tight too, this is a finite exact question with no bound on the coefficients: the wire is a point lying on
several lines, each through two tight wires. I enumerated every such point (the coefficients are whatever the intersection
of two lines dictates).

**8 points, all levels.** 128 tight wires, 6,532 lines through two of them, **3,248 non-tight points on two or more such lines**.

| s | s' | tight lines through it | entries | how many | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8 | 7: three (pair, pair), four (input, block of four) | 1 | 56 | the brick's hub on a half-cube |
| 8 | 4 | 7: three (four, four), four (pair, output) | 1 | 56 | the same hub one level up (the staircase's top hubs) |
| 4 | 5 | 7: three inside one level, four between neighbouring levels | 1 | 448 | four addresses that are not a sub-block |
| 5 | 4 | 7: the same | 1, 2 | 448 | its mirror image |
| 3, 6 | 8, 4 | 4: one of them jumps two levels | 1 | 224, 224 | three inputs; its mirror image |
| 4 | 6 | 4: one jumps two levels | **1, 3** | 224 | a block of four with one entry tripled |
| 8 | 3 | 4: one jumps two levels | **1, 3** | 224 | its mirror image: a sum of three outputs |
| 3, 6 | 6, 3 | 3, none jumps | 1, 2 | 672, 672 | |

No line jumps three levels: a lone over-limit wire is never one gate from an output with a single input as partner.
At most four lines jump two levels, and only for the two kinds of brick hub. Entries 1 and 3 appear by themselves (nothing was
bounded) and those wires are weaker hubs, not stronger.

**Outputs only, 8 and 16 points.** Every non-tight wire that is one gate from an output with a tight partner and lies on a second
tight line (so: every non-tight wire that is one gate from two or more outputs with tight partners is in the list).

| N | such wires | most outputs one gate away | the wires that reach it | their partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1,176 | **4** | 56 sign vectors with s' = 4; 224 vectors with entries 1, 3 and s' = 3 | four pairs; or one pair and three blocks of four |
| 16 | 11,760 | **4** | 560 sign vectors with s' = 4; 2,240 with entries 1, 3 and s' = 3 | four blocks of four; or one block of four and three of eight |

At 4 points the same hub has four single inputs as partners. So the count of outputs stays 4 while N goes 4, 8, 16, and the
partners' size goes 1, 2, 4 = N/4. This is g-1's hub theorem (two outputs need partners of total support N/2) with the
coefficients freed, and it is met with equality. The 448 sign vectors at 16 points that are flat on both sides do not
appear: they are not one gate from two outputs with tight partners at all (g-1 measured partners of support 6 for them).
About `e2-0.md` leak 2, the "hub one gate from all N outputs": that hub is an output with one entry flipped, and
output_j = output_0 - 2*(indicator of a half-cube); the partners are tight wires of the last level. It is the textbook's last step
read backwards, not an over-limit phenomenon [derived].

**Answer to "is there anything between 4 outputs with free partners and N outputs with dear partners?"** With tight partners:
no, at 8 and 16 points, for any coefficients. Whatever pays better than one for one has over-limit partners.

## 5. Where a better rate could live [derived]

- **A cluster.** Call a set of over-limit wires that feed one another a cluster. A lone wire (cluster of one) is covered by
  section 4. The 19 has one cluster of five: the centre, two wires one input-flip away, two more one further flip away; six
  outputs come out of it, two by a single input, four by a pair. The staircase has clusters of one only.
- **What a cut says** (a-2's count, with every over-limit wire placed below every cut): saved <= -V + sum over the tight
  wires made from an over-limit wire of (their level - 1). So at 8 points an L1-breaker needs, per over-limit wire, more than one output
  made directly from it (or more than two blocks of four). The 19 has 6 outputs from 5 wires, which passes this
  test; the test is loose because it does not price the partners.
- **What prices the partners.** Tight partners of level d cost the textbook's price. A program of linear size cannot afford
  them in bulk, so nearly all its gates must read two over-limit wires and its only tight wires are the N outputs. Then
  Q = 2*gates - N(k+1), negative as soon as gates < N(k+1)/2.
- **L1 and the open minimum at 8 points.** V <= gates - 8, so every circuit with 15 gates breaks L1, and so does 16 gates with
  one tight helper, 17 with three, 18 with five. The exact minimum at 8 points is only known to lie between 15 and 19. L1 at
  8 points is therefore not a separate question from "is 16 possible": it is that question, graded by the number of tight
  helpers. At 16 points: any circuit with at most 39 gates breaks L1 (the record is 52).

**Is the law true level by level? No** [ran: `perlevel.py`, `perlevel_c19.out`, `perlevel_text.out`]. In the textbook each of the
levels 1 and 2 holds 8 tight wires. Guess tried: T_d + 2 V_d >= 8 for d = 1 and for d = 2 separately, where T_d counts the
tight helpers of level d and V_d the over-limit helpers whose s' is that of the level below (s' = 8 for d = 1, s' = 4 for
d = 2); the sum of the two is L1. It holds with equality at both levels on the 19 (4 + 2*2 and 2 + 2*3) and on the 20
(4 + 2*2 and 4 + 2*2). On 1,015 circuits visited by two short annealing runs it holds on 1,012 and **fails on 3**, each
time by one unit at level 2 (3 + 2*2 = 7) in a wasteful circuit with 4 to 6 units to spare at level 1. So an over-limit wire
with s' = 8 can pay into level 2: which level a wire pays into is not decided by its s'. The total never went below 16.
A proof of L1 by counting level by level with this banding is therefore closed; a proof must let a wire choose its level.

## 6. A lens nobody had tonight: two copies of E8 [ran: `e8lens.py`, `e8lens.out`]

Scale every stabiliser vector at 8 points to length one. Then [ran, exact]:
- they split into copy A (the 56 pair wires and the 64 full sign vectors with a quadratic sign rule) and copy B (the 8 inputs
  and the 112 sign patterns on half-cubes), 120 lines each; inside a copy two different wires are at 90 or 60 degrees and
  every wire has 56 neighbours at 60 degrees: each copy is the 120 lines of the **E8 root system**;
- between the copies the angles have cos^2 = 0, 1/8 or 1/2;
- **the transform swaps the two copies** (inputs in B go to outputs in A; pairs in A go to blocks of four in B);
- each copy has 64 tight wires and 56 over-limit wires (the hubs).

Every gate, by the angle between its two inputs:

| circuit | 90 degrees | 45 degrees (B with A) | 60 degrees inside one copy | cos^2 = 1/8 and others | wires outside both copies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| textbook, 24 [derived: its two inputs always have disjoint supports] | 24 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| e2-0's 20 | 8 | 4 | **8** | 0 | 0 |
| g-1's 19 | 7 | 2 | **4** | 6 | 2 (the two wires with s*s' = 8N) |
| e-2's 21 | 9 | 1 | 5 | 6 | 4 |
| e2-3's 21 | 7 | 1 | 0 | 13 | 8 |

Reading: the textbook only ever adds two orthogonal wires. A saving is made of gates u - 2v with u, v, u - 2v three unit
vectors at 60 degrees (the smallest root system that is not a product, A2), all in one copy, or of steps that leave the two
copies. On the two law-abiding circuits: saved = (gates that are neither at 90 nor at 45 degrees) / 2: 8/2 = 4 and
(4 + 6)/2 = 5. On the two wasteful ones the same number is an upper bound (5.5 and 6.5 against 3). [guess] At 16 points the
4,320 stabiliser vectors should be two copies of the shortest vectors of the Barnes-Wall lattice (2,160 lines each;
the real Clifford group is that lattice's symmetry group: recalled, not checked tonight). I do not know whether this is in
the notes already (`R/tools/e8/` is the published unit's E8, which lives in the batch of arrays, not in one array's addresses).

4. **Use the mirror image**: Q differs between a circuit and its transpose (8 against 2). Search on min(Q, Q of the transpose),
   or on their sum; a circuit with Q = 0 one way and Q > 0 the other way would show which side carries the law.

6. **The all-over-limit program**: a circuit whose only tight wires are the outputs. At 16 points it breaks L1 below 40 gates.

## 8. Criticism, last

- Nothing here is exhaustive. The exchange runs cover radius 3 around two circuits; the annealer visited 6,787 circuits, all
  descended from three seeds; from an empty start it finds nothing, so the search sees only what is near the known circuits.
- The pools asked for and not searched: all vectors with entries 0, 1, -1, 2, -2; chains of several wires with unbounded
  entries. The unbounded part of this report is "one wire with any coefficients" (sections 3 and 4), which is the weakest
  honest form of that request.
- The census counts lines through TIGHT wires only. A wire that serves many gates through over-limit partners is invisible to
  it; that is exactly where section 5 says to look.
- The floor is a regularity in four annealing runs of 100 to 200 thousand moves each on a loaded machine. It may be a theorem
  of the pool or an artefact of where the seeds sit.
- The tensor lemma, the restatement of L1, the cut inequality in section 5 and the remark on leak 2 are hand derivations of
  tonight. The row for the 304 in section 2 comes from the lemma, not from metering that circuit; the lemma itself was machine-checked on one instance only (the 54).
- The E8 reading is checked at 8 points only (angles, neighbour counts, the swap). Calling the copies "E8" rests on
  120 lines with angles 60 and 90 degrees and 56 neighbours each; I did not build an explicit isomorphism. The rule
  "saved = oblique gates / 2" holds on two circuits and is slack on three (the 4-point brick: 4 oblique gates, 1 saved).
- L1 itself must fail at very large size if the published program reads as a circuit in count (G) (`j-cross.md` assumes it).
  Nothing here bears on that size.
- I did not read the web. The second wave's skeptics (`b-0.md`, `b-1.md`, `b-2.md`) do not discuss L1; b-0 confirms e2-0's family.
