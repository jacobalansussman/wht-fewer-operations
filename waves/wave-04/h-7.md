Words. An *array* is one working copy of the data, n = 2^(45 f) points for the fourth result (f = number of digit columns, any f).
A *unit* is the fixed small program on W = 1,263 arrays (480 data + 783 helpers) that the engine repeats at every scale; it is five
*invocations* of one helper circuit plus idle blocks. A *block* of rank r = r moves on one array, done as one call to a smaller
transform. A *wire* = a linear form the program holds; s, s' = its non-zeros written in the inputs and in the outputs; *over the
limit* = s*s' above what the uncertainty principle forces. L1 and L2 are the two guessed laws of `j-cross.md` section 4.

## 0. In eight lines

- **The dictionary exists and is exact** [read + derived]: every wire of the unit is (a vector w over the batch index) x (a tight
  wire on the address). It is a product across the cut batch | address, always. All of its excess is on the batch side:
  s*s' = t^2 * n, t = number of sources the array mixes. So j-cross's assumption "the program is a circuit in (G)" is right, and the
  guess "entangled between batch bits and address bits" is wrong wire by wire. What ties batch to address is the *design*: each
  array stands in its own oblique frame (946 different subspaces in one invocation, 944 of them not coordinate subspaces, and
  611 of them degenerate for the dot product [ran]).
- **No wire in the file pays for more than one gate** [ran]. One level of the unit saves 120 moves and makes at least 28,135 moves
  on mixtures: 0.0043 gates saved per over-limit wire in (G). L1 holds inside one level with a factor 234 to spare. The premise of the brief is wrong.
- **L1 breaks by nesting, not by a wire** [derived]: each level multiplies the cost by (1 - 2.11e-3); L1 can first fail after
  about 328 nested levels, arrays of about 2^(2^1152) points.
- **L2 breaks too** [derived]: a wire inside d nested units has log2(s*s'/N) at most about 2.8 d (ceiling 20.6 d), which grows like
  d, while gates saved per wire grow like e^(0.0021 d). L2 fails from about 4,100 levels (ceiling: 5,150). Neither guessed law is a law.
- **The meter s*s' is blind to the published saving** [derived]: saving = (moves saved) / (fragmentation); how big the mixtures
  are cancels out. What the unit trades in is *rides*: a move on a mixture of t sources carries t - 1 riders. One unit has
  389,730 rides and 120 saved moves: 3.1e-4 saved per ride. In the larger second unit: 2.3e-5.
- **The wires that do pay, in the engine's count**, are the 8 *totals* of each invocation: each mixes 57 sources, is read by 92
  targets, costs 9 moves and delivers 12 (96 gross, 24 net per invocation). Together they are 15 times a projector of rank 8 [ran].
- **Toy (part d): none, for a reason** [derived]. At f = 1 an addition costs as much as a move, so a unit is a genuine (G) circuit
  below the textbook only if it saves more moves than it makes additions. The published units make 161 and 131 additions per saved move.
- State: transformed into a better question (section 8).

## 1. The dictionary, stated before measuring

[read: `R/OAI/Computability/FourierTransform/BinaryFrames.lean`, `DirectionalWords.lean`; `R/Work/Block/Word.lean`; `R/tools/gx/gx.py`]

- An array is a function on f digit columns, each column an m-bit label (m = 45): n = 2^(45 f) points.
- A **move** along a direction z (an m-bit word) applies `dir z = ca*1 + cb*shift z`, ca = (1+i)/2, cb = (1-i)/2, to every digit
  column of ONE array. In (G): f layers of n gates, pairing the addresses a and a + z in one column. Fixed coefficients in Q(i).
- A **free letter** `localM g` replaces array r by sum over j of g(r,j) * array j, point by point, g rational. In (G): n gates per term.
  A `shift` is a relabelling. So the whole program is a linear circuit with fixed coefficients: **(G) applies as it stands** [read].
- OpenAI's gate lemma `Path.gate` allows an addition only between arrays in the same *frame*; the certificate checker enforces it
  ("equal frame at a gate"). Consequence [derived]: at every moment array r holds
  **frame_P ( sum over sources t of w_t * x_t )**, P = its label (a subspace of the label space, dimension d), w = its *content
  vector*. A wire is (array, moment, address a) and as a linear form it is **w (on the batch index) x row a of frame_P (on the address)**.
- Address side. For a subspace with an orthonormal basis, frame = `wrap(lum o proj)` [read] and a row is supported on a coset of P
  in each column: 2^(d f) points in the inputs, 2^((m-d) f) in the outputs [derived]. For ANY subspace, degenerate ones included,
  the release's representative is `core G s = kg G * pk s * (kg G)^-1`: the kernel on d coordinates between two "adapters" (a linear
  address map times a diagonal phase), with the entry formula "non-zero exactly on x + y in U, all of one modulus" [read:
  `R/Work/GFrame/Clifford/Core.lean`, header], and the release's frame lemma says that from any subspace U the whole kernel is
  reached by ONE block of rank m - d between two free matrices [read: `Base.lean`, `frame_lemma`; free = an address map times a
  phase]. So written in the outputs a row of the frame is a row of (free)(inverse block of rank m - d)(free): 2^((m-d) f) points
  [derived]. **The address factor is always tight**,
  and it is a product wire seen through a linear address map. In the standard address bits it is not a product wire, so j-cross's
  product-wire lemma does not see it; a-2's tight-wire theorem does.
- [ran: `frames.py`, `frames.out`] Of the 946 frames of the E8 invocation 611 are degenerate subspaces (the dot product restricted
  to them is singular); in the second result 12,982 of 18,248. Two thirds of the frames in use are ones the first calculus could not reach.
- Batch side: w has t non-zeros in the inputs and, for the x-content, t in the outputs (target S ends with exactly x_S). So
  **s = t * 2^(d f), s' = t * 2^((m-d) f), s*s' = t^2 * n.** Over the limit exactly when the array holds a mixture.
- What the replay tracks is the x-content (the 120 sources of one invocation), as the release's own checker does. A helper also
  carries its own old value and a target its own data, so true mixtures are one larger; every "over the limit" count below is a floor.

## 3. Where L1 and L2 fail [derived; numbers `depth.py`, `depth.out`, floating point, rough]

- **Inside one level nothing fails.** Saved gates (120 f - 19,380) n against at least 28,135 f n over-limit wires.
- **L1 fails by counting alone.** L1 gives saved <= over-limit wires <= gates, so it needs gates >= (1/2) N log2 N. One level
  multiplies the rate (gates / N log2 N) by 1 - 2.111e-3 while the size of the calls shrinks by e^-2.407 (e2-2's law). The rate
  passes 1/2 after about **328 nested levels**, at about 2^1152 address bits. No single wire does it: every level takes 0.2 %, and it compounds.
- **L2 fails as well.** A wire at depth d is a sum of (product over levels of t) tight wires of one shape, if each batch is taken
  from cosets of one block (the program is free to do that), so log2(s*s'/N) <= sum over levels of log2(t t'): 2.77 bits per level
  on average [ran], at most 2 log2 W = 20.6. The L2 budget per wire grows like d; gates saved per wire are (1 - 2.111e-3)^-d - 1.
  They cross at d = 4,088 (measured mean) or d = 5,147 (ceiling), about 2^14,200 to 2^17,900 address bits.
  At the L1 crossing the wires have s*s' about N * 2^910 with log2 N = 2^1152: nearly tight on the scale of N.
- **What survives** [derived]: savings multiply under nesting, log-excess adds. A rate law of this kind has to read
  rate >= 2^(-gamma * mean log-excess), and then, since log-excess is at most log2 N, it forbids nothing down to N^(1-gamma) log N.
  Measured gamma: published unit 1.1e-3 per bit; the 7-gate brick 0.67; the staircase at 64 points 0.64 [ran from j-cross's table].
- **But gamma is not a constant of the unit** [derived]: saving = delta / F (moves saved over fragmentation); the mixture sizes
  drop out. The same block list with larger mixtures would have the same saving and a smaller gamma. **s*s' measures
  something the published saving does not depend on.** This is the main thing the meters say.

## 4. The wires that pay: eight totals, a projector of rank 8 [ran: `scatter.py`, `scatter.out`, exact]

- After the scatter, target S holds row S of a 120 x 120 matrix M: 1 on the diagonal, +1/2 or -1/2 on the 56 sources it "knows",
  0 on the other 63. M is symmetric, has rank 8, and **M*M = 15 M**: M / 15 is an orthogonal projector of rank 8; M - 1 has full rank 120.
- The "knows" graph is strongly regular with parameters (120, 56, 28, 24), and "knows" = even overlap of the two port words, as the README says.
- [derived, with one recalled fact: the non-singular vectors of such a form are its reflections] The ports are the complements of the 9-bit words of weight 2 and 6, that is of the 120 non-singular vectors of the
  form "half the weight, modulo 2" on the even-weight words. So the batch index of the unit is the set of 120 reflections of an
  8-dimensional quadratic space over two elements; this agrees with e2-2's guess, and with the E8 Gram matrix (roots of norm 2, inner products 0, +1, -1).
- Engine's count: per invocation the 8 totals are copied, each copy comes down 9 dimensions (72 moves), and all 120 targets are
  served. The README's accounting: 96 moves saved gross, 72 taken back, 24 net. **In that accounting these 8 arrays are where the
  saving is made: 12 moves each for 9.** In (G), before additions, each point of such an array pays 3 f gates net of its own
  cost: (1/15) log2 n, growing with the size. Counting as hubs only the 40 n wires per unit that the targets read (the copies at
  frame 0), "one gate per wire" is broken at one level from f = 162: (120 f - 19,380) n saved against 40 n.
- Everything else over the limit (27,775 of 28,135 mixture moves per unit) is a move the helper or target owed anyway, with
  riders on board. A gate there does two jobs: it advances the array's own content and the riders. That is the mechanism in
  wire language: **not a wire that saves gates elsewhere, but a gate that works for several contents at once**, followed by
  whole-array additions that take the unwanted riders off again (6,720 unwanted halves per invocation for 120 wanted terms).
- The helper circuit of the second result contains the 4-point brick: its "K block" is the matrix 1 - J/2 on four arrays
  [read: `gx.py`, `KJ`], the same reflection as j-cross's Hybrid 4 at tau = -1, used on the batch index.

## 5. Part (d): is there a toy that is a genuine circuit on a few hundred points?

[derived] No, and the reason is a number. In (G) one level saves (D f - A) n gates, D = moves saved, A = additions. A move costs
f n, an addition n. So:
- at f = 1 the unit is below the textbook only if **D > A**: more moves saved than additions made. Published: A / D = 161.5 and 131.2;
- with A / D = a the first size is f > a, arrays of 2^(m a) points;
- and L1 in j-cross's count needs D > (moves on mixtures) at one level: each saved move paid by less than one move on a mixture.
"Yes, and" twice: (1) count hubs in place of over-limit wires and the law breaks at one level (section 4), but only from f = 162.
(2) A tiny unit of the kind `wht38/tiny` hunts, with D = 1 and A <= 2, would be a genuine circuit from f = 3: three arrays of
2^9 points at block size 3. It would be the smallest circuit of this family below the textbook; the cut-set lemma there says it
needs an address map. So the toy the brief asks for and the tiny unit the other folder hunts are the same object, with one
extra demand: few additions.

## 6. The question the brief said to keep asking

"In (G) on a single array, what are the whole-array additions?" [derived] With the batch index as the high address bits, an
addition of array j into array i is n gates, each joining the addresses (i, a) and (j, a): the same low address, two values of the
high bits. They are one-sided butterflies (shears) on a single pair of slices, where a textbook layer would touch all pairs.
They never advance the transform (the frame stays), they only change w. The moves advance the transform on one slice at a time,
along a direction that depends on the slice. A textbook layer does the same thing on all slices and therefore commutes with any
mixing of the slices; the unit's saving lives exactly in that failure to commute (e2-2 says the same from the other side).
Wire by wire the batch bits and the address bits are never entangled.

## 7. Regularities worth keeping

1. Product across batch | address for every wire, with 946 frames in play: the entanglement is in the family, not in the wire.
2. All target moves happen at t about 57; all 783 helpers make their first block (2.7 moves on average) before they hold any source.
3. Block rank falls as the mixture grows (2.6 down to 1.06).
4. M*M = 15 M, rank 8, on a strongly regular graph; M - 1 has full rank. The helpers' whole job is the map M - 1.
5. Additions per saved move: 161.5 and 131.2 in the two units. Saved moves per ride: 3.1e-4 and 2.3e-5.
6. Helpers are borrowed, not scratch: 62 % of the arrays are other arrays that need the same transform and carry riders during
   their own moves. With fresh scratch arrays in their place (480 data arrays, 21,600 moves plain) the unit would cost about twice the textbook.
7. Two thirds of the frames in use are degenerate subspaces (611 of 946; 12,982 of 18,248).

## 8. The better question, and mutations

**The question.** Not "which wire pays for many gates" but: **how many of the rides can be made useful?** One unit carries
389,730 riders and saves 120 moves. A saving of 1e-1 needs about 12,000 saved moves at these block shapes (e2-2: 9.7 moves per
array of 45), that is a ride efficiency near 3e-2, a hundred times the present one; or riders that do not have to be taken off.

1. **`rides`**: for every ride, is it wanted (it advances a content toward where it has to end) or unwanted (it must be cancelled)?
   Compute wanted / unwanted per frame dimension from the certificate. The 6,720 to 120 ratio at the scatter is the first such number.
2. **`a-over-d`**: a floor for additions per saved move. Is A >= D forced for every unit? If a unit with A < D existed it would
   be a small genuine circuit in (G). Smallest case: the `wht38/tiny` game with the additions counted.
3. **`hubcount`**: restate L1 with hubs (wires read by three or more gates) in place of over-limit wires, and test it on the brick,
   the staircase, the 19-gate circuit and the unit. In the unit a hub pays (1/15) log2 n; in the small circuits, one.
4. **`nesting hub`**: the brick converts excess to saving 600 times better than the unit (0.67 against 1.1e-3 per bit) but does not
   nest; the unit nests and converts badly. Look for a program whose paid steps are calls (so it nests) and whose mixing is a
   reflection of the brick's kind. The second result's K block (1 - J/2 on four arrays) is such a reflection already inside a unit:
   measure what it saves there.
5. **`projector`**: the scatter is 15 times a projector that vanishes off the "knows" graph. Which other projectors of small rank
   vanish off the orthogonality graph of a set of odd-weight words? (`wht37/avenues/v-imports.md` has the min-rank reading; it says
   8 is optimal for E8. The open part is other families and frames of dimension above one, the "fat labels" of `wht37/avenues/NOTE.md`.)
6. **`multiplicative law`** (for the brief `provethewrongthing`): any proof of L1 or L2 must fail at the step "the excess of a
   nested wire is the sum of the excesses of its levels while the saving is the product". That step is the drawing asked for.
