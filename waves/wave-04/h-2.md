Words (from `e2-2.md` section 2). An *array* is one working copy of the data. The engine repeats one small program, the
*unit*. Each of the W live arrays must be transformed along m *directions* (m = *block size*; a direction is a non-zero
m-bit vector z); one direction on one array is a *move*. *Old letters*: free constant mixing of whole arrays, free shifts,
paid moves; no address maps, no phases; any number of extra *scratch* arrays. A *form* q is a polynomial of degree two
without constant term on the m-bit vectors; q *selects* a move along z when q(z) = 1; q(I) = q(e_1) + ... + q(e_m).
A unit must, for every q, multiply the live arrays by (-1)^q(I). *Counting*: a form with q(I) = 1 selects at least W
moves; it is *tight* if it selects exactly W. A *count vector* says how often each direction is used.
With k moves for W arrays the engine's exponent is z = log(k/W) / log m, and the *saving* is 1 - z.

2. **Block size 4: no saving for 4 to 14 arrays** [ran + derived], by the same method (e2-2 had 4 arrays, and 31 of 32
   for 8). Every count vector below 4·W that passes counting is excluded: 1, 1, 2, 2, 7, 9, 14, 16, 37, 52, 77 vectors
   for W = 4 ... 14.
3. **Then the method stops, and I can say exactly why** [ran + derived]. Both lemmas speak only about tight forms. A
   count vector in which every form with q(I) = 1 selects at least W + 1 moves has no tight form and passes silently.
   Such vectors exist as soon as the batch is large: block size 4, **15 arrays in 59 moves** is the first survivor;
   19 arrays in 75 moves (every direction five times) has no tight form at all. At block size 5: every odd-weight
   direction five times is **19 arrays in 80 moves, a saving of 1.07e-1, about which counting and both lemmas say
   nothing**. So the 1e-1 case of the old-letter game is closed for 4 arrays and open from 5 arrays on. A survivor is
   not a unit: nothing here builds one. Measured at block size 4 up to 24 arrays: the two lemmas add three moves to the
   counting bound (two for a quarter of the batch sizes), whatever the batch (section 4).
4. **The unit problem became a small algebra problem** [derived]: with unlimited scratch arrays, a unit is exactly a
   list of vectors u_i, w_i (W numbers each, one pair per move) and numbers c_ij (i before j) with
   U_A (1 + 2 C_A)^(-1) W_A^T = q(I)·1 for every form q, A the moves q selects. Every such triple is realised with one
   scratch array per move. This is what a search for constants should solve; and it turns the lemmas into statements
   about ranks, which can be pushed further (section 6).
5. **Address maps** [derived, one small run]: the lemma survives word for word on the forms that the address maps leave
   unchanged, but those forms see only the part of the transform the maps do not touch (one swap of two address bits:
   12 of the 20 moves). On the other forms an address map acts as a free sign change, and there the lemma has no
   analogue yet.

## 1. The lemmas, re-derived

**The expansion** [derived independently; agrees with e2-2]. Number the moves 1..k in the order of the word; move i acts
on array l_i. For a form q let A be the moves it selects. Write the word as g_0 D_1 g_1 ... D_k g_k with D_i = 1 - 2 E_i
(E_i the projection on array l_i) when i is selected and D_i = 1 otherwise. Let u_i = column l_i of g_0...g_(i-1) read on
the live arrays, w_i = row l_i of g_i...g_k read on the live arrays, c_ij = entry (l_i, l_j) of g_i...g_(j-1) for i < j.
Multiplying out: live part = 1 - 2·U_A N_A W_A^T with N_A = (1 + 2 C_A)^(-1), C_A strictly upper triangular.
So the unit condition is **U_A N_A W_A^T = q(I)·1** (a W x W matrix equation) for every q. The vectors u_i, w_i and the
numbers c_ij do not depend on q; only the set A does. That is the whole content of the game in this sector.

**Conversely** [derived]. Any u_i, w_i, c_ij whatever are realised by a word with k scratch arrays: take reflections
1 - 2 a_i b_i^T with a_i = (u_i ; e_i) and b_i = (w_i ; b'_i), where (b'_i)_i = 1 - w_i·u_i and
(b'_i)_j = c_ij - w_i·u_j for j > i. So "old-letter unit in this sector, scratch free" and "solution (U, W, C) of the
equations" are the same thing. Unknowns: 2kW + k(k-1)/2. Equations: one W x W matrix per selection pattern.

**Clique lemma** (e2-2) [re-derived, I agree with it]. If q is tight, U_A and W_A are square, so W_A^T U_A = 1 + 2 C_A:
w_i·u_i = 1, and w_i·u_j = 0 when i comes after j. For a set T of moves, any two of which lie together in some tight
form, the table (w_i·u_j) on T is triangular with ones on the diagonal, so it has rank |T|; it factors through W
dimensions; so |T| <= W. In particular the u_i of such a set are linearly independent, and so are the w_i.

**Zero-form lemma** (new tonight) [derived]. Let q0 be a form with q0(I) = 0 that selects the set A0. Then
U_A0 N W_A0^T = 0 with N invertible, so **rank U_A0 + rank W_A0 <= |A0|**. If A0 contains a set T that is pairwise
joined by tight forms, both ranks are at least |T|, hence **|A0| >= 2·|T|**. (When T lies inside one tight form this is
counting applied to q + q0; when T is joined through several tight forms it is new.) At equality the vectors of all
moves of A0 lie in the span of those of T; that is used in section 3.

**Tests on units that are not the coordinate schedule** [ran: `tests.py`, `tests2.py`]. An *orthonormal frame* is a set
of m directions with z·z = 1 and z·z' = 0; then the sum of z z^T is I, so transforming an array along a frame is right.
At block size 5 there are exactly 6 frames. Four arrays, each along its own frame, is a right word with 20 moves and a
count vector different from the plain schedule; there are 5 such vectors up to symmetry, with 5, 17, 17, 36 and 43 tight
selections. In all five the largest pairwise joined set is exactly 4, and the zero-form lemma excludes none. (A wrong
lemma could have failed here. These are still schedules without mixing; no right word with mixing and fewer moves is known.)

## 2. The picture that made everything readable: six frames

[ran: `tests.py` part 3; the rest derived] The maps of the 5-bit labels that keep z·z' form a group of 720 elements
(it contains the 120 permutations of the bits); they keep q(I), so they are symmetries of the whole problem. They permute
the 6 frames in all 720 ways. Every odd-weight direction other than 11111 lies in exactly two frames, and two frames
share exactly one direction: **the 15 odd directions other than 11111 are the 15 pairs of frames**, the edges of the
complete graph on 6 points. I call 11111 the *centre*. Each even direction is 11111 plus an odd one, so it is a pair too.
- A frame is a star (the 5 edges at one point). The plain schedule is a sum of stars.
- On the odd directions, the forms with q(I) = 1 are exactly the sets of edges in which every point has odd degree; the
  centre is selected exactly when the number of edges is odd [derived: a quadratic function on a 4-dimensional space
  has an even number of ones, and the sum over a frame is q(I)].
- The 16-move design of e2-2 is the complete graph plus the centre. Its 75 tight forms are the 15 perfect matchings
  (with the centre) and the 60 sets "three edges at one point plus the edge on the two points left over" [derived;
  agrees with the 75 of `clique.out`].
- Under the 720 maps the directions fall into three classes: the centre, 15 odd, 15 even.

## 5. Why it stops, and what is open at 1e-1

[derived] Both lemmas need a tight form. Take any design that meets counting for W' arrays and use it for W' - 1 arrays:
every form with q(I) = 1 then selects at least W' = W + 1 moves, nothing is tight, and both lemmas are silent.
- Block size 4, every direction t times: 15t moves for 4t - 1 arrays against 16t - 4. Below plain from t = 5 (the 75 above).
- Block size 5, every odd-weight direction t times: 16t moves for 4t - 1 arrays against 20t - 5:

|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 7 | 32 (35) | 5.6e-2 |
| 3 | 11 | 48 (55) | 8.5e-2 |

| large | | 4 per array | 1.39e-1 |

So the growth law of this method is: the floor "counting plus tight-form lemmas" equals the plain count for small
batches and falls back towards the counting floor as the batch grows (3.75 per array at block size 4; at block size 5 the design
above gives 4 per array and the counting bound itself is 3.875). **The 1e-1 case in the old-letter game is closed for 4 arrays and not for 19.** Whether anything between 5 and
18 arrays reaches 1e-1 with only a few tight forms I did not compute. [guess] If block size 5 follows the same law as
block size 4 (counting plus a constant; the constant is at least 4 there, from W = 4), the first case not excluded
would be somewhere between 11 and 16 arrays.

## 6. What could replace tightness: a rank programme

[derived; not run] The equations give rank statements for every form, tight or not. For a form with q(I) = 1 that
selects W + d moves, U_A has rank W, so any s of those moves have u-vectors of rank at least s - d, and the same for w.
For a form with q(I) = 0 that selects s moves, rank U + rank W <= s. Ranks are submodular. So a count vector is
excluded whenever no pair of rank functions (one for the u, one for the w) satisfies all of these at once: a finite
question that does not need tight forms. Example of what it gives: for "every direction t times" at block size 4 with
4t - 1 arrays, each of the 49 product forms with q(I) = 0 has u-rank or w-rank at most 2t, while any three directions
inside one product form with q(I) = 1 have rank at least 3t - 1; two of the first kind of the same type that share a pair of directions (a pair that
also lies in a product form with q(I) = 1, so that its 2t moves have rank at least 2t - 1) span at most 2t + 1 dimensions, so if three directions of one of the second kind fall inside, t <= 2.
Whether the 49 forms can be typed to avoid that everywhere is a small exhaustive check I did not have time for.
**This is the experiment I would run first: it is the only thing in sight that could give a floor uniform in W.**

The same equations read as a network [derived]: each move is a node that multiplies by its label z z^T (an element of
the group of symmetric bit matrices); u_i, w_i are the weights into and out of node i, c_ij the weight of the edge from
i to j; the unit condition says that the sum over all paths, each weighted and labelled by the sum of its nodes' labels,
is the identity at label I and zero at every other label. The plain schedule is W disjoint paths of m nodes. A saving
is a set of paths that share nodes, whose unwanted crossings cancel. This is a linear network code with one coding
matrix for all labels; the counting bound is its cut-set bound.

## 7. Address maps

[derived] An address map g on one array permutes the basis vectors in which the old letters are diagonal. Let G be the
group generated by the address maps a unit uses. For a form q unchanged by G, restrict to the vectors of its sector that
are symmetric under G: there every address map acts as the identity, and the word is an old-letter word. So **counting,
the clique lemma and the zero-form lemma hold word for word for the G-invariant forms.** On the parts of a sector that
are not symmetric, an address map acts as a permutation or a sign on ONE array: with a single swap g and a pair of forms
q, q∘g it is a free sign change of one array on the antisymmetric part, and a move with q(z) different from q(gz) swaps
the symmetric and antisymmetric parts. One array then already carries a non-commutative group (of order 8). That is
where the lemma has no analogue: its proof needs the vectors u_i to be the same for all forms.

[ran on the Linux machine: `gamma_floor.py`, `gamma_floor.out`; integer programme, floating-point solver, every case
reported proved optimal] What counting on the invariant forms still demands, block size 5, 4 arrays (plain 20):

| address maps used | invariant forms with q(I) = 1 | fewest moves counting allows |
|---|---|---|
| none | 16,384 | 16 (and the lemmas give 20) |
| one swap of two address bits | 1,024 | 12 (the three untouched bits, four times each) |
| one orthogonal map z -> z + (a·z)a, a = 01111 | 1,024 | 12 |
| one shear (not orthogonal) | 1,024 | 15 |
| one 3-cycle of bits | 64 | 12 |
| two disjoint swaps at once; a 5-cycle; all 120 permutations; all 720 orthogonal maps | 256; 4; 2; 1 | 4 |
| one map of order 31 | 0 | nothing |

So the invariant forms see only the part of the transform that the maps leave alone. This agrees with the cut-set lemma's
verdict that savings at block size 3 must live with address maps, and it says where to look: the non-symmetric parts.
