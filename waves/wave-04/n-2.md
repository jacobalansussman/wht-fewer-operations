This note counts the engine's unit, *moves*. No gates are counted anywhere, so neither count (G) nor count (O) is used.

Words (from h-2). An *array* is one working copy of the data. The engine repeats one small program, the *unit*. Each of
the W arrays has to be transformed along m *directions* (m = *block size*; a direction is a non-zero m-bit vector z). One
direction on one array is a *move*. *Old letters* = free constant mixing of whole arrays, free shifts, paid moves; no
address maps, no phases; extra *scratch* arrays allowed. A *form* q is a polynomial of degree two without constant term
on the m-bit vectors; q *selects* a move along z when q(z) = 1; q(I) = q(e_1) + ... + q(e_m). *Counting*: a form with
q(I) = 1 must select at least W moves; it is *tight* if it selects exactly W. A *count vector* says how often each
direction is used. k moves for W arrays give the exponent z = log(k/W)/log m and the *saving* 1 - z.

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

Block size m = 5, W = 4 arrays, old letters, any number of scratch arrays. *Claim*: every unit uses at least 20 moves
(the plain schedule uses 20), so no saving at all; in particular 17 moves (saving 1.0e-1), 18 (6.5e-2) and 19 (3.2e-2)
are impossible. *Proof shape*: (a) a unit's count vector passes counting; (b) an exhaustive search lists all count
vectors with at most 19 moves that pass counting: 70 up to the 720 symmetries; (c) the clique lemma excludes 69, the
zero-form lemma the last. What it does not say: anything with address maps or phases (the published units use them),
and anything for 5 arrays or more.

The model behind (a) is inherited from `e2-2.md` section 2 and I did not audit it [read]: in the basis where all old
letters are diagonal, on the vectors where every shift is +1, a move along z multiplies ONE array by (-1)^q(z), and a
unit must multiply the W live arrays by (-1)^q(I) **for every form q**. "Every q" needs the unit to be right for every
number of digit columns; e2-2 cites the floor note for that. Everything below is conditional on this model.

## 2. The lemmas, attacked

**The expansion** [derived again, independently]. Write the word as g_0 D_1 g_1 ... D_k g_k, with D_i = 1 - 2 E_i on the
moves that q selects (E_i = projection on the array of move i) and D_i = 1 otherwise. Multiplying out the selected
factors, a term for moves i_1 < ... < i_s is (-2)^s u_(i_1) c_(i_1 i_2) ... c_(i_(s-1) i_s) w_(i_s)^T on the live arrays.
Summed: live part = live(g_0...g_k) - 2 U_A (1 + 2 C_A)^(-1) W_A^T. The form q = 0 gives live(g_0...g_k) = 1. So the
unit equation is U_A (1 + 2 C_A)^(-1) W_A^T = q(I)·1, as h-2 says.
- [ran: `expand_test.py`, exact rationals] The identity holds on 1,878 selections in 60 random words with mixing and
  up to 3 scratch arrays: 0 failures. On real units WITH random mixing (direction e_j on all arrays, a random rational
  mixing after each direction, the inverse at the end; m = 3, 4, 5; with and without scratch) the unit equation holds
  for every selection, the table w_i·u_j is triangular on every tight selection, and rank U + rank W <= |A0| on every
  form with q(I) = 0. h-2's converse (any u, w, c is realised with one scratch array per move) also checks: 0 failures.
  This matters because h-2's own tests used schedules without mixing, where u and w are unit vectors.

**Clique lemma** [derived again]. Tight means U_A and W_A are square; then both are invertible and W_A^T U_A = 1 + 2 C_A.
So w_i·u_i = 1 and w_i·u_j = 0 when i comes after j. A set T of moves, any two in a common tight form, has a triangular
table with ones on the diagonal: rank |T|, but the table factors through W dimensions. |T| <= W. Correct.

**Zero-form lemma** [derived again]. For q0 with q0(I) = 0: U N W^T = 0 with N invertible of size s = |A0|. The column
space of N W^T has dimension rank W and lies in the kernel of U, of dimension s - rank U. So rank U + rank W <= s. If
A0 contains a pairwise joined set T, the u of T are independent and so are the w of T (previous paragraph), so both
ranks are at least |T| and |A0| >= 2|T|. Correct. Copies of one direction are selected by the same forms, so they are
joined to each other whenever the direction lies in a tight form; the programs (h-2's and mine) weight a direction by
its count, which is right.

**Where I looked for a hole and found none.**
- Scratch arrays: u and w are read on the live arrays only, and g_0, g_k are arbitrary, so copies and any final
  remixing are covered.
- Only forms with even diagonal are used (h-2 says so). That is a necessary condition, so exclusions stand.
- **Blocks of rank 2 or more: h-2's paragraph is not needed, and its "few groupings checked by hand" need not be
  trusted** [derived]. A block along z_1 ... z_r is, in every sector, the product of the r signs (-1)^q(z_j): it IS r
  single moves on the same array with identity mixing between them. So a block word of total rank at most 19 is a
  single-move word with at most 19 moves, and the theorem applies as it stands. And since the engine's exponent solves
  (sum over blocks of (rank/m)^z) = W, a saving exists exactly when the total rank is below W·m. So "20 moves" means:
  **no saving of any size for 4 arrays at block size 5, blocks or not.**

## 3. The search, attacked

**Reading `cover.c` line by line** [read]. A node stands for all feasible vectors n* >= n that agree with n on the
frozen directions. It picks a form still short of 4; some unfrozen direction of that form must be raised; the branch
for the j-th such direction takes "this one raised, the earlier ones not" (they are frozen for it). That is a partition,
so every feasible vector is exactly one leaf plus extras on directions not frozen there, and `post.py` adds exactly
those. The three cuts are valid: a form short by d needs d more moves; a form with no unfrozen direction is dead; a
move lowers the total shortfall of the 240 product forms by at most 64 (the program checks the 64 itself). Forms: 15
monomials, q(I) = parity of the diagonal coefficients, 16,384 forms. I found no bug.
The symmetry split for 19 moves needs: the 720 maps keep q(I), fix 11111, and are transitive on the other 15 odd
directions. All three hold [ran: `mypost.py --checkgroup`; I built the group as the orthonormal bases, not from
generators; q∘g keeps q(I) because g I g^T = I].

**My own search** [ran: `mycover.c`]. Same partition principle (I know no other that is exhaustive at this size), but
written from scratch: forms indexed differently, branches in the opposite order, and a sharper bound (product forms
still short, divided by the largest number of them through one unfrozen direction). It needs about a tenth of the nodes,
so the 19-move case runs **in full, without the split**, in 73 seconds.

## 4. Is it known?

- The setting (the engine's old-letter game) is private to this project; I found no paper with this statement.
- The two lemmas are standard linear algebra in new clothes. Zero-form lemma = Sylvester's rank inequality,
  rank(AB) >= rank A + rank B - n (Sylvester, 1884). Clique lemma = the triangular criterion: vectors u_i, w_i with
  w_i·u_i = 1 and w_i·u_j = 0 for i after j number at most the dimension; this is the rank-one case of the skew
  two-families theorem (Lovász 1977; Frankl 1982; textbook: Babai and Frankl, Linear Algebra Methods in Combinatorics)
  [from memory; not re-read tonight, so check the references before quoting them].
- The counting step is a covering problem [derived]: send z to the symmetric matrix z z^T; a form is a hyperplane of the
  15-dimensional space of symmetric 5 x 5 bit matrices; counting says "I stays in the span of the chosen matrices after
  any W - 1 of them are deleted". Multisets that meet every hyperplane often enough are studied as *minihypers* or
  *multiple blocking sets* (for example arXiv:2502.13271 and arXiv:1504.00503, which I saw only as search results, not
  read); that theory is about all hyperplanes, not the half that miss a fixed point, and I found nothing for this case.
- What the known theory predicts for "linear or not": these tools bound how many moves can be pairwise joined; they
  say nothing when no form is tight, which h-2 already saw. The counting bound is a cut bound; it is about 4 moves
  per array at every block size [read: e2-2], so inside old letters the exponent is at least about log 4 / log m, never 0.

## 6. An anomaly worth keeping

[ran: `mypost20.out`, `k6_W3.out`] h-2 asked (its "next" item 3) whether the 20-move vectors for 4 arrays that pass
both lemmas are exactly the sums of four frames. **No.** Of the 252 classes with 20 moves, 237 fall to the clique lemma,
9 to the zero-form lemma, and 6 survive: the 5 frame sums, and

    the 15 odd-weight directions other than 11111, once each, plus one frame once more   (15 tight selections, joined set 4).

In h-2's picture (the 15 directions are the edges of the complete graph on the six frames, a frame is the star at a
point) this is the complete graph plus one star. It is a sum of stars only with halves: 3/2 of one star and 1/2 of each
of the other five. It saves nothing (20 moves is the plain count). I could not exclude it and did not try to build it.

**One size down the same shape CAN be excluded, by an argument that neither lemma contains** [derived tonight, read by
nobody; the facts it uses are checked in `k6_second.out`]. The complete graph alone: 15 moves, one per edge, for 3
arrays (the plain count). It passes counting; its tight forms are exactly the 15 perfect matchings; the largest joined
set is 3; both lemmas pass it [ran: `k6_W3.out`]. It is six half-frames, not three frames. Claim: it is not a unit.
1. A perfect matching is tight, so the three u-vectors of its edges are a basis of the 3-dimensional space.
2. Each of the 45 four-cycles is exactly what some form with q(I) = 0 selects [ran]. It has 4 moves and contains two
   disjoint edges, which are joined: equality in the zero-form lemma, so the u-vectors of the cycle span only a plane.
   The three 4-cycles on the same four points share that plane: for every four points a, b, c, d, all six edges among
   them have their u-vectors in one plane P(abcd).
3. Take three points a, b, c; the others are d, e, f. If u(ab) and u(ac) are not parallel, they span a plane, and
   P(abcd), P(abce), P(abcf) all contain both, so all three are that plane. It then contains u(ad), u(be), u(cf). But
   ad, be, cf is a perfect matching: by step 1 those three are a basis. Contradiction. So u(ab) is parallel to u(ac).
4. Then u(ab) is parallel to u(ac), which is parallel to u(cd). But ab, cd lie in a perfect matching. Contradiction.
So here is a count vector that passes counting and both lemmas and is still impossible. The step that does the work
is the **equality case** of the zero-form lemma turned into geometry (points forced onto common lines and planes).
h-2 tried this kind of test once at block size 4 (`second.py`, no contradiction); here it bites. It is the natural
third lemma, and unlike the first two it might survive in vectors with few tight forms, because it needs only forms
with q(I) = 0 that are small, not forms with q(I) = 1 that are tight... but it still needs some joined pairs, so on a
vector with no tight form at all it is silent too. [guess] Worth one run: the complete graph plus a star (20 moves,
4 arrays), and h-2's survivor at block size 4 (15 arrays, 59 moves), with this argument mechanised.

## 7. In the user's terms

- Circuit size for n points: none. Saving exponent: none. This object only removes candidates.
- What it removes: in old letters at block size 5, the 1e-1 saving is impossible with 4 arrays (h-2, confirmed) and with

- Linear: impossible in this game for a reason much simpler than the lemmas [derived from numbers both programs
  print]: 240 product forms each need W moves and one move serves at most 64 of them, so k >= 3.75·W at block size 5,
  an exponent of at least log 3.75 / log 5 = 0.82 (e2-2's linear programme gives 3.875 and 0.84 [read]). Linear needs
  k = W. Whatever reaches linear size does not live in old letters; that agrees with the cut-set note in `wht38/tiny`
  that every saving needs address maps.
