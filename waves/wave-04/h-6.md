# h-6 classhub: nothing dies on the way. Every record of tonight is one program for ALL 2 x 2 kernels, written on integer tables

Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (files in `h-6-classhub/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, [read] = from tonight's reports, [guess].
**Count (G) throughout**: a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free; an output counts when a wire is proportional to it.
No claim below the best known counts (7, 19, 20, 52) and no growth law below N log N. What is new is WHERE those counts hold.

Words. N = 2^k points, addresses x are k-bit strings. *Kernel*: K_tau = [[1,1],[1,tau]]; every 2 x 2 matrix with no zero entry is K_tau
after scaling rows and columns, tau = (K00 K11)/(K01 K10); tau = -1 is the Walsh-Hadamard kernel; tau = 1 has rank one; tau = 0 and
tau = infinity are the kernels with a zero (subset sums, after scaling). The *target at tau* is the N rows of the k-fold tensor power:
row w has entry tau^(number of common 1-bits of w and x) at address x. *Wire*: a linear form a program holds, up to a scalar.
*Staircase*: e2-0's family, 7, 20, 52, 128, 304 gates at 4 to 64 points. *The 19*: g-1's 8-point program.
*Frame*: a renaming of the inputs x -> A x + t by an invertible bit matrix A and a shift t (an "address map").

## 1. The staircase for every kernel  [derived, then ran]

Take a cube with quarters LL, LR, RL, RR and one row gamma of the quarter-size target, restricted to each quarter: g0, g1, g2, g3.
The four rows of the cube above gamma are (1,1,1,1), (1,tau,1,tau), (1,1,tau,tau), (1,tau,tau,tau^2) on (g0,g1,g2,g3).
Hub M = (g0 + g1) + c*(g2 + tau*g3) = (1, 1, c, c*tau). For the half problem "top bit e":
- row (e,0) = (1,1,tau^e,tau^e) is M plus one quarter piece: g3 if c = tau^e, g2 if c = tau^(e-1);
- row (e,1) = (1,tau,tau^e,tau^(e+1)) is (tau^e/c)*M plus one quarter piece: g1 if c = tau^e, g0 if c = tau^(e-1).
The grandchildren have already made g0, g2 when gamma's top bit is 0 and g1, g3 when it is 1. Hence **c = tau^(e - 1 + top bit of gamma)**.
At tau = -1 this is e2-0's rule "c = -(sign asked)*(top bit of gamma)". Nothing else in `gen.py` changes.

## 2. The 19 for every kernel, and a tool that tests whether a circuit moves  [ran]

`deform.py` (exact, first order). Keep a circuit's wiring, give every gate w = u + theta*v its own unknown theta, and ask that the
outputs stay proportional to the rows as tau leaves -1. That is a system F(theta, tau) = 0. If dF/dtau is not in the column space of
dF/dtheta at tau = -1, no family of programs with that wiring passes through the circuit: it is *rigid*. At tau = -1 all
168 * 8 = 1,344 frames give correct programs; for other tau they are different problems, so all are tested.

| circuit | frames that move (first order) | rank of dF/dtheta |
|---|---|---|
| the 7-gate brick (4 points, 24 frames) | 12 | 7 of 7 |
| e2-0's 20 (`slp_8.txt`) | 156 of 1,344 | 20 of 20 |
| g-1's 19 (`h8_19gates.txt`) | **36 of 1,344; not the frame it was written in** | 19 of 19 |
| e2-3's 21 with wires entangled on three bits (`h8_21gates_drain.txt`) | 60 of 1,344 | 21 of 21 |

Full rank means each program is locally the only one with its wiring. Every circuit below the textbook that I tested moves in some frames.
The 21 is not only first order: `solve19.py` carried it in the frame columns (1, 2, 5) to tau = -2, -3, -1/2, -3/2 and the rounded
ratios replay exactly, all 8 outputs proportional to their rows (`solve21.out`, `drain21_tau_*.txt`; run on the Linux machine because

## 4. What the staircase turns into at the three degenerate kernels  [ran, `limit.py`, `limit19.py`, `limit.out`]

A wire is a projective point, so its limit is its leading part: for a wire tau^(table) that is the set of addresses where the table is
largest (tau -> infinity) or smallest (tau -> 0), or the whole support (tau -> 1). Scaling the inputs by tau^-(weight of x) first gives a
second limit in each direction; four limits with a zero in the kernel, one of rank one. A gate is *alive* if its limit is a new point
on the line through the two different limits of its inputs, *free* if its limit is a point already held, *dead* otherwise.

- **tau -> 1.** No gate dies. Every shift gate becomes free and exactly the N - 1 joins that build one all-ones wire stay alive:
  3, 7, 15, 31, 63, the true minimum for a rank-one target. The staircase is optimal at tau = 1.
- **tau -> 0 or infinity** (staircase; the best of the four limits, "tau -> 0 with inputs scaled"):

| N | staircase | free | alive | dead | limit program after repairs | textbook for a kernel with a zero |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| 8 | 20 | 9 | 10 | 1 | 12 | 12 (the exact minimum, d-0) |
| 16 | 52 | 24 | 25 | 3 | 33 | 32 |
| 32 | 128 | 60 | 60 | 8 | 82 | 80 |
| 64 | 304 | 144 | 140 | 20 | 198 | 192 |

  Free = (3/8) N log2 N exactly; alive = (5/16) N (k+1) and dead = N(k-1)/16 from 8 points on [fitted to five sizes].
  The other three limits: dead 1, 2, 6, 16, 40 = N(k-1)/8; 1, 4, 10, 24, 56 = N(k+1)/8; 0, 2, 5, 12, 28 = N(k+1)/16; limit programs
  5, 13, 36, 91, 217; 4, 13, 34, 89, 215; 4, 13, 33, 84, 200. Repairs are upper bounds (one gate if two held sets span the dead set,
  two with one helper, else a partition into held sets; the dead sets mostly cost 3). A repair cheaper than 3 on average would put
  subset sums below (N/2) log2 N; none of the four limits does it.
- **Every dead gate, at every size and in every limit (240 in the staircase runs, 5 in the 19), is of one kind: its two inputs have the same limit.** The output
  is then the next-order term, a difference quotient. In the 4-point brick with the subset-sum scaling: hub = x0 + eps (x1 + x2 + x3),
  and the all-ones row is (hub - (1 - eps) x0)/eps. This is d-5's "coefficients blow up near a limit point", seen gate by gate.
- The 19 at tau -> infinity with inputs scaled: 13 alive, 6 free, **0 dead**: it degenerates without loss into a 13-gate
  subset-sum program (minimum 12). In another limit it gives 12 after two repairs.

**Answer to the brief.** No saved gate disappears at any tau other than 0, 1, infinity. At tau = 1 each shift merges with its input
(ratio (tau-1) -> 0) and the program is optimal. At a kernel with a zero, a saved gate is not lost one for one: the target itself gets
cheaper by half, three eighths of the gates become unnecessary, and what dies is a thin layer (one gate in 12 to one in 6) of
difference-quotient gates, each replaced by about 3 honest ones. The hub is a device for a kernel without a zero; with a zero the
rows are already sparse and it has nothing to do.

## 5. One object: joins and shifts on integer tables  [ran, `gatekinds.py`, `gatekinds.out`]

In the staircase (4 to 64 points) and in the 19, every wire is tau^(e(x)) on its support with coefficient 1 (the exponent tables read
at tau = 2 and tau = 3 agree at every size; the symbolic listings show it at 4 and 8 points). Every gate is one of two moves:
*join* (the two supports are disjoint) or *shift by +1 or -1* (one support lies inside the other, and the new wire is the bigger
one with its table raised or lowered by one exactly on the smaller support). No other kind occurs.

| program | gates | joins | shifts +1 | shifts -1 | (N/2) log2 N |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| brick, 4 points | 7 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| the 19, 8 points | 19 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 12 |
| staircase 8 | 20 | 8 | 6 | 6 | 12 |
| staircase 16 | 52 | 20 | 16 | 16 | 32 |
| staircase 32 | 128 | 48 | 40 | 40 | 80 |
| staircase 64 | 304 | 112 | 96 | 96 | 192 |

The textbook is N log2 N joins and no shift. The records keep (N/4)(k+1) joins (staircase) or N - 1 (the 19: one centre) and add
**exactly (N/2) log2 N shifts**; the plain tensor power of the brick has (3/8) N log2 N joins and again (N/2) log2 N shifts [derived].
Two things I cannot explain: why the shift count is the subset-sum count in every program, and why raises equal lowerings in the
staircase and the 19 (they do not in the 21: 5 and 7, section 2).
[guess] A law of the form "fewer than N log2 N joins forces (N/2) log2 N shifts" would be an N log N lower bound for this kind of
program; g-1's one-centre result (exactly 12 shifts at 8 points, at least in its sign model) is the first instance.
The 4-point hub is the table (0,0,0,1) = x0 AND x1: it is one entry away from each of the four linear tables 0, x0, x1, x0 + x1.

**A partial explanation, for programs with one centre** [derived]. With one merge tree (N - 1 joins) the only free partners for a shift
are the tree's own nodes. If the tree splits one address bit at a time (b_1 at the top, b_k for the pairs), then, ignoring the rule
that the partner must still agree with the wire, two rows that differ in bit b_i are 2^(i-1) shifts apart (the half cube of b_i is a
union of 2^(i-1) nodes), and the cheapest tree that spans the N rows uses N/2^i links of length 2^(i-1) for each i:
**(N/2) log2 N shifts in all**. So in the 19 the shift count is the weight of a minimum spanning tree of the rows in the metric of
its own merge tree. Whether the staircase's count has the same reading I do not know.
Test at 16 points [ran late, `onecentre16.c`, on the Linux machine, tau = -1, g-1's sign model with the agreement rule]: see section 5b.

## 6. The question this turned into: the table game

*A wire is a partial table of integers on the addresses, up to adding a constant. Start: the N single addresses. Goal: the N full
tables e_w(x) = number of common 1-bits of w and x. Moves: join two tables with disjoint supports (any relative constant); shift a
table by one on the support of a held table that agrees with it there up to a constant. Count the moves.*
- A program in this game is a program for EVERY kernel of the class at once, and for different kernels on different bits.
- At tau = -1 a shift by two is free, so the Walsh-Hadamard transform is the same game with tables read **modulo 2**; at a root of unity
  of order n it is the game modulo n. That is the honest answer to "is there a special tau of another kind": the candidates are
  ordered by n, tau = -1 is the most special, then a cube root of unity, then i. At 4 points no n helps (section 3).
- Every record of tonight (7, 19, 20, 21, 52, 128, 304) lives in the integer game or moves to first order. **Nothing found so far uses
  "modulo 2".** If the order of the transform is to change, either the integer game itself has a short program (then the whole class
  is linear, as W5 said it must be), or there is a program that exists only modulo 2 and is rigid in all 1,344 frames. `deform.py` detects the second kind.
- The game is combinatorial and small: it should be searchable exhaustively at 8 points (is the minimum 19? 18?) and attackable by
  counting arguments, because a table has a discrete derivative along each bit and a shift changes it only on the boundary of a support.
- What a short program in the game would have to do [derived]. Two rows that differ in one bit differ by 1 on a half cube, so any table
  is at least N/4 single-address shifts away from one of them: shifts with single inputs as partners cost about N^2/4 in all and are
  useless beyond 4 points (the 4-point hub is the one case where N/4 = 1). Every useful shift is therefore on a large support, and its
  partner must be a held wire that still agrees with the current table there. The whole cost of the game is the cost of keeping
  large partners in agreement: the textbook rebuilds them (joins), the staircase reuses each half piece twice, the 19 uses the
  nodes of one merge tree. A program of linear size needs each large partner to serve about log N shifts.

## 7. Special values of tau, asked directly  [ran, `ident.py`, `ident.out`, tau a symbol]

1. K x K = u u^T + (tau-1) P, u = (1,1,1,tau): true.
2. R = P (K x K)/(tau-1) = I + v u^T/(tau-1), v = P u = (-tau, 1, 1, -1), satisfies R R = I for every tau: an oblique reflection.
   R x R at 16 points is an involution with trace 4, so its mirror has dimension 6 for every tau. v = u only at tau = -1.
3. K diag(1, -1/tau) K is diagonal for every tau: every kernel is its own inverse up to diagonals.
   K diag(1, -1) K = -(tau-1) [[0,1],[1,tau+1]]: a kernel with a zero. So **for every tau except -1, subset sums are two calls of the
   kernel with a sign change between**; exactly at tau = -1 the product collapses to a swap, and g-4's identity for that point uses three calls. For this identity the Walsh-Hadamard point is the worst one.
4. K^2 has a zero entry only at tau = i, -i (and is diagonal at -1). K^3 is a multiple of the identity only when tau^2 + tau + 2 = 0,
   tau = (-1 +- sqrt(-7))/2, the number e2-0's leak 2 met at 8 points [coincidence noted, not pursued]. These are properties of the
   matrix [[1,1],[1,tau]], not of its class (scalings destroy them), and they do not show in any count: the staircase and the 19 run at all of them, and the 4-point minimum stays 7.
