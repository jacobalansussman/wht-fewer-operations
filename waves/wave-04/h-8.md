**No circuit below the best known count and no growth law below N log N was found.**
Marks: [ran] = exact run of mine tonight (integers only; files in `h-8-provethewrongthi/`), [derived] = by hand tonight, checked by
nobody, [read] = from tonight's reports, [guess]. Gate count everywhere: **(G)**, a gate is any a*u + b*v, scalings free.

Words. N = 2^k points. *Wire*: a linear form the program holds, up to a scalar. s, s' = its number of non-zeros written in the
inputs and in the outputs; always s*s' >= N. *Tight*: s*s' = N; then s = 2^d and d is the wire's *level*. *Over the limit*: s*s' > N.
V = number of over-limit wires of a circuit, G = its number of gates. **L1** is the guess "N log2 N - G <= V" (j-cross).
*Tight line*: the set of all combinations a*u + b*v of two tight wires u, v; a gate is three wires on one line.
For any wire put sigma = log2 s and tau = log2(N / s'); tau <= sigma, with equality exactly for tight wires. The pair [tau, sigma]
is the wire's *interval*. *Threshold* t: a whole number 0..k-1. A gate *crosses* t when its output is above t and one input is at or below t.
*Jump* of a gate: the number of thresholds it crosses.

## 1. The frame, and why it is empty without a jump bound [derived]

**Lemma 0 (count of crossings).** Give every wire a level phi in 0..k, inputs 0, outputs k, any way you like. For every threshold
t the wires above t lie in the span of the outputs of the gates that cross t (a wire above t is not an input; its gate crosses t
or has both inputs above t). The outputs are independent, so at least N gates cross each t. Hence **sum over gates of (jump) >=
N log2 N**, and G >= N log2 N - sum over gates of (jump - 1).

**Remark.** Put every helper at level 0. Then only the gates that make an output jump, each by k, and the lemma reads N*k >= N*k.
For every circuit, min over level assignments of sum (jump - 1) equals the saving exactly. So the count proves nothing until the
levels are tied to s and s', and that is the only place where the uncertainty principle enters.

**Natural levels.** Tight wire: its level d. Over-limit wire: any whole number we choose; its interval says which choices are useful.
With tight wires only, a-2's gate lemma gives jump <= 1 and the count gives N log2 N.

**The accounting identity** [derived]: saving = (extra crossings) - (gates that cross nothing) - (crossings above N at some threshold).
- 4-point brick (g1 = x0+x1, g2 = x2-x3, hub = g1+g2, four outputs hub +- 2 x_i): each output gate jumps 2 (partner at level 0),
  4 extra crossings; the hub's own gate crosses nothing (1); threshold 0 is crossed 6 times (2 too many). 4 - 1 - 2 = 1.
- g-1's 19 gates at 8 points, hub-like wires at level 2 and the two (8,8) wires at level 0: jumps 1,1,1,1 (pairs), 1,1 (fours),
  0 (centre), 0, 0 (g8, g9), 2, 2 (g10, g11), 2,2,2,2 (y7, y1, y6, y0), 3, 3 (y2, y3), 1, 1 (y4, y5): total 26.
  Extra 10, gates that cross nothing 3, threshold 1 crossed 10 times (2 too many): 10 - 3 - 2 = 5.
So "each over-limit wire spares at most one crossing" is false already in the brick (one wire, four extra crossings). L1 survives
there because the waste is forced. A proof of L1 has to prove the waste.

## 2. What the uncertainty principle says about one gate [derived]

**Lemma 1 (gate lemma for intervals).** In a gate each of the three forms is a combination of the other two, so the largest s is
at most the sum of the other two, and the same for s'. Hence the two largest sigma of a gate are within 1 of each other, and the
two smallest tau are within 1 of each other. For tight wires this is a-2's lemma.

**Lemma 2 (one jump per wire).** Let h lie on the line through tight wires of levels b < c with c - b >= 2. Then
2^c - 2^b <= s_h <= 2^c + 2^b and N(2^-b - 2^-c) <= s'_h <= N(2^-b + 2^-c). The windows for different c do not overlap, nor do
those for different b. So b and c are functions of h: an over-limit wire can do ONE jump between tight wires, the jump across its
own interval. (Brick hub: s = s' = 4 at 4 points, interval [0, 2], jump from the inputs to the outputs.)

**Lemma 3 (no lone wide wire).** Let h lie on two different tight lines. Then each spans at most 2 levels and s_h s'_h <= (25/16) 4N.
Proof. Suppose one line is (u0 at level b, v0 at level c), c - b >= 3, h = a u0 + b' v0, and the other is (p, w), h = (w - e p)/f.
If the other line also spans 2 or more levels, Lemma 2 puts p at level b and w at level c. Then q = w - f b' v0 = f a u0 + e p is
written with two level-c wires and with two level-b wires, so s_q <= 2^(b+1) and s'_q <= N 2^(1-c): s_q s'_q <= 4N / 2^(c-b) < N,
so q = 0 and the two lines are the same line. If the other line spans at most 1 level, it gives s_h s'_h <= 4.5 N, while the
first line gives s_h s'_h >= (49/64) 8 N. Contradiction.
[ran, `gen1.py`, `gen1_4.out`, `gen1_8.out`: every non-tight wire that is the meeting point of two tight lines, with no assumption
on its entries.] 4 points: 84 such wires. 8 points: 3,248, in ten classes:

| (s, s') | how many | tight lines through it | widest line | tight wires one gate away, by level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (4,8) and (8,4), the full hubs | 56 + 56 | 7 | 2 levels | 4, 6, 4 |
| (3,8) and (8,3) | 224 + 224 | 4 | 2 | 4, 3, 1 |
| (4,6) and (6,4) | 224 + 224 | 4 | 2 | 1, 3, 4 |
| (4,5) and (5,4) | 448 + 448 | 7 | 1 | 10, 4 |
| (3,6) and (6,3) | 672 + 672 | 3 | 1 | 1, 5, 1 |

Largest s*s'/N: 4. The 64 tight lines of 8 points that span 3 levels (an input and an output) carry no non-tight wire that is on
a second tight line. 16 points [ran, `gen2.py wide 4`, on the Linux machine, `wide16.lx.out`]: 7,936 pairs of tight wires 3 or 4
levels apart, each tested against all 1,072 tight wires: no non-tight point of such a line lies on a second tight line. The 112 full hubs together with the 128 tight wires are the 240 stabiliser vectors of g-1's pool.

**Lemma 4 (hub theorem, g-1, used as read).** At most four tight wires of one level are one gate from h through partners two
levels down; with jump 3 or more, at most one.

## 3. The part of L1 that can be proved [derived]

**Proposition A.** If no gate of the circuit touches two over-limit wires, then G >= N log2 N - V.

Proof, as far as it goes. Every over-limit wire h that matters is made from two tight wires and read into tight wires, so Lemmas
2 to 4 apply. Wires whose tight lines all span at most 1 level get a level at which no gate jumps 2: they save nothing. The others
have a pair (b, b+2); put h at level b+1. The only gates that jump 2 are the *jumpers* (h, partner at level b) -> tight wire at
level b+2, at most four per hub. (For the eight classes at 8 points whose lines span at most 1 level I checked the level by
inspection of the census, not by a general argument.) Fix a threshold t. It is crossed by: ordinary tight gates and the jumpers
of hubs with b = t-1, whose outputs are all tight wires of level t+1 (call their number a_t); hubs with b = t; and the jumpers
of those hubs. Lemma 0 needs rank N.
*Rank-2 lemma.* A hub made from two tight wires u0, v0 of level b+1 is, on its four cells p1..p4 (tight wires of level b), a sum
with u0 = p1 +- p2 and v0 = p3 +- p4. A jumper output is w_i = (number) h + (number) p_i, so modulo u0 and v0 the outputs w1, w2
are parallel, and so are w3, w4. u0 and v0 come before h and lie in the span of the ordinary crossing outputs. So a hub and its
jumpers add rank at most 2. Hence a_t >= N - 2 V_t (V_t = hubs with b = t), level t+1 holds at least N - 2 V_t tight wires, the
hubs are V_t more gates, and summing over t gives G >= N k - V. QED for hubs with parents of level b+1.
The partial hubs ((3,8), (4,6) and their mirrors) have at most one jumper and need no rank lemma.
*The case I closed only at 8 points:* a full hub made by a flip, h = v0 - c*p0 with v0 tight at level b+2 (an output with one
cell flipped) and three jumpers. For b = 0 the rank-2 lemma still holds (the level-1 wires that make v0 are signed edges of a
graph on the points, and v0 in their span forces a second relation). For b >= 1 the count per level gives only rank 3, that is
G >= N k - 2 for that hub, and the missing gate has to be found one level lower: if the two halves of v0 are not unions of cells,
the level-b wires under them satisfy "their sum = the sum of the four cells", one wire too many at level b. At 8 points this
closes [derived, section 5]. At larger N I did not write the general case.

**What it covers** [read + derived]. e2-0's staircase (hubs with s*s' = 4N, everything else tight, no gate with two hubs) and all
tensor products of bricks. So: **in the class "over-limit wires never meet in a gate" the exchange rate is at most one gate per
wire, for every N.** [guess] At most N/4 hubs fit per level in that class; if so, (3/4) N log2 N + N/4, e2-0's count, is the exact
minimum of the class, and every circuit below it must contain a gate with two over-limit wires. g-1's 19 (below 20) does.

## 4. Where the proof stops [derived; ran]

**The first case I cannot do: two over-limit wires in one gate.** The proof used twice that the neighbours of h are tight:
Lemma 3 (the jump is at most 2) and the rank-2 lemma (jumper outputs are congruent to their partners modulo two TIGHT parents,
which sit at a known level). With an over-limit parent both go. The jump is no longer bounded, and "modulo the parent" is modulo
a wire that has no level of its own, so the count for one threshold becomes a statement about the whole connected group of
over-limit wires, which is L1 again.

**Smallest configuration** [ran, `gen2.py chain 3`, `chain8.out`; the same run on the Linux machine, `chain8.lx.out`, agrees].
8 points. h1 a full hub. h2 = a*h1 + b*t, t tight. h2 on a tight line (p, w). Classes found: 22 for the (4,8) hub, 22 for the
(8,4) hub. Among them, for each hub, exactly 16 pairs (h2, landing) in which **h2 has s = s' = 8 and the landing line is (an
input, an output): a gate that crosses all three levels.** Examples:
- h1 = (1,1,1,1,1,1,-1,-1); h2 = h1 + 2 x7 = (1,1,1,1,1,1,-1,1); output (1,...,1) = h2 + 2 x6. This is the kind of wire g-1's
  circuit uses (g8 = g7 - 2 x7, y2 = g8 + 2 x1).
- h2 = h1 + 2 (x6 - x7) = (1,1,1,1,1,1,1,-3); output (1,...,1) = h2 + 4 x7. Not a sign vector: the entry 3 is forced.
Three gates (h1, h2, landing), two over-limit wires, one landing of jump 3. In the count: the landing has 2 extra crossings, and
h1 and h2 can both be made by gates that cross nothing. Net zero. It becomes a gain only when h2 is read a second time, by another
over-limit wire (g-1: g10 = g8 + 2 x6, which then lands twice with jump 2).

**The step that fails, stated exactly.** L1 would follow from:
  *(W) a wire that lands on a tight wire with jump J is made, together with whatever else its making leaves behind, at the price
  of at least J - 2 gates that cross nothing or cross a threshold already crossed N times, and no two wires share that price.*
For a wire born from tight wires, (W) is Lemmas 3 and 4 plus the rank-2 lemma. For a wire born from an over-limit wire, the
making is one gate, h2 = h1 + c*t, it crosses nothing, and the price J - 2 would have to be found further back along the chain.
Nothing in the uncertainty principle says it is there: Lemma 1 bounds a gate by its LARGEST two sigma and SMALLEST two tau, and
in a gate (wide wire, small tight wire) -> (wide wire) those are the two wide wires. The gate is invisible to both ledgers
(rise from the smaller input: large; but s_w <= s_u + s_v is satisfied with room). At 8 points J <= 3, and in g-1's circuit
every landing of jump 3 is paid by one gate that crosses nothing [derived from the count in section 1]; [guess] that is why
8 points shows L1 with equality. At 16 points a wire "an output with one entry changed" lands with J = 4, and (W) asks for two
wasted gates per such wire. [derived] Such a wire is not one tight step from a full hub at 16 points (the difference has 3
non-zeros); the shortest chain I see is hub (16,4) -> output with a pair changed -> output with one entry changed, three
over-limit wires made by three gates that cross nothing, for one landing with 3 extra crossings: net zero again, unless the
chain is shared. **So the first place where (W) can be false is a TREE of such chains at 16 points: wires with s = s' = N,
each one gate from the next by a partner that is already held, each landing once on an output with jump 4, with shared trunks.**
[ran, `gen2.py chain 4`, on the Linux machine, `chain16.lx.out`] 16 points, all three kinds of full hub ((4,16), (8,8), (16,4)),
every tight partner t, every tight landing line: one tight step from a hub the widest landing spans 3 levels (48, 32 and 48
such landings), never 4. At 8 points it is 3 as well (16 per hub). So one tight step widens the jump of a hub by exactly one
at both sizes, and a wire of jump 4 is at least two tight steps from any hub. [guess] A wire of jump J is at least J - 2 tight
steps from a hub at every size; then an unshared chain always nets zero, and everything turns on how much of a chain can be shared.

**What forces "once": the wire, by Parseval; the price then moves to the partners** [derived]. For a sign vector f and the N
outputs y, the sum over y of (f . y)^2 is N^2. If f is within N/2 - delta*N sign changes of m outputs, then m (2 delta N)^2 <= N^2,
so m <= 1 / (4 delta^2). At distance N/4 (jump 2): at most 4 outputs, the hub theorem. At distance N/8 or less (jump 3 or
more): at most 1. A wire cannot be near many outputs. So a wire that serves many gates must serve them through LARGE partners
(a wire flat on both sides is about N/2 - sqrt(N)/2 from every output), and the question "can one wire pay for many gates"
becomes "can the large partners be made cheaply", which is the same question one step back. Two outputs differ on N/2 points,
so the walk from the neighbourhood of one output to the neighbourhood of another needs a partner of that size or a passage
through a hub. That is the geometry the 19-gate circuit lives in, and I see nothing in it that forbids a better passage at
16 points; I also did not find one.

**The drawing of an order-changing gate that the failed proof gives** [derived, a-2 section 6 read with Lemma 1]:
a gate's rise in log2 s, counted from its smaller input, is at most log2(1 + s_large / s_small). Gates between wires of equal
size rise by at most 1, whatever their excess. So a program with c*N gates needs most of its gates to be **"small wire + large
wire"**, with size ratio about N^(1/c), and, by the mirrored ledger of g-1, the output must be small on the output side. The
landing (input, flat wire) -> output is exactly that gate. The hub theorem says a flat wire lands this way once. So linear size
needs about N flat wires, each made in O(1) gates from another, each landing once: a walk of length about N among flat wires
that passes within one small step of every output. Two outputs differ on N/2 points, so the small steps of a walk between their
neighbourhoods must themselves be large-on-one-side wires made cheaply: a second walk, one level down (g-1's Round A says the same).

## 6. The published unit (d) [read: h-7.md, R/README.md section 6, h-7's `unitmeter.py` header; derived]

- **Does the unit do "that"?** In h-7's reading every wire of the unit is (a mixture of t sources) x (a tight wire on the address),
  and 28,135 of 56,715 moves act on mixtures. A layer of such a move is n gates, each with three over-limit wires of the same
  mixture. So half of the unit consists of the one kind of gate on which the proof of section 3 has no hold. In that sense yes.
  But no wire of it lands with a large jump: the excess is t^2 <= 107^2 at every size.
- **A contradiction to resolve** [derived]. a-2's ledger is rigorous: in any (G) circuit for W independent n-point transforms,
  the sum over gates of the rise of log2 s (from the smaller input to the output) is at least W n log2 n, and a gate whose two
  inputs have supports in ratio rho rises by at most log2(1 + rho). Take h-7's dictionary: (i) every wire is a mixture of at most
  108 sources times a frame row with 2^(f*dim) non-zeros; (ii) a move layer raises the dimension of one array by one in one
  column (rise 1 per gate), an addition joins two arrays in the same frame (rise at most log2 109 = 6.8); (iii) the unit takes W
  arrays from dimension 0 to dimension 45. Then moves >= 56,835 - 6.8 * 19,380 / f. The unit has 56,715. So for f above about
  1,090 the three statements cannot all be right, and h-7's own line "(G): saved gates (120 f - 19,380) n, first saving at
  f = 162" cannot hold for all large f.
  What I checked, in h-7's replay script only: additions are made at one common frame per gate (every register is first
  brought to the gate's frame), and a block's rank is the difference of two frame dimensions (the scratch copies come DOWN,
  72 moves per invocation, which the ledger counts as pure loss). With that, the number of upward moves is at least what the
  ledger demands for the registers' start and final frames, whoever's content ends where. **So the 120 saved moves cannot be
  inside an invocation as the certificate describes it. They must be in the bookkeeping between invocations** (the re-framing
  lemma, the twins "whose labels are equal at three moments", the five-stage word): places where the same array is read in
  two frames, or where the unit is not W independent transforms from dimension 0. I did not read the engine. [guess] If "the same
  array, two labels" is what happens, it is the level interval of this report in the engine's language: a wire that is allowed
  two levels.
- Whichever of (i) to (iii) gives way is where the published saving is made, in wire language. It is a finite question about
  files that exist.

## 8. The better question, and mutations

**The question.** Not "can one wire save two gates" but: **can a wire of full support on both sides be made in O(1) gates from
another such wire, land once on an output, and leave nothing wasted behind?** At 8 points the answer is yes with exactly enough
waste to keep L1. The first size where the waste could be missing is 16 points, in chains of wires "an output with one entry changed".

1. **`chain16`**: 16 points. Pool = tight wires + all wires one tight step from an output or from a hub (finite, from `gen2.py`).
   Search for a connected group of over-limit wires whose landings have total (jump - 1) larger than the group's size plus its
   forced waste. This is (W) tested where it can first fail.
2. **`ledger-vs-unit`**: resolve section 6. For the published certificate print, per addition and per boundary between
   invocations, the support sizes of the wires on both sides. Find the gates whose two inputs differ in size by a factor that
   grows with f, or show that the unit is not W independent transforms.
3. **`n-over-4`**: prove or break "at most N/4 isolated hubs per level". With Proposition A it would make (3/4) N log2 N + N/4
   the exact minimum for circuits without meetings: a clean theorem about e2-0's family.
4. **`flip-general`**: finish Proposition A for flip-made hubs at b >= 1 (the two-level relation of section 3).
5. **`minj-levels`**: rewrite the integer programme with level-indexed variables and the proved per-level counts as cuts; then
   "least count with at most j over-limit wires" at 8 points in the pool of 304 for j = 2, 3 is a morning's run.
6. **`small-plus-large`**: count, in every record circuit, the gates whose inputs differ in size by 4 or more, and their rise.
   Section 4 says the saving should be carried by exactly those gates (c19: the six "input + full wire" gates).
